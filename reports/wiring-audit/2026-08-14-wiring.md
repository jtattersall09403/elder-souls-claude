## 0. The headline finding, before the five gaps

**Three of the five candidate gaps are already substantially built in the tree, and the roadmap's own
per-item state text and the gap ledger's `what` field describe the *round-1* state of the work, not
today's.** This is not a criticism of the roadmap audit (it is dated `2026-08-14`, honestly labelled
"derived from `ROADMAP-COVERAGE-AUDIT`", and its own §0 already flags `derived_state` as unreliable) — it
is a fact about how fast wave 1 has been moving under it, and it means **the real answer to "is wiring
cheap" cannot be read off the ledger table; it has to be read off the tree**, which is what this audit
did.

The mechanism, found three separate times independently below (§1, §3, §4), is structural and worth
naming once: **`tools/gap-ledger.mjs` only accepts a gap closure filed by a *later wave's* critic** (rule 2,
`GAP-LEDGER.md` header). Wave 1 has had many internal remediation rounds (r1→r2→r3…) that functionally
fixed a gap and had a critic re-measure it — but because both the opening and the closing verdict are
still wave 1, the ledger **cannot credit the closure**, and the gap sits at `status: open` forever until
wave 2 exists. `crit-w1-00-r2-k4m9`'s own `gap_closure` note says this explicitly (§3). The practical
effect: **`0 closed` (GAP-LEDGER.md header) is true by construction, not because nothing has been fixed.**

### 0.2 What this changes about "is cheap-wiring a first-class kind of increment"

The roadmap's own claim 2 (`ROADMAP.md` "Three things this ordering encodes") — *"wiring what already
exists is the cheapest visible progress available"* — turns out to be doing less work than it looks like,
**because for I1, C1 and F11 there is no more wiring left to do.** The actual cheap increment sitting in
front of the orchestrator right now is **not "wire it", it is "judge it"** — dispatch independent critics
against work that is already built, which is a different (and genuinely cheaper) kind of ring-0/ring-1
increment than the roadmap currently has a slot for. This matters for sequencing: none of these three
should be scheduled as *build* work at all; scheduling them as build work wastes a builder round re-doing
something already done, which is exactly the "manufactured work" failure `CLAUDE.md`'s width discussion
warns about.

### 0.3 The sweep, briefly

The regex-13 and hand-25 counts in `AUDIT.md` §2 are themselves undercounts, by direct check: I traced
the consumption census's #1 finding — `combat/movesets/*.json` every slot's `trigger: {button, modifier,

census's 648 and the ledger's ~25 are not fully overlapping sets even on this one sample; a real
reconciliation is a follow-up task in its own right, not something this pass had budget for.

## Question 2 — does character/creature *design* have a home?

**Short answer: creature design has a home (`RI-VIS05` §D4, cross-referenced from `RI-VIS08` §D and from
`RI-VIS01`'s bifurcation table row `P04`). Playable-race and NPC humanoid character *design* — as opposed
to creation mechanics, race's social/mechanical effects, or fidelity/model-quality — does not, and
`RI-VIS01`'s own property table declares itself exhaustive and closed, which makes this a checkable
absence rather than a guess.**

**Where RI-VIS08 §D stops, read directly from the file** (`corpus/70-visual/RI-VIS08-character-animation-
fidelity.md` lines 213-239): the seam table is explicit —

| question | side | item |
|---|---|---|
| Is the naga shaped like something that could not be a person? | ART | `RI-VIS05` §D4 |
| Is the naga's silhouette readable at 50 m? | FIDELITY | here (§D1) |
| Is its skin the right colour for Black Marsh? | ART | `RI-VIS05` §C |
| Does its skin respond to light like skin? | FIDELITY | §B6 |
| Does it move wrongly (alien-design sense)? | ART | `RI-VIS05` §D4 |
| Does it move badly (technical sense)? | FIDELITY | §C |

So VIS08 stops at readability/technical-execution; **design** (shape, "could not be a person," alien
movement) is handed to `RI-VIS05` §D4 — but §D4, read in full, is a *fauna* rule: "no horses, no wolves,
no bears... fauna reads as arthropod, reptile, or amphibian." Its one humanoid mention is the **naga** —
"the humanoid apex: heavier, longer-tailed and lower-slung than Argonians, crested" — a *creature*, not a
playable race.

**Checked for a home on the other side of the seam** — playable/NPC humanoid race design (what an
Argonian, Dunmer, Khajiit etc. is *designed* to look like in this game, as distinct from mechanics):
- `corpus/22-character/RI-CHR01/02/03` — read in full. `RI-CHR01` is creation *flow* (`judges:
  character.creation.flow/identity/irreversibility, ...`). `RI-CHR02` is race's *social and mechanical*
  effect ("a Dunmer and a Saxhleel walking into the same room must get a different room" — dialogue,

  judges appearance.
- `RI-VIS01-bifurcation-protocol.md`'s property table (lines 44-73) — the file states outright, line 41:
  *"This table is exhaustive and closed. A property not listed is `UNASSIGNED` and the critic MUST file a
