CONFIDENCE: high

- A is a dialogue table on visible display: the Mossanon reply is stamped out three times verbatim because three NPCs share the topic, and the bundle runs alphabetically ("A High Elf…", "A mabrigash…", "A miserable place…", "A prosperous day…", "A rare thing…", "A reputation…", "A rumor says…"). Sort order is not writing.
- B is a rumour list and every single item is the same rhetorical object: a concrete anomaly plus a withheld explanation. "There is a guar standing in the herbwife's yard that is not hers, will not move, and has been there since the rain stopped." "A hunter walked back out of the deep marsh standing up and has not moved since. He is warm and he is looking at us." Eleven rumours, one shape, one intelligence behind all of them.
- B's tell is that it *has* contractions and they are unevenly distributed — "The Legion hasn't been paid since Sun's Height", "There's a milestone", "you can't find", "the customs house would rather it wasn't", "That's a customs matter" — sitting beside untouched formal lines like "It cannot be walked quietly" and "there is a mortuary town down the coast that has missed a reading". That patchiness reads like a pass applied to some lines and not others, rather than speakers who differ.
- B's numbers are the authored kind again: "two seasons", "four hundred years", "Eleven boxes came off an imperial bond at Archon", "six crates of lamp oil". "Eleven boxes" doing plot work in a rumour is a designed motif.
- B lands the reversal beat on line after line: "and everyone has agreed not to say so", "and answered none", "and he says it twice", "The scribe there wants a name." A's lines end flatly on facts and referrals ("Why don't you ask Sinyaramen?", "Too cold for me").
- A carries emotional temperature B has none of — "it's about damn time", "A rare thing indeed. Thanks." B is uniformly cool and knowing across every rumour.

WEAKEST POINT: rumour banks are supposed to be uniform hooks, so "every item has the same shape" may be the register doing its job rather than a machine leaving a fingerprint.
