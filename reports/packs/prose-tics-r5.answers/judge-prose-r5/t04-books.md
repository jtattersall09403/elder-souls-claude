CONFIDENCE: high (both axes)

- B is a gazetteer of harvestable ingredients with the effect stated for each one — glide, resist fire, poison, enhance appearance, strength, stamina, sleep, marksmanship, sneak. Eight entries, eight distinct potion effects, one province named per entry. That is an alchemy table paraphrased into paragraphs, and it is a form only a shipped game ships.
- B's hedging formula recurs verbatim across entries — "It is said that…", "It is said, however, that…", "Legend has it that…", "is reputed to be…" — which is the house style of an in-world reference book written to be non-committal about the numbers in the actual item.
- B addresses "the adventurer" as a species ("the only nourishment for adventurers who find themselves in the wilderness without rations", "lethal to the touch", "needs to be avoided by adventurers, especially unprotected ones"). Nobody in a real herbal writes to adventurers.
- A is a single-voice antiquarian monograph with a method and a grudge: one rod, four seasons, a constant ratio ("each terrace is seven parts in nine of the one below it. Not approximately."), and a falsifiable measurement offered so the reader can check it — 140 paces against 109.
- A's best move is epistemic honesty rather than lore: "On the interiors I can say almost nothing and I will not pretend otherwise", and the refusal to draw the obvious conclusion about the imported limestone "in a volume of measurements".
- A also builds the world sideways rather than by assertion — the causeway that runs *between* third terraces and ignores the ground, the informant who says the second building was "built lower", the man of the marsh warning that "the wrong sort of insistence in this province is a way of being found later". Its last line — "The reader who wants a theory is directed to the second volume, which he will enjoy more and should trust less" — is a thesis about evidence, in a book.

WEAKEST POINT: A's narrator is the same measuring, self-correcting, fragment-punctuating first person as several other passages in this pack ("Site after site. Four seasons. One rod."), so its quality may be one writer's single good trick rather than range.
