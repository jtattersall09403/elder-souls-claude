- A is a systems manual in fiction's clothing: it enumerates the raise-undead tiers the game will implement (fresh/decayed corpse → skeleton → zombie → mummified servant), each with an input cost and a stated mechanical benefit — "Your servant may be weaker each time this is done"; "will offer some protection as well"; "a much stronger servant who will follow your commands with more independence". That is a stat table with paragraphs round it.
- A carries the two habits of shipped in-world how-to books: the second-person instructional address to an apprentice reader ("If you wish to create a zombie servant, one need only…") and a regional gazetteer aside naming a province for flavour ("The ravenous mudcrabs … can strip a corpse down to its bones in a matter of days. Lesser crabs in other provinces can do the same in a matter of weeks").
- A also slips person mid-sentence — "If you wish to create a zombie servant, **one** need only" — an unedited-copy tell.
- B is a folk parable with a designed sting: the clerk counts 400 fish, the heron eats while he counts, next year there are 60, and the lease is still sold for 400 "because that is the number in the bigger book."
- B's last move is the one A never attempts: it turns the fable on the listener — "the question is not what the clerk did wrong. The question is: what did the village do wrong? The answer that is wanted is: nothing, and that is the trouble with it." That is a piece of writing with a position on its own moral, told as something adults tell children before letting them near water.
- B's repetition ("The heron said nothing, because a heron says nothing") is doing oral-transmission work rather than filling space.

WEAKEST POINT: B's flatness and its refusal of any proper detail could equally be an LLM's default "folk tale" register, and A at least contains domain knowledge (natron, catgut, the salt bath) that someone had to go and look up.
