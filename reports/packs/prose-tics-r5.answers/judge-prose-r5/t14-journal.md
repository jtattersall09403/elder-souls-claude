CONFIDENCE: high (both axes)

- A is two consecutive quest tables run together: a love-potion quest (delivery, thanks, reward, the outcome-A "he is unhappier than he could have imagined", the outcome-B "he took my advice and courted the homely [X]") followed immediately by the opening state of an unrelated sabotage quest.
- A states its own success/fail conditions in the log — "Khaudra tells me I must place the bone in a chest near the merchant's bed… If I am spotted doing this, though, the magic will not work" — followed by both resolutions as adjacent entries ("I have successfully placed…" / "While trying to sneak… I was spotted"). That is a stage table.
- A's rewards are inventory events dressed as sentences: "He gave me a light ring as a token of his thanks"; "He also rewarded me with a [named item]". It also carries a masking-orphaned article — "He finds the annoying, clinging, and vapid" — which I note as machinery, not evidence.
- B is a branch set where every ending is a defeat of a different kind: the target files correctly and survives, or is relieved within the day, or is read out of a resignation he never wrote — "he thanked me for it, which I did not expect and did not enjoy."
- B's costs are relational and they persist: "Braer has stopped writing to me and the [superior] has started"; "He has written to the [superior] about me."
- B converges its branches on one world-state that outlives the quest, and states the stakes as a rule rather than a feeling: "Anything sealed cannot be reopened in this province and can only be reopened in [the capital], and [the capital] is two letters a year and has never once written back."

WEAKEST POINT: B's four branches are so close in tone and vocabulary that a player would struggle to tell from the log which one they are in — the failure mode A's blunt, redundant entries exist specifically to avoid.
