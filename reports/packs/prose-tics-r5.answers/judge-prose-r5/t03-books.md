CONFIDENCE: high (PROV), medium (QUAL) — this is the one trial where I think the reference is the better writing

- A is liturgical/apocalyptic verse-prose of a kind only a shipped game has ever put in front of a player at length: a god narrating himself as a city, in the second person of scripture, with a closing formula — "The ending of the words is [X]" — that reads as a sermon's ritual sign-off, and a numbered-lesson frame implied by "The [title] of the [X]:" opening.
- A's diction is deliberately anachronistic-cosmic in a way our own prose never risks: "star-wounded", "trans-immortal", "Hyper eyed signposts along my traffic arm", "I am a million-eyed insect dreaming". A placeholder writer does not produce "world without am."
- B is a witness deposition: measured, first-person, retrospective, with a legal spine ("The terms were put to the elders over four nights… That we would not be taken by the sickness, and that this would hold for all of us and not for some").
- B is very good — its argument that "the thing that is wrong with the bargain is not in the terms, it is in what we thought the words meant" is a real idea, and the detail that "by the following spring there were four households saying no and the four were not the same four" is the best single line in the trial.
- But B is the same narrator I keep meeting: plain declarative, anaphoric "That…" clauses, self-interrupting asides ("and I am glad she asked it"), a fondness for "which is" and for closing on a distinction. A is a voice no one else could have written; B is a voice I have now read four times today.
- I am scoring QUAL on invention and on whether the passage does something only it can do. On that test A wins and it is not close, even though B is the more competent sentence-by-sentence performer.

WEAKEST POINT: A is arguably not writing at all but glossolalia that cannot be wrong, and "judged as writing" could reasonably mean "does it hold together", on which B wins outright.
