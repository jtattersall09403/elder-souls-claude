CONFIDENCE: high (PROV), high (QUAL) — the two axes disagree here and that is the point

- B is a lecture-frame lore delivery of a kind shipped RPGs use constantly: a named teacher, two named students who exist only to ask the questions the designer wants answered ("He codified the praxes and rituals used by all modern potionmakers, itemmakers, and spellmakers, didn't he?"), and a closing "It's time for your test" that ends the fiction the moment the systems exposition is done. The passage is a manual for the enchanting/alchemy economy wearing a story.
- B's register slips in a way in-house game text does and edited fiction does not: "The tools he invented were like robotic master enchanters" — "robotic" in a magic-guild history — and "a unschooled yokel" (article disagreement) survives into shipped text.
- A is built as a pastiche with a payoff: five chapters of a deliberately bad orientalist romance ("Imagine fountains. Imagine, in each fountain, a fish of gold.") followed by two publisher's notices that dismantle it, ending on the bookseller's letter — "there is not one other book in the whole of your printing in which one of us is looked at for a whole book by a writer who is trying to work out what we are, rather than what we are for."
- That structure is the argument: A is *about* how the colonising culture writes the colonised, and it earns its ending by first spending 2,000 words being the bad book. B has no ambition beyond delivering the guild's founding and stops mid-anecdote deliberately.
- A's prose control is visibly higher at sentence level: "she had learned the seasons of that country as she had learned its speech, badly and completely"; "in a gown of spider-silk … ascended those terraces one at a time with her chin up and her heart, if the truth be told, in a state."
- A's chapter headings are corrupted by the masker ("CHAPTER THE Vrias", "CHAPTER THE Dournfaush") which is machinery, not authorship, and I have ignored it.

WEAKEST POINT: A is *so* aware of its own device — the joke about the city's name meaning "paid-for-underneath", the correspondent "becomes technical" — that a judge could read the whole thing as a modern literary writer performing a fake-19th-century pastiche, which is exactly what a placeholder written to impress a critic would look like.
