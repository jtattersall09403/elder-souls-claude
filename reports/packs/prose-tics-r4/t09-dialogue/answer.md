- A prints the same 46-word line three times, verbatim, back to back: "A [NAME-1] [NAME-2] named [NAME-3]? I don't know anyone by that name... Why don't you ask [NAME-7]? He's a [NAME-1] [NAME-2] here in the [NAME-8], and a trader. He might know." Three NPCs share one answer; the export keeps all three. No writer types that twice, let alone three times.

The manifestation of her dark power is called a 'ghost snake' that paralyzes and drains a victim's vitality." Paralyse-and-drain is a status effect with a fiction wrapped round it.
- A also mixes in a scheduling line and a politics line that no single speaker could both say: "Too cold for me. I understand there's someone in [NAME-11] who will take folks there, though" and "A rumor says the [NAME-15] has repealed the [NAME-16] [NAME-17]... it's about damn time."
- B is one merchant, continuously, with a consistent sales philosophy and a running joke about a surcharge. It is tighter and it is more interesting: "There is no surcharge on you. There is one on nearly every soul who asks me that, and the fact that you had to ask means you have not been away long enough to be charged it yet." Nothing in A is written that well. It is still one person where a shipped game needs a population.

Both of those are the sap and there is no version of it that is only the first thing" — is exactly the kind of two-beat item description a professional writes to make a consumable memorable, and it is better than any item text in A.
