I know you are concerned with security."
- B carries a whole quest chain compressed into a letter: two partners, an artifact, faked letters, a faked death, a lycanthropic caretaker, a family crypt, and the artifact vanishing at the end "as great [NAME-44] artifacts always do" — the standard shipped-RPG excuse for why the reader cannot have it.
- A is a magistrate's casebook: three cases, each headed in capitals, each ending in a finding. It has no addressee, no prop function, and nothing in it can be picked up or fought.
- Recorded against my own pick: A is much the better prose, and one paragraph in it is better than anything in B — the interpreter's argument that "a summons which arrives written and unread is not a summons at all, and that a man who cannot read it and will not say he cannot read it will do the only thing that leaves his dignity where it was, which is nothing." B has no sentence within reach of that.

WEAKEST POINT: A's three case headings and its willingness to record a decision it lost ("I could not decide it on the books... The [NAME-5] has not brought it again") is exactly the kind of structural discipline a professional writer imposes and an improviser does not.
