CONFIDENCE: high

- B contains a systems manual pasted into an NPC's mouth: "A soul gem's power limits the number of an item's uses, but the gems renew their expended power over time", "A magic scroll is the simplest enchanted item; anyone can use it to cast a single spell with 100% reliability, but the scroll is destroyed in the process." The phrase "100% reliability" is a tooltip, not speech.
- B contains a bare directory of who holds which post — "[NAME-7] [NAME-8] is the priest of the [NAME-9] [NAME-10]", "[NAME-17] [NAME-18] is publican of [NAME-9] [NAME-19]", "[NAME-20] [NAME-21] is publican of..." — six or seven in a row, which is a table of NPC records read aloud.
- B opens with a paragraph of comparative anthropology settling a taxonomy dispute ("on the basis that individuals of all three groups can mate with one another"). That is a lore document, and a shipped RPG puts lore documents in NPC topics because that is where the player will look.
- B leaves the masking incomplete in one place — a personal name survives as "gro-Bagrat" because it starts lowercase — which tells me the underlying source names people in a fixed pattern that a redaction rule tuned for capitalised words could not catch. That is a property of a large pre-existing name set.
- A is five location hooks, each a compressed argument in the same voice: "Two clerks, one lamp, and a strongbox that is only open between the eighth and ninth bell... It is very hard to be robbed in an hour you choose yourself." Better lines, one throat.

WEAKEST POINT: A's causeway hooks give the same place three times from three different jobs — a scholar's version, a quartermaster's joke, a villager's rumour — and writing one location from three professional angles is a deliberate, expensive craft choice that argues A is the professional's.
