CONFIDENCE: high

- B is not one passage but five discrete fables bundled together, each with its own title and each closing on an explicit tagged moral: "And so [NAME-1] [NAME-2] discovered that the idle amusements of one may be the solemn tortures of another"; "things are not so bad as they seem. For there is always someone worse off than you"; "thoughtless companions may bring more harm than help." A shipped library ships collections; an essayist writes one thing.
- B lifts recognisable real-world fables wholesale and re-skins them with local fauna — the boiled-frog parable told with a kagouti, the hares-and-frogs fable told with two local vermin, physician-heal-thyself told at a wizard. Re-skinning is a production technique, not an authorial one.
- B's creature nouns are unglossed and load-bearing: scribs, shalk, kagouti, foyada. Nothing stops to explain them, which only works if the reader is inside a world that already ships them.
- A is a single archivist's note about a unit change in a ledger — ninths becoming twelfths on "leaf two hundred and six" — and its whole content is a measurement problem the author refuses to resolve.
- Recorded against my own pick: A is the better piece of writing and it is not close. "it never occurred to this house that a stick was a document" and the closing "if you have come down here to be told, the door is behind you and it sticks" are two of the best lines in the pack. But a book whose entire payload is an unresolved units dispute is a piece of worldbuilding apparatus, not the sort of thing a player is handed.

WEAKEST POINT: A's refusal to settle its own question — "I have added it both ways. I am not going to tell you which way I added it first" — is a confident authorial choice, and confident authorial choices are what a game writer is paid for.
