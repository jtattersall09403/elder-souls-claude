PICK: A
CONFIDENCE: high

- A embeds a combat tutorial in dialogue, which is the most game-writerly thing in the whole pack: "it takes twice as much power to send force than it does to deflect it. When your eye can recognize whether your opponent is striking from above, or at angle, or in an uppercut from below, you learn to pivot and place your shield just so." That is a Block skill-book teaching a mechanic through a character.
- A has a plot with a withheld reveal and a body count: the mother's suicide, the twin sold as a babe, the mirror-duel, and the witch who "knew the truth that the twins had met already. She remained silent and stared into the fire, banishing the thoughts from her head, too wise to tell all." The last line exists to be re-read.
- A carries the small quantitative brags shipped in-world books are made of — "I have been in thirty-six battles, and I haven't a scar to show for them", "scattering him across the valley floor in eighty-seven pieces", "never once in all his thirty-four years had he lost in hand-to-hand combat."
- B is a traveller's memoir in the same first-person analytic voice as the essayistic side of several other trials — capitalised section headings, an argument with a named scholar, an ethnographic finding ("On the government of the interior I can report the following, which is the whole of what I established: there is none").
- Recorded against my own pick: B contains the single best passage in the pack, and I want it on the record because no metric will find it — the old woman at the well asking "to you. When you are finished. Where do you go", and then "she said: I am sorry. I thought you had been told and were keeping it." Nothing in A is in the same room as that.

WEAKEST POINT: B's willingness to end a travel book by telling the reader not to travel — "Go to [NAME-14]. Take the coast. Do not go inland" — is a structural joke of the kind a professional makes deliberately, and it argues B is the professional's work.
