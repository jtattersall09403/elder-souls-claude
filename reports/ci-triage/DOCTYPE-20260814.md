## 1. The types that actually exist

I did not assume the split was two-way. I read the top-level field layout of all 76 documents in
`corpus/90-verdicts/`. Sixty-nine carry the verdict shape. Seven do not, and they fall into two
groups that are not the same kind of thing at all:

**Group A — a real type, three members, one discipline.** `W1-PROSE-BLIND-r1`,
`W1-PROSE-TICS-r4`, `W1-22-B2-blind`. All three declare `critic.role: "judge.blind.<domain>"`.
All three record a pack, a seal, a reveal outcome, a leak audit, a no-peek attestation and a
ruling on admissibility. They are scored against `corpus/80-methods/RI-MTH03-...md`, not against a
build bar. **The type was in the file the whole time and nothing read it.**

**Group B — four ad-hoc serialisations, no type.** `W1-TOUCH-r1` and `W1-MAP-r1` declare
`"schema": "elder-souls/verdict@1"`; `W1-DEPLOY-r1` and `W1-FACTIONS-r1` use the same family of
field names (`piece`/`item`, `role`, `verdict`, `score`, `gate`, `axes`) without declaring
anything. **No two of the four share a field layout.** One name per instance is the absence of a
type, not the presence of one.

I also checked the three candidates the brief named as possible hidden types. **None of them is
one, and none of them is in this directory:** arbiter S-rulings live in
`corpus/00-doctrine/ARBITRATION.md` as prose sections; plan critiques live in
`orchestration/status/*-critic.json` (e.g. `PLAN-COST-EXPERIMENTS-critic.json`), outside the
gate's sweep entirely; instrument self-tests are a tool's `--self-test` exit code, not a document.
Adding types for them would have been inventing contracts nothing files against.

So: **two types.**

| | `elder-souls/critic-verdict@1` | `elder-souls/blind-judgement@1` |
|---|---|---|
| schema | `corpus/00-doctrine/verdict.schema.json` | `corpus/00-doctrine/blind-judgement.schema.json` (new) |
| what it is | a critic measured a built piece against reference items | a judge answered a masked pack without seeing the piece |
| evidence | `artifacts[]`, each with a `produced_by` command, resolving in a fresh clone | the pack itself, named in `pack` |
| the build | `arbitration.ar1/ar2` mandatory | **none — there is no build to arbitrate** |
| reference items | scored: `native_scale`, `score_0_10`, `evidence[]` | context only, and may be declared **unread** |
| outcome vocabulary | `PASS`/`FAIL`/`VOID`/`PROVISIONAL`/`RECUSED` | RI-MTH03's: meets the bar / below bar / **inadmissible** |
| may decline to score | no — unmeasurable is 0, fail-closed | **yes**, in writing (§4) |

### It is additive, and that was a constraint rather than an aspiration

Dispatch (`tools/verdict-validate.mjs` `documentType()`), in order:

1. explicit `document_type` wins;
2. otherwise a registered `schema` identifier;
3. otherwise a `critic.role` beginning `judge.` infers `blind-judgement`, **with a warning** —
   inference is a migration aid, not the contract;
4. otherwise **critic verdict**.

Step 4 is the load-bearing one. The default is the *stricter* type, so omitting the field can
never buy leniency, and the ~90 documents that were already conformant take exactly the code path
they took before — no `document_type`, no `judge.` role, no change.

`elder-souls/verdict@1` is **retired, not renamed**, and the validator refuses it by name with the
reason. Adopting it for either side would have handed the ambiguity forward.

## 2. Obligations, not field names

The three judgements record all six RI-MTH03 obligations and **no two spell any of them the same
way** — `pack` vs `blind_comparisons[].pack_ids`; `answers_sealed_before_reveal` vs
`blind_discipline` vs `sampling_rule_declared_before_sampling`. Requiring one spelling would have
left all three red for a naming choice.

So each obligation is checked **by role**, against a closed, enumerated table of the field names
this corpus actually uses, and every non-canonical hit warns naming the canonical key:

| obligation | why it is not optional | canonical key |
|---|---|---|
| the pack | you cannot re-open a judgement without it | `pack` |
| the seal | RI-MTH03 M3 — hash the answers before the reveal. The one claim that makes a blind result auditable rather than trusted | `seal` |
| the reveal outcome | RI-MTH03 hard-fails a document reporting the pick but not the reveal | `results` |
| the leak audit | M6. r1 of the prose pack was decidable 17/17 on setting proper nouns; r4's fix for that leaked mask-token density at 15/15 | `leak_audit` |
| no-peek | RI-MTH03 hard fail; RULES rule 25 | `quarantine` |
| admissibility | below 75% of applicable points the blind result is inadmissible and the item falls back to non-blind scoring. **This, not PASS/FAIL, is a judgement's outcome** | `admissible` |

The table is finite and auditable — a future judge cannot invent a seventh spelling — and
`--self-test` deletes each obligation in turn and requires the document to go red. That is what
separates this from a lenient escape hatch, and it is checked rather than promised.

## 3. `W1-TOUCH-r1` — a malformed critic verdict, ruled, not relabelled

The brief allowed either reading. I rule it **a critic verdict, not a distinct type**, and I did
not edit the file.

**Why it is a verdict.** `role: "critic"`, `verdict: "FAIL"`, score 6 against gate 7, judging a
built piece against `RI-JRN04 §G`, `RI-JRN01 O17` and `RI-MTH07`. It has every defining property
of the type. It is also, on reading it, one of the better critic documents in the corpus — nine
findings, four of its own instrument errors published, a CONSUMPTION block with four perturbed
models and a null control that is genuinely inert. The triage's guess that it "reads as an
unfinished draft" is wrong and I am correcting it here.

**Why it is not a type.** A type with one member per instance is not a type (§1, Group B).
Declaring a `narrative-verdict@1` to make one document validate would legitimise a hole every
future critic could step through, and it would be exactly the "nothing counts because someone says
so" failure this gate exists to prevent.

**What I changed:** nothing in the file. The validator now reports the false claim as its own
named error rather than as eight anonymous "missing required field" lines:

> `declares "schema": "elder-souls/verdict@1" but does not implement it. That identifier is
> RETIRED: four documents in this corpus claim it and no two share a field layout, so it names no
> contract. State "document_type": "critic-verdict" or "blind-judgement" instead.`

Its error count goes 21 → **22**. That is the right direction: the instrument names the lie and
the author keeps their words.

**Which fields are missing, and which of them can be supplied by anyone at all.** This is the part
that decides the owner.

| field | recoverable? |
|---|---|
| `schema_version`, `piece_id`, `wave` | **yes, clerical** — `piece: "W1-TOUCH"`, `round: 1`, and the file is in `wave1/` |
| `subsystem_paths[]` | **no** — a canonical taxonomy path is a coverage claim; picking one for someone else's piece is inventing what it covers |
| `bifurcation` | **no, structurally** — it asserts the axis was declared *before* any visual reference was cited. A temporal claim cannot be retrofitted a week later |
| `reference_items[].score_0_10` / `native_scale` / `evidence[]` | **no** — the critic scored seven **axes**, not items. Per-item 0–10 scores were never taken. Deriving them from the axes would be a fabricated measurement |
| `arbitration.ar1/ar2` | **partly** — a full CONSUMPTION check was run and passed; whether it satisfies AR-1 or AR-2 is the arbiter's mapping, not mine |
| `artifacts[].produced_by` | **no** — six artifact paths are cited inside `findings[]`, but the exact command that made each is a claim about how it was run |
| `self_audit` (7 booleans) | **no** — self-attestations only the agent that did the work can make |

**Owner: the W1-TOUCH critic, or a fresh critic taking round 2.** Counted rather than estimated:
of the twenty-two errors, **ten are clerical** — the file already carries the value under another
name (`piece` → `piece_id`, `verdict: "FAIL"` → `status`, `score: 6` → `score.overall_0_10`,
`gate: 7.0` → `pass_threshold`, `scoring: "min-over-axes"` → `aggregation`,
`critic.task_id` → `run_id`, plus `schema_version`, `wave` and the retired-identifier line) — and
**the other twelve are the rows in the table above**: fields whose values were never taken in the
form the schema asks for. W1-TOUCH-r1 already carries a five-item round-2 brief. The cheapest honest path is a round 2 that files a conformant
verdict, at which point r1 stops gating under the round rule and stays as history. **A backfill
pass over r1 is the wrong instrument and I am naming it as such rather than leaving it as an
option.**

## 4. The two answers the schema could not represent

Both are the same failure at different depths: *an instrument that cannot express an honest answer
will get lied to.*

### 4a. "NOT READ - quarantined"

`W1-22-B2-blind`'s judge wrote that string into a `path` slot, for `RI-AUD03` — the very item its
pack served, deliberately unread, *"which is the point of the exercise."* It is the most honest
line in the file, and the gate went red at it. **The schema was what was wrong.**

The structured form is now `reference_items[].not_read: { reason, why }`, with `reason` from a
closed enum. Two guards stop it becoming an escape hatch, and both are self-tested:

- **A `not_read` item may carry no `score_0_10`.** You cannot score an item you did not read.
- **It is permitted only on a blind judgement.** A build critic that could not measure an item
  already has a fail-closed way to say so — `measured: "unmeasurable"`, `score_0_10: 0`. Letting a
  critic declare an item unread would be a route around scoring it, and the validator refuses it
  by name.

The legacy free-text form is grandfathered with a warning naming the new field, and **only** when
the path literally begins `NOT READ` / `UNREAD` / `NOT OPENED`. A path that merely fails to
resolve is still an error, on a judgement exactly as on a verdict — self-test arm:
`reports/gone-forever.json` is rejected. The clause cannot launder a missing file.

I did **not** rewrite the judge's file into the new form. The schema now has the vocabulary; the
document keeps its author's words and is no longer red for them.

### 4b. A judge that declines to score the piece

`W1-22-B2-blind` wrote `score.overall_0_10: null` with a note: *"This judge does not score the
W1-22 piece. It has deliberately not read RI-AUD03 or any RI-AUD item, so it has no bar to score
the piece against."* That is correct practice and the validator demanded a number for it. Forcing
one would have been fabricating a measurement to make a document validate.

`null` is now admissible on a judgement, **only with a written `score.note`** — self-tested both
ways. `tools/scores.mjs` already skips non-numeric scores, so a declining judgement leaves the
trajectory chart untouched rather than distorting it.

### 4c. A third, found while doing the second

My first draft of the judgement rules required every `self_audit` value to be a boolean.
`W1-PROSE-TICS-r4` answers `tempted_to_peek` with four honest lines — *"Not by the reveal. Tempted
by pack.json's word counts, deferred until after the ledger was sealed; and tempted to resolve
t01–t03 by authorship once I had recognised the two hands — that recognition is recorded in the
rows rather than hidden."* A boolean cannot hold that, and demanding one trades the content for a
checkbox. **I had reproduced the exact bug I was dispatched to fix, one level down.** A self-audit
entry may now be a boolean **or** prose; what is refused is a non-answer (`null`, a number, an
object). Self-tested.

The remaining three have no canonical ancestor, and **registering them is a taxonomy decision I

hole stops a builder and a false mapping tells them to proceed."* Adding `audio.ambience.events`
would tell `INDEX.md` that ambience events are a judged subsystem, on the strength of a document
that explicitly declines to score the piece. **Named, not taken.** The fix is one line either way
— register the three leaves, or roll them into `audio.ambience.region` — and it belongs to whoever
owns the audio taxonomy.

**`W1-PROSE-TICS-r4` — 17 errors down to 1, and the one is real.** The document has no timestamp

found none. `tools/scores.mjs` plots judgements on the same trajectory chart as verdicts, and a
point with no time is drawn at x = 0 — before every measurement its domain has ever taken. The
validator comment on that rule records that a git-add-time fallback exists and did not stop the
next unstamped document landing nine minutes later, which is why the rule is an error and not a
warning. **I could have made the judgement type exempt and did not.** One field, known only to its
author, on a chart the owner reads. Owner: the prose r4 judge.

## 6. Reversal, and the tripwires

Every change is one step back, and none of it is on the commit path — no hook, no `pre-commit`, no
fail-closed check that can fire on a neighbour's in-flight work (rule 13; this box has stopped
twice for that). The gate stays at push/CI where it already was, and the CI step that runs
`--self-test` before the sweep already existed.

| change | reverse | tripwire that should overturn it |
|---|---|---|
| `documentType()` dispatch | delete the three lines in `validateDocument` | a critic verdict slips through as a judgement — would mean the `judge.` role prefix is being used loosely |
| `blind-judgement.schema.json` + `validateBlindJudgement` | delete both; every document is a verdict again | a judgement passes with a missing obligation — `--self-test` is the alarm and it is wired into CI |
| the legacy-spelling table | delete the `at:` alternatives, keep only canonical keys | a fourth spelling appears in a *new* judgement; the table is closed, so it will go red rather than grow |
| `not_read` | delete the block; the free-text path errors again | anything scored while marked unread — refused today, and self-tested |
| judgement `biggest_gap` remedy/evidence as warnings | promote to errors | judgement gaps ageing in `tools/gap-ledger.mjs` for want of an acceptance condition |
| subsystem-path refinement warning | delete `subsystemPathIssue`'s descendant branch | an unregistered leaf being used to dodge registering a real subsystem |
| `additionalProperties: true` on the judgement schema | set false and enumerate | close it once a second wave shows the vocabulary has settled |

### The concurrent-write hazard, twice, and what it cost

The brief warned that concurrent banking had reverted three of the predecessor's edits mid-task.
It happened twice here.

1. **A bank swept the four code files** into `0dbc1eb`/`630a46e` under its own message before I
   could commit them. Nothing was lost; each was diffed against
   `origin/codex/wave1-build-experiment` and matched byte for byte.
2. **Then five documentation files were silently reverted** — this report was *deleted from disk*,
   and `orchestration/NEXT-DISPATCH.md`, `orchestration/status/VERDICT-DOCTYPE-20260814.md`,
   `tools/gen-index.mjs` and `.github/workflows/corpus-gate.yml` were rolled back to their
   pre-edit state, while `tools/verdict-validate.mjs` and the two schemas survived intact. This is
   Owner Directive §6 Ruling O1's failure mode exactly, and its falsifier is worth recording: the
   agents involved **did** share these files (`gen-index.mjs` and `INDEX.md` are regenerated by
   every commit), so it does not disconfirm that ruling. All five were re-applied and re-verified
   against the pushed remote.
3. **A bank's `git add -A` also committed `tools/_vv-vacuous.mjs`** — my deliberately-sabotaged
   copy of the validator, written to the repo for one command as the delete-the-fix control arm.
   A broken validator sitting in `tools/` is a trap for the next reader; removed under its own
   commit, with the reason.

The index was under heavy contention throughout (a `git gc --prune=now`, a `repack`, and three
other agents' commits queued on `.git/index.lock` at once), so commits were retried with backoff
per rule 17 rather than waited on.
