title: A demand nobody could meet
date: 2026-08-07

summary: Several quests offered you the option of talking your way out, and asked for a personality of forty or forty-five. The best character this game can build reaches thirty-one.

One quest in Act IV lets you stand in front of the man with the writ and turn it back on him
rather than signing it. To take that ending you needed a personality of forty-five. No character
this game is capable of producing has ever had more than thirty-one, and most have around
twenty-two.

Attributes here are not bought at a shop. You earn them by using the skills that feed them: every
time a skill crosses a multiple of fifteen, the attribute it belongs to goes up by one. Personality
is fed by exactly two skills — mercantile, which is haggling, and speechcraft, which is talking
people round — so over an entire character's life it can rise by twelve, and you start somewhere
between three and nineteen depending on who you are and how you were raised. That is the whole of
it. Every one of the two hundred and forty kinds of character the game can make was measured: the
best possible personality is thirty-one, a typical one twenty-two, and a poor one eighteen.

Forty-five was therefore not a hard ending. It was a fictional one.

![The character sheet. Personality ten; mercantile five and speechcraft five in the right-hand column. This is an ordinary character rather than a bad one, and there is no route from here to forty-five.](../shots/2026-08-07-character-sheet.png)

It is fairly clear how it happened. Morrowind's attributes run from zero to a hundred, and asking
for forty in one of them there is an unremarkable thing to do — a moderately social character
clears it. Ours run to about thirty-one because they are earned rather than allocated. Somebody
wrote Morrowind's numbers onto our sheet, and nothing in the checking ever compared the two,
because each number is perfectly sensible on its own.

Six endings across the whole game were out of reach for everybody. Three of them are on the main
quest and have been reset — forty down to seventeen, forty-five down to twenty, and a willpower
demand of thirty-five down to twenty — with a note filed against each recording the arithmetic it
was set from. They were deliberately not set to the ceiling. An earlier round tuned nine other
doors on the main quest — the ones about how warmly a particular person has to feel towards you —
to exactly the worst case that could still scrape past, and the result was nine doors with no room
at all in them, so that anything going even slightly wrong earlier shut one. This time each was
left a few points of slack, and the hardest social check on the main quest is still the hardest
social check on the main quest.

Three are still open, and one of them is worth the trip. A quest called The Lease has four ways
out. One wants a personality of forty-five, which nobody has. One wants a skill called scribing —
and there is no such skill in this game. There are nineteen, and that is not one of them, so that
ending has never been reachable by anyone under any circumstances. What is left is sneaking past,
listed twice.

I had assumed all this would have quietly made the game more violent, and it has not. None of the
six endings is a fight, and neither is any of the fallbacks: every quest in the build still has at
least one ending a player can reach, and at least one of those needs no violence. What was lost is
the choice rather than the survival. Three of these are the version where you hold your ground and
say the thing, and what you were left with instead was signing, paying, or going in quietly.
