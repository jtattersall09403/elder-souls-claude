There is a main quest now. Thirty-two quests over five acts, six endings, two routes through it,
and as of last night the whole thing has been played from the first errand to the last without
anyone touching the controls.

Until this week there wasn't one. Four quests carried the label "main" and were errands about
spells, mislabelled by a generator and never checked.

It starts as a courier job. An undersexton of the Drowned Court pays you a hundred and twenty in
advance to carry a sealed return from Soulrest to Gideon, which is more than the work is worth, and
asks twice whether anyone spoke to you on the stair. The factor at the other end opens the seal,
reads three lines and stops: the undersexton knows what is in her own quarterlies. You were not
carrying a book. You were carrying a man in a good coat from one town to another, so that somebody
could see who he was.

![The Stone Wastes, where the sound under the ground is first audible. Almost nothing here has been
dressed yet: the shapes are being judged at this stage, not the materials.](../shots/2026-08-07-stone-wastes.png)

The run itself is the boring part. The intended route is twenty-nine of the thirty-two quests and
all twenty-nine finished, from a cold start with no money and no rank in any faction, so nothing is
quietly leaning on standing you might not have. What makes it worth believing is that the same tool
was then told to sabotage the game three ways, and it stopped in three different places, each time
saying why in the game's own words: a subject that had never come up, a thing the character did not
yet know, a person who did not like you enough to hire you. One of the three got five quests in
before the gate bit, which is the shape you want.

Neither route needed anyone killed. Every quest in the game has at least one way through that isn't
violence, and across both runs none was closed by fighting.

The point of no return is a stone sill, ankle high, worn in the middle. Nobody tells you it is one;
the journal works it out from a keeper's offhand remark three weeks earlier and a dockmaster paying
off half her workers early:

> I am standing on the near side of the sill. Everything above me stops being reachable when I put
> my weight on the other side of it. Nobody has said this. I have worked it out from a keeper's
> remark and a dockmaster paying people off, which is how I have found out everything else in this
> business.

Then, one entry later: *"I stepped over it. It is a stone about as high as my ankle and it took less
than a second and nothing happened at all."*

All two hundred and fifty-three journal entries are stamped the same morning, incidentally, because
a run driven this fast never lets a day pass in the world.

The run also turned up a defect of a kind we have now found eight times: every character in the game
carries a number for how much they like you, and nothing in the build was reading it. Any quest
whose giver wanted to be on decent terms with you first could therefore never be offered at all —
thirty-six of the sixty-nine quests in the game, the first errand above among them, so the main
quest entire. It is read now; wiping the numbers makes that errand refuse to open and name the
reason.
