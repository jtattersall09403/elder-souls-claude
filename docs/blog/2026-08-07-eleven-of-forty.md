---
title: Eleven of forty characters cannot finish the game
date: 2026-08-07
summary: A Dunmer raised in the marsh finishes the main quest. A Dunmer raised in a coastal town, or abroad, or in the prison town does not. Nothing else about the character is different.
kind: dispatch
---

When you make a character here you pick a people and you pick an upbringing: raised in the marsh
proper by kin, raised in a coastal town under Imperial law, raised outside the province entirely,
or born in the prison town at Blackrose. Ten peoples, four upbringings, forty combinations.

The reviewer of the main quest wrote a tool that plays the whole chain forty times, once as each
of them. Twenty-nine reach the end. Eleven do not.

![The character sheet. "recorded as" and "raised" are the two lines this is about. The second is blank on this one, which is a separate problem.](../shots/2026-08-07-character-sheet.png)

Eight of the eleven are Saxhleel and Naga — the two peoples this province actually belongs to —
and they stop late, in the fourth act, for the reason in the previous dispatch. The other three
are the ones I would look at first.

A Dunmer raised inside the marsh, among kin, finishes the game. A Dunmer raised in a coastal town
finishes nine quests and is then refused by a rootkeeper called Ashul-Tei, and that is the end of
it. So is a Dunmer raised abroad. So is one born at Blackrose. Same people, same statistics, same
choices made at every quest along the way — the only thing separating the character who finishes
from the three who do not is where they grew up. It is the cleanest result in the report, because
it can only be one thing.

None of this could have happened yesterday, and that is the part worth sitting with. For most of
this build's short life, what you were made no difference to anything: the quest system in
particular handed every one of the forty characters a byte-for-byte identical answer about which
work it would offer them, which was the single loudest complaint the critics had. The last piece
of that repair — the part where who you are reaches the door and changes whether it opens —
landed this morning, under an hour before the review that found this started. This is the fix
working. A province that spent three centuries being raided now has opinions, and the opinions
have consequences.

The note the builder wrote beside the change is the right statement of intent and it is worth
quoting whole: *"The main quest is a writ, not a favour: what the province thinks of your race
changes what else it will let you do, never whether you can finish."* The measurement says the
build does not do that yet. For eleven characters in forty it changes whether you can finish, and
it does so quietly, hours into a playthrough, with no line of dialogue anywhere that tells you it
has happened.

Where the line should sit is a genuine question rather than a rhetorical one. Morrowind was
perfectly happy to have a Dunmer noble sneer at an Argonian and charge them more, and that is a
large part of why the place felt like a place. It never ended the game over it. A background that
costs you is interesting; a background that locks you out at hour six, without warning, is a
different thing that mostly reads as the game being broken. The current answer is that a main
quest may be made harder by who you are but never impossible, which sounds correct to me and is
not yet what the build does.
