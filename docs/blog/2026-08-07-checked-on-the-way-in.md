title: Checked on the way in, not on the way out
date: 2026-08-07

summary: The guilds ask who you are when they offer you work and never ask again when you hand it in. A character who had joined nothing finished one of the Wet Ledger's senior jobs and was paid by the rival guild.

Three guilds went in this week. You can join the Wet Ledger, the Imperial Assize or the Deep-Kin,
and joining one shuts the other two properly — a reviewer counted thirty distinct refusals, every
one a sentence somebody would say out loud. *"The Imperial Assize will not deal with you: you are
The Wet Ledger."* That had never worked before and it works now.

Then the reviewer tried to climb a ladder without any help, and found that a guild checks who you
are when it offers you a job and never checks again when you hand it in.

The sequence is short. A character who had joined nothing and held no standing anywhere asked for
the Ledger's second-from-top job, *Nineteen Years in the First Chair*, and was refused by name
with four reasons at once — you have not done the previous job, two subjects have not come up, you
are rank nought of six, your reputation is nought of ninety. As a control the reviewer then tried
to finish the quest without opening it, and got the right answer: *quest not open*. Then one call
filed a single piece of knowledge the character had supposedly learnt, and the identical finish
call went through.

That emptied the first chair of the Ledger, unlocked the succession quest above it, paid the
character forty reputation with the Imperial Assize — the Ledger's rival — twenty-five with the
dockhands, and cost them forty with House Dres. None of which they had any business touching.

![A room in the game, lit by one hearth. The character is standing in it. Whether they were allowed in is, as of this week, a question the building asks only once.](../shots/2026-08-07-hearth-interior.png)

The honest caveat, which the reviewer made himself before anybody else could: you cannot do this
by playing today. The route runs through a kind of world event that no quest currently uses — none
of the hundred and ten of them — so it is a door standing open in a corridor nobody can get into,
one line of data away from being real.

What it is live for is measurement, and that is why it was called the biggest problem in the
piece. The tool written to prove the guilds could be climbed files that knowledge immediately
before finishing each quest, every time, and throws away the answer when it asks to open one. So
every walk it has recorded was taken with the gate switched off, and its headline — you can reach
the top of a guild by playing — is the one thing it cannot claim.

The same report settled a straight argument about a number. The side that wrote the quests said
the top job asks for a reputation of a hundred and twelve and that playing the line gets you there
comfortably. A separate tool said the most anyone can reach is a hundred, so the top job is
unreachable and not one of five hundred and forty possible characters can play the game at all.
The reviewer drove the actual gate across eleven values, watched the complaint about reputation
vanish at exactly a hundred and twelve and never come back, and added up what the quests pay: you
hold well over twice the demand by the time it is tested. The quests were right.

The tool was wrong for a good reason. To find out whether a door can ever be opened it invents an
ideal character and walks it at every door in the game. That character gets a billion gold, every
subject of conversation, every flag the world can set and the top rank in everything — and then
its reputation with each guild is pinned at a flat hundred, which was the right ceiling back when
the ladder stopped at a hundred. The ladder moved and the number did not. The tool's own report
quotes the refusal it gets, *reputation 100/112*, and reads it as a fact about the game rather
than about itself.

It still does. I ran it this afternoon and it still says one gate stops all five hundred and
forty.
