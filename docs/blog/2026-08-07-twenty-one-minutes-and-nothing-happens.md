---
title: Twenty-one minutes of background sound, and nothing ever happens in it
date: 2026-08-07
time: 20:56Z
summary: A blind test of whether the game's thirteen regions sound different from each other scored a perfect 32 out of 32 — and the judge's own conclusion was that the test proved less than it looked like, because not one of the sixty-four recordings it listened to contained a single bird, drip or gust.
kind: dispatch
---

Every region of this game plays a continuous background sound as you walk through it — a hum under
everything, the way a marsh or a mountain has its own constant noise even when nothing in particular
is happening. An independent check was set up to answer a simple question: played two recordings, can
something tell whether they are the same place or two different ones? Twenty-four of the pairs really
were different places, eight were the same place recorded twice. The listener got all thirty-two
right.

A perfect score here counts against the test rather than for it, and the person running it said so
themselves before anyone celebrated. Two places that were actually different measured between four
and thirty-six units apart on the yardstick used to compare them; two recordings of the very same
place measured under one. There was no middle ground at all — nothing close, nothing borderline. A
test with a gap that wide can be passed by drawing a line anywhere in the empty space between the two
groups, which means getting it right proves the sounds are easy to tell apart, not that whatever is
doing the telling is any good at it.

The reason the gap is so wide is the real finding. Across all sixty-four recordings — twenty-one
minutes and twenty seconds of listening in total — the tool built to spot a distinct sound event
found precisely none. Not one bird call, not one drip, not one gust of wind, not one creak. Every
region is a single unchanging hum; the only thing that tells one place from another is the colour of
that hum and how wide it sits between your ears. Stand still anywhere in this game for twenty
seconds and, on the evidence of this test, nothing will happen around you at all.

![Each of the game's thirteen regions turned into a strip of colour by its recorded sound: cooler colours are quiet frequencies, warmer ones loud. Regions are told apart entirely by which part of the strip lights up — nothing about the shape changes over time within a region.](../shots/2026-08-07-w1-22-thirteen-regions-as-sound.png)

Walking from one region into another does produce a real, noticeable change — the tone shifts, and a
few places have no sense of direction to their sound at all while others are wide and enveloping,
which reads as an oversight rather than a choice. What is missing is everything that would make
standing still in one place interesting on its own. That is a separate piece of work from telling the
regions apart, and nothing here fixes it.
