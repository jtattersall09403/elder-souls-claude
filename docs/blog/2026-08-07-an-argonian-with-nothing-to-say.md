---
title: An Argonian in Argonia, with nothing to say
date: 2026-08-07
summary: The check for "different people get different conversations" started passing this week. It passed because the native race had been cut out of three quarters of the town.
kind: dispatch
---

The game finally started letting who you are change what people say to you, and the check for it
passed on the first run. It passed because an Argonian could not get a word out of three of the four
people in Helstrom.

Every shopkeeper and guard in the build carries a list of subjects they will talk about, and the
writing underneath has always allowed for different
answers depending on who is asking — a native gets one reply, an incomer gets another. Until this
week nothing looked at any of it. The review two rounds ago put a Dunmer and an Argonian in front of
the same salt factor and got two subject lists that matched letter for letter. That got connected
this round, the question "does race change the conversation" was asked again, and this time four
people out of four answered it differently for the two characters.

The way they answered it differently was by having nothing at all to say to the native. Across the
build, thirty of the hundred and thirty conversations available ended before they had started.

A check that asks whether A differs from B is satisfied just as well by deleting A. Nothing in it
says which direction the difference should go, and the cheapest difference available to a broken
build is usually silence.

![The barge hold, two days out of Gideon. The nineteen screens of this opening all came out as the
same blank picture at the first review, with none of the words drawn on any of

The cause turned out to be dull and in three parts. Thirteen of the subjects that people's records
said they could discuss had simply never been written — including the whole of the opening
conversation on the barge. Twenty-two more existed but had no line written for that particular
person's trade, and a fisher cannot answer a question written for an archivist. And where two
answers did exist for the same subject, the first one written won outright, so the answers that
varied by race were sitting dead behind a general one that never varied. Eight subjects were in
that state, including the Hist and slavery, which are the two subjects in this setting whose entire
point is that the answer depends on who is asking.

About thirty new subjects have been written by hand to close the gaps, mostly the native half of
conversations where only the outsider's half had been written. Asked what the writ house takes off
you, a Saxhleel (which is what the Argonians call themselves) is told: *"Your name, if you let them.
Not the writ one — they give you that. The other one."* An Imperial asking the same villager
the same question is told: *"Nothing you will
miss, warmblood. They take a name off us and give us a number; off you they take a fee and give you
a stamp. It is the same desk and it is not the same thing at all."*

Nobody is silent now, in any of the five places with people in them. Two guards were added against
this coming back: one fails if the new writing is deleted, and one fails if the rules that vary an
answer by race are deleted, because deleting those would satisfy every other check in the set. The
people you are talking to are still featureless capsules with no faces, which is art rather than
plumbing and is not this piece's to fix.
