The same count that turned up [the file nothing in the game had opened](#2026-08-07-the-file-that-says-what-is-true)
found two other things on the way past. Neither is a bug. A bug gets fixed because somebody trips
over it; these get fixed only if somebody goes looking.

**Nothing here is old.** Across every book, every line of dialogue and every quest the game ships,
about twenty distinct years are named, and all of them are in the current era. The dated history of
the province runs from roughly 3E 344 to the present day of 3E 427 — eighty-odd years, every bit of
it inside the living memory of somebody you can stop and talk to. Meanwhile the writing gestures at
antiquity constantly: "four hundred" turns up 121 times as a vague span, "four thousand" eighteen.
The province waves at depth every other paragraph and dates nothing.

Morrowind works the other way round. Everybody in it is arguing about something that happened
before all of them, and the argument is old enough that nobody left alive can settle it. A world
whose oldest named thing is younger than its oldest inhabitant has no floor under it.

Two things have changed. Twelve facts now carry a date, and four of those dates are deliberately
blank — the Duskfall is undated in every source in the world, because the three books that try give
three different spans, and "nobody knows when" is a fact about the province rather than a hole in a
file. And five of the world's twenty-one running arguments are now about the horizon before that.
History people are wrong about in front of you is the only sort a game can actually hand over.

**There are no Argonian tribes.** Not few — none. Of the names the setting is known for, Agacephs,
Paatru, Archein, Sarpa, Dead-Water and Miredancers all score zero, and the only place any of them
now appears anywhere in the game's data is the file that records their absence. Kothringi and
Lilmothiit do appear, and both are extinct in this setting, so they are history rather than
society. What the province has instead is five bodies invented for this project: the Deep-Kin, the
Xul-Aneekh, the Drowned Court, the Sap-Cutters and the Wet Ledger. That is defensible — five groups
a player can tell apart beat nine tribe names they cannot — but it is ours rather than the
setting's, and it is now written down as a decision so that nobody later scatters tribe names
through dialogue as flavour and implies a society this game does not model. If tribes are wanted,
they need a faction each, not a mention each.

**And the slave trade is present mostly as the Empire's word for it.** House Dres has raided Black
Marsh for slaves for centuries and slavery is legal in Morrowind; that is not our invention. In the
books and dialogue this game ships, the word "lease" outnumbers "slave" and "slavery" by about ten
to one.

![Thorn, one of the stilt settlements. The buildings are still plain shapes. Nobody living here belongs to a tribe, and nothing you can find in it is more than about eighty years old.](../shots/2026-08-07-w1-04-settlement-street-thorn.png)

A world that only ever says "lease" has taken the Provincial Office's vocabulary as narration
rather than as one side's position, which is the exact move this project's own rules about
contradiction forbid. The correction is not to sprinkle the harder word about. It is that the
euphemism has to belong to somebody. So there is now a registered argument about it, with the
Empire's coastal address recorded as *holding* that line rather than stating it, against a salt
factor's stock column and a note in the margin of a borrowed copy: *"Morrowind is in the Empire. My
mother is in Tear. Both of these are true at once and this sentence is printed by an office that
knows it."* There is a ruling on which side is right. It is sealed out of the build, and nothing in
the game will tell you.

One correction to our own report while I am here. It gave that ratio as fifty to one, having
counted "Blackrose" as part of the euphemism. Blackrose is also a city on the trunk road with a
population file, four interiors and a signpost, so most of those two thousand mentions are an
address rather than a policy. Counted over the words the world actually speaks, it is ten to one.
Bad enough.
