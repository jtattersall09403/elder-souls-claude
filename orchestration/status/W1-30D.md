# W1-30D — characters, creatures, animation: builder status

**State: build-partial.** Not build-satisfied, and the gap is named in §6 rather than rounded off.
Owner: the W1-30D builder. Branch `codex/wave1-build-experiment`. Written 2026-08-14.

---

## 1. The headline, in one line

The character's transparency defect was measured properly for the first time, found to be five
separate geometric holes rather than one, and closed to **24% of its baseline pixel count** — with
six null controls, two of which are the plausible wrong answers rather than the trivial ones, and
all six behave as declared.

## 2. What was measured, and how

`tools/visual/actor-orbit-holes.mjs` — new. It rasterises the actor and **nothing else** (so it is
the world-hidden pass by construction) at the standard's own 24 stops × {1.5, 4, 12} m ladder,
across **80 poses sampled from `game/data/combat/clips.json`'s twenty archetypes** through the
shipping `addPose()` on a real `Rig`. 5,760 frames per subject. A pixel counts as a defect when it
is background, **enclosed** by body pixels, at a ring depth spread ≤ 8 cm, and not bordered by a
held object.

Those last two conditions are the whole difficulty and they cost two wrong versions:

- Without the depth rule, a character standing with an arm out fails, because orbit far enough and a
  leg crosses behind the arm-to-ribs gap and encloses it. That would have rewarded welding the arm
  to the ribs.
- Without the held-object rule, a greatsword hanging beside a thigh reads as a seam.
- And the **first** version of the second rule was too strong: it required every bordering surface
  to be on the same bone or an adjacent one, which quietly reclassified *four crest cones hovering
  13 cm off the character's back* as correct rendering. That version was discarded. It is recorded
  in the tool because a discriminator that excuses the largest real defect in the subject is the
  exact failure this project keeps having, and the next person should see it fail rather than
  re-derive it.

**Which input does every arm of this test fabricate?** (HAZARDS.md §0.) Two, both declared in the
tool's header: the material set (a plain stand-in, safe *only* because no material property moves a
vertex and this measures vertices) and the camera stops (the standard's, fixed before the fix). The
poses are deliberately **not** fabricated — that was the input most likely to make the test unable
to falsify itself.

## 3. Numbers

Same instrument, same 5,760 frames, `HEAD` against this build:

| | crack px | frames affected | worst frame | mean silhouette |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| before (`HEAD`) | **9,254** | 1,562 / 5,760 | 1.35% of silhouette | 39,730 px |
| after | **2,204** | 978 / 5,760 | 0.18% of silhouette | 38,614 px |

**-76% of the defect; the worst single frame improved 7.4×.** The worst frame before was 1,796 px
of sky through the small of the back, between the floating crest and the rump.

Silhouette area moved **-2.8%**, inside the 3% guard but worth saying out loud rather than rounding:
it is real, it is one-directional, and it has one cause — the crest that used to stand 35 cm off the
back now hugs it, so the character's outline genuinely got smaller there. That is the fix, not a
side effect of it. It is also uncomfortably close to the guard, and if a later change adds another
2% in the same direction the guard will fire and should be believed rather than widened.

Other families, at the 640-frame profile: `humanoid` 84 px, `undead` ~205 px → both dominated by the
same residual, `beast` **0 px**.

## 4. The six null controls, and the two that matter

`tools/visual/actor-hole-control.mjs`. Each one patches a **copy of the source tree** — never a flag
inside the instrument, because a flag tests the instrument's own branch. Copies are `game/src`,
`game/data/combat`, `game/vendor` (~7 MB, not 1.2 GB — HAZARDS.md §5) and are removed on exit.

At the 640-frame profile the current build measures **178 crack px against `HEAD`'s 1,858** — the
same -90% the full profile shows as -76%, the difference being that the full profile includes the
12 m stops where small seams are sub-pixel and both builds score near zero.

| control | what it breaks | required | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| `break-joints` | joint balls back under their limb radius | go red | **red, 178 → 391** |
| `unbed-belt` | belt back to 3 mm clearance | go red | **green, 178 → 139 — NO LONGER ISOLATES, see below** |
| `refloat-crest` | dorsal crest lifted back off the back | go red | **red, 178 → 251** |
| `unweld-tail` | tail root back on the pelvis surface | go red | **red, 178 → 179** (marginal) |
| **`hide-arm`** | delete the whole left arm | **be caught while the crack count IMPROVES** | caught: 178 → 136 px, silhouette **-3.3%** |
| **`weld-arm-to-rib`** | fatten the torso until it swallows the arms | **be caught while the crack count IMPROVES** | caught: 178 → 108 px, silhouette **+9.0%** |

**`unbed-belt` fails, and it is left failing.** It went red as required (230 against 214) when the
belt was the dominant remaining crack. After the morph work — which scales presentation offsets with
`build` — it comes back green. The belt fix was not wrong; the control stopped isolating, because at
a residual of 178 px across 640 frames the metric is near its floor and a belt standing proud of the
waist is a large extra surface that opens and merges neighbouring pockets for reasons that have
nothing to do with the seam it used to have. **A single-variable control stops isolating once the
variable it moves is no longer the biggest thing in the frame.** Deleting it would hide the evidence
that this metric has a floor; tuning it green is the move the file exists to prevent. So it is
reported as a failing control, and the honest reading is that the crack count below roughly 200 px
at this profile should not be trusted to attribute cause.

`unweld-tail` at 178 → 179 is marginal for the same reason and should be read the same way.

The last two are the ones the brief asked for — the control that *hides* the subject and improves
the headline number. Neither is caught by the crack count; both are caught by the silhouette-area
guard at 3%. A green crack count on its own would pass both, and that is exactly why it is not the
gate on its own.

`hide-arm`'s first version removed only the upper-arm **tube** and moved the silhouette by 0.8% —
the shoulder ball, the shoulder plate and the forearm covered the hole. It proved nothing and was
strengthened. A control that fails to sabotage is indistinguishable from a build that passes.

## 5. What was built

**The transparency defect — five holes, not one.** Diagnosed by the tool, not by reading:

1. **Joint balls were smaller than the limbs they joined.** Two tapered tubes bending through an
   angle leave a wedge on the outside of the bend, sized by the tube radii; a ball smaller than
   either tube cannot reach it. The rule is now **derived from the body plan** — a joint ball is the
   largest radius any segment has at that joint, which is the smallest sphere that bridges the wedge
   and is tangent to the widest tube, so it cannot read as a bead. A future radius edit cannot
   silently reopen the gap. **The ankle had no joint ball at all.**
2. **The dorsal crest was four cones floating up to 35 cm behind a 14 cm-deep back.** It is now
   welded crest tubes inside the skinned surface. Same read, no gap, structurally impossible to
   separate.
3. **The tail root was tangent to the pelvis, not inside it**, and its start ring was 50% weighted
   to `spine_00` — so bending the spine dragged the tail off the rump. Now embedded, and rigid to
   `pelvis`, which is what the combat rig assumes (`skeleton.json` declares no tail bones).
4. **The belt had 3 mm of clearance** at a 0.205 radius against a 0.178 waist. Every rigid fitting
   (belt, pack, helm, oblique straps, garment tab) now sits at least 3 cm inside the body surface.
5. **`spine_02` stepped from 0.142 to a 0.072 neck** — a ledge, not a joint, and the reason the
   derived rule would otherwise have put a 28 cm collar at the throat. The chest volume is carried
   by its ellipsoid, so the tube now tapers into the neck properly.

**`esCurvature`, as `MATERIAL_API.md` §6a asked.** Computed, not authored: an edge shared by two
faces whose normals differ by more than 35° scores 1, a face interior 0, an open rim 1, one
smoothing pass for band width, positions quantised to 0.1 mm so UV/skin seams do not read as
boundaries. Baked on **every** geometry this file makes — body, equipment, presentation, weapons,
shields. C's `wearFrom: 'geometry'` has its input.

**The reuse mechanism, made structural.** `render/lib/rigs.js` is a real registry:
`es.humanoid.v1`, two humanoid bases, **17 characters as variant specs**, fail-closed on an unknown
axis or an unknown morph key. `ensureBuilt()` stamps `userData.rigId` / `characterId` /
`rigVariantKey` on every mesh, so `library-census.mjs`'s `bypass` row has something to read.

**The glTF ingestion path.** `render/models.js` + `game/assets/w1-30/models/manifest.json`. The
r180 loaders are vendored **byte-exact** with their four transitive addons. **An unmanifested model
does not load** — and that rule binds the procedural bases too, today, rather than being a promise
about assets we do not have. Refuses: unknown id, missing field, licence outside the allow-list,
CC-BY with no attribution, file-backed model with no `sha256`, asset over its own LOD0 ceiling.

**The sourcing spike and its stop decision** — `orchestration/status/W1-30D-SOURCING-SPIKE.md`.
Ruled: pivot, reversibly, for two reasons that are not licensing. Every sourced rig is on the wrong
skeleton and retargeting silently desyncs the drawn body from the hurtboxes; and there is no CC0
Saxhleel, so a sourced humanoid would produce exactly the incoherence the parent's falsification
audit names as worse than uniformity.

## 6. What I could not do — plainly

- **No human has looked at these characters.** Every number here is an automatic check, and this
  project's own doctrine is that statistics can fail a build and can never pass one. The naive
  character pack, the Protocol A pairing against `refs/modern/character_closeup/`, and the
  silhouette-readability read are all **not run**. This build is not "good-looking" on any evidence
  I have; it is *measurably less broken*.
- **No GPU frame, no Deck run, no motion sequence.** The instrument is a CPU rasteriser and knows
  nothing about shading, antialiasing, or the twelve declared motion sequences. Motion matters more
  for this child than any other and the sequences remain uncaptured.
- **Item 4, the real clip set, is untouched.** No new clips, no authored crossfade times, no
  additive upper-body layering, no root-motion agreement measurement. `CLIP_NAMES` in
  `rig-contract.js` is still empty. The `clips:` axis on all 17 characters records an intent and
  drives nothing.
- **LODs are declared in the manifest and not generated.** There is one geometry per character.
  The triangle budget is asserted for file-backed models and unmeasured for the procedural ones.
- **Faces and hands are improved, not solved.** The hand has three fingers and an opposed thumb and
  the head has a jaw, brow and mouth line; neither is at Morrowind fidelity, and the plan's own
  "least certain" line says this is the hardest thing here. Nothing I did settles it.
- **The residual 2,204 crack px is not all defect, and one control proves the metric has a floor.**
  The largest remaining item is a forearm passing close to the hip at the same depth — genuine sky
  between arm and body, which the depth rule cannot distinguish from a seam. `unbed-belt` failing to
  go red is the same fact from the other side. **Below roughly 200 px at the 640-frame profile this
  instrument can tell you that something changed and not reliably what.** I did not build a
  finer-grained attribution and I did not pretend the residual is all real.
- **`beast` and `undead` were checked at the 640-frame profile, not the full 5,760.**
- **One file outside my paths was edited:** `game/index.html`, to add an import map. It is
  unavoidable — official three.js addons import the bare specifier `'three'`, and rewriting that
  line would fork a vendored file, which W1-30 R4a makes a hard fail. It is additive and inert:
  `grep -rn "from 'three'" game/src` returns nothing, so no existing module resolves through it.
  Reversal is deleting one element. Flagged loudly rather than buried.
- **`renderer.js` still calls `makeRiggedActor` with four arguments**, so which of the 17 characters
  an NPC gets is *derived* from `group.name` (stable per eid, deterministic, same frame hashes)
  rather than authored. A one-line renderer change would make it authored; I did not make it,
  because `renderer.js` is W1-30A's.

## 7. Library rows (`W1-30-LIBRARY.md` §3)

**CONSUMED** — `visual-foundation.js`: `worldMaterial` and the frozen `wearFrom: 'geometry'` axis;
`world-art.js`: `creatureArt()`; `rig-contract.js`: joint/socket vocabulary; `combat/skeleton.js`
and `combat/clips.js` as the pose authority.

**PUBLISHED** —
- `render/lib/rigs.js` (rigs registry) — consumers: `render/actor.js` (build path),
  `tools/visual/rig-variant-proof.mjs` (census). **Two distinct consumers.**
- `render/models.js` (`loadModel`, `assertAdmissible`, `modelManifest`) — consumers: W1-30D's
  procedural bases; W1-30E's architecture kits (declared consumer in the plan's reuse rows).
- `bakeCurvature()` from `render/actor.js` — consumers: this file's five geometry builders; offered
  to W1-30E, which has the same §6a request.
- `base.saxhleel`, `base.humanoid` — 9 and 6 consumers respectively.
- `base.slitherfang` — **1 base, exempt, in writing**: a quadruped is not a variant of a biped. It
  shares the skeleton and none of the body plan. Counting it as reuse would be a reuse claim rather
  than reuse. **1 exemption; the limit is 3.**

**VARIANTS** — 17 character specs across 5 axes (`morph`, `scale`, `material`, `sockets`, `clips`),
17 distinct structural keys, 0 duplicates. Proven by `tools/visual/rig-variant-proof.mjs`:
17/17 distinct by **silhouette IoU** (worst pair 0.9809, floor 0.985), and widening the shared
humanoid body plan by 6% on a copy moved **15/17** characters — 9/9 saxhleel, 6/6 humanoid, 0/2
quadruped, which is the exemption behaving as written rather than a miss.

Colour is deliberately **excluded** from the distinctness measure. W1-30K found Morrowind's own nine
regions are not separable by colour alone (9.1% against an 11.1% chance baseline) and the terrain
builder found the same independently; a variety claim resting on palette rests on nothing. This
measures shape.
