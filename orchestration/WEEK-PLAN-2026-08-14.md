# The plan for the rest of this week's cap

**Written 2026-08-14, 17:20 BST, by the orchestrator, because the owner asked and the honest answer
was that it did not exist.** Everything below is a decision, not a proposal. Where a call is
contestable I have said which way I went and what would change my mind.

## 1. The arithmetic, first, because it decides everything else

- **32% of the weekly cap spent**, in **8.3 hours** (09:00 → 17:17 BST today).
- That is **3.86% per hour** at today's parallelism (mean 14.1 agents, $124/h).
- **68% remains**, and the window runs to roughly next Friday morning.

Two futures follow, and only one of them is any good:

| | burn rate | what happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Carry on as today** | 3.86%/h | ~17.6 more hours of work. **Cap exhausted around 11:00 Saturday.** Five and a half dead days. |
| **Spread across the week** | ~1.1%/h | ~60 more working hours (≈10/day × 6 days). Work every day to Friday. |

**1.1% against 3.86% is 29% of today's rate.**

That number should look familiar: the standing efficiency goal is **25% of baseline**. Which means the
thing I have been treating as a parallel efficiency programme is not parallel to anything. **It is now
the mechanism that decides whether there is compute on Wednesday.** The owner's framing — *"I don't
want to save cost by running more slowly or performing worse, I want to increase efficiency"* — stops
being a principle and becomes the only way both halves of this table are true at once.

### The ruling

**Spread it, and pay for the spread with efficiency rather than idleness.** Not by running fewer
agents — parallelism is a separate guard with its own floor, and the owner has twice said not to idle.
By making each agent-hour cost less:

1. **Model routing, adopted today as Ruling C5.** The fleet is 91% Opus (94,831 requests against
   9,113). Judgement work — critics, plan reviewers, arbiters, blind judges, anything producing a
   score — **stays on Opus and is not negotiable**. Mechanical work — census, sweeps, enumeration,
   applying a decision already made, anything a downstream critic checks — moves to Sonnet. This is
   the largest untouched lever in the programme and it is a dispatch-time act, which is why no agent
   could ever land it.
2. **The split trial**, now dispatched with real arms, tells us whether chunking long pieces costs or
   saves. Its falsifier has already run and points the helpful way.
3. **Fewer wasted rounds.** Today's biggest single waste was not model choice — it was two agents
   sent at the same four findings because I said they were unowned. Ownership checks before dispatch
   cost me seconds and save an agent-hour.

**What would change my mind:** if Protocol A's verdict says the game's visual fidelity is far below
the bar (likely), the case for front-loading gets stronger, because a week of depth work on a game
that looks poor is a week aimed at the wrong problem. In that case I would spend 40% over the next two
days rather than 22%, and accept finishing Thursday.

## 3. Where I am aiming to be when the cap runs out

Stated as things the owner can check by playing, not as scores:

1. **The game looks like a game.** Materials respond to light, surfaces have detail, edges are clean, regions read differently. Judged by eye against real references — Protocol A running every wave, not once.
2. **The first ten minutes work.** You come out of character creation in Thorn facing somewhere sensible, in a town built to a reference rather than to a guess, and can walk, look, open the map, read the journal, and talk to someone.
3. **Conversation has depth.** Asking about one topic opens others. Speakers disagree. The window looks like Morrowind's.
4. **The quests that exist are reachable.** 21 finished quests currently stranded above a rank ceiling are openable.
5. **The bar is honest.** No score passes on statistics alone; the unjudged backlog is triaged; the reverted delivery is back.

**What I do not expect to reach:** Wave 2's full systems depth, combat polish beyond what exists, or
anything approaching Morrowind's content volume. This week ends with a good-looking, honest, playable
slice — not a game.

## 5. Honest caveats about this plan

- **The percentages are intentions, not commitments.** I will report actuals against them rather than
  quietly reshaping the plan to match what happened.
- **The burn-rate arithmetic assumes ~10 working hours a day.** If the box runs longer, the money goes
  faster and the week is shorter. I would rather say that now than explain it on Tuesday.
- **This is the first time any of this has been written down**, which is itself the finding. A plan
  held only in an orchestrator's context is a plan that dies at the next compaction — and this one has
  compacted twice today.
