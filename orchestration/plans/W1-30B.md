Plan-State: satisfied

**Light is the second-largest lever after geometry and it currently costs the game more than anything
except the character models.** Two defects dominate: image-based lighting is 128 texels, and shadows
stop at 60 metres.

Dispatch the moment W1-30S lands. Runs concurrently with A; the seam between them is `lighting.js`.

## Owned paths (exclusive write)

`game/src/render/sky.js`, `game/src/render/lighting.js`, `game/src/render/lib/lighting-recipes.js`.

Read-only: `renderer.js` (A's — B pushes through `renderer.setLightingFrame()`, created by S).

## Where we are, precisely

- **IBL is a 16×8 DataTexture.** `sky.js:185` — `new Uint8Array(16 * 8 * 4)`, 128 texels, assigned
  directly as `scene.environment` with no `PMREMGenerator`. There is therefore no roughness-convolved
  mip chain, so every `MeshStandardMaterial` gets the same near-uniform specular blob whatever its
  roughness. Metal, wet chitin, resin and water all lose the one cue that separates them. The comment
  at `sky.js:182` calls this "genuine specular environment sampling"; at 128 texels it is not.
- **One shadow map over a 120 m box.** `sky.js:139-148` — 2048², ortho ±60, near 40, far 210. Beyond
  ~60 m from focus, nothing is shadowed. Vistas — the shots a player judges a world by — lose their
  depth cue exactly where they need it.
- **Fog is a single `FogExp2` at density 0.0022** (`sky.js`), recoloured by region and weather. There
  is no height dependence and no aerial perspective, so a marsh has no layered depth and a ridge has
  no atmosphere above the valley.
- **Ambient is a `HemisphereLight` plus a 0.24 `AmbientLight` fill.** Correct as support; today it is
  doing IBL's job.
- Good and to be preserved: one authoritative sun/moon direction shared by dome, fog, light and
  shadow; texel snapping (`sky.js:242`); deterministic bounded precipitation driven by simulation
  frame; moon as the exact inverse of the celestial direction.

## The work

**Items 0a and 0b come first and are probably an hour's work between them. They move more pixels
than everything else in this child combined.** Both were found by the VISUAL-TRUTH-AUDIT's frames
(`docs/shots/2026-08-14-visual-truth/`), not by code reading, and neither was visible in any gate.

**0a. Fix the fog.** `FogExp2(0x9aa79a, 0.0022)` erases the world. In the captures a settlement ~40 m
away is a white smear with no readable silhouette, distant trees are white ghosts, and hills are flat
paper cut-outs. Every other improvement in the visual tree is invisible past 40 m until this changes.
Replace with a distance profile that keeps silhouettes readable to the intended draw distance,
region- and weather-dependent, coupled to sun direction, with the *heavy* fog reserved for weather
that earns it. Also fix rain: it currently renders as uniform full-intensity white lines at every
depth, so it reads as scratches on the lens rather than weather in a world — attenuate by distance
and by the same atmosphere the fog uses.

**0b. Find out why nothing casts a shadow.** Across all 24 orbit frames at noon there is not one
directional shadow — not from the player, not from boardwalk posts, not from buildings —
despite `shadowMap.enabled = true` and `sun.castShadow = true`. The prime suspect is
`sun.shadow.camera.near = 40` (`sky.js:141`) clipping the scene given where the light is placed. **Do
this before the cascade work**: cascades over a shadow system that is not drawing are wasted, and a
shadowless noon frame is a large part of why everything currently looks pasted on.

1. **Real IBL.** Render the procedural sky dome to a small cube target (128²/face is ample) on
   time-of-day/weather change — not every frame — and run it through `PMREMGenerator`. Assign the
   result to `scene.environment`. Add `scene.environmentIntensity` driven by region and weather.
   Interiors get their own probe (generated by the same path, consumed by G).
2. **Shadow cascades.** Three cascades: 0–25 m, 25–90 m, 90–300 m, each texel-snapped, with per-
   cascade bias and a blend band so the transition is not a visible ring. Keep the single-map path as
   the `low` quality fallback. Vista shots must show terrain and canopy shadow structure to the fog
   horizon.
3. **Contact shadows.** A short screen-space ray march for the first ~0.5 m, which is what makes feet
   touch ground and props sit on tables. Cheap, and it fixes the specific "floating" read that AO at
   3 px cannot.
4. **Height fog and aerial perspective.** Replace flat `FogExp2` with a depth-reconstructing pass
   that has a height falloff and a sun-direction-dependent inscattering term, excluding sky, surviving
   resize and teleport. This is what makes a marsh look like a marsh: mist in the hollows, clear air
   on the ridge, and a sun that you can see the shafts of when you face it.
5. **`lighting.js`, the published frame contract.** One frozen object per frame:
   `{ sunDir, sunColour, sunIntensity, moonDir, moonColour, skyLuminance, ambientColour, fogColour,
   fogDensity, fogHeightFalloff, aerialInscatter, exposureTarget, regionId, weatherId, timeOfDay }`.
   A consumes this and never reads `sky.js`.
6. **Named lighting recipes.** `lighting-recipes.js` gains `noon-marsh`, `dusk-canopy`,
   `overcast-flat`, `storm`, `night-moon`, `interior-hearth`, `interior-cold`, `cave-emissive`, each
   a variant spec over a base rig. G, E and the Deck's reference-plate renderer all consume them.
7. **Weather as material response, not recoloured fog.** Rain raises wetness on the material families
   that declare it (C's `wetness` option), darkens albedo, sharpens specular and adds a sky-ward
   reflection term. Dry highland, wet marsh and emissive interior must respond differently.

## Falsification audit

The strongest attack is that **PMREM from a procedurally generated sky is still a procedurally
generated sky** — it will convolve correctly and still contain no interesting environment detail,
because there are no clouds with structure, no distant landmarks and no ground bounce in the source.
The mitigation is that C's materials and F's terrain supply the ground half of the environment
through the cube capture; if the resulting reflections still read as flat, the honest answer is a
legal authored HDR environment per region, and B should say so rather than tuning intensity.

Second: cascades can be made to pass a shadowed-pixel-fraction gate while being blurry mush at
distance. The guard is the human read on the 13 vistas specifically, plus the requirement that vistas
must not be among the worst three shots.

Least certain: whether the height-fog pass and A's GTAO can share one depth-normal prepass without
the two children stepping on each other. They should — S's `registerPrePass` is the mechanism — but
this is the one place where the seam might prove too thin, and if it does, the correct resolution is
that A owns the prepass and B consumes its output, decided in writing before either ships.
