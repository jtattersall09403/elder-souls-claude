## Where we are, precisely

**There is not one authored 3D model in the repository.** `grep -rn "GLTFLoader\|\.glb\|\.gltf"` over
`game/src` returns nothing. Characters are assembled in `render/actor.js` from 17 `SphereGeometry`,
9 `CapsuleGeometry`, 9 `ConeGeometry`, 6 `TorusGeometry`, 6 `CylinderGeometry` and 3
`DodecahedronGeometry`, skinned onto a hand-built `THREE.Skeleton` (`actor.js:294`, `:419`).

The rigging work is real — there is a genuine skeleton, genuine skinning, bone-matrix-driven
presentation, a fade shadow and an attack-arc indicator. **The geometry is the problem, not the
animation architecture.** RI-VIS08 and RI-CAM07 measure silhouette IoU, blend continuity, root-motion
agreement and attachment correctness, all of which a primitive assembly passes. A person looking at
it sees a snowman with a snout.

### The transparency defect is geometric, and this is now proven

**Stop looking for an alpha bug.** The VISUAL-TRUTH-AUDIT ran the material audit across the whole
player subtree (`docs/shots/2026-08-14-visual-truth/player-root-audit.json`): every visible mesh is
`transparent:false, opacity:1`. The only transparent surfaces are the contact shadow and the
(invisible) attack-arc indicator, both `MeshBasicMaterial, depthWrite:false`, both correct.

The world-hidden capture (`seethrough/player-noon-000-holes.png`) shows the actual cause: **the body
parts are disjoint primitives that do not join.** There is sky between the shoulder plate and the
arm, sky between the head and the shoulders, and the torso is a flat shield-shaped plate rather than
a volume. That is what a capsule-and-sphere assembly does at every joint, and it is why this defect
has been declared fixed more than once and keeps coming back: **each fix addressed the material,
and the material was never the problem.**

The fix is welded or authored geometry — item 2 below. Nothing else closes it. This is recorded here
so no future agent spends another round on `transparent`, `depthWrite` or render order.

The same capture shows four more defects this child owns: the shield renders as a **pure black
polygon** (a hole in the world where a lit surface should be); the greatsword is a **flat untapered
plank** that clips through the shield and reaches the ground; **legs are roughly twice the torso** and
there is no neck; and an **NPC renders doll-sized and floating** in mid-air at the wrong scale or
position.

## The work

1. **The glTF ingestion path.** Fill `render/models.js`: vendor r180 `GLTFLoader` (+ `DRACOLoader`,
   `KTX2Loader`), a manifest recording licence, source, real-world scale, bounds, LOD chain, material
   family mapping and consumers, and fail-closed admission — an unmanifested model must not load.
2. **Two base meshes, one skeleton.** `base.saxhleel` and `base.humanoid` on skeleton
   `es.humanoid.v1`, authored or sourced, with three LODs, correct real-world scale, clean quads
   where it matters for silhouette (jaw, shoulder, hand, tail root), and UVs that consume C's
   material families and trim atlas.
3. **Every other character is a variant.** Morph/scale variant specs, material variants and equipment
   attachments — never a second mesh. See `W1-30-LIBRARY.md` §5 for the exact shape. The acceptance
   counts distinct characters produced from the two bases.
4. **A real clip set.** Idle, walk, run, sprint, four-direction roll, jump/fall/land, 180° turn,
   block, hit reaction, three attack chains per weapon class, recovery, and the transitions between
   them, with authored crossfade times, additive upper-body layering where justified, and root motion
   that agrees with the simulation rather than sliding.
5. **Faces and hands.** The two features a person looks at first. Even at Morrowind's fidelity a head
   needs a jaw, brow, eye sockets and a mouth line; a hand needs a thumb. This is where a base-mesh
   improvement compounds across every NPC in the game.
6. **Equipment sockets and sets.** Named sockets in `rig-contract.js`; three authored equipment sets
   per slot as the item requires; equipment as kit parts (E's registry) so a Thorn spear and a
   Helstrom spear are variants.
7. **Secondary motion.** Deterministic, seeded, simulation-frame-driven: tail sway, cloth, pack
   swing. Never wall-clock, so captures stay hashable.
8. **Fix the transparency defect** and prove it fixed with the orbit gate at the commit that claims
   it, from 24 angles at 3 distances at 2 times of day, for the player and every creature class.

## Falsification audit

The strongest attack on this child is the sourcing risk itself: a half-populated glTF world with
primitive fallbacks looks *worse* than a consistent primitive world, because incoherence reads as
broken where uniformity reads as stylised. The guard is that the pivot is all-or-nothing per base
mesh, and the census `bypass` gate makes a mixed state impossible to ship silently.

The second is that the transparency defect may not be in `actor.js` at all — it could be a
render-order, `depthWrite`, or shadow-material interaction owned by A or B. If the orbit gate
localises it outside D's paths, D reports it and the owning child fixes it; D does not reach into
another child's file. That handoff must not become a way for the defect to sit unowned, so the
orchestrator holds it: **the transparency defect is closed by whoever the orbit gate points at, and
W1-30V tracks it until `verified-fixed`.**

Least certain: whether faces and hands at a Morrowind-appropriate fidelity are achievable without a
dedicated character artist's judgement. They are the two things a person looks at first, and they are
the two things hardest to reach procedurally.
