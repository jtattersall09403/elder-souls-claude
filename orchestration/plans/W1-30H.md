## Where we are, precisely

**Water** (`visual-foundation.js:179-248` today, `water.js` after S) is genuinely good: three crossed
incommensurate wave terms chosen specifically to avoid the axis-aligned light/dark lanes that
independent X/Z sines produce; a projected reflection blended with a screen-space lookup at 0.62;
distance-widened grazing response so a broad marsh vista keeps its sheen; reflected radiance colour-
graded back toward regional water rather than copied as a white mirror; shore foam; capillary and
micro ripple terms deliberately kept below wallpaper threshold. The comments record two real bugs
found by hardware capture — a reflection applied to `diffuseColor` before lighting, and a `normal`
reference before `normal_fragment_begin` declares it. **This is the standard the rest of the tree
should be held to.**

**Spell VFX** (`spell-vfx.js`, 48 KB) has pooled/instanced systems, a depth/colour prepass gated on
non-zero particle counts (a real, recorded optimisation), and residue decals. It has never been shown
to a person.

The gap in both: they were built when there was no bloom chain, no GTAO, no real IBL and no MSAA.
Every one of those changes what these effects should do — an emissive tuned against a 0.075-gain
4-tap bloom will be wrong against a 5-mip chain, and soft particles reading a depth buffer now share
that buffer with A's prepass.

## The work

1. **Re-tune every emissive against A's real bloom.** Per-effect emissive gains, thresholds and
   colours re-derived once the bloom chain lands. This is not polish; the current values are
   calibrated against something that no longer exists.
2. **Soft particles reading the shared depth prepass** rather than any private copy, registered
   through `renderer.registerPrePass`.
3. **Mesh spells lit, not unlit.** Any spell with volume uses a lit material consuming B's lighting
   and C's families, so a summoned thing sits in the world rather than floating in front of it.
4. **Residue that persists and weathers.** Bounded decal/projection pools; residue darkens, dries and
   fades on a timescale a player notices; wet residue responds to B's rain.
5. **One opaque-scene copy** for any distortion, shared, never per-effect.
6. **Per-school visual language.** RI-MAG05's binding per-school palette, matter/wetness/asymmetry
   rules, ≥ 3 distinct systems per released spell, ≥ 1 residue decal (Veiling excepted), ≥ 4
   mesh-shaped spells. Generic hues, abstract-light vocabulary, symmetry and flash-in summons are
   native failures — this is art direction, not effects volume.
7. **Water: keep, and extend to the new pipeline.** Re-verify against real IBL (the `esSky` constant
   `vec3(0.20,0.37,0.44)` should become B's actual sky luminance), against cascaded shadows on the
   surface, and against MSAA. Add depth-based absorption so shallow and deep water differ, and shore
   wetness on the adjacent terrain material (C's wetness mask).
8. **Preserve determinism.** Water phase is already driven from simulation frame. Every VFX system
   must be too, and the frame-hash gate proves it.

## The bar

| gate | acceptance, with units | null control |
|---|---|---|
| **emissives read as light** | Every released spell shows a bloom halo ≥ 12 px at half intensity and casts measurable light on ≥ 1 nearby surface (luminance rise ≥ 8%) | disable the spell's light; the surface rise must vanish while the halo remains — proving they are separate mechanisms |
| **soft particles** | Zero hard intersection lines where a particle meets geometry, across all spell sequences; measured as edge-gradient width ≥ 3 px at every particle/geometry intersection | disable depth read; hard lines must appear |
| **residue persists and weathers** | ≥ 1 residue per spell (Veiling excepted), visible ≥ 20 s, with a measurable darkening/fading curve; under rain, wet-declaring residues change specular ≥ 25% | freeze the weathering curve; the change must vanish |
| **schools are distinguishable** | Naive judges shown unlabelled spell frames correctly group them by school ≥ 70% | apply one palette; must fall to chance |
| **not abstract light** | RI-MAG05's matter/wetness/asymmetry rules pass on all nine language-carrying effects; zero symmetric flash-in summons | — |
| **water survives the new pipeline** | Across the 12 Deck motion sequences and the water-bearing stills, water shows: temporal variance above the M12 floor; reflections that track B's actual sky colour through a full day cycle; no axis-aligned lane artefacts; shore foam at every shore | pin `esSky` to the old constant; the day-cycle tracking must break |

| **determinism** | same seed → identical frame hashes across all spell and water sequences | switch any system to wall clock; hashes must diverge |

## Falsification audit

The strongest attack is that **H's biggest risk is regression, not absence.** The water shader is the
best thing in the tree and the pipeline is changing underneath it: real IBL, cascaded shadows,
MSAA, a real bloom chain and a real grade all change how it resolves. A builder re-tuning against the
new pipeline could easily make it worse while every check stays green, because the checks were
written against the old one. That is why the water gate explicitly requires no regression against
pre-H captures and why the day-cycle sequence is mandatory rather than a still.

The second: spell VFX are the easiest place to score well on statistics — they are bright, they move,
they raise every temporal and colour metric. The school-grouping test and RI-MAG05's
matter/asymmetry/residue rules are the counterweight, because they ask whether the effects *mean*
anything.

Least certain: whether `esSky`'s replacement with B's real sky luminance improves or harms the look.
The constant was presumably chosen because it looked right; the principled version may look worse
under overcast. The gate requires day-cycle tracking; if the principled version genuinely reads worse,
the honest resolution is a graded blend between the two, recorded, not a silent revert.
