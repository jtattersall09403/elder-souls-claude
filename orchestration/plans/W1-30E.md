# W1-30E — architecture, settlements and the model kit

**The other half of the geometry deficit, and the child that carries the owner's "nice building model,
tweaked and reused" example.** Settlement street shots are mandatory in the Deck precisely because
building quality at 4–8 m is where a world stops being able to hide.

## Owned paths (exclusive write)

`game/src/render/exterior.js`, `game/src/render/places.js`, `game/src/render/lib/kits.js`,
`game/assets/w1-30/kits/**`.

Read-only: `render/world-art.js` (the settlement grammars live there and are a shared vocabulary),
`world/province.js` (F's — placement stays there).

## Where we are, precisely

`exterior.js` is 153 KB and contains almost no explicit geometry construction beyond
`TorusGeometry` ×9 and `ConeGeometry` ×1 — everything else is `box()`, `cyl()` and `part()` helpers
assembling boxes and cylinders into named features (`hel_rootgate`, `lil_wet_arcade`,
`sou_drowned_court_step`, `tho_bow_rack` …). There are dozens of these and they are thoughtfully
composed: each names a real cultural feature, and `BatchedMesh` batching landed for performance.

The problem is that **a box with a cone on it is a box with a cone on it.** There is no bevel, no
edge break, no trim, no decal, no wear, no thickness where thickness reads. At 4 m these are
blockout, and blockout is the single strongest "unfinished" signal a game can send.

`world-art.js` gives each of eight settlements a `grammar`, `support`, `trim` and `imperial` flag —
Helstrom is `shell-pier-market` on `walking-root-piles` with `bone-lashings`. Today those are strings
attached to nothing. Making them assembly rules over shared parts is this child's core job and is
exactly the reuse mechanism the owner described.

## The work

1. **A kit of parts, small and good.** Target ≤ 25 parts: `wall`, `wall.window`, `corner`,
   `roof.hip`, `roof.reed`, `roof.shell`, `door`, `shutter`, `stair`, `balcony`, `trim.course`,
   `awning`, `sign`, `pier`, `buttress`, `post`, `railing`, `rope`, `crate`, `barrel`, `basket`,
   `lantern`, `bracket`, `plank`, `net`. Each authored (or generated by a committed script) with
   **bevelled edges**, real thickness, UVs onto C's trim atlas, and three LODs.
2. **Grammars, not buildings.** A per-settlement rule set that assembles parts: footprint, storey
   rules, roof selection, support type, trim placement, asymmetry seed, decoration density. Eight
   grammars over one kit. `world-art.js`'s strings become the grammar identifiers.
3. **Variation from variants.** Palette, wear, storey count, footprint, asymmetry seed. Two houses in
   one street must differ; a house in Helstrom and a house in Thorn must differ *more*, and along the
   axis the grammar names.
4. **Silhouette work.** Rooflines, overhangs, chimneys, masts, banners, the things that make a
   skyline. Morrowind's towns are recognisable from their silhouette at 200 m; ours must be too, and
   the Deck's settlement-approach shots are the gate.
5. **Landmarks as authored exceptions.** A small number of hero structures — Blackrose's prison, the
   Hist, Gideon's Imperial forms as deliberate contrast — outside the kit, each with a written
   exemption. Cap the count so the kit carries the world and the heroes carry the memory.
6. **Street furniture and dressing.** Doors that look like doors, lamps that light, signs, cargo,
   drying racks, nets, the accumulated stuff that makes a place inhabited rather than modelled.
7. **Interior thresholds and door surrounds** so that G's interiors and E's exteriors meet cleanly at
   the seam a player walks through.

## The bar

| gate | acceptance, with units | null control |
|---|---|---|
| **kit economy** | ≥ 40 distinct buildings assembled from ≤ 25 parts; census reports zero building meshes without a `kitId` | hand-author one building outside the kit; census `bypass` must go red |
| **variation within a street** | In each of the 8 settlement street shots, no two adjacent buildings share the same silhouette hash; ≥ 5 distinct roof profiles per settlement | force one variant spec for all; the hash collision count must jump |
| **settlements are attributable** | Naive judges shown unlabelled street shots correctly attribute them to settlements at ≥ 55% (chance = 12.5%) | apply one grammar to all eight; attribution must fall to chance |
| **silhouette reads at range** | From the 8 approach shots, naive judges correctly attribute at ≥ 45%, using skyline alone | flatten rooflines to one profile; must fall to chance |
| **edges are broken** | ≥ 95% of kit part edges carry a bevel or trim; measured as the fraction of silhouette edge pixels with a shading gradient over ≥ 2 px rather than a hard step | ship un-bevelled parts; the fraction must collapse |
| **street level survives** | Deck Craft on the 8 mandatory street shots ≥ 7.0 — this child cannot pass while its own shots are the worst three | — |
| **Gideon is the exception** | Imperial forms appear only where `world-art.js` marks `imperial:true`, and read as deliberate contrast rather than default; naive judges asked "which of these looks like it was built by a different culture?" pick Gideon/Stormhold ≥ 70% | mark every settlement imperial; the signal must vanish |
| **budget** | ≤ 900 draw calls in the worst settlement street shot; ≤ 1.2 M triangles; `BatchedMesh` batching preserved | — |
| **no copy-paste read** | WLD03's copy-paste matrix: no settlement shows the same building placement pattern as another | — |

**Hard fails:** a building outside the kit without a written exemption; more than 6 landmark
exemptions total; a part without LODs; a part not consuming C's trim atlas; loss of the existing
`BatchedMesh` batching; a settlement whose grammar is not derived from `world-art.js`.

**Builder** — the kit (authored or scripted, with the sourcing spike and stop decision as in W1-30D);
the eight grammars; variant specs; landmarks with exemptions; street dressing; thresholds; offline
gates (census, draw-call and triangle budgets, silhouette-hash variation, bevel fraction); a bounded

**Fresh critic** — the full Deck; all 8 street and all 8 approach shots at 3 times × 2 weathers; the
naive attribution packs (built by a different agent); WLD03's complete settlement anatomy/identity
visual population and copy-paste matrix; the exemption audit; the census sample; minimum aggregation;
open defect inventory re-run.

## Reuse rows

**Consumed:** C's material API, trim atlas, wear/wetness masks, palette variants; D's `models.js`

footprint, trim selection, asymmetry seed.

## Dependencies and stop conditions

Depends on **W1-30S** and **W1-30C phase 1**. Benefits from **W1-30D**'s loader; if D has not landed
it, E may implement the loader in `models.js` **only by agreement**, since D owns that file —
otherwise E waits, or E scripts geometry generation instead, which needs no loader at all and is the
preferred unblocked route.

Stop and record if kit economy cannot be reached — if eight distinguishable settlements genuinely
need more than 25 parts, that is a finding about the world's ambition, not a failure, and the cap is
raised in writing with the count.

## Falsification audit

The strongest attack: a kit produces *uniformity*, and uniformity is the failure mode this child is
supposed to cure. Eight grammars over one kit could easily read as one town eight times. That is why
attribution-by-naive-judge is the primary gate, at both street and skyline range, and why the
adjacent-silhouette-hash check exists at street level.

The second: the existing named cultural features in `exterior.js` represent real thought about what
Helstrom and Lilmoth *are*, and a builder rebuilding from scratch would throw that away. The plan
requires them to be rebuilt as kit parts, and the critic should check that the specific named
features survive the transition.

Least certain: the bevel-fraction metric. It is a proxy for "does this look made", it is measurable,
and it is gameable by bevelling everything uniformly, which itself looks wrong. The human read on
street shots is the real gate and the bevel metric is a tripwire, consistent with the evidence
standard.
