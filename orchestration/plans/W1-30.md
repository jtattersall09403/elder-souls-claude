### R1 — the human read is RI-VIS06 Protocol A, not a new ladder

**The defect.** Part 1 below correctly convicts the old contract of gating on instruments rather
than pictures. But `W1-30-EVIDENCE.md` §2 then made the *primary* signal a fresh naive 1–10 "Craft"
score against *"anchored comparison plates … placed at known ladder positions"*, and named no
instrument by path. Two things are wrong with that, and both are the failure this rewrite exists to
cure, recurring one level up.

First, **it is a second implementation of `RI-VIS06` Protocol A** — rule 10, the rule that gave this
project a good detection model and a broken one at the same time. Protocol A is already a blind,
paired, anchored comparison with a defined ladder mapping (`RI-VIS06` §C: ours loses every pair at
high confidence → cap 5; loses some and wins some post-escalation → cap 8) and a defined penalty for
not running (**zero pairs run caps FIDELITY at 7**). A tree whose target is ≥ 7.0 that never runs
Protocol A is capped at exactly its own bar and cannot prove it.

Second, **the premise for inventing a replacement is stale.** Part 1 says Protocol A is skipped
"because the legal paired modern pixels largely do not exist". That was true when the old plan was
written and `RI-VIS09` §3 was drafted; it is not true now. `corpus/70-visual/refs/modern/` holds
**131 files across seven folders** — `exterior_daylight` 43 (39 `pixel_metrics_valid`, 1920×1080),
`exterior_lowlight` 24, `interior_darkemissive` 22, `character_closeup` 13, `combat` 12,
`material_closeup` 9, `ui` 8 — landed by the reference acquisition of 2026-08-06 (`refs/MANIFEST.json`,
`refs/ACQUISITION-PROGRESS.md`). Every appearance profile the Deck shoots now has a reference
population. `RI-VIS09` §2's routing table and §3's "five of six folders are empty" predate that
acquisition and were never updated.

And third, the invented ladder has no artefact at its own load-bearing rung. Every modern plate is a
shipped AAA frame, i.e. near the top. Nothing in the corpus sits at 7. So `Craft ≥ 7.0` — an
**absolute** row in W1-30D, W1-30E and W1-30G — is an unanchored opinion wearing a number.

**The fix.**

1. The primary human read is **`RI-VIS06` Protocol A**, run per Deck shot against a
   **profile-matched** plate from `refs/modern/` (a street shot pairs against `exterior_daylight`, a
   night exterior against `exterior_lowlight`, an interior against `interior_darkemissive`, a
   character close-up against `character_closeup`, a material close-up against `material_closeup`).
   Its native ladder mapping and its caps apply unchanged. `RI-VIS07` and Protocol B against
   `refs/morrowind/` remain the art-direction side, unchanged.
2. **Absolute `Craft ≥ 7.0` rows in D, E and G are replaced by their Protocol A equivalent**: the
   blind pair-preference rate against the profile-matched plate, at the confidence Protocol A
   defines. The pre-child baseline of our own frame is the bottom anchor, `refs/anti-generic/` is
   the negative anchor for Place, and the modern plate is the top anchor. **Delta rows —
   `Craft ≥ pre-X + 1.5 on the minimum` — survive as written**, because a same-pack comparison needs
   no external anchor and is the most robust number in the whole standard.
3. The owner's question — *"is this a screenshot you would send someone to show them a game you were
   excited about?"* — is **retained and reported as a yes-rate, and is not a gate**. It is the thing
   the owner actually described and it belongs in every verdict; it is also a single unanchored
   judgement and must not be load-bearing.
4. The planted tell, the pack quarantine, the build-and-judge separation and the unseen census
   sample are all **kept exactly as written**. They are the strongest anti-gaming machinery in the
   document and none of them depends on the ladder.

**First action, and it belongs to W1-30V before any pack is built:** confirm which `refs/modern/`
folders the fidelity side may cite. `RI-VIS09` §2 grants Protocol A only `refs/modern/character_closeup/`
and bars `refs/modern/hud/` outright, and it was written when the other folders were empty. If the
routing genuinely does not extend to the new folders, that is a **corpus** question and goes to
`ARBITRATION.md` — it is not a builder's or a critic's judgement call, and it must not be resolved by
quietly citing whatever is on disk. The same pass checks two things this review could not: whether the
acquired frames are HUD-free (`RI-VIS09` bars HUD-bearing frames from the ref side of any blind pair,
and the acquisition deliberately kept HUD material), and their licence position (`MANIFEST.json`
records `licence: null` on the `exterior_daylight` records sampled here).

**Overturned by:** an arbiter ruling that the acquired `refs/modern/` folders may not be cited by
the fidelity side. In that case Protocol A stays capped, the FIDELITY cap of 7 binds, and the honest
consequence is that this tree **cannot demonstrate above 7.0 on the fidelity axis** — which is a
finding to report, not a reason to substitute an unanchored score.

### R2 — the concept-art child is ruled in, as W1-30K, and it blocks nothing

The previous reviewer flagged in its own falsification audit that E and F *"may not cohere without
one"*, declined to add it because it could not establish we can generate or license usable key art,
and wrote that a reviewer adding it would be right. **Ruled: add it.** Art direction nobody has
written down becomes whatever each builder individually imagined, and eleven parallel children is
exactly the population that turns that into eleven different games. `world-art.js` gives a shared
*vocabulary* — a terrain word, three flora scalars, a lean, a grammar string — and the parent's own
Part 5 concedes that whether that vocabulary reaches pixels is unmeasured.

**How it gets its art without blocking, which is the objection that stopped it last time: it draws
nothing and licenses nothing.** W1-30K is a **numeric look target**, derived entirely from plates the
repository already holds and is already permitted to cite:

- `refs/morrowind/REF-A*` (89 files) for the art-direction half — `RI-VIS09` §3.3 states plainly what
  those thumbnails *are* valid for: palette, silhouette, built form, flora and creature design
  language. That is precisely a look board's content, and it is the one use the thumbnails support.
- `refs/modern/` per profile for the fidelity half — value range, contrast, atmospheric depth.
- `refs/anti-generic/` (5) as the negative anchor: the things Black Marsh must not drift toward.
- `refs/context/` for Shadowfen/Murkmire context only, never as a target (`RI-VIS09` §2).

Its output is numbers and named vocabulary, not pictures: per region and per settlement, a hue /
value / chroma range, a key-to-fill ratio, a fog density and horizon visibility distance, a silhouette
language (roofline, canopy and landform verbs), and a wear vocabulary. Those are consumed by **A**'s
grade recipes, **B**'s fog profiles and lighting recipes, **C**'s palette variants, **E**'s grammars
and **F**'s landform profiles, and each child's critic checks its own shots against the board's
ranges. A board expressed as numbers is checkable; a text style board is what we have had, and Part 5
records that it has never produced a coherent look.

It cannot block: it touches no game code, it starts now, and children in flight consume it when it
lands. Until it lands they proceed against `world-art.js` exactly as today — the board tightens their
targets, it does not gate their start. **Owner: a new child, not W1-30V** — V judges, and an agent
must not author a target it also scores against.

**Overturned by:** W1-30K finding that the board's numbers cannot honestly be derived from plates the
corpus already holds. Then the board is an opinion, and the correct action is to delete it and say so
rather than to publish a target every child is then measured against.

creature class gets **24 stops at one distance at one time (24 frames)**, escalating to the full orbit

- **delta and paired rows survive** — `Craft ≥ pre-X + 1.5`, sabotage matrices, null controls,

### R5 — cross-child acceptance rows need a named evaluation point

## Part 1 — The judgement: will the previous plan produce a game that looks good?

**No.** A build that passed every gate in the old §A–§D could still look like a prototype, and the
current build — which is a substantial, honest attempt at that plan — demonstrates exactly that.
This is not a criticism of the builders. They did what the plan asked. The plan asked for the wrong
things, in a specific and diagnosable way.

### The structural defect: the contract measured instruments, not pictures

Read the old acceptance contract as a whole and count what it actually gates on:

| what §A–§D gate on | rows | what a person sees |
|---|---|---|
| instrument validity — does the metric implement the literal equation; does the sabotage go red; is the population complete; is the capture settled | the large majority | nothing |
| reference legality — manifest hashes, routing, preregistration, provenance | ~8 rows | nothing |
| image statistics (RI-VIS03 M1–M12) | 12 | weakly, and gameable |
| a human being shown a frame and asked whether it is good | **effectively one**, RI-VIS06 Protocol A — which the plan itself says is *"skipped/capped exactly by VIS06/VIS09"* because the legal paired modern pixels largely do not exist | the whole question |

> **Corrected by R1.** That last row was true of the *old plan* and is no longer true of the world.
> `refs/modern/` was populated on 2026-08-06 and now holds 131 files across seven profile folders,
> including 43 `exterior_daylight` frames at 1920×1080. **Protocol A is not structurally unrunnable;
> it was unrun.** The diagnosis in this section is correct — the old contract's load-bearing signal
> really did reduce to noise-raisable statistics — but the remedy is to *run the anchored instrument
> the corpus already defines*, not to build a second one. See R1.

RI-VIS07 is the other human read, and it asks *"could this be Skyrim?"* — a **genericness** test, not
a quality test. A blockout of grey boxes in a swamp passes VIS07 with distinction: nobody would
mistake it for Skyrim. It is a necessary gate and a completely insufficient one.

So the old contract's load-bearing quality signal reduces to M1–M12 image statistics. And M1–M12 are
the family of statistics that **procedural noise raises**. The plan's own falsification audit spots
this — *"Metrics reward noise, resampling or empty support"* — and prescribes an injected-noise red
control. But that control checks the metric *responds* to noise; it does not check the *build* is not
noise. The control is inverted. And the shipped build then did exactly the thing the inverted control
cannot see: `game/src/render/visual-foundation.js:112` generates a 96×96 multi-scale hash-noise field
per material family and binds it as albedo, height, AO and roughness for the whole world.

That is the gap the owner named, stated mechanically. **Every gate below is now written so that a
person looking at pictures is the primary signal and the statistics are a regression tripwire.**

### Ten concrete reasons the current build does not look good

Each is a specific file and number, and each has a named owner in the decomposition.

1. **The world frame has no antialiasing at all.** `renderer.js:64` requests `antialias:true` on the
   WebGL context, but `renderer.js:879` renders the world into `this.worldTarget`
   (`renderer.js:253`), and a render target's MSAA comes from its `samples` property, which is never
   set. Context MSAA applies only to the default framebuffer. The world therefore resolves with zero
   multisampling. The composite's substitute (`renderer.js:~890`) is a depth-edge detector that
   blends 4 neighbours at `edge*0.38` — it cannot see a colour edge at constant depth, cannot see an
   alpha-tested foliage edge, and against sky the depth delta saturates so it smears rather than
   resolves. Jagged, crawling silhouettes in motion are the single most reliable "this is a hobby
   project" tell. → **W1-30A**
2. **Image-based lighting is 128 texels.** `sky.js:185` allocates `new Uint8Array(16 * 8 * 4)` and
   assigns that 16×8 DataTexture as `scene.environment`. There is no `PMREMGenerator`, so there is no
   roughness-convolved mip chain: every `MeshStandardMaterial` in the game samples the same
   near-uniform blob for specular regardless of roughness. Metal, wet chitin, resin and water all get
   a flat tint where they should get a recognisable environment. The old plan's §3 explicitly asked
   for PMREM; the gate ("IBL present and sabotage-detectable") passed a 128-texel stand-in, because
   switching it off does change pixels. → **W1-30B**
3. **Shadows cover a 120 m box and nothing beyond it.** `sky.js:139-148`: one 2048² map, ortho
   ±60 m, near 40, far 210. Past ~60 m from the focus everything is unshadowed. A vista is exactly
   the shot where a player judges a game, and ours loses its depth cue precisely there. The old plan
   permitted cascades ("if one map cannot span the required range") and no gate required them. →
   **W1-30B**
4. **The colour grade is a rounding error.** The composite applies saturation ×1.035, contrast
   ×1.015, a 0.08 smoothstep toe and a 7.5% vignette. That is not art direction; it is a no-op with
   a comment. Black Marsh has no tonal identity in the pipeline, and per-region/per-weather grading —
   the cheapest large win available in any renderer — does not exist. → **W1-30A**
5. **Bloom is four taps at a two-pixel radius.** Threshold 0.72, add 0.075. Real bloom needs a
   multi-resolution downsample/upsample chain; at 2 px it is an imperceptible halo, so emissives
   (windows, spells, sapwells, fires) do not read as light sources. → **W1-30A**
6. **Ambient occlusion is a 3-pixel depth ring clamped to 0.12.** Contact darkening — under eaves,
   at foliage bases, in crevices, where a prop meets ground — is what makes objects look *placed*
   rather than *floating*. At 3 px and 12% it is invisible at any normal viewing distance. →
   **W1-30A**
7. **There is not one authored 3D model in the repository.** `grep -rn "GLTFLoader\|\.glb\|\.gltf"`
   over `game/src` returns nothing. Characters in `render/actor.js` are assembled from 17
   `SphereGeometry`, 9 `CapsuleGeometry`, 9 `ConeGeometry`, 6 `TorusGeometry`, 6 `CylinderGeometry`
   and 3 `DodecahedronGeometry`, skinned onto a hand-built `THREE.Skeleton`. Buildings in
   `render/exterior.js` are boxes, cylinders, cones and half-tori. No material work makes a
   capsule-and-sphere Argonian look like a character; no lighting makes a box with a cone on top look
   like a house. RI-VIS08 and RI-CAM07 measure silhouette IoU, blend continuity, root-motion
   agreement and attachment correctness — **all of which a primitive assembly can pass.** The old
   plan's §9 said "upgrade existing rigs **or** load legal skinned glTF"; the builder took the
   cheaper arm, legitimately, and the gates did not object. This is the largest single gap between
   "passes" and "looks good". → **W1-30D** (characters), **W1-30E** (architecture)
8. **The entire province is textured by three authored material sets.**
   `game/assets/w1-30/materials/` holds full 1k albedo+normal+roughness for exactly three slugs —
   `brown_mud`, `bark_brown_01`, `plastered_stone_wall` — shared across 9 of the 20 semantic
   families. Nine more families get a single 256 px **albedo-only** jpg (no normal, no roughness).
   `skin` and `water` get procedural noise only. So most of the world has no normal map and no
   roughness variation, which is why surfaces read as flat plastic under any light. → **W1-30C**
9. **Foliage is not alpha-tested.** `visual-foundation.js:159` defaults `alphaTest: 0`, and no
   consumer overrides it for leaf/reed. Vegetation is therefore opaque geometry rather than
   alpha-cut cards or cut-out canopies, which is why crowns read as solid lumps rather than layered
   silhouettes. Related: no LOD fade/dither, so `province.js`'s near/far band swap pops. → **W1-30F**
10. **8-bit output with no dither.** A marsh sky is a wide, low-contrast gradient composited through
    fog; at `SRGBColorSpace` 8-bit with no ordered/blue-noise dither this bands visibly, and banding
    is a second reliable amateur tell. One line in the composite. → **W1-30A**

### What the old plan *did* get right, and is preserved

- **The shared-foundation instinct.** `visual-foundation.js` and `world-art.js` are the right shape:
  a semantic family registry that world builders request from, instead of inventing flat materials.
  `exterior.js` (153 KB) contains **zero** direct `new THREE.MeshStandardMaterial` — everything
  routes through the registry. That is a genuine, hard-won structural win and every child plan
  inherits it rather than replacing it.
- **HDR-linear until the final composite.** `renderer.js:253` is a `HalfFloatType` target with
  `LinearSRGBColorSpace`, tone-mapped exactly once. That is correct and rare; keep it.
- **The sabotage-switch discipline.** `renderer.js` `setVisualFeature()` gives every feature a live
  off-switch that changes shipping pixels. That is the right instrument and the children keep it.
- **Fail-closed vocabularies.** `regionArt()`/`settlementArt()`/`creatureArt()` throw on unknown IDs.
- **Determinism.** Water phase is driven from simulation frame, not wall clock, so captures hash
  reproducibly. Every child plan must preserve this.
- **The cost-order instinct** — cheap offline gates before browsers — is retained wholesale.

### Three.js constraints, named rather than dropped

The old plan's envelope (r180 core only, `WebGLRenderer`, additive vendoring of same-version official
addons) is correct and stays. Two things it left ambiguous, ruled here:

- **Vendoring the official r180 post-processing addons is approved and expected**, not a last resort:
  `EffectComposer`, `RenderPass`, `OutputPass`, `SMAAPass`, `UnrealBloomPass`, `GTAOPass`/`SSAOPass`,
  and `PMREMGenerator` (core). Licence files travel with them. Hand-rolling a bloom chain in a
  fullscreen `ShaderMaterial`, which is what we have, cost more and delivered less.
- **`GLTFLoader`, `DRACOLoader` and `KTX2Loader` are approved and required.** Reaching a 7/10 look
  with zero authored geometry is not achievable in Three.js or any other engine. This is the
  constraint the old plan should have named and did not.
- **What genuinely is out of reach in WebGL2/r180**: ray-traced reflections, real global
  illumination, and hardware VRS. The substitutes are named per child: screen-space reflection only
  on water, baked/probe ambient for interiors, and an SSAO/GTAO+contact-shadow stack for grounding.
  Do not silently drop these; state the substitute.

### Why children dispatch now rather than waiting for recriticism

`PLAN-LOOP.md` rule 1 governs the *parent*'s state. The children are new plans in their own right and
each carries its own `Plan-State`. Blocking all visual work behind a parent recriticism round would
idle the owner's top priority for a review cycle, which rule 0 and directive §4 both forbid. The
ruling, **reversible**: children S, A, B, C and V dispatch on their own plan states; the parent's
recriticism runs in parallel and any finding is written back. What would overturn this: a reviewer
finding that the decomposition's seams are wrong, in which case in-flight children stop at their next
commit boundary rather than merging.

## Part 4 — Reuse, made structural

Full mechanism in `orchestration/plans/W1-30-LIBRARY.md`. The parent binds every child to three
required outputs, each mechanically checked by `tools/visual/library-census.mjs`:

1. **Consumed** — the list of library IDs the child used. A child that built something a library
   entry already provides fails.
2. **Published** — the list of new library entries the child added. Every entry needs **two distinct
   consumers** or a written exemption naming why it is genuinely singular. A child that produces a
   good result by a bespoke route and leaves nothing behind has done half the job (directive §3).
3. **Variant, not copy** — a second instance of anything is a *variant spec* against an existing
   entry (palette swatch, wear amount, scale, trim selection, grammar rule), never a duplicated
   definition. The census flags near-duplicate definitions by structural hash.

Concretely, this is what makes the owner's two examples work:

- *"a fantastic looking player character → reuse for NPCs"*: W1-30D builds **one** base humanoid mesh
  and **one** base Saxhleel mesh on **one** skeleton. Every NPC and every creature is that skeleton
  with a morph/scale variant, a material variant and equipment attachments. D's acceptance counts the
  number of visually distinct characters derived from the base, and the census fails if any character
  carries a mesh that is not a registered base or variant.
- *"a nice looking building model → tweak and reuse"*: W1-30E builds a **kit** — wall, corner, roof,
  door, window, stair, trim, awning, sign — and a per-settlement **grammar** that assembles them.
  Eight settlements share the parts and differ by grammar, palette and wear. E's acceptance requires
  that the kit part count is small and the resulting building count is large, and that no settlement
  has a bespoke building outside the kit without a named exemption.

## Part 5b — What the frames actually show

**The VISUAL-TRUTH-AUDIT reported mid-review.** Its first captures —
`docs/shots/2026-08-14-visual-truth/`, 24 orbit angles plus a 16-step look sweep plus a
world-hidden "holes" pass, from the running game on the GPU pod — were read directly for this
review. Part 1's ten defects were derived from code; these are from pixels, and they change the
priority order.

**The single most destructive setting in the game is the fog.** `sky.js` sets
`FogExp2(0x9aa79a, 0.0022)`. In the captures, a settlement roughly 40 m away is a white smear with no
readable silhouette; distant trees are white ghosts; the hills behind them are flat paper cut-outs;
the whole frame is a narrow grey-green band. It is not moody, it is *erasing the world*. Every other
improvement in this tree — cascades, IBL, terrain layers, settlement silhouettes, region character —
is invisible past 40 m until this is fixed. **It is close to a one-line change and it moves more
pixels than anything else in the plan.** Elevated to W1-30B's first item.

**Nothing casts a shadow.** Across all 24 orbit frames at noon there is not one directional shadow —
not from the player, not from the boardwalk posts, not from buildings. `shadowMap.enabled` is true
and `sun.castShadow` is true, so this is a defect, not an absence: the most likely cause is the
shadow camera's `near = 40` (`sky.js:141`) clipping the scene depending on where the light is placed.
A shadowless noon frame is why everything looks pasted on. Elevated to W1-30B and it should be checked
before the cascade work, because it may be a bug rather than a feature gap.

**The player's transparency defect is geometric, not material — and the audit proved it.** The
material audit found every visible mesh in the player subtree at `transparent:false, opacity:1`. The
world-hidden capture shows why you can still see through him: **the body parts are disjoint
primitives that do not join.** There is sky between the shoulder plate and the arm, sky between the
head and the shoulders, and the torso is a flat shield-shaped plate rather than a volume. Every
previous attempt to fix this as an alpha or render-order problem was doomed, which is exactly why it
kept being declared fixed. **The fix is welded or authored geometry — W1-30D — and nothing else will
close it.** Recorded here so no future agent spends another round on material flags.

Other defects the frames show that code reading did not:

| defect | owner |
|---|---|
| tree canopies rendering with no trunk — discs floating in the sky at the top of the frame | W1-30F |
| terrain is a flat plane with a visible ~1 m quad grid and hard tile seams; zero relief, zero scatter | W1-30F |
| roads and boardwalks are flat hard-edged quads sitting *on* the ground with no blend, no contact darkening, and visible overlap | **W1-30F closes it** (R4d); E supplies the boardwalk kit parts F blends in |

| character proportions: legs are roughly twice the torso; no neck; hands are cylinders | W1-30D |

## Falsification audit: the strongest attack on this document

**The most likely way this plan is wrong** is that it over-weights authored assets. If W1-30D and
W1-30E cannot source or produce legally clean glTF characters and building kits at the quality
implied, the plan will have spent its budget on an asset pipeline and delivered fewer authored assets
than the primitives it replaced — which would look *worse*, because a half-populated glTF world with
primitive fallbacks is less coherent than a fully primitive one. **The guard**: D and E each open
with a cheap sourcing spike whose stop condition is explicit — if legal assets at the required
quality cannot be identified in the spike, the child pivots to *high-quality procedural geometry with
authored trim sheets and bevelled silhouettes*, which is reachable in-repo, and says so in writing
rather than continuing to shop.

**The second strongest attack** is that A, B and C together could raise every statistic and every
human read without D, E, F ever landing — good light on bad geometry — and the parent would then be
tempted to declare a demo. That is a real risk and it is the reason the Deck's aggregation is
**minimum over shots** with a mandatory character close-up and a mandatory street-level building
shot in the fixed list. A build cannot reach 7.0 while its characters are capsules, no matter what
the vista does.

**Where I am least certain**: whether the 7.0 art-direction axis can be reached at all without a
dedicated concept pass — a small set of authored key images that define what Black Marsh looks like
here, which every child then matches. The old plan's "style boards" are text records
(`world-art.js`), and text style boards have never in this project's history produced a coherent
look. I have not added a concept-art child because I cannot establish that we can generate or license
usable key art, and inventing a blocked child is worse than naming the risk. **A reviewer's strongest
move against this document is to argue that W1-30E and W1-30F cannot cohere without one, and to add
it.** I would not resist that edit.

> **Ruled in by R2, and the objection dissolved rather than overridden.** The blocker was licensing
> and generation: we cannot draw key art and cannot license it. **W1-30K does neither.** It derives a
> *numeric* look target from plates the corpus already holds and is already permitted to cite —
> `refs/morrowind/REF-A*` for the exact uses `RI-VIS09` §3.3 says those thumbnails support (palette,
> silhouette, built form, flora and creature design language), `refs/anti-generic/` as the negative
> anchor, `refs/modern/` per profile for value and depth. Nothing is drawn, nothing is licensed,
> nothing blocks. The reviewer's instinct was right and its stop condition was the wrong one: the
> question was never "can we obtain key art", it was "can art direction be written down in a form a
> builder can hit and a critic can check". Numbers can be; prose has not been.

### What this recriticism round attacked hardest, and what survived

The instruction to this round was to test whether the rewrite swapped one unfalsifiable gate for
another, on the grounds that a corrected document carries more authority than an uncorrected one.
**It half did.** The demotion of statistics is real and mechanical — noise cannot raise a blind
pair-preference rate, and the census sample, the planted tell, the minimum-over-shots aggregation and
the motion/orbit mandates are all genuinely hard to game. But the instrument sitting on top of that
machinery was a fresh 1–10 opinion with an anchoring story (`plates at known ladder positions`) that
nothing in the repository could supply, resting on a claim about missing reference pixels that had
been false for eight days. That is one unfalsifiable gate replacing another, in the specific place
where it matters most, and R1 is the repair.

The honest summary for the next reviewer: **the diagnosis was right, the architecture was right, and
the top of the measurement stack was not.** Everything in R6 stands.
