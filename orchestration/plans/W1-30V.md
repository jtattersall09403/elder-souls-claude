# W1-30V — visual truth: the Deck, the orbit, the defect inventory

Plan-State: satisfied
Parent: [W1-30](W1-30.md) · Contract: [Builder execution contract](BUILDER-EXECUTION-CONTRACT.md)
Evidence: [W1-30-EVIDENCE.md](W1-30-EVIDENCE.md) · Reuse: [W1-30-LIBRARY.md](W1-30-LIBRARY.md)

**This child touches no game code, so it can start immediately and can never collide with anyone.**
It builds the instrument every other child is judged by, and it is the direct answer to directive §2.

> *"something may seem ok when inspected statically in isolation, but if you actually play the game
> for a short time then major issues become immediately clear."*

## Owned paths (exclusive write)

`tools/visual/**`, `reports/visual-truth/**`, `docs/shots/**` (dated additions).

Zero write access to `game/src/**`. If V finds a defect it files it in the inventory; it never fixes
it. That separation is what makes V's findings usable as evidence against the children.

## The work

1. **`tools/visual/deck.mjs` and `deck.json`.** The fixed, versioned shot list from
   `W1-30-EVIDENCE.md` §1: 48 still setups × 3 times × 2 weathers, 12 motion sequences of ≥ 180
   frames, and the census sample of 12 commit-seeded random shots. Deterministic: fixed seed, fixed
   simulation rate, fixed camera transforms, settled captures, per-frame hashes.
2. **`tools/visual/orbit.mjs`.** 24 stops × 3 distances × 2 times per character and creature, with
   the four automatic checks (transparent-fragment count, alpha-surface invariance, silhouette-area
   smoothness, see-through geometry). This is the instrument that closes the transparency defect
   class permanently.
3. **`tools/visual/contact-sheet.mjs`.** Every motion sequence tiled at every 6th frame. A reviewer
   physically cannot review one frame of a motion sequence when the artefact is a contact sheet, and
   that is the point.
4. **`tools/visual/library-census.mjs`.** The reuse enforcement tool from `W1-30-LIBRARY.md` §4:
   bypass, orphan, duplicate, unknown, regression. Hard-fails on bypass and regression. Ships with
   its own red control.
5. **`tools/visual/judge-pack.mjs`.** Builds quarantined, sealed judging packs: our frames plus
   anchored reference plates at known ladder positions, shuffled, keyed, with a planted tell the
   judge must detect before their scores count. Pack-building and pack-judging are always different
   agents.
6. **`tools/visual/tripwire.mjs`.** Runs RI-VIS03 M1–M12 with their literal definitions against the
   previous stamped commit and fails on regression. Statistics can fail a build and can never pass
   one (`W1-30-EVIDENCE.md` §2).
7. **`reports/visual-truth/INVENTORY.md`.** The standing defect inventory: ID, plain-English
   description, the shot or orbit stop that shows it, the owning child, severity, state. Never closed.
   A defect moves to `verified-fixed` only when reproduced fixed from three angles by someone other
   than whoever fixed it.
8. **The first inventory pass.** Play the game — properly, on the GPU pod, for a sustained session
   across regions, settlements, interiors, combat and spells — and write down everything that looks
   wrong, ranked by how often a player would see it. **This reordering has authority over the
   parent's Part 1 priority list**, because the parent's ranking is inferred from code and this one is
   observed.
9. **Absorb the running VISUAL-TRUTH-AUDIT.** That agent's findings land in
   `reports/visual-truth/`; V merges them into the inventory rather than duplicating the work.
10. **Progress visibility.** A dated selection of contact sheets into `docs/shots/`, and the
    inventory's open/verified counts into `docs/status.json`, so the twice-daily roundup and the
    progress pages have real pictures and a real trend (directives §5, §7).

## The bar

| gate | acceptance, with units | null control |
|---|---|---|
| **the Deck is deterministic** | Two runs at the same commit and seed produce byte-identical frame hashes for all 288 stills and all 12 sequences | change the seed; ≥ 95% of hashes must change. A capture harness whose determinism cannot be falsified is not an instrument |
| **the Deck is complete** | Every one of the 48 setups resolves to a reachable camera in the live game; zero setups silently skipped; a skipped setup is a red row, never an omission | remove a region from the world; its setups must go red rather than vanish |
| **the orbit catches the known defect** | Run the orbit against the current HEAD player. It must either reproduce the owner's transparency defect **or** establish with 144 frames that it is not present. Either is a valid result; "inconclusive" is not | plant a translucent shell on a copy; all four checks must go red |
| **the census tool goes red** | Insert a bare `MeshStandardMaterial` on a world surface on a copy; `bypass` increments and the tool exits non-zero | — |
| **the tripwire goes red** | Degrade one M-metric's input on a copy; the tripwire must fail | pass an unchanged commit; it must not fail |
| **the judge pack cannot be gamed** | Every pack carries a planted tell; a judge that misses it has their scores voided; pack-builder and judge are different agents in every recorded run | submit a pack with no tell; the harness must refuse it |
| **the inventory is honest** | Every open row names a shot or orbit stop that shows it; zero rows without evidence; zero rows closed without three-angle reproduction by a second party | close a row from a single still; the check must reject it |
| **it is actually used** | Every W1-30 child's builder and critic report cites a Deck run at a stamped commit | — |

**Hard fails:** a non-deterministic capture; a silently skipped setup; a defect closed on static
inspection; a pack judged by its builder; SwiftShader captures used for an appearance claim.

## Builder/critic execution allocation

Unusually for this tree, V is mostly tooling, so the split is by *who validates the instrument*.

**Builder** — all ten items; every tool ships with its own red control demonstrated; the first
inventory pass on the GPU pod; the determinism proof; text-only evidence plus the committed contact
sheets.

**Fresh critic** — independently re-runs the Deck at the same commit on a fresh pod and verifies hash
identity; attacks the harness with the specific failures this project has already had (a check that
asserts a canvas exists rather than that pixels are visible; a fixture that does not move; a reading
taken one frame after the event instead of at 600; a blind pack containing nothing that can express
its question); verifies every red control genuinely goes red; audits the inventory for rows closed
without three-angle evidence; confirms the judge-pack quarantine holds.

## Reuse rows

**Consumed:** W1-24's declaration, visual-reading, capture-settle and reference-register instruments —
V repairs and extends them rather than writing a second implementation (rule 10); `tools/runpod/` for
GPU capture; `tools/contention.mjs` before every browser; the existing `tools/metrics/image-metrics.mjs`
where it already implements a literal M-definition. **Published:** every tool above, consumed by all
nine sibling children and by every future visual piece — this is the highest-consumer publication in
the tree. **Variants:** deck profiles (`full`, `child-scoped`, `smoke`) as variant specs over one
manifest, so a child's cheap gate runs a subset and the critic runs the whole thing.

## Dependencies and stop conditions

None. Starts now, runs continuously, never blocks and is never blocked.

Stop and escalate immediately if Deck determinism cannot be achieved — that finding blocks the entire
visual programme, because every gate downstream assumes reproducible captures, and it is better
discovered on day one by V than in week three by a critic.

## Falsification audit

The strongest attack on V is the one the plan loop already records against this project: **a harness
that measures the wrong thing passes forever.** Four real examples are named in the critic's brief
above, and V's own red controls are the answer — but a red control can itself be inert, which is why
the critic re-runs every one of them independently rather than reading V's report.

The second: V is a bottleneck by construction. Every child's critic needs a Deck run, and if the Deck
is slow or the pod is contended, nine children queue behind one tool. The `child-scoped` deck profile
exists for exactly that, and the orchestrator should watch for V becoming the thing everyone waits on.

Least certain: whether naive human judges can be sourced at the volume the evidence standard implies
— several packs per child, different agents for building and judging, fresh each round. If they
cannot, the honest fallback is fewer, better-anchored judgements on the minimum-scoring shots rather
than diluting the standard, and V should say so rather than quietly reusing judges.
