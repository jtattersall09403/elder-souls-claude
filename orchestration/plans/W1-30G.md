# W1-30G — interiors and practical lighting

Plan-State: satisfied
Parent: [W1-30](W1-30.md) · Contract: [Builder execution contract](BUILDER-EXECUTION-CONTRACT.md)

**Morrowind is a game people remember for its interiors** — the cluttered shops, the guild halls, the
dim lit rooms with a shaft of light through a shutter. Ours are 85 KB of generated rooms whose
lighting has never been judged.

## Owned paths (exclusive write)

`game/src/render/interior.js`, `game/src/world/interior-lighting.js`.

Read-only: everything else. Interior *content* (which rooms exist, what services they offer, who is
in them) stays with W1-04; G owns how those rooms look and light.

## Where we are, precisely

`interior.js` is 85 KB with 4 `MeshStandardMaterial` and 1 `MeshBasicMaterial` constructed directly —
so unlike `exterior.js`, interiors have **not** fully adopted the shared material path, and those five
sites are census bypasses to close. `world/interior-lighting.js` exists and is unjudged.
`visual-foundation.js`'s `FEATURE_CONSUMERS` names `interiorDressing:
'room-kind-bays-coffers-and-focal-zones'`, and `renderer.setVisualFeature('interiorDressing', …)`
rebuilds the interior record — so a dressing system exists and toggles. Whether a room reads as a
place someone lives in is unestablished.

The lighting deficit is inherited from B: with 128-texel IBL and no probes, an interior has no
ambient bounce, so anything out of direct lamp range goes to near-black — which is why
`consumeStyleboard`'s diffuse floor exists.

## The work

1. **Close the five material bypasses** so interiors route entirely through C's registry.
2. **Per-room environment probes.** Call B's PMREM generator per room kind (hearth-lit, cold stone,
   emissive cave, shuttered daylight, market hall) so surfaces out of direct light still receive
   plausible bounce. This is the change that makes the diffuse floor unnecessary indoors.
3. **Practical lights that belong to objects.** Every lamp, hearth, candle, sapwell and brazier is a
   light *and* an emissive mesh *and* a bloom source, with a bounded count per room and a falloff
   that reaches the walls. Not uncontrolled per-prop lights: a small authored set per room kind.
4. **Light shafts.** Where a shutter, hatch or roof gap exists, a bounded volumetric shaft consuming
   B's atmosphere parameters. Two or three per interior is the difference between "a room" and "a
   place". Cheap when bounded; disastrous when not, so the count is capped and the cap is a gate.
5. **Dressing that reads as lived-in.** Consume E's kit props: shelves with things on them, hanging
   bundles, cookware, bedding, tools of the occupant's trade, wear on the floor where people walk.
   Density and content driven by room kind and occupant, deterministic by seed.
6. **The threshold.** Exterior→interior transition must not flash: exposure, ambient and fog adapt
   over a short, deterministic interval, and the door surround (E's) and interior wall must meet
   without a gap. This is a specific, common, very visible defect class.
7. **Interior variety.** Room kinds must be distinguishable from each other and instances of a kind
   must differ. A Helstrom market hall and a Blackrose cell should share nothing but the kit.

## The bar

| gate | acceptance, with units | null control |
|---|---|---|
| **no bypasses** | Census reports zero material bypasses in `interior.js`; zero `MeshBasicMaterial` outside declared emissive/decal exemptions | restore one direct `MeshStandardMaterial`; census must go red |
| **rooms are lit, not floored** | Removing `consumeStyleboard`'s diffuse floor does not drive any interior Deck shot below 0.10 mean luma; and ≥ 90% of wall pixels receive measurable light from a probe or practical | disable interior probes; the figure must collapse and the near-black return |
| **practicals read as sources** | In each of the 4 interior Deck shots, ≥ 2 emissive sources show a bloom halo and a matching falloff gradient on the nearest surface (luminance decaying with inverse-square within 20%) | detach the light from the emissive mesh; the falloff correlation must break |
| **shafts** | ≥ 2 interiors show a visible light shaft whose direction agrees with B's sun direction to within 5° | rotate the sun; the shaft must follow |
| **the threshold is clean** | Across a 120-frame walk through each of 8 doorways, no frame shows a luminance jump > 25/255 attributable to the transition, and no frame shows a gap between door surround and interior wall | remove the adaptation; the jump must appear |
| **rooms are distinguishable** | Naive judges shown unlabelled interior shots correctly identify room kind ≥ 65% and correctly answer "are these the same room kind?" ≥ 75% | apply one dressing set; both must fall to chance |
| **lived-in** | Naive judges rate "does someone live/work here?" ≥ 6.5 on the 4 Deck interiors | strip dressing; must fall sharply |
| **budget** | ≤ 8 dynamic lights per room; ≤ 3 shafts per interior; ≤ 500 draw calls in the worst interior | — |
| **human read** | Deck Craft on the 4 interior shots ≥ 7.0 | — |

**Hard fails:** an unbounded per-prop light; a shaft that does not follow the sun; a threshold flash;
a room kind without dressing; determinism broken by non-seeded dressing.

## Dependencies and stop conditions

Depends on **W1-30S**, **W1-30B** (probe generator — this is a hard dependency; without it G cannot

Stop the shaft work if the bounded volumetric exceeds budget in the worst interior; fall back to
authored shaft geometry with a soft material, and record the substitution.

## Falsification audit

The strongest attack: interiors are the easiest place in the game to make one beautiful room and
leave forty untouched, because a player only sees the ones they enter. The guards are the census
sample (12 unseen shots per Deck run, which reaches interiors) and the requirement that room-kind
identification be measured across the *population*, not the showcase.

The second: light shafts and warm practicals flatter everything, and could carry the human read while
geometry and dressing stay poor. The "does someone live/work here?" question is the counterweight,
because clutter cannot be faked with light.

Least certain: whether per-room PMREM probes fit the frame budget when a player moves between rooms
quickly. Probe generation is not per-frame, but a burst of room transitions could stall. The mitigation
is prewarming probes for adjacent rooms at load; whether that is enough is unmeasured.
