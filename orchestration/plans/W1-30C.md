# W1-30C — the material and texture library

Plan-State: satisfied
Parent: [W1-30](W1-30.md) · Contract: [Builder execution contract](BUILDER-EXECUTION-CONTRACT.md)
Evidence: [W1-30-EVIDENCE.md](W1-30-EVIDENCE.md) · Reuse: [W1-30-LIBRARY.md](W1-30-LIBRARY.md)

**This child is the reuse mechanism.** Every other visual child consumes it. It therefore runs in two
phases: an **API phase** that publishes the frozen interface on day one so D, E, F and G can start,
and a **content phase** that fills the library with real materials.

Dispatch the API phase **now** — it needs neither S nor anything else. The content phase follows S.

## Owned paths (exclusive write)

`game/src/render/visual-foundation.js`, `game/assets/w1-30/materials/**`, `tools/assets/**`,
`game/src/render/MATERIAL_API.md`.

## Where we are, precisely

The structure is right and the content is nearly absent.

- **20 semantic families** declared in `visual-foundation.js:38`, with roughness/metalness/bump per
  family, and `exterior.js` (153 KB) contains **zero** direct standard-material construction — it all
  routes here. This is real work and it is preserved wholesale.
- **Three authored texture sets for the entire province.** `game/assets/w1-30/materials/` holds full
  1k albedo+normal+roughness for `brown_mud`, `bark_brown_01` and `plastered_stone_wall` only. Those
  three cover 9 of 20 families via `AUTHORED_FAMILY` — so mud, wet mud, bark, root, timber, thorn,
  stone, clay and salt are **three textures wearing nine hats**.
- **Nine families get a single 256 px albedo-only jpg** (`GENERATED_FAMILY`): leaf, reed, cloth,
  chitin, wet chitin, resin, bone, metal, shell. **No normal map, no roughness map.** Which is why
  they read as flat plastic under any light: the shading has nothing to work with.
- **Two families — skin and water — have procedural noise only.**
- The procedural fallback (`detailMaps`, `visual-foundation.js:112`) is a 96×96 hash-noise field used
  as albedo, height, AO *and* roughness. It raises texture-detail statistics without raising
  perceived quality, which is exactly the metric-gaming risk the old plan named and then shipped.
- `consumeStyleboard` applies a diffuse floor because the first hardware capture showed facades
  collapsing to charcoal. That fix is a symptom: it is compensating for the absence of texture detail
  by clamping colour, and it should become unnecessary once real maps land.

## Phase 1 — the API contract (day one, unblocks four children)

Publish `MATERIAL_API.md` and freeze it. `worldMaterial(family, options)` keeps its current
signature; options may only be **added**, never removed or repurposed. The frozen set:

```
color, roughness, metalness, map, normalMap, alphaTest, transparent, opacity, side,
emissive, emissiveIntensity, envMapIntensity, vertexColors, depthWrite, lod,
wetness, wear, tilingScale, trim, palette, boundedException
```

`wear`, `tilingScale`, `trim` and `palette` are new variant axes and may be no-ops in phase 1 — the
point is that D, E, F and G can write against them immediately and get correct results when the
content lands. Also publish the family list as frozen and the rule that a new family requires a
plan-level addition, not a builder's judgement call.

## Phase 2 — the content

1. **A real PBR set per family.** Target: **all 20 families with albedo + normal + roughness at
   1k**, sourced CC0/CC-BY with provenance recorded in `manifest.json`, converted to KTX2/Basis with
   mipmaps (vendor `KTX2Loader`), bounded anisotropy, stable texel density in metres-per-texel rather
   than per-object UV guesses. This is the single largest content deficit in the visual tree.
2. **Detail normals as a second layer.** One shared 512 px detail-normal tile per material class
   (organic / mineral / fabric / hard), blended at ~8× the base tiling. This is how a 1k texture
   survives a close-up, and it costs four textures for the whole game.
3. **Trim sheets.** One 2k trim atlas of edges, mouldings, planks, lashings, bolts, shell rings and
   bone bindings, consumed by E's kit parts. A trim sheet is why authored architecture reads as
   *made* rather than extruded, and it is the cheapest possible route to that read.
4. **Wear and wetness as real masks**, not scalars: a shared curvature/cavity-derived wear mask and a
   world-height-derived wetness mask, so edges are worn and hollows are wet, automatically and
   consistently across every family. This is what makes B's rain land on materials rather than on fog.
5. **Palette variants.** Region swatches from `world-art.js` become variant specs on the material,
   applied to base colour and to the trim atlas tint, so the same stone family reads as Blackwood
   stone or Salt Hills stone without a second texture set.
6. **Retire the noise fallback** for any family that now has authored maps; keep it only as the
   declared, visible fallback for a family whose asset failed to load, and make that fallback
   *obviously* wrong (magenta-tinted) rather than plausibly wrong, so it can never ship silently.
7. **Foliage alpha support.** `alphaTest` currently defaults to 0 (`visual-foundation.js:159`), which
   is why vegetation is opaque geometry. Publish an alpha-cut foliage material path with mipmapped,
   alpha-coverage-preserving atlases for F to consume.

## The bar

| gate | acceptance, with units | null control |
|---|---|---|
| **coverage** | All 20 families carry albedo + normal + roughness at ≥ 1k, with provenance in `manifest.json`. Zero families on the noise fallback in a shipped frame. | remove one family's normal map; the coverage gate must fail and the frame must show the magenta fallback |
| **close-up survives** | At 0.5 m, ≥ 15 of 20 families show resolvable surface structure: local luminance standard deviation in a 64×64 patch ≥ 0.06 **and** normal-map-driven shading variation ≥ 0.04 (a flat texture with noise albedo passes the first and fails the second — that is the point) | substitute the 96 px noise fallback for a family; the second figure must collapse |
| **families are distinguishable** | A naive judge shown 20 unlabelled close-ups of the families under one light correctly groups them into the 6 declared material classes ≥ 80% of the time | render all 20 with one texture set; grouping must fall to chance |
| **wear reads** | On ≥ 100 sampled edges across E's kit parts, mean luminance on the edge differs from the face by ≥ 8% | zero the wear mask; the difference must vanish |
| **wetness reads** | Under B's rain recipe, hollows show ≥ 25% higher specular than adjacent high ground on the same family | zero the wetness mask; the difference must vanish |
| **palette separates regions** | The same family in two different regions differs by ≥ the corpus swatch distance in hue+value | apply one palette everywhere; the separation must collapse |
| **the styleboard floor is no longer load-bearing** | Removing the diffuse floor at `visual-foundation.js:79` does **not** collapse facades to charcoal in the 8 settlement street shots (mean luma stays ≥ 0.14) | keep the old textures and remove the floor; it must collapse, proving the floor was compensating for missing detail |
| **budget** | Total texture memory ≤ 320 MB at `high` after KTX2 compression; ≤ 120 MB at `medium` | — |
| **human read** | Deck Craft ≥ pre-C + 1.0 on the minimum; the street shots specifically must improve | — |
| **library census** | zero bypasses; zero unexplained orphans; zero structural duplicates | insert a bare `MeshStandardMaterial`; census must go red |

**Hard fails:** a shipped frame using the noise fallback; a family without provenance; a texture
without mipmaps; an authored asset admitted outside `manifest.json`; a licence that is not
redistributable; texel density varying by more than 4× between adjacent surfaces in one shot.

wetness masks, palette variants, the alpha foliage path and the magenta fallback; run the family

compressed textures themselves.

**Fresh critic** — full Deck; the complete 20-family close-up matrix at three lights and three
distances; the naive grouping test with a pack it did not build; independent licence and provenance
audit of every asset; the wear/wetness/palette populations at full size; the diffuse-floor removal
test; texture memory measurement; M1–M12 tripwire; minimum aggregation; open defect inventory re-run.

## Reuse rows

**Consumed:** the existing family registry, `world-art.js` region swatches, the existing manifest
discipline. **Published:** the frozen material API (consumed by D, E, F, G, H — five consumers), the
trim atlas (consumed by E's kit and D's equipment), the detail-normal tiles (consumed by every
family), the wear and wetness masks (consumed by B's weather and E's architecture), the alpha foliage
path (consumed by F). **Variants:** palette, wear, wetness, tiling scale and trim slot are the
declared variant axes; a second texture set for the same family is a census `duplicate` failure
unless exempted.

## Dependencies and stop conditions

Phase 1 depends on nothing. Phase 2 depends on **W1-30S** (so `water.js` has moved out and C is not
maintaining a shader).

Stop and escalate if legally redistributable CC0/CC-BY sets cannot be found for a family — the
fallback is a **generated** set produced by a committed, reproducible script (not hash noise: real
procedural material synthesis with structure, e.g. Voronoi-based cellular for stone, fibre for
cloth), and that substitution is recorded per family. Do not stop the whole child for one family.

## Falsification audit

The strongest attack: **more texture is not more quality.** Twenty 1k sets applied at wrong texel
density with no art direction will look busier and no better, and could even score worse on the human
read while scoring better on M1–M12. That is why the texel-density hard fail is in the list and why
the human read is on the minimum.

The second: the family close-up rig is a controlled fixture, and materials can be tuned to look good
in it and bad in the world. The guard is that the *street shots* — real geometry, real light, real
distance — carry the human-read gate, and the census sample adds twelve unseen ones.

Least certain: whether 20 families is the right decomposition at all. It was inherited, it is
sensible, and it may be too coarse — "stone" covering both a settlement wall and a basalt column in
Stone Forest is a lot of load for one family. The variant axes are the intended answer; if the critic
finds they cannot carry it, adding families is a plan edit, not a builder decision.
