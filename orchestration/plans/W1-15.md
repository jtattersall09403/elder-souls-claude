## Played gate — the human read this piece did not have (Ruling W2)

The received plan had no blind pack, no fresh judge, no naive participant and no play-through
anywhere: a build could satisfy every arithmetic row in S43 and ship sneaking that is unreadable,
unfair or trivially exploitable. This section is the minimum sufficient repair. It is a **closure
gate**, not a change to any native threshold: nothing below lowers a bar, and a green S43 arithmetic
sweep without this gate is not closure.

**The protocol** — borrowed from `W1-05` step 6/7 (a fresh navigator receives only player-available
information, plus a load-bearing ablation arm) and `W1-28` step 8 (*"the question must be the item's
quality question, not SAME/DIFFERENT"*), rather than new machinery.

1. **Population, bounded.** Two routes, chosen before the run and published: **(a) a theft** — enter
   one shipped interior with placed lights, reach an owned container, take from it, and leave; and
   **(b) an escape** — commit a witnessed crime in one shipped settlement and get away or be
   arrested. Interiors are drawn from the sites the live instrument already covers, including
   `thorn-hall` (the worst room in the corpus, 14 deduped lamps) and one `render/places.js` site
   (`writ-house` or `barge-hold`), because those are the two lighting paths that already diverged.
   Ceiling: **≤ 10 minutes of driven play per route per arm**, four sessions total. No full
   play-through is authorised and none is needed.
2. **Conditions.** Production render, shipped HUD, no debug overlay, no `getLightAt()` readout
   visible, no route coordinates. The agent receives the URL, the driver protocol and one sentence
   of premise — nothing from this plan, the corpus or the source.
3. **The questions, asked after playing and recorded verbatim before any reveal.** These are the
   items' own quality questions:
   - `RI-STL01` §3: *"Could you tell, from the screen alone, where it was dark enough to move and
     where it was not? Point at where you were wrong."*
   - `RI-STL01` §3 / AR-2: *"Did anything on screen tell you whether you were hidden?"* Any answer
     naming a meter, eye icon or hidden/detected word is an AR-2 hard fail, already binding.
   - `RI-AI01` T02 via `RI-STL01`: *"Did you get a warning before you were caught? What was it?"*
   - `RI-CRM01`: *"When someone saw you, could you tell from across the street that they had, and
     what they were about to do?"*
   - `RI-CRM01`/`RI-CRM02`: *"Was the punishment something you could have planned around?"*
   - And the one that decides the gate: *"Was sneaking through this good?"* with reasons.
4. **The null arm that must come out worse.** Repeat both routes on a copy with the interior
   lighting consumer disconnected so darkness is decorative — the `LIT_CAP`/extinguishable-light
   consumer removed, world geometry, guard placement, schedules and seeds otherwise identical. The
   ablated arm must be **judged worse on the darkness-legibility question by a fresh agent that does
   not know which arm it played**, and the shipped arm's answer to (3a) must locate dark cover the
   ablated arm's cannot. **Effect size:** the shipped arm reports usable dark cover on ≥ 2 of the
   two routes' decision points where the ablated arm reports none, *and* the two arms' verbatim
   answers are separable by a third fresh reader with the arm labels removed. If the two arms read
   the same, this gate is **inert and cannot pass** — it is reported as inert, exactly as an inert
   producer is, and the piece does not close on it. This is the arm that stops the gate becoming a
   rubber stamp, and it is the highest-value line in this section.
5. **Aggregation.** Worst route, worst site — never a mean across the four sessions (Ruling W1).
6. **Independence.** The builder produces the capture and the two arms; a **fresh agent plays and a
   fresh agent judges**, and neither is the builder. Arm identity is quarantined until after the
   verbatim answers are recorded. Run `node tools/blind/leakcheck.mjs` over any packaged material.

**Instruments reused, by path — no new tool is authorised for this gate.**
`tools/stealth/critic-w1-15-r4-live.mjs` already launches one browser, walks the player into
`thorn-hall` and the `render/places.js` sites, and asks the running world which cell is drawn versus
what the stealth system thinks lights it; `tools/harness/w1-15-r4-live.mjs` and
`tools/harness/w1-15-r4-lights.mjs` are the builder-side pair for the same question
(*"does the room the player sees agree with the room the game simulates?"*), and
`tools/harness/stl-live.mjs` asks W1-15 of the running game through `window.__HARNESS`. The played
gate rides those existing live paths and fixture sets; it commissions no new harness.
`tools/capture/client.mjs` (`CaptureSession`, one connection for a whole sequence) supplies the
motion sequences Directive §2 requires — *many* frames from *many* angles, never one still.
`tools/blind/leakcheck.mjs` supplies the tell audit. `tools/play.mjs` serves the build.

**Cost.** Four ≤10-minute driven sessions plus one capture pass on a browser this piece already
pays for. That is a small fraction of one build round and it is the only evidence in the piece that
a person ever met the system.
