## 0. What is true, said first, because it is the largest thing here

**The prose is excellent, and the prose is not the gap.** I read nine texts end to end before
measuring anything, and the writing clears the bar this piece was set by a comfortable margin.

The bar said: *a book has an author with opinions, a date, a bias, and a reason to be wrong; two
books that disagree are the point.* Here is the ford, from both sides. Prefect Ancus Meline, who was
four years old when it happened, has built a battle out of four words in a day-book:

> *A column does not form line to cross a river. A column forms column to cross a river. A line is
> formed facing something.*

and then, in his own section IX, undoes himself:

> *It is the only eyewitness in the file and it is against me, and every time I have set out to
> explain it away I have found myself doing the thing I accuse Vell of, which is arranging paper.*

Neekus-Ei of the weir households answers him without having read him, and the answer is not a
correction — it is a different account of the same afternoon:

> *We were in front of them. Not in a line and not with weapons out. About 20 of us, on the far
> bank, sitting down, in plain sight… A line is what you form when you do not know.*

> *Because a story with one woman in it is a story your Empire can carry. A story about 30 people
> deciding something together, with no one to punish and no one to negotiate with, is the exact
> thing your Office has been unable to find a signatory for in 300 years.*

> *We made the story small on purpose. It was the second thing we did that week and it has worked
> better than the gates.*

She then withdraws part of her own attack, having been read a fuller copy. Neither book wins. Nothing
in the game tells you who was right. That is the whole assignment, done properly.

It is not one lucky pair. *Songs for the Weir* (T5, Argonian, 324 words) is the best short text in
the corpus:

> *You are cold now. / You will be colder. / No one is coming. / Set the stake. / Now go home.*

> *He set the stake. / The stake is holding. / That is the whole of it. / Do not make a song of a
> man. / This is not a song about him. / It is a song about the stake.*

with a translator's note that names its own distortion — *"in Tamrielic a short sad thing sounds like
a joke"*. *The Lizard-King's Bride* is trash fiction that knows it is trash fiction and is also the
sharpest thing in the library about being looked at, and its last word is given to the Lilmoth
bookseller who keeps ordering it:

> *I sell it because there is a girl in it who is taken to a place she does not understand and who
> does not scream and offers no thanks and does not go home, and because there is not one other book
> in the whole of your Empire's printing in which one of us is looked at for a whole book by a writer
> who is trying to work out what we are, rather than what we are for.*

*What the Water Went Round* refuses the Imperial version of the Knahaten Flu by refusing its shape —
*"a man with three names and no village is a hole with a story stuffed into it"* — and ends on the
thing the teller did not want written: *"we did not go into them because we were ashamed, and she
does not know of what, and she has thought about it for eighty years."*

**The canon is never explained to the player.** I checked directly: there is no compendium, no
summary entry, no in-game encyclopedia. `getCanonState()` is a harness surface. The shipped register
carries no narrator adjudication. The one place that looks like a resolution — the publisher's
afterword in *The Lizard-King's Bride* listing everything the novel got wrong — is an in-world
document with a commercial motive, arguing, not the game talking. It is the closest call in the
corpus and it survives.

**And the Black Marsh material is not generic fantasy at the level of the writing.** "Sitting the
road" is a real invention with rules, a cost, and a second-order effect the Empire cannot see. The
Hist is handled in-voice: "Hist" appears only 9 times in 110,420 words, but *tree/trees/sap* appear
98 times and *keeper(s)* 105, across 32 texts — Argonians saying "the tree" rather than the
scholar's word is correct, not a shortfall, and I checked before saying so.

None of what follows is a claim that this is decorative work. It is a claim about who can read it.

## 2. Prose tics (B) — the corpus is cleaner than my own first instrument said

`critic-w1-23-r3.mjs` §B reports 26 hits. **I do not stand behind that number and I am reporting it
against myself.** It fires on `simply` and `you must` regardless of voice, and on inspection the hits
are in-voice inside T6 manuals, whose *defining move in §2's own table is "numbered procedure,
imperative mood"*: *"THE PLATE IS SMALL AND YOU MUST STOP WANTING IT TO BE BIG"* is a drill sergeant,
not a quest brief.

A strict pass — second-person imperative that directs **the player**, location hints, stated stakes,
stated morals, "in conclusion", "what it all means" — returns **3 hits in 110,420 words**, and all
three are false positives on reading (*"you will ask"*, *"you will go"*, *"you will find"*, each
addressing an apprentice inside a manual). **The named failure modes are absent.** No book ends by
telling you what it all means. Quest-brief phrasing: zero. Signposting: zero.

One measured caveat rather than a finding: 45 of 152 texts (29.6%) carry a quest mechanism
(`gates` / `knowledge_key` / `revealed_by_id`) against §2's *"≤10% may contain a direct quest hint."*
Almost all 45 are evidence ledgers and charter files from the readables piece, where the document
**is** the evidence rather than a hint pointing elsewhere. I record the number and decline to call it
a breach; a critic reading `the-blackrose-lease-roll` as "a direct quest hint" would be reading the
field, not the text.

## 3. Contradiction (C) — required, and present in the register

57 directed `contradicts` edges resolve to **19 mutual pairs** (both books name each other), **0
naming a book not on disk**. A directed edge alone would let eight books point at eight silent
partners and satisfy a naive count; these are real two-sided fights. Against RI-LOR03 §2's bar of
≥8 pairs, 19 is comfortable, and the disagreements are about *what is the case* — who built the
xanmeers, what caused the Flu, whether subjects of the Empire are held as property — not about what
is worth doing. **The library is not a codex with margins.** This axis is genuinely met.

## 4. CONSUMPTION (D) — mandatory, and this is where it breaks

Measured against **today's** tree: 578 topic nodes / 468 distinct topic ids / 1,280 infos, 347 NPCs,
**162,396 (npc, topic) pairs** swept per arm.

**The register half is consumed, and I demonstrate it.** Striking every holder from `CF-D008` — *"Are
subjects of the Empire held as property in this province?"* — silences **78** (npc,topic) answers.
`CF-D001` silences 20. **Control (RULES #4/#6): striking an undisputed fact changes 0 of 162,396.**
My first control run reported 18,607 and that was my own bug — I passed a register where a sweep was
expected — which is the reason the control exists. Both arms differ. The instrument can go red.

**The library half is consumed by nothing.** Three independent measurements, each by a different
route:

- **99 of 152 texts are referenced nowhere under `game/` outside the file that defines them** —
  not an interior readable, not an item, not a container, not a quest, not a line of code. The test
  is the most generous available: a single mention anywhere counts.
- **177 of 268 taught topics appear nowhere in the tree outside the books that teach them.** Of the
  91 that do, **0 are root topics** — and `converse.js:342 topicsFor()` surfaces a held topic only if
  it is `root`, so a non-root topic in `topicsKnown` cannot reach a topic list. **11** are named by a
  quest gate, and **all 11 are also ordinary dialogue topics reachable through an info `to:` edge**,
  so conversation supplies every one of them without a book ever being opened.
- Per-dispute perturbation: **12 of 28 registered disputes change nothing anybody says.** They are
  the province's biggest questions — *Is the Hist one mind or many trees? What caused the Knahaten
  Flu? Who cut the first sapwell? Were the xanmeers built by Argonians? Does the labour-lease at
  Blackrose raise any question of consent?*

This is the eighteen-plus pattern the project keeps finding, in its most painful form: not a model
nothing reads, but **a model that is read, wired to a corpus the reader cannot reach.**

## 5. The single biggest gap

**Three of nineteen registered contradiction pairs have both sides reachable. Fifteen have neither.**

```
  BOTH     the-seventh-recension        <-> for-the-ninth-clause
  BOTH     for-the-ninth-clause         <-> against-the-ninth-clause
  BOTH     the-drowned-tally-sixth      <-> the-drowned-tally-eleventh
  ONE      the-sap-and-the-knife        <-> what-the-hollow-has-to-trade
  NEITHER  the-relief-of-the-reman-ford <-> the-water-does-not-take-sides
  NEITHER  the-blessings-of-the-coast   <-> ledger-and-journal-of-andrel-vorin
  NEITHER  the-knahaten-years           <-> what-the-water-went-round
  NEITHER  the-egg-speaks-twice         <-> the-sap-and-the-ledger
  … 11 more
```

The three that survive are all the same argument, about one document, in one building. **The ford
pair quoted in §0 — the best writing in this project — is unreachable. So is the province's central
moral fact:** `CF-062` / `CF-D008`, whether subjects of the Empire are held as property, is voiced by
*The Blessings of the Coast* and by Andrel Vorin's ledger, and **neither is referenced anywhere in
the world.** The Empire's uncomfortable footing in Argonia is registered, sealed, gated and argued —
and a player cannot read either side of it.

RI-LOR06's discipline is satisfied. RI-LOR03 §2's *"≥8 pairs of books must contradict each other"* is
satisfied **as a registry claim and fails as a world claim, 3 against a bar of 8**, and no gate in the
tree can tell the difference, because every gate checks registration and none checks reach.

**Remedy, concrete and buildable:** place the 30 books carrying the 15 unreachable disputes as
`readable` entries in rooms that already exist — the new `game/data/world/readables/site-marks.json`
is the pattern and the mechanism is already shipped. Then arm the check: extend
`tools/lore/canon-census.mjs` with the §A measure from `critic-w1-23-r3-reach.mjs`, so a dispute whose
two sides are not both placed fails CI instead of passing as "registered". Pair reach, not pair count.

## 7. Naming (RI-LOR04) — the failure mode the brief named, found directly

The brief said generic fantasy in Argonian names is the failure mode and to check for it directly. It
is there, and it is not in the hand-authored names — `Wuleen-Kus`, `Ee-Vashum`, `Ixtu-Meer`,
`Sesh-Anaat`, `Ojal-Weeth` are good Jel. It is in the generated settlement rosters.

RI-LOR04 §4, verbatim: *"A settlement roster in which most Argonians carry hyphenated English names
has failed this section however well each individual name scores."* Its own attested table puts the
Tamrielic-descriptive form at **11%**.

**Measured: 121 of 217 named Argonians — 55.8% — carry a hyphenated-English descriptive name.
"Most". §4 is failed by its own stated rule.** With it:

- **33 Argonian NPCs carry an established Dunmer or Khajiit given name** bolted to an Argonian
  epithet: `Ahnassi Dark-Water`, `Falura Nine-Teeth`, `Llarara the Elder`, `Bevene the Patient`,
  `Nartise Wet-Foot`, `Onwen Quick-Tally`. `Sedura Nine-Teeth` is not even a name — *sedura* is a
  Dunmer honorific, so the roster contains an Argonian called "Sir Nine-Teeth".
- **43 distinct names are reused across settlements** — `Wanan the Shorter` is six different people,
  `Llarara the Elder` five, in five different towns.
- **18 names carry an unarticled template slug**: `Llarara the Elder of salvage yard`, `Deek
  Slow-Rain of docks`, `Beem-Kiune Nine-Teeth of high street`. Not "of the salvage yard". That is a
  string join, visible in the shipped name.

No apostrophe appears in any Argonian name, so RI-LOR04's hard fail is clear. But the languages bleed
into each other exactly as its band-2 text describes.
