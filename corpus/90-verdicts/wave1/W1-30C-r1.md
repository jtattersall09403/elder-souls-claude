## 2. The seventh defect: the world-height wetness mask does not survive instancing

A round that found six of its own defects had not found the seventh. Here it is, and it is in a check
that stayed green throughout.

`installSurfaceShader` computes the world height the wetness mask rides on as

```glsl
vEsSurfaceWorldY=(modelMatrix*vec4(transformed,1.0)).y;
```

inserted after `#include <begin_vertex>`. three.js applies `instanceMatrix` in **`project_vertex`**,
which runs later — its own `worldpos_vertex` chunk is
`worldPosition = instanceMatrix * worldPosition; worldPosition = modelMatrix * worldPosition;`. So
for an `InstancedMesh` every instance is handed the **base geometry's** height, and the whole mesh
shares one position on the wetness ramp regardless of where its instances stand.

**Measured, with a control that saturates so a null result cannot be ambiguous.** Wet band `topY 0.4`
/ `bottomY -0.4`; one quad at y = −1.2 (entirely below the ramp, so fully wet if the shader sees it)
and one at y = +1.2 (entirely above it, so fully dry):

| arm | low quad moves | high quad moves | separation |
|---|---|---|---|
| two separate `Mesh`es — **positive control** | −0.02393 | **0.00000** | **0.02393** |
| two **instances** of one `InstancedMesh` | — | — | **0.00150** |

The instanced arm retains **6%** of the control. The control itself saturates exactly as predicted
(the high quad moves by precisely zero), so this is not a dead instrument. Reproduced identically on
SwiftShader (0.023926 / 0.001497) and on the A4500 (0.023896 / 0.001511).

**Why it matters:** the live census counts **377 InstancedMeshes carrying 31,143 instances**, and the
families drawn that way are `bark, leaf, reed, stone` — the trunks, canopies, reeds and rocks that
fill a Black Marsh frame. `setWorldWetness()` is published as W1-30B's rain consumer. B's rain
currently lands on everything except the marsh.

**Why no arm of the builder's proof could see it.** `wetnessProof` in
`tools/assets/rig/family-closeup.html` renders **one non-instanced plane at the origin**, where
`modelMatrix * transformed` *is* the world position. Every arm — wet, dry, all five families —
fabricated the same untested input. That is HAZARDS §0's shape exactly: *"which input do all my arms
supply by hand, and what would happen if the real value were not what I assume?"*

The same line also misses `instanceMatrix`'s sibling `batchingMatrix`, and it is taken before
`skinning_vertex`, so a skinned character's wetness rides its bind-pose height. Neither is exercised
today; both are the same one-line fix.

## 3. Is the builder's wear diagnosis right? — **yes, and I executed the route it published**

The builder left `wear reads` **NOT MET** at ~1 percentage point against an 8% bar, with a structural
diagnosis and a published input (`wearFrom: 'geometry'` reading a per-vertex `esCurvature`) instead
of a faked output. Nothing in the tree bakes that attribute, so **the route had never been run**. I
ran it.

- **It executes.** With `esCurvature` baked by hand onto a 12-sided prism, `wear 0` and `wear 0.9`
  produce different frames and a column-profile selectivity of 0.0080 against the texture route's
  0.0016 on the same geometry — **five times more selective**.
- **Its ceiling, in the gate's own units.** Two identical planes, same material, same light, one at
  `esCurvature = 1` everywhere (a pure arris) and one at `0` (a pure face): at `wear 0.9` they differ
  by **50.4%**, against the gate's **8%**. Control: at `wear 0` the two planes are **byte-identical**,
  so the difference is wear and nothing else. Same on both rasterisers (50.38% / 50.41%).
- **The texture route's own numbers corroborate the diagnosis.** On a flat plane `wear: 0.9` moves
  mean luminance by −0.0014, while `wetness: 0.9` moves it by −0.0426 — wear is thirty times weaker
  than the axis beside it.

So: the diagnosis is right, the published input is real, it clears the bar with a factor of six in
hand, and the row is properly **deferred to W1-30D and W1-30E under amendment R5**. This is how an
unmet gate should be handed on, and it is the strongest single thing in the piece.

## 4. The frozen contract declares axes the implementation does not apply

Four children are writing against `MATERIAL_API.md` right now, and W1-30E is being dispatched as the
trim atlas's first consumer. Three things in that document are not true of the code.

**(a) `trim` is documented "live" and changes nothing.** MATERIAL_API.md §3 lists `trim` under *"the
declared variant axes"* with today-state **live**, and `C-DELIVERY.md` says *"Only `trim` was
recorded-only until the atlas landed, and it is live now."* Measured:
`worldMaterial('timber', {trim: 'plank'})` produces a **byte-identical frame** to
`worldMaterial('timber', {})` — hash `709da03f` both. `worldMaterial` validates the slot, writes it
to `userData.w1_30.trim`, and never touches the atlas; `trimAtlasTextures()` has **zero callers in
`game/src`**. The positive control in the same run moves: `palette: 'blackwood'` shifts luma by
−0.0178. So the instrument works and the axis does not.

This is the same defect the builder found and fixed for `options.normalMap` in this very commit —
*"declared by the API and silently ignored by the implementation"* — reintroduced one row down in the
same table. E will pass `trim: 'plank'`, get nothing, and get no error.

**(b) The detail normal is tiled at `8 / tilingScale` × the base, not the published fixed 8×.**
`materialTiling()` publishes `detailRepeat = DETAIL_NORMAL_TILING / metresPerTile`, i.e. a fixed 8×
the base repeat. The shader multiplies `vNormalMapUv` — which already carries the base repeat of
`2 / tilingScale` — by `uDetailTiling = DETAIL_NORMAL_TILING / tilingScale`. The delivered ratio is
`8 / tilingScale`: correct at the shipping default of 1, and 4× wrong at `tilingScale 2`, 8× wrong at
4. Latent today, and D/E/F are told to use the axis.

**(c) The texel-density seam has zero consumers, including C.** `materialTiling`, `TEXEL_METRES` and
`metresPerTile` appear **nowhere in `game/` or `tools/`** outside `visual-foundation.js` itself.
`authoredMaps()` hardcodes `repeat.set(2,2)` for every family regardless of `TEXEL_METRES`. Meanwhile
`C-DELIVERY.md`'s PUBLISHED table lists this entry with **three consumers** — "D (character sheets),
E (kit parts), F (terrain and foliage)" — without the *not yet realised* caveat the trim row
correctly carries. The plan's hard fail *"texel density varying by more than 4× between adjacent
surfaces in one shot"* is consequently **unmeasured and unmeasurable as designed**: every surface
gets a repeat of 2 over whatever UVs its author laid down. The far and near terrain use `uv = pos/3`,
giving 1.5 m per tile against `TEXEL_METRES.mud = 3.5` — a 2.3× departure from the published seam by
the province, and visible as flat olive ground at range in
`street-archon-A-shipping-rtx-a4500.png`.

**(d) A smaller documentation error.** §5 says *"the table's widest legal adjacency (resin 1.0 m
against mud 3.8 m) is 3.8×"*. `TEXEL_METRES.mud` is **3.5**, not 3.8, and the widest adjacency is
`root 0.9` against `mud 3.5` = **3.89×** — which is what the comment in the source correctly says.

`modelMatrix * transformed` at `begin_vertex` and therefore hands every instance of an
`InstancedMesh` the base geometry's height. On the shipped world that is 377 meshes and 31,143
instances across `bark, leaf, reed, stone`. It binds the score at 4.0, it is a one-line fix, and
fixing it raises the minimum to 5.0; nothing else on the list does.

Full remediation specification — what to preserve, the narrow delta, the check order, acceptance and
the stop conditions — is in `W1-30C-r1.json` under `biggest_gap.remedy`. In one line: replace that
vertex line with three.js's own `worldpos_vertex` form
(`instanceMatrix` → `modelMatrix`, guarded by `#ifdef USE_INSTANCING` and `USE_BATCHING`), move the
insertion point after `skinning_vertex`, and take the three contract repairs in §4(a)–(c) in the same
commit because they are three lines in the same two files and one instrument re-run covers all of
them. Acceptance is `node tools/critic/w1-30c/mech.mjs` going from four failures to zero with its
controls still green — the instrument already exists and already goes red on today's tree.

## 10. Two findings that belong to the plan, not the builder
