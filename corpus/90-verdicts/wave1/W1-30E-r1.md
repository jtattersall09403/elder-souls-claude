# W1-30E — architecture, settlements and the model kit: fresh critic, round 1

**Verdict: FAIL at 4 against a wave-1 gate of 7.0**, aggregated as the minimum over six named axes.
Critic: fresh, not the builder, no conflict. Branch `codex/wave1-build-experiment`, HEAD `318ecdf2`.

The builder's headline red was its own method: *"No frame was rendered. The premise all six arms
share — that geometric separation reaches pixels — is the one none can falsify."* **That is now
closed.** 288 stills and 400 motion frames were captured on real hardware for this verdict (RunPod
NVIDIA L4, `ANGLE (NVIDIA, Vulkan 1.4.303)`, `HARDWARE — valid for appearance claims`, `full` Deck
profile, seed 20260814, $0.054). The answer is more interesting than either option the builder
offered, and it is in §2.

## 2. Lilmoth's skyline, photographed — and it is neither of the builder's two answers

The builder's negative result: the geometric skyline indicator does not distinguish the shipped
build (0.1835) from one with every roofline flattened and every mast deleted (0.1862). It offered two
readings — *the statistic is insensitive*, or *the skyline work is too timid* — and said only a frame
could separate them.

**A frame separates them, and the answer is a third one: the skyline is there, and the atmosphere is
eating it.**

`docs/shots/2026-08-14-w1-30de-critic/approach-lilmoth__t1300__clear.jpg` — noon, clear weather, the
best case. Lilmoth's silhouette *does* carry relief: stepped roof masses, a tiered ziggurat-like
form, and at least six masts or poles breaking the roofline. The skyline work is **not** timid. But
the entire town is rendered as a pale wash barely separable from the sky, because the fog at the
approach camera's distance has removed almost all of the contrast the silhouette would need to read.
A statistic computed on untextured geometry with no sky and no fog cannot see that, which is exactly
why it showed no signal: **the offline indicator and the rendered frame disagree about the world,
not about the roofline.**

Compare `approach-helstrom__t1300__clear.jpg`, where the fog is thinner and the buildings *do* read —
roofs, windows, a gantry, trees. Helstrom shows the second half of the answer: its roofline is
legible and it is **the same wide hipped roof repeated across the whole town**, which is precisely
the uniformity failure the plan's own falsification audit predicted a kit would produce. So on the
approach shots the piece has two different problems in two different towns, and neither is the one
the builder was looking for.

**Consequences, and they are not all E's.** The plan's row *"silhouette reads at range — naive judges
correctly attribute at ≥ 45% using skyline alone"* cannot be passed by any amount of work inside
`exterior.js` while Lilmoth's approach frame looks like this. The fog density at the settlement
approach distance belongs to the atmospherics child (`W1-30B`), not to E. **Remedy:** raise this as a
defect-inventory row against B with this frame as its evidence, and re-run E's approach gate after
it moves. Until then E's approach row is **blocked, not failed**, and this verdict scores it as
blocked-with-a-visible-uniformity-problem rather than as a clean red.

**Against the board.** `docs/art-direction/measure-plates.mjs` was re-run as the board's own
instrument. `ART-SET-ROOFLINE-RELIEF-lilmoth`, `-soulrest`, `-blackrose` and `-gideon` are `set`;
**`-helstrom` (n=2), `-archon` (n=1), `-thorn` (n=0) and `-stormhold` (n=0) are UNSET for want of
plates.** That is a corpus shortage, not a failure of this piece, and no target was invented for
them — the builder was right to refuse to invent one and this critic refuses too. The builder was
also right that its 0.1835 must not be quoted against the board's bands: it is a rasterised
silhouette of untextured geometry and the board's row is defined on a rendered frame.

## 3. Ruling E1 — the reading is upheld; the row still fails; and the falsifier fires in three settlements

**The ruling, as given:** the *"≥ 5 roof profiles per settlement"* row counts distinct roof
**silhouettes**, not kit **parts**, because the row exists so settlements read as distinct on
approach and a parts reading makes the plan self-defeating against its own 25-part cap. **Its
falsifier:** if that variety is parameter jitter reading as noise rather than recognisably different
roofs, the ruling is wrong.

**The falsifier was fired.** A probe built every building in every settlement, kept only the meshes
whose `kitId` begins `roof.`, projected each roof orthographically, **normalised it to its own
bounding box** — so the same roof at two sizes lands on itself, which is what "profile" has to mean —
and merged any two classes overlapping at IoU ≥ 0.95 as jitter. Threshold and decision rule fixed
before running.

| settlement | kit roof parts | classes E1 counts | after merging jitter | max pair IoU | E1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| archon | 2 | 9 | **9** | 0.938 | survives |
| blackrose | 1 | 9 | **6** | 1.000 | survives |
| **gideon** | 1 | 6 | **4** | **0.9995** | **falls** |
| helstrom | 2 | 5 | **5** | 0.902 | survives (at the bar) |
| lilmoth | 3 | 13 | **11** | 0.972 | survives |
| **soulrest** | 2 | 3 | **3** | 0.760 | **falls** |
| **stormhold** | 1 | 5 | **3** | 1.000 | **falls** |
| thorn | 2 | 6 | **6** | 0.880 | survives |

**The ruling on Ruling E1, and it is in two parts.**

**(a) E1's reading is upheld.** The falsifier's condition is *"that variety is parameter jitter"* —
all of it, reading as noise. It is not. Five of eight settlements carry five or more roof
silhouettes that are mutually distinct by a wide margin (median pair IoU 0.50–0.74 in helstrom,
lilmoth, archon), and those are recognisably different roofs, not the same roof jittered. The
ruling's reasoning is also right on its own terms: three roof parts cannot yield five roof *parts*
per settlement without breaking the 25-part cap in the same plan, and a row that no build can satisfy
is a defect in the row.

**(b) The row still fails under E1's own reading, and E1 does not rescue it.** Aggregation on this
plan is minimum-over-constituents (Ruling W1: *"an aggregate may never be the binding predicate for
something a player meets one at a time"* — a player walks into one town). The worst settlement is
**3**, against a bar of 5. So the honest number moves from the builder's **1** to **3** and the row
is still red. Ruling E1 changes what the number means; it does not change the verdict.

**(c) And the falsifier does fire, precisely, in three places.** Gideon and Stormhold are exactly the
two `imperial` settlements, and Blackrose the third `roofMix: [1]` grammar. In Gideon two "distinct"
roof classes overlap at **0.9995** and in Stormhold and Blackrose two overlap at **1.000** — literally
the same roof silhouette counted twice. In those settlements the variety *is* parameter jitter, and
E1's falsifier correctly identifies them. **The builder's own proposed rescue is worse than E1's**:
its report offers *"32 distinct silhouettes in Lilmoth over 33 buildings and the row passes handily"*,
but those are whole-**building** silhouettes, which include storey count and footprint. Substituting
building variety for roof variety would pass the row while the roofs stayed identical, which is the
opposite of what the row is for.

**Remedy, and it is cheap and does not cost a 26th part.** The three failing settlements each draw
from one roof part. Give `roof.hip` a discrete **gable/monopitch flag** — the same part, one boolean
in the spec, two genuinely different silhouettes — and put it in Gideon's, Stormhold's and
Blackrose's grammar mixes. That is a variant, not a part, so the 25-part economy is untouched, and it
is the mechanism the plan already calls "variation from variants". Then re-run the probe: the bar is
`classesAfterMergingJitter ≥ 5` per settlement, minimum over the eight.

## 4. The 3,284 untagged building meshes — the refusal is upheld

The builder refused to tag the untagged meshes, saying *"tagging them would launder the census"*.
**That refusal is correct and should be recorded as correct**, for three reasons that this critic
checked rather than took on trust.

1. **The composition is published, not hidden.** `bypassComposition()` splits the number four ways —
   icosahedral organic masses, the public realm, the half-tori, and buildings — and the census is
   reproducible. A number that is broken down and named is not a number being hidden.
2. **The mechanism that would have laundered it was built to prevent laundering, not to enable it.**
   `carryKitProvenance()` exists because batching *was* silently erasing `kitId` and reading whole
   settlements as bypass; it folds member ids onto the batch **and records how many members were not
   kit parts**. A builder inclined to launder had the tool in its hands and used it in the honest
   direction.
3. **The bulk of the number is not a tagging decision at all.** ~9,945 public-realm meshes across
   eight towns are plan item 6, declared not started. Tagging unstarted work would make an unbuilt
   thing report as built — the precise failure Owner Directive §9 describes.

Tagging a moss cushion as a `wall` to turn a gate green would be the coupled-yardstick move in its
plainest form. **The refusal is upheld. The row stays FAIL** — the bar is zero and the value is
3,284 — but it fails for the right reason, which is that the work is not done, and the builder said
so.

**Remedy:** the builder's own suggestion in its exemption table is the right one — a 26th kit part
for organic masses with the cap raised in writing and the count stated, or an explicit
`kitExempt: 'organic-mass'` tag that the census counts separately from `bypass` so the public realm's
9,945 stop being averaged in with a design decision about blobs.

## 5. Two further findings

### E-1 (major) — the eight mandatory street shots are unusable, and they are E's primary gate

The plan's street row is the one that decides this child: *"settlement street shots are mandatory in
the Deck precisely because building quality at 4–8 m is where a world stops being able to hide"*, and
*"Deck Craft on the 8 mandatory street shots ≥ 7.0 — this child cannot pass while its own shots are
the worst three"*.

**In all three street shots examined on hardware, the camera is inside geometry.**
`street-lilmoth__t1300__clear.jpg` is filled by the courses of a single reed wall with a grey ceiling
and black ground — no street, no buildings.  `street-helstrom__t1300__clear.jpg` has the camera
jammed against a stone wall with the player half-clipped into it. `street-gideon__t1300__clear.jpg`
is a dark timber post filling the frame with the player entirely inside the wall except one foot.

This is a **`W1-30V` Deck-setup defect**, not E's — the `gameplay` camera at yaw 270 is being placed
inside buildings — but the consequence for this verdict is direct: **E's primary human gate could not
be run, and a critic may not pass a piece on a gate that did not execute.** Recorded as a shortage
with a named owner, in the same category as the four UNSET board rows.

**What the broken frames do evidence, in E's favour**, because it should be said: the masonry is
genuinely made. Gideon's rubble walls have mortar courses and timber framing with real posts and
beams; Helstrom's stone has visible chamfers, a lintel and a door surround with thickness. At 1–2 m
this is not blockout, and the `edges are broken` claim reads as true on pixels, which is the reading
the builder correctly said it could not make offline.

**Remedy:** `W1-30V` to move the eight `street-*` setups to a validated position — the simplest
robust rule is to place the camera at the settlement's own street centreline with a collision push-out,
which `settlementApproach()` already has the data for — and re-run. Until then E cannot be scored on
its own primary gate.

### E-2 (moderate) — the `edges are broken` row passes with a nearly inert single-variable control

`null:no-chamfer` moves the metric from **0.971 to 0.9637** — a 0.7-point change on a bar of 0.95,
i.e. the control that is supposed to remove the thing being measured leaves the row passing
comfortably. The arm that does move it, `kit:false` at 0.4816, replaces the **entire kit**, so it
confounds the chamfer with every other difference between the kit and the legacy path. The builder
noticed half of this — it correctly identified that `kit:false` left the primitive chamfer in place
and introduced `setKitChamfer(false)` — but the arm that ships in the gate output still reads 0.9637.

So the row's status is: **the metric is real and the frames support it, but the row has no isolating
null control.** Under the project's own rule that a control failing to sabotage is indistinguishable
from a build that passes, this row is currently evidenced by the *frames* rather than by the
statistic and its control.

**Remedy:** make `null:no-chamfer` call `setKitChamfer(false)` — the switch that actually removes the
chamfer — and report that arm, so the row has a single-variable control that collapses it.

## 7. Biggest gap and its remedy

**GAP-W1-w1-30e-street-gate-never-ran** — the eight settlement street shots, which the plan names as
this child's deciding gate, put the camera inside building geometry in every case examined, so the
one measurement that could confirm or refute the whole piece has never executed.

Everything else about this child is well-evidenced in one direction or another. This is the hole in
the middle: the kit's entire justification is how it reads at 4–8 m, the offline proxies deliberately
cannot answer that, and the instrument that could is broken in a way nobody had looked at because
nobody had rendered a frame. The remedy is in E-1 and it is small — reposition eight camera setups in
`tools/visual/deck.json` against the settlement centreline with a push-out, re-run the `child-scoped`
profile, and score the row. It is also the cheapest high-value thing available to this tree right
now: one edit to a manifest unblocks the gate for E, and the same eight frames feed the naive
attribution pack that E's `settlements are attributable` row needs.

## 8. What this critic could not do

- **No naive attribution pack, at street or skyline range.** Both of E's primary rows are
  naive-judge rows, the pack must be built by a different agent from the judge (`ARBITRATION` S51,
  `RULES.md` rule 25), and this critic is one agent. The 288 hardware frames are in the tree for a
  pack-builder; the packs did **not** run and no number is offered for either row.
- **No WLD03 copy-paste matrix, and no exemption audit.** The plan assigns both to this critic. The
  copy-paste matrix was not run; the landmark-exemption audit is trivially satisfied only because the
  builder attempted no landmarks and claimed no exemptions (plan item 5, declared not started), which
  is not the same as passing it.
- **The 12-shot unseen census sample was not drawn.** The `full` profile was captured instead, which
  is a superset of the fixed list but is *not* the census sample — the census sample's whole purpose
  is to be shots the builder could not anticipate, and drawing it is a separate seeded step this
  critic did not perform. **The anti-beauty-shot arm therefore did not run**, and no claim in this
  verdict is protected by it.
- **The open defect inventory was not re-run**, so this verdict does not report whether E's changes
  closed or opened rows in `reports/visual-truth/INVENTORY.md`.
- **Interior thresholds (plan item 7) were not examined** against `W1-30G`'s side of the doorway.
- **Draw calls were not measured per street shot**, only per settlement (106 worst). The plan's
  ≤ 900 row is frustum-scoped and remains unmeasured; it stays CARRIED.
