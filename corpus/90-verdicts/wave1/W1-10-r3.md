## 1. Did the arc/blade trade happen as the builder says? **Yes, and it should be said plainly.**

The builder reports that before the joint arc/blade solve `SEP` read **1.4605 — a pass** — with
21.1% of 606 clips outside the ±10° arc tolerance, and that after solving arc and blade together
`SEP` fell to **1.3858 — a fail** — with arc nonconformance at 7.59%. It chose the failing number
and published both.

**Verified, and the account holds.** The mechanism is visible in the source and reproducible:
`_yawGain` now takes the blade length, keys its cache on it, and `_bladeLength` builds its own
pass-1 clip so there is no cycle (`game/src/combat/moveset.js` §`_yawGain`). Solving the gain
against the registry capsule while playing the clip at the calibrated one is a genuine
inconsistency, and fixing it genuinely costs `SEP`, because `SEP`'s `W_max` is dominated by D5 and
D6 and both moved. **A builder that publishes a number that got worse because the thing under it
got righter is doing the job.** Say it plainly: this is the behaviour the project wants, and it is
the opposite of what round 1 did when it published `SEP = 1.4469` off a broken reach column.

**But the earlier build was not passing for a good reason either.** `SEP = 1.4605` was measured on
a build in which the *same* `_bladeLength` solve was already in place — so its `W_max` was already
suppressed by fitting D5 to the declaration (§2). The honest reading is not "a passing number was
traded for correctness"; it is **"two numbers, both of them readbacks of the JSON, differed by
0.075 depending on which solver ran first."** The trade is real and creditable. The number it was
traded against was never a measurement.

## 2. The `observed` column of D5 and D6 is a readback of its own setpoint

`WEAPON-CRITIC` §3.3, clause 1: *"A number computable from `game/data/combat/movesets/*.json` with
the game disconnected is `unmeasurable ⇒ 0`, not 'passing on the declared side'."*

Round 3 added two solvers to `game/src/combat/moveset.js`:

- `_yawGain(...)` solves a per-clip yaw gain **until the arc the rig sweeps equals the slot's
  declared `arc_sweep_deg`**;
- `_bladeLength(weaponId)` solves the socket-B distance **until the tip's max horizontal radius
  over the lead slot's active window equals the weapon's declared `reach_m`**.

D5 is `reach_m`. D6 is `arc_sweep_deg`. Both are dimensions of `D_min`, both are `GRAMMAR_DIMS`
(G5 and G3), and `BAR-CRITIQUE-W1-10-R1` §R1 admitted reach to `Dg` on the explicit ground that
*"reach is geometry, not mass"*. The build's own observed column then measures the fitted rig with
the same walk the solver optimised. Three tests
(`kritik3-setpoint.mjs` → `kritik3-setpoint.json`):

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| **T1 residual** `\|observed − declared\|`, 82 weapons, `r1.1` | reach **median 0.0012 m**; arc **median 0.000°**, 74/82 inside 0.1° |
| **T2 animation-blind** — halve every clip's registry `capsule_length_m` and scale every registry `arc_deg` by 0.4 | observed arc moved on **0 of 82 weapons** (max shift **0.01°**); observed reach moved on 30 of 82 (max 0.21 m) |
| **T3 declaration-bound** — `reach_m + 0.50 m`, `arc_sweep_deg × 1.30` in the JSON | observed reach followed within 0.05 m on **67 of 82**; observed arc followed within 10% on **74 of 79** |

You can scale the shipping animation's arc to 40% of itself and **the measured arc does not move
by a hundredth of a degree**, because the solver re-fits to the JSON. You can move the JSON and
the measurement follows it exactly. That is not a measurement of an animation; it is a control
loop whose setpoint is the design document, read back through a ruler.

This is the same shape as the failures ARBITRATION §3 lists — a probe that calls a function
measures the function — and it is why the builder's `declared` and `observed` columns agree to
0.0002 on `D_min`. **It is not fraud and it is not carelessness.** Fitting geometry to a declared
contract is a defensible authoring choice, and the builder wrote the reasoning out at the call
site. What is wrong is *scoring the result as the observed column*, and the corpus asked for this:
`RI-WPN02` §B defines `Reach (m)` as a horizontal radius and never asks where the blade is. §3 is
what that omission bought.

### Is `CR = 0.4219` right, and do three classes really connect zero percent of the time?

**Partly. The mean is wrong; the zeros are real.**

`tools/weapons/connect-rate.mjs` counts its denominator by re-seat events inside a loop that runs
`gap × PRESSES + 200` frames, so it books **12 presses for 10 scripted presses** whenever
`gap ≈ 100`. Every healthy axe, sword and mace in the report reads exactly `10/12 = 0.833` with
`hits_at_reach: 10` — ten hits from ten presses, scored 0.833. `CR_mean = 0.4219` is deflated by
that bug and should not be quoted.

The zeros are not affected by it, because `hits_at_reach = 0` is zero however it is divided.
Confirmed against the report's own rows and reproduced independently:

- **HLB 6/6 weapons, TSW 5/5, WHP 4/4 land nothing at their own declared reach − 0.20 m.**
- SPR 6 of 7, GHM 4 of 5, UGS 4 of 6, CGS 3 of 5 likewise.
- But `weapons_landing_nothing` is empty and `CR_band` averages 0.828: every one of them connects
  *somewhere* closer in. **"A weapon class that connects zero percent of the time" is too strong.**
  The true statement is worse in a different way: *the long classes connect only at short-weapon
  distances.* A halberd that only works inside 2.0 m is a straight sword with a worse recovery.

The builder's own blind pack says the same thing without being asked to: across twelve
1 200-frame traces, **2 to 4 hits land from 8 to 40 swings.**

## 4. **The single biggest gap — bigger than the one the builder named**

The builder named the blade inclination as *"the single biggest remaining player-facing defect"*.
It is a real defect and §3 is its measurement. **It is not the biggest one, and the reason it

> **`game/src/render/` does not draw the animation. Nothing in the renderer reads a bone, a

## 6. Adjudicating 17/17 against 2/6, and ruling on `AMENDMENT-W1-10-03` §E

Both existing instruments write `game/data/weapons/classes.json` **on disk** and restore it in a
`finally`. In a tree thirteen agents share, that is one crash from a corrupted data file and one
concurrent `git commit -a` from banking it — which is precisely how HEAD acquired a reverted
`socketsFor` this round. `kritik3-consumption.mjs` mutates an in-memory copy and never touches the
disk. It also splits every observation into **behavioural** (damage dealt, frames the animation
actually stalled, where the bodies ended up, the move's real length) and **recorded** (fields the
impact record carries), and calls a probe CONSUMED only when a *behavioural* quantity moved.

| Suite | CONSUMED | ECHO | INERT |
|---|---|---|---|
| the round-2 six, on the round-2 fixture (`dummy_passive`, flesh) | **3** | 0 | 3 |
| the round-2 six, each on a fixture that can read the row | **6** | 0 | 0 |
| **negative controls** (rows a correct build must *not* respond to) | 0 | 0 | **3** |
| load-bearing rows on able fixtures | **6** | 1 | 0 |

**Ruling.**

1. **The builder is right and `AMENDMENT-W1-10-03` §E is upheld, with one correction against the
   builder and one against the round-2 critic.** Four of the round-2 probes address rows a flesh
   dummy cannot read or quantities its observation vector does not record; run on a fixture that
   *can* express them, all six fire behaviourally.
2. **Against the round-2 critic:** its `2/6` was itself one probe too pessimistic. `knockback_m
   ALL → 9` **is** consumed on its own flesh fixture — it moves the victim 9 m — and the round-2
   probe missed it only because its observation vector was `{dmg, hitF, attacker hitstop}`. My run
   of the unmodified round-2 rows scores **3/6**, not 2/6.
3. **Against the builder:** `17/17` is one probe too optimistic *as a count*, for the mirror-image
   reason. `hitstop.victim_delta.flesh 4 → 60` moved only the impact **record** against a passive
   dummy that has no animation clock to stall. Driven against `inf_trash` mid-attack it is real:
   the victim's animation holds **16 → 72 frames**. So the row is consumed; the probe that
   demonstrated it was not the probe that could. `17/17` is the right conclusion reached through
   one wrong cell.
4. **§E's proposed rule is ADOPTED as written**, and I add the clause my controls show is needed:
   *a CONSUMPTION suite must also carry at least one probe a correct build leaves INERT.* Three of
   mine did. Without that, "17/17 CONSUMED" is indistinguishable from an instrument that fires on
   everything, which is the `RI-MTH07` failure in its other direction.
5. **The round-2 verdict's acceptance criterion is therefore not met and should not have been
   written that way.** A verdict naming a specific script as acceptance must state which of its
   probes are load-bearing. That correction belongs in `CRITIC-DOCTRINE`, not only in this area.

**The round-2 gap `GAP-W1-weapon-impact-and-material-model-has-no-consumer` is CLOSED.** It is
closed on my instrument, not the builder's, with a named consumer
(`game/src/combat/resolve.js` step 1b → `game/src/combat/impact.js`), and AR-3 is exercised in the
browser: `mce_bog_iron_mace` deals 64.64 to stone while `tsw_bog_rapier` deals **0** and eats
+16 f of recovery. That is a lore fact acting as a combat answer, and it is driven.

## 9. `bar_pressure` — the five answers, and one amendment

**1. Is a passing score here the thing the user asked for?** No, and this is the round where that
becomes provable rather than arguable. Take `hlb_garrison_bill` at random from HLB. What makes it
different from its class baseline: a 145° `sweep`, 2.85 m of reach, 38/14/56 frames, 0.45 m of
lunge. Would a player notice? **A player cannot see it swing** (§4) and **cannot hit anything at
2.65 m with it** (§3). Six items and thirty methods score it as a well-separated class. The bar is
measuring a hitbox capsule and calling it a weapon.

**2. Which threshold is doing no work?** `TDV`'s hard fail, *"any weapon = 0"*. BOW has no `2h.*`
slot table by `RI-WPN01` §A's own construction, so all five bows sit at exactly 0 on every possible
build. This is the identical defect `BAR-CRITIQUE-W1-10-R1` §R2 rejected `Rh_min < 0.03` for — *"a
threshold that fires on a whole class by construction is not a measurement"* — and it survived the
same pass that wrote that sentence. `RI-WPN06` M1 must scope `TDV` to weapons declaring a
two-handed stance.

**3. Which threshold is gameable, and how cheaply?** `D5` and `D6`. One `sed` over
`movesets/*.json` moves `reach_m` and `arc_sweep_deg`, and §2 shows the engine's solvers make the
*observed* column follow within 0.05 m and 10% on 67 and 74 of 82 weapons. `D_min`, `Dg_min`,
`SEP`, `W_min` and `W_med` all read those columns. **The cheapest edit that moves `SEP` from 1.3988
to a pass is a text substitution in the design document.** The companion check is the amendment
below.

**4. The tenth-hour version.** A player forty hours in has settled on two weapons. They complain
that every swing looks the same, because it is: one 0.95 m box on the hip, never posed. Nothing in
the nine numbers asks whether the animation is *drawn*. `ARI`, `W_min`, `SEP`, `D_min` all measure
*clip identity* and none measures *clip visibility*.

**5. Is the roster the right size?** Yes, and for once the evidence is positive: `ARI = 0.4277` with
`UNQ` mean 3.82, a clip-share histogram with no bar above 4, and zero weapons at `UNQ = 0`. Eighty-
seven is carrying itself. Cutting the roster would not buy anything §3 or §4 needs.

### Proposed amendment — `RI-WPN02` §B and §D, `Reach (m)` and D5

> **Replacement text for §B's `Reach (m)` definition:**
>
> `Reach (m)` is **usable blade reach**: the greatest horizontal distance from the attacker's own
> root, over the active window and with root translation suppressed, at which **some point of the
> damaging capsule lies between 0.30 m and 1.70 m above the attacker's foot plane** — a standing
> humanoid's torso band, per `RI-CMB04`'s hurtbox rig. A tip that is outside that band is not
> reach; it is a point in the air or in the ground.
>
> **Added to §D, D5:** D5 is measured under this definition. Report both figures and their
> difference as `reach_shortfall_m`; **any class at `reach_shortfall_m > 0.30 m` is a FAIL of
> §B's conformance row**, and the item must state which.
>
> **Rationale, with the arithmetic.** Under the published definition the shipped build reads D5
> equal to the declaration on 85 of 87 weapons and every class passes. Under this one, WHP is short
> by 1.90 m, SPR by 1.60 m, TSW by 1.01 m, CGS by 0.79 m and HLB by 0.78 m, which is exactly the
> set of classes that land nothing at their own declared spacing. The bar as written cannot
> distinguish a weapon from a weapon pointed at the floor.

A second, smaller amendment is filed against `WEAPON-CRITIC` §3.3: **the CONSUMPTION box must
require a *rendered* consumer as well as a simulation one**, since a model consumed only by the
solver that grades it is the case §3.3 exists to catch.
