## 1. The round-1 gap is closed, and it is closed properly

`GAP-W1-magic-effects-no-behaviour` was the whole of round 1: one generic applicator behind 55
labels, five working effects, sixteen resolving as HP damage equal to their own magnitude,
thirty-three countdowns nobody read.

The brief told me to disbelieve the fix, and told me why: **the builder shipped the tool that
grades the fix.** So I did not re-run `tools/harness/mag-census.mjs` and report its number. I
built my own instrument — `tools/harness/critic-w1-14-r2a.mjs` — on a deliberately different
principle. It has **no per-effect probe table anywhere in it.** For each of the 55 effects it runs
three identical arenas from the same seed: a null control that casts nothing, and two runs at two
well-separated magnitudes. It then diffs a mechanically flattened snapshot of every consuming
system — player, enemy, stealth, quest, the ward registers, the status meters, fall state, the
entity list, and the damage taken from an identical scripted hit — **treatment-after against
control-after**, so the only difference between the two worlds is that one of them cast the spell.

| | round 1 | claimed | **measured by me, independently** |
|---|---|---|---|
| `EFFECT-FUNCTION` | 5 / 55 | 55 / 55 | **55 / 55** |
| `HP_DAMAGE_ONLY` | 16 | 0 | **0** |
| `UNREAD_TIMER` | 33 | 0 | **0** |
| `GENERIC-APPLICATOR` | 49 / 55 | 0 / 55 | **0 / 55** |

**55/55 survives.** `open_lock` unlocks locks. `demoralise` ends a fight. `feather` moves the
equip-load tier across the 30.00 cliff without moving the cliff. `charm` writes a disposition.
`speak_to_the_dead` writes a topic. `shatter` breaks three things and raises three world flags.
`wall` puts a box in the collision cell. `bind_lesser` spawns an ally. Nothing in this file is a
countdown any more.

Five more things that were broken in round 1 and are not now, each measured here rather than taken
from the checkpoint:

  state `AIRBORNE`. I pressed `light`, `heavy`, `block`, `roll` and `parry` for real: all five

## 2. The single biggest gap

### `GAP-W1-magic-skill-frozen` — the mage cannot get better at magic

> **`MagicSystem.skills` is a private object initialised to
> `{sorcery: 30, root_speech: 30, warding: 30, veiling: 30}` that no gameplay path writes.**

Every magic probe in this tree opens its arena with the same line:

```js
H.setMagicSkills({ sorcery: 100, root_speech: 100, warding: 100, veiling: 100 });
```

`mag-probe.mjs` does it. `mag-census.mjs` does it. `mag-quest.mjs` does it. **My own first two
probes did it**, because I copied the arena from the file I was auditing. `setMagicSkills` is a
harness call with no in-game counterpart, and while it is in the arena the gate behind it is
invisible.

`tools/harness/critic-w1-14-r2f.mjs` is the only magic probe in the tree that does not call it.

**What the game gives you, in four shipped states:**

| State | Attunable | Refused | Refused by tier |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `default` | 47 / 72 | **25** | all 17 tier-3, all 8 tier-4 |
| `arena_flat` | 47 / 72 | **25** | all 17 tier-3, all 8 tier-4 |
| `dungeon_primary` | 47 / 72 | **25** | all 17 tier-3, all 8 tier-4 |
| `helstrom-market` | 47 / 72 | **25** | all 17 tier-3, all 8 tier-4 |

**And nothing a player can do moves it.** Perturbation, RI-MTH07 §B2, one variable at a time,
observing whether a tier-3 spell can be attuned and whether `quoteSpell().attunable_now` flips:

| Perturbation | tier-3 attuned | `attunable_now` |
|---|---:|---|
| cold, as the game ships | 0 / 1 | false |
| **40 casts at a live target** | 0 / 1 | false |
| **a HEARTH rest** | 0 / 1 | false |
| **`setSkills({warding: 100, sorcery: 100, veiling: 100, 'root-speech': 100})` — the character sheet** | 0 / 1 | false |
| **`grantSkillUse('cast_effective', {cost: 40, spell_skill: 'warding'})`** | 0 / 1 | false |
| `setMagicSkills({warding: 100, …})` — **harness only** | **1 / 1** | **true** |

**Coupling 0.00, both directions, with a clean null control.** Driving the character sheet to 100
changes nothing magic-side; driving `magic.skills` to 100 changes it immediately — which proves the
probe is driving a live gate and not a stub, exactly as RI-MTH07 §C requires.

Three consequences, all measured:

1. **A third of the catalogue is unreachable for the whole game.** `setAttuned()` drops an
   under-skilled spell **silently** — it returns a shorter array than it was given, with no event
   and no reason. A player who buys `the_unmaking` gets no error; the spell simply never appears in
   the loadout.
2. **Spellmaking is capped at two effects forever.** At the shipped skill, a three-effect spell is
   refused: *"3 effects needs skill 50; your best relevant school is 30 (allows 2)."* The
   commission system whose arithmetic this piece got exactly right is permanently restricted to its
   simplest case.
3. **RI-MAG04's quest gate reads the other register, and that one is empty.**
   `requires.skills: {warding: 45, sorcery: 25}` is evaluated against `sim.progression.skills`,
   which magic never writes and which is `{}` in `default`, `arena_flat` and `dungeon_primary`
   alike. **60 casts at a live target produced 0 `skill_use` events.** The Warded Ledger's magic
   resolution only became available after I called `H.setSkills()` — RI-MTH07 §C3's hand-feed
   failure, on this item's central check.

And the register that *does* advance by casting advances the wrong thing:
`game/src/character/skilluse.js` maps `spell_hit`/`effect_apply` to
`ctx = {spell_skill: e.skill || 'sorcery'}`, but neither event carries a `skill` field — so every
spell of every school would bank into `sorcery`, if there were anything to bank into.

**Why it matters.** A mage who plays this game for forty hours casts exactly the spells they could
cast in the first minute. Every tier-3 and tier-4 spell, and every three-effect spell they might
commission, sits behind a number that nothing they do can move — and the quest resolutions magic
exists to unlock read a different number that starts empty and stays empty.

**Remedy and acceptance are in the JSON.** The short version: make `MagicSystem.skills` a *view* of
`sim.progression.skills` rather than a private copy (they even spell the school differently —
`root_speech` against `root-speech`); put `skill` on the `effect_apply` and `spell_hit` payloads so
`stepSkillUse` banks the spell's own school; make `setAttuned()` emit a refusal instead of
dropping. **Acceptance is `critic-w1-14-r2f.mjs` re-run: 72/72 attunable at skill 100, ≥1
`skill_use` event with `skill == 'warding'` after 40 warding casts, and `Q-MAG-01`'s magic
resolution available with no `setSkills`/`setMagicSkills` call anywhere in the probe.**

## 3. 55 handlers, 33 outcomes

The brief asked whether 55 distinct code paths produce 55 distinguishable outcomes in play. They do
not. Grouping every effect by the **set of world-side leaf paths it moves**, computed mechanically:

**33 distinct signatures for 55 handlers.** Six collision groups:

| Effects | Shared signature |
|---|---|
| `resist_element`, `resist_disease`, `shield`, `sap_ward` | `damage taken from an identical hit` |
| `fire_damage`, `frost_damage`, `shock_damage`, `poison_damage`, `damage_health` | `enemy.hp` + `status` |
| `calm_beast`, `demoralise` | `enemy.state`, `yielded`, `in_combat`, `quest.flags` |
| `open_lock`, `lock_lock` | the lock register + `quest.flags` |
| `bind_lesser`, `bind_greater` | `listEntities()` + the summon register |
| `corrode`, `cure_paralysis`, `burden`, `silence`, `paralyse` | `status` only |

Some of those are legitimate — `feather` and `burden` *should* share a field with opposite signs.
One is not, and it is the one the checkpoint reports as a success.

### The mitigation quartet is one number with four names

`body.mitigation` is a single multiplier that `resolve.js:mitigate()` applies to every incoming
damage number regardless of where the damage came from. There is no element channel.

**An identical scripted physical hit of 100:**

| Buff (magnitude 20) | Damage taken |
|---|---:|
| none | **100** |
| `shield` (physical) | 69.12 |
| `resist_element` | **15.00** |
| `resist_disease` | **15.00** |

A **resist-disease** buff blunts a sword by **85%**. And the converse, cast on an enemy and then
hit with a fire spell:

| Buff on the target | Fire damage taken |
|---|---:|
| none | **405** |
| `shield` (physical) | **5** |
| `resist_element` | **5** |

The checkpoint reports *"an identical scripted 100-damage hit takes 100 unbuffed, 53.68 with
shield, 30.52 with `resist_element`"* as evidence the mitigation effects work. It is the same
measurement I just made. The two numbers differ only because the two effects scale their magnitude
differently — not because either of them knows what kind of damage it is stopping. In Morrowind,
Resist Fire does not stop a sword.

### Four of the five status procs are written and never read

The S11 buildup meter is real: an integer per contact against an integer threshold, never a coin
flip, and `paralyse` never goes 0 → applied in one frame. But of the five proc kinds, **only
`PARALYSED` is consumed** — `magic/system.js` writes `b.state = 'PARALYSED'` and the enemy stops,
which I measured across five consecutive samples.

`BURNING` procced **twice** on a live enemy and its state stayed `REPOSITION` for all twelve
samples. `FROSTBITE`, `CONCUSSED` and `POISONED` are the same shape: they set
`body.statusProc[kind]`, and nothing outside the expiry deleter and the status readout ever reads
it. That is round 1's `UNREAD_TIMER` failure moved one level down, below where RI-MAG06's census
looks.

### Eight effects act on registers nothing outside the magic module reads

| Effect | Writes | Read by anything outside `game/src/sim/magic/**`? |
|---|---|---|
| `telekinesis` | `M.reachM` | **No.** The interaction that actually reaches for a prop tests `o.reach_m` in `engine._censusStep` |
| `detect_life`, `detect_key` | `M.markers` | **No.** No renderer pass, no entity |
| `mend_item` | an item-condition table | **No.** No durability system exists elsewhere in the build |
| `buoyancy`, `breathe_water` | `M.water.{buoyant, breathes, drownF}` | **No.** The water you actually swim and drown in is `engine.traversal` (`water_band`, `breath_s`, `submerged`) |
| `restore_attribute` | `progression.attributes` | Real field, **no reachable precondition** — nothing in the build ever lowers an attribute |
| `cure_disease`, `cure_poison` | `sim.quest.afflictions` | **Only `H.addAffliction()` writes it.** The diseases the game can give you are pushed by `hazards.js` onto `sim.player.afflictions`, a different array |

These are RI-MTH07 §A's *orphan model* and *orphan predicate*, one layer in from where round 1 found
them. The verbs fire; the tables they write are the magic module talking to itself.

## 4. Teleport out of a fight

S19 is explicit: *"no recall out of a fight, no warping past a shortcut you have not opened, no
intervention as an escape button."* The shipped `mark`, `recall` and both `intervention` spells are
`RITUAL` class, and a 210-frame ritual cannot finish with an enemy on you — which is the only
reason round 1 passed this.

`quoteSpell()`/`makeSpell()` accept **any class for any effect**. There is no
`can_cast_in_combat` check anywhere on the cast path.

| Commissioned spell | State at cast | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `intervention`, LIGHT class, magnitude 45 | aggroed enemy at 3 m, `in_combat: true` | player moved **44.77 m** to `[-40, 0, 20.10]`, `in_combat` → false |
| `recall`, LIGHT class, magnitude 45 | aggroed enemy, `in_combat: true` | player moved **84.74 m** from `[60,0,60]` to the mark |

A 56-frame escape button, purchasable for gold, in the build.

I do **not** fail AR-2 on this. AR-2's enumerated leak is *bonfire warp*, and S7/S19 explicitly
**permit** Intervention as part of the travel network out of combat — so this is not the enumerated
mechanic, and S26 forbids widening an AR check to cover an ordinary defect. It is a direct breach
of a named S19 prohibition, it is charged to RI-MAG01 and RI-MAG03, and it is on the record here
rather than absorbed. I have also filed it as an arbitration question, because the corpus never
says whether a fence lives on the effect or on the cast class, and spellmaking is exactly where
that matters.

## 6. Arbitration

**AR-1 — pass.** 30 identical casts collapse to one damage value (53) with stdev **0.000000**; no
`failure_chance`/`resist_chance`/`proc_chance` token exists in the shipped magic data; the
determinism guard reports 0 violations across every cast at all five classes; commitment holds; no
cast i-frames on any frame of any run, including the 60 levitation frames where round 1 measured
`iframe: true`; the S11 meter is integer buildup against an integer threshold and never a coin
flip; skill still changes the Focus discount and the tier gate and never whether a spell connects —
now vacuously so, because the skill never changes.

**One AR-1 check moved the wrong way and is recorded rather than waved through.** Round 1's A7 read
*"projectile heading change 0.000°/s — no player homing exists."* That sentence is no longer true. A
spell fired on the release heading **hit a body standing 15° off axis at 14 m** (53 damage) and
missed one at 30°. Inside the 20° acquisition cone, aiming is optional. It is bounded, it stops at
the declared cutoff, and the cutoff behaviour is declared by `cast-classes.json` — so I charge it to
RI-MAG01 rather than failing AR-1, because it is not one of the six mechanics AR-1 enumerates and
S26 forbids diluting the check.

**AP-M3 could not be run at all.** `MagicSystem.hitboxRecords()` computes per-projectile
`turn_rate_dps` and `travel_f`, but `engine.getHitGeometry()` does not include it and no other
harness surface exposes a projectile's heading. The checkpoint's headline — *"60.00 deg/s before
the cutoff at travel_f 53 and 0.000 after"* — **cannot be reproduced by a critic**. Recorded
`not_run` with the reason, not repeated. A number nobody can check is not a measurement, and the
fix is one line in `getHitGeometry()`.

**AR-2 — pass.** `hist_sight` writes prose and zero HUD markers; `detect_*` write diegetic records
with `hud: false` and `hud_elements` stays 0; gold is the only currency and the magic line
recomputes to 29,800 g with `souls` never read or written; B12's clause (c) works. The in-combat
teleport is named under B-warp and reasoned about in §4 rather than being quietly swallowed.

**AR-3 — no longer sterile.** Round 1 had 0 of 7 declared crossings demonstrable and 3 measured to
fail. I independently confirmed **3 of 7**: X1 (a spell ends a fight with a living enemy and zero
deaths), X4 (`feather` moves the player across RI-CMB01's roll cliff), X6 (`soul_trap`/`charm` move
province-wide disposition — `quest.dispositions.e0` = 100). The other four I did not re-run and
they are recorded as the builder's claim, not mine.

**CONSUMPTION — mixed, and the mixture is the story.** 55 of 55 effects move a system. Eight of
them move only magic-private state. And the two models the whole piece turns on — the character's
magic skill and the magic system's magic skill — have coupling **0.00** to each other.

## 7. Which builder claims did not survive

The checkpoint is again unusually honest, and five of its headline claims survived my own
instruments unchanged: 55/55, `HP_DAMAGE_ONLY` 0, levitation on all six assertions, the two
structural bugs, and the 29,800 g magic line. These did not:

1. **"MITIGATION measured: 100 unbuffed, 53.68 with shield, 30.52 with resist_element."** True, and
   it is the defect. The two numbers differ by scaling, not by element; `resist_disease` blunts a
   sword by 85% and `shield` blunts fire identically to `resist_element`.
2. **"M6 10/10 magic resolutions played through end to end."** The state machine advanced ten
   times. The gate in front of it opens only to `H.setSkills()`, and the resolution is
   location-blind: I completed *The Warded Ledger* standing in `arena_flat`, having cast
   `open_lock` and `shatter` on the arena's own ward registers, never at the customs loft.
   `requires.spell_effects` names the effect, never the object — the
   `lock:warded_ledger_door:open` flag exists and feeds journal index 30, but no *resolution*
   requires it.
3. **"Projectile tracking arc MEASURED: 60.00 deg/s before the cutoff and 0.000 after."** Not
   reproducible: no harness surface exposes it. And the acquisition cone it implements makes a
   15°-off-axis shot connect.
4. **"the census records its own reading alongside `declared_consumer` so the two can disagree in
   public."** They cannot, meaningfully. The census never compares the two consumer strings; its
   only disagreement channel is `self_report_disagrees`, which fires solely when the build says
   `changed: false` and the census still classed the row `FUNCTIONS_AS_SPECIFIED` (6 rows do). All
   36 rows where the declared and measured consumer strings actually differ are cosmetic wording
   differences the tool cannot see — and its `consuming_system` field is a hardcoded label from its
   own `PROBES` table, not a measurement. **If a handler declared the wrong consumer, this census
   would not notice.**
5. **"The birthsign Focus-absorption conflict needs a doctrine ruling."** S27 ruled it, in the same
   commit. `engine.hearthRest()` correctly implements the new drawback, but
   `game/data/progression/birthsigns.json` still declares *"You absorb 55% of the magnitude of any
   spell effect that lands on you, as Focus"* and *"Focus is refilled only by absorption and by
   consumables"* — which now contradicts the hearth denial the engine applies. `spell_absorption` is
   derived in `character/derive.js` and consumed by nothing. `magic-audit` still reads 10 pass / 1
   fail on exactly this.

**The defensive guard papers over nothing.** `engine.js:215` reads
`this.sim.censusDriver = (input) => (this._censusStep ? this._censusStep(input) : null)`.
`_censusStep` is a prototype method at `engine.js:964` and is always truthy, so the guard is dead
code — there is no state in which the census driver is missing for it to mask. It was added to
survive a concurrent commit that had removed the method. Harmless; worth deleting rather than
keeping as a permanent apology.

**Regressions, run by me:** `mag-probe` 19/19 including `AR3_X1` and `AR3_X4`; `save-audit`
round-trip EQUAL on all four states (the `manifest-extra 13/1` belongs to W1-15's crime ledger and
W1-07's NPC/prop records, not this piece); `lint-determinism` 0 errors over 87 files; `magic-audit`
10 pass / 1 fail.

## 8. My own instrument was wrong first, and the build was right

The first run of `critic-w1-14-r2a.mjs` reported **22 effects as "moved nothing"** — which, had I
believed it, would have been a spectacular and completely false finding. Two bugs, both mine:

- the flattener recorded only each array element's `id`, so a lock going `locked: true → false` was
  invisible;
- I subtracted the control by key, which silently deleted every hp-moving effect because the
  control's own scripted hit also moved hp.

`diag-touch.json` is the artifact that caught it: the spells were resolving all along, emitting
`lock_opened` and `status_proc` events I was throwing away. The protocol's warning — *break the
thing your instrument measures on purpose and confirm the instrument goes red* — is what found it,
run in reverse. It is recorded here because this verdict charges other people with exactly this
class of error and it would be dishonest not to.

Boot was confirmed before anything was measured (`smoke.mjs` 6/6, `api-probe.mjs` 33/33 present,
0 page errors) and the tree stayed bootable throughout; no retry-for-churn was needed. Every number
here comes from a trace, a screenshot, a draw-call series or a data recomputation I ran myself.
Source was read thirteen times and every read is declared in `source_reads` with its permitted
purpose; eleven of the thirteen were *after* the corresponding finding was already demonstrated
from output, to make the remedy name the right file.

**The escalation ladder mattered, and it mattered at rung 4.** Rung 1 was the builder's own census
re-run at HEAD: 55/55, and a comfortable pass. Rung 2 was building an independent instrument on a
different principle: also 55/55 — the honest answer, and I have said so plainly. Rung 4, *measure
the second-order property*, is what produced this verdict. Not "does an effect move a system" but
**"how many distinguishable outcomes do 55 handlers produce"** (33), and **"can the character who
casts them ever get better at it"** (no). The gap that decides this round was standing directly
behind the gap that decided the last one, and every probe in the area — including my first two —
had been walking past it with `setMagicSkills({...100})` in its opening line.
