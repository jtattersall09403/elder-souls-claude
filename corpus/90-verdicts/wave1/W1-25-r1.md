# W1-25 r1 — the experience instrument and the seam crossings

**Verdict: FAIL — 2.0 / 10** (min over axes; wave-1 gate 7.0). Measured at `fdef3d6`.
Never judged before; this is round 1.

**The one-line finding.** The best instrument in this corpus, pointed at nothing. The sabotage
facility is real, falsifiable and honest — it reproduces three historical inert controls from the
artifacts that produced them and it goes red when broken three ways on purpose. Its *own* control
against the project's most common failure is delegated to an optional integer that the caller
chooses and nothing validates. And the piece's most consequential number — *only 4 of 41 declared
seam crossings have any data behind them* — is wrong, in the direction that condemns fourteen
other pieces, because the scanner behind it never opened the one file where four of the missing
crossings live.

Every number below was produced by `tools/experience/critic-w1-25.mjs`, which ran 30 checks and
missed none, and whose every section carries a teardown that was **run and watched go red**
(RULES #6). Artifact: `reports/experience/critic-w1-25.json`. Picture:
`docs/shots/2026-08-08-critic-w1-25-one-integer-decides-the-verdict.png`.

## A — the facility, pointed at a control another builder declared sound

`W1-14-r3` shipped `--break=nocast` as the control arm for its magic-dials census and recorded
*"the instrument cannot manufacture a coupling out of arena noise"*. A human critic found it inert
in RULES #6's second sense: the teardown makes every one of the 55 rows exit at `NOT_DELIVERED`
**before `readDial()` is called**, so the comparator under test never ran.

Replayed through `runControl` from the builder's own two artifacts, under the three support
declarations a caller could honestly write:

| arm | `support` means | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** | effects the census *examined* — 55 in both arms | **`OK`** |
| **A2** | effects that *delivered*, i.e. reached the comparator — 55 vs 0 | **`VACUOUS`** |
| **A3** | not declared at all | **`OK`** |

Same control. Same two files. `33 → 0` in every case. The facility catches the strongest real
inert control available on this tree **only** under one of the three readings, and it is not the
reading a careless caller reaches for. `support` is optional — `Number.isFinite(undefined)` is
false, the arm's support becomes `null`, and `unsupported = arms.filter(a => a.support !== null && …)`
skips it. Omit the field and the entire W1-13 vacuity check is silently disabled for that control.
Nothing warns.

**My teardown, run:** forge the control arm's support up to 55 and A2 flips `VACUOUS → OK`. The
verdict is carried entirely by one caller-supplied integer and by nothing the facility observed.

**A fourth failure shape, with no name in the taxonomy.** W1-14-r3 is not arms-agree, not
no-single-factor-effect and not empty-population. It is *the teardown short-circuiting upstream of
the mechanism under test* — the arms differ loudly and the difference is about the short-circuit.
The five verdicts have no category for it and it is caught, when it is caught, as a side effect of
support accounting.

**A fifth, found by accident while grading.** My first run of section A read `magnitude_coupled`
off the top level of the artifact instead of off `.summary`, so **both arms measured `undefined`**.
`canon()` maps `undefined`, `null` and a missing field all to the string `'null'`, the arms
"agree", and the facility returned **`INERT`** — a confident, publishable claim that breaking it
changed nothing, about a measurement that never happened. That is the piece's own flagship failure,
produced by a typo, inside the tool built to prevent it. Kept as check `A4`.

## B — the five verdicts are separable, and three of them collide under pressure

Six canonical cases produce six distinct verdicts. That is the floor. Four adversarial cases:

| case | shape | verdict | should be |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B-COLLIDE-1** | two guards for one defect, joint effect 5%, 50% margin declared | `UNDERPOWERED` | `MASKED` |
| **B-COLLIDE-2** | factor A moves it 100→40; A+B restores it to 100 | `INERT` | not `INERT` |
| **B-COLLIDE-3** | empty population **and** agreeing arms | `VACUOUS` | both, reported |
| **B-COLLIDE-4** | the canonical W1-13 case with `supportArms:'intact'` and no `support_note` | **`OK`** | `VACUOUS` |

Each with its teardown run: drop the margin and B-COLLIDE-1 becomes `MASKED`; drop the cancelling
factor and B-COLLIDE-2 becomes `OK`; restore the default support scope and B-COLLIDE-4 becomes
`VACUOUS`. The collisions are in the cascade, not in my fixtures.

Three things follow.

1. **`MASKED` is last in the cascade** (`!marginRes.ok` is tested before `masked`), so the one
   verdict that exists to stop an agent deleting a guard in good faith is shadowed by any unmet
   margin or wrong direction. The record still carries `factors_inert_alone`, but the *verdict* —
   what the exit code and the report headline carry — says the control is weak.
2. **`armsAgree` is decided on two arms.** It compares intact against all-broken and nothing else,
   so under cancellation the facility reports `INERT` while its own record says
   `factors_that_move_it_alone: ["a"]` and `minimal_breaking_set: ["a"]`, and its `why` string
   asserts "changed nothing". `distinct_values === 1` is computed one line above and is the honest
   reading of *the arms agree*.
3. **The documented obligation on `supportArms:'intact'` is enforced nowhere.** The docstring says
   a caller that narrows the support scope *"must say why, in `support_note`"*. No code path reads
   `support_note`; `runControl` does not even copy it onto the result, so a downstream report
   cannot audit for it. The W1-13 failure the facility was built for is one spec key away from
   passing, unauditably.

## D — "only 4 of 41 crossings have any data behind them at all" is wrong

This is the piece's most consequential number and it condemns fourteen other pieces. It is a
property of `matrix-scan.mjs`'s hand-written pattern table, not of the build.

All 56 patterns are rooted in the **source** system's own data directory. Four of the 37 crossings
reported as having no data of any kind are declared on the **target's** side, and all four sit in
one file that produced **zero** of the tool's 30 claims out of the 564 files it read:

| cell | field in `game/data/world/encounters.json` | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| `QST->ROS` | `members[].absent_when_flag: "the_sixty_are_protected"` | `AR-3`'s own worked example — *"a quest whose resolution changes an encounter's composition"* |
| `WLD->ROS` | the same field read as world-state → roster | a world flag removes two bodies from a spawn |
| `DIS->ROS` | `hostile_below_disposition: 15` | an encounter hostile only below a disposition threshold |
| `LOR->ROS` | `parley.true_name_topic` ×2 (`the-sallow-wife`, `the-twin-lamps`) | `AR-3`'s *"a lore fact that is also a boss's weakness"* |

`QST->ROS` is not merely data-backed. It was **built deliberately by W1-19 round 2** to answer a
`seam_sterile: true` verdict, its own data file carries a paragraph explaining why, and a
purpose-written CONSUMPTION probe sits on the tree — `tools/quests/encounter-seam-probe.mjs`,
which spawns the encounter with the flag clear and set and counts bodies. W1-25 cites none of it.
Nor does it cite `tools/quests/critic-ar3-endtoend.mjs`, `tools/quests/faction-seam-probe.mjs`,
`tools/world/crossing-consumption.mjs` or `tools/world/crossing-deletefix.mjs`, all present.

At least **8 of 41**, not 4. My teardown: the same key search for a field that does not exist in
that file returns 0, so the four hits are four real fields and not a loose match.

**What the piece should have published instead.** The 37 no-data crossings are not scattered.
Every one has `ROS` or `BOS` as its source or its target. The finding is not a count — it is that
**the enemy roster and the bosses are the seam-dead systems and everything else touches
something**. That statement survives the scanner being wrong about any individual cell, and it is
directly actionable.

**What the tool accepts as a demonstration.** `RI-CMP01` requires each crossing *demonstrated
firing in a trace*. `matrix-scan.mjs` accepts a JSON pointer existing in a data file. To its
credit the tool says so itself, in its own output: *"Nothing in this file is a demonstration."*
`matrix-probe.mjs`, the thing that would be one, has never run: `matrix.json` records
`ran: false`, `demonstrated_crossings: 0` against a wave-1 floor of 4.

## E — the zero-event session: fault located, claim overstated

The narrow claims are true and load-bearing. `reports/sessions/exp-w1-opening/trace.jsonl` is
36,000 frames and 79 MB with **zero** events; the journey traces carry five each, all boot
plumbing (`first_input`, `first_control`, `surface_enter`, `input_dropped`).

The generalisation — *"`verified_anecdotes_per_hour` is 0 by construction on every trace this
project has ever recorded"* — is **false as written**. 77 traces on this tree carry 5,267 events.
Thirty-four carry more than five, and thirty of those already existed when W1-25 wrote its own
anecdote report, so this is not a timing artifact. The top five are W1-09's `RI-CMB07` combat
exemplars at **493–553 events over 24–28 kinds** each, including `hit`, `stagger`, `whiff`,
`guard_up`, `block_success`, `death`, `lock_break` and `detect`.

**So which of the three candidates is at fault?**

- **Not the recorder.** The same trace writer emits 553 events in an exemplar.
- **Not the event vocabulary.** `game/src/sim/events.js` holds 179 names and every consequence
  name an anecdote needs — `journal`, `journal_write`, `quest_stage`, `topic`, `topic_select`,
  `item`, `death`, `level_up`, `bonfire_rest`, `dialogue_open`, `save_write`, `player_respawn` —
  is present. RULES #15 does not bite here.
- **The fixture.** A ten-minute session that never enters combat, opens dialogue, writes a journal
  entry, takes anything or dies. RULES #8 exactly: a still target hides every steering defect, and
  the easiest fixture collects the most data and distinguishes nothing. 82 MB of it.

This matters because the piece's phrasing points the remedy at the recorder. The cheapest real
number in the whole piece is to point `anecdote-trace.mjs` at
`reports/w1-09/exemplar/RI-CMB07-exemplar-F3-trace.jsonl`, which has a death, six staggers, a guard
break and a block success in 9,000 frames. It will score near zero on A2 (proper noun) and A4
(locatable) because it is an arena — **and that is the finding `RI-EXP02` wants**: a build whose
only recorded consequences happen in a room with no name.

**Incidental, and it belongs to the harness rather than to this piece:** 275 of 553 events in an
exemplar are `{type, f, mirrors: "UPPERCASE"}` stubs carrying no payload. The closed `HARNESS.md`
§5 vocabulary is satisfied by empty shells over a parallel uppercase vocabulary that carries the
data — RULES #10's two parallel implementations, living in the trace format.

## G — CONSUMPTION, decided rather than applied

The piece ships two models, `corpus/95-experience/RI-CMP01.cells.json` and `RI-EXP06.probes.json`,
and states plainly that both are instrument data with tool-side consumers and that nothing in the
running world reads either.

**Accepted for this piece.** `ARBITRATION` §3's CONSUMPTION rule exists for a specific harm — a
*world* model that the running world never reads, so that from the player's chair a correct model
and a missing one are the same thing. An instrument's own data has no player-facing surface to be
dead in. Both artifacts are generated from corpus items and consumed by tools, and both
consumptions are demonstrated by perturbation: `cells-from-md --self-test` mutates a copy of the
item four ways and requires the generator to go red, 4/4; `probes-from-md` the same, 4/4. Scoring
these 0 would assert the instrument does not work, which is false. The builder declared this rather
than papering over it and that is the right behaviour.

**But the rule's spirit transfers exactly, and it fails.** For an instrument piece the equivalent
prior question is *is any of it plugged in?* — and it is measurably not. `grep` for importers of
`lib/sabotage.mjs` across `tools/` and `game/` returns `matrix-probe.mjs`, `breakage-probe.mjs`,
`anecdote-verify.mjs`, `sabotage.mjs` and `sabotage-cases.mjs`. **Every one is W1-25's own.** Zero
adopters outside the piece; `matrix-probe.mjs` has never touched a browser; `breakage-probe.mjs`
performs no breakage. A general facility with no caller outside the piece that wrote it is a
library, not an instrument, and it is the same failure the piece exists to name, one level up.

## The single biggest gap

**`GAP-W1-25-the-control-facility-has-an-optional-control`**
`subsystem_path: experience.permissiveness.register` · **severity: blocking**

The facility fails when its arms agree, and whether it can *see* that they agree is delegated to
`support` — a field that is optional, undefined by default, unvalidated, and whose two honest
readings give opposite verdicts on the strongest real inert control available on this tree. On top
of that: `undefined` in both arms canonicalises to agreement and returns `INERT`; `MASKED` is
shadowed by any unmet margin; `armsAgree` is decided on two arms out of the factorial; and the one
documented obligation on the escape hatch (`support_note`) is enforced nowhere. Every one of those
is the same defect the piece was built to catch, occurring inside it.

**Remedy — all four are small, and all four are in one file.**

1. **Make `support` mandatory.** `runControl` already throws on a spec with no factors
   (`a control with no factors is not a control`). Throw the same way when any arm returns no
   finite `support`: *a control that cannot say what it ranged over is not a control*. Change the
   docstring's *"the number of units the measurement ranged over"* to *"units that **reached the
   comparator**"* — that one word is what separates A1 from A2.
2. **Add `NO_MEASUREMENT`.** When every arm's value canonicalises to `'null'`, say so instead of
   saying `INERT`. It is the same argument the piece already makes for `VACUOUS`.
3. **Add `SHORT_CIRCUIT`** for the fourth shape: the broken arm's support collapses relative to
   the intact arm's. Both numbers are already on every arm record.
4. **Hoist `masked` above margin and direction**, make `armsAgree` read `distinct_values === 1`,
   and throw when `supportArms: 'intact'` arrives without a `support_note` (and carry that note
   onto the result so a report can list every control that opted out).

**Acceptance.** `tools/experience/critic-w1-25.mjs --a --b` re-run against the amended facility:
A1 and A3 must become `ERROR`/`NO_MEASUREMENT` rather than `OK`; A4 must become `NO_MEASUREMENT`;
B-COLLIDE-1 must become `MASKED`; B-COLLIDE-2 must stop being `INERT`; B-COLLIDE-4 must throw. The
existing `--self-test` 6/6, the three facility breaks and `--cases` 8/8 must all still pass, and a
fourth break — `--break=support-optional` — must be added and shown going red. The critic
instrument is on the tree and is the acceptance harness; it does not need writing.
