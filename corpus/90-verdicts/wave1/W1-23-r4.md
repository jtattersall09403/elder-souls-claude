`a-short-account-of-the-pacification` is **one half of a registered contradiction pair** — the
drowning at the ford, against `the-account-the-marsh-keeps`. So one of the nineteen pairs this
round's headline is about has a side that is placed and cannot be opened, and neither the
substring test, nor `place-library --check`, nor my own twelve legs could see it. That is the shape
this project keeps rediscovering, one layer further in than last time: not *unplaced*, and not
*unread*, but **placed, drawn, and inert under the button**.

**Diagnosed, and it is not crowding and not the door.** `tools/lore/critic-w1-23-r4-blocked.mjs`
stands at each of the four, presses, and reads back the world state that decided what the press did
— with two controls, one book in the *same room* that does open and one in a clean room. The
pre-registered hypothesis was the door: `render/interior.js` filters document slots to more than
3.8 m from `continuity.interior_spawn` precisely because `sim/settlement.js` takes `interact` for
the way out within `DOOR_REACH_M` = 2.6 m and takes it first, and the filter is applied to the slot
rather than to where the player stands. **The hypothesis is wrong** — the four are 10.2 to 12.2 m
from the spawn — and the real answer is better:

```
  gideon-court   a-short-account-of-the-pacification  nothing opened
      also within reach: "A tally sheet with a crease across it, folded before it was
                          written on"                              0.72 m
  gideon-court   the-legates-seal-register            nothing opened
      also within reach: "A distraint order, the wax under the seal too pale for the
                          office's"                                0.50 m
  gideon-court   the-casebook-i                       OPENED   (control, same room)
  helstrom-undertemple  the-coast-survey-line         nothing opened
      also within reach: "A kin-post kept clear, with no name cut on it"   0.90 m
  helstrom-undertemple  the-helstrom-kin-list         nothing opened
      also within reach: "A kin-post, four fourth-days unanswered"         0.77 m
  lilmoth-customs  the-blessings-of-the-coast         OPENED   (control, clean room)
```

`engine.js:3270` takes the **nearest** prop inside its reach — `for (const o of this.sim.props) …
if (d <= o.reach_m && d < bestD)`. The competitor in every one of the four cases is a
`game/data/world/readables/site-marks.json` entry, placed at a **hard-coded coordinate**
(`"pos": [-3.6, 0.9, -4.6]`), by a different code path, in the same room.

**So there are two placement mechanisms in this tree and they cannot see each other.** The 2.6 m
minimum separation in `render/interior.js` is computed over the interior record's `readable[]` list
and over nothing else; the twenty-five site-marks are laid down by their own route and know nothing
about the wall-slot allocator. The round's own status note says *"PLACEMENT REUSES W1-READABLES
ROUND 2 AND ADDS NO SECOND MECHANISM"* — which is true of the mechanism it chose, and beside the
point, because **the tree already had two**, and this round put ninety-nine documents into rooms
without either mechanism checking the other's coordinates. Nothing in the tree can see the
collision: not the substring reachability test, not `place-library --check`, not `boot-check`, not
`check-content`, and not my own twelve chosen legs. It took walking all forty-five.

### 1b. The blindness the round declared unfixable is a two-line diagnosis, and it is fixed here

`orchestration/status/W1-23-r4.json` lists under `not_done`:

> *"THE RENDERED-TEXT ACCESSOR IS BLIND TO THE BOOK SCREEN. `getRenderedText()` returns 0
> characters on every leg with a book open… Not mine to fix."*

**It is not blind.** `ui/system.js build()` returns early on
`builtFrame === ctx.frame && lastMode === mode && lastTouchSig === sig`, and while a screen is up
the world is paused, so `ctx.frame` never advances and the cache key is constant for the entire
life of the screen. The round-4 tool cleared the text register **after** the book was already open
and then asked for a frame; `build()` returned immediately, nothing was drawn, and the register it
had just emptied stayed empty. **Zero the register before the player presses the button** and the
forced build that opens the screen draws straight into it. That is the only difference between my
tool and theirs on this point — no harness door, no engine back door, both asserted against my own
bytes — and it moves the answer from *0 chars, judged on the paginator instead* to *1,531
characters of the Blessings of the Coast, on the screen, with `chartered` and `grandparents` in
them*.

This matters beyond one tool. The round judged its perturbation arm on
`bookPagination()` because it thought it had to. It did not have to, and neither does anyone else.

**Screenshot:** `docs/shots/2026-08-08-w1-23-r4-critic-a-book-open-in-the-lilmoth-customs-house.png`
— the customs house, one metre from the shelf, the Empire's own answer to whether subjects are held
as property, open. **This is the first picture anyone has taken of a book in this game.** The
round's own filed image, `docs/shots/2026-08-08-w1-23-r4-both-sides-of-the-argument.png`, is a bar
chart of the before-and-after counts — a perfectly good chart, and not a photograph of anything in
the world. A round whose headline is "a player can now read these" and whose only image is a chart
of how many is exactly the substitution ARBITRATION §3 was written about, in miniature.

### 3a. Fifty names, read by ear

Deterministically sampled across the eight rosters. The Jel is mostly good — *Vosh-Vastei,
Tsith-Zuka, Neekhuj, Deekzash, Shekhuj, Tuk-Wuxal, Jul-Kaan, Seil-Shaa, Xal-Tsona, Naheesh-Meer*
are names, and repetition is genuinely low (the commonest first element appears 3 times in 186).
Three things I can hear, in increasing order of seriousness:

**(i) The `x` is laid on far too thick, and the item told the round to do it.** RI-LOR04 comparison
method §5 demands *"at least 40% [of Jel-classified names] must contain `x`"*; the round hit 42%
(39.2% over all generated Argonians) and cleared it. But **the attested corpus this project mined
itself carries `x` in 48 of 324 Jel-shaped names — 14.8%** (`tools/uesp/mine-argonian-names.mjs`,
re-derived today). The pre-fix roster was at 4%, which was too flavourless; the fix overshot to
nearly three times attested, and it did so *because the item's own §5 threshold contradicts the
item's own §4 table*. **This is an item defect and the round followed the item correctly.** It
should be fixed in RI-LOR04, not blamed on the builder — but the roster does now rasp:
*Ixuxith, Thuxis-Uxith, Anxthari, Wuxshaneekh, Wuxvakh, Ixvakh, Jix-Ixt* in one sample of fifty.

**(ii) The Tamrielic-facing names are a cross-product, and a third of them do not land.**
`namegen.mjs descriptiveCandidates()` is every verb × noun and verb × determiner × noun from the
lexicon's sanctioned inventories — which is why every one is *legal*. RI-LOR04's `register_rule` is
not a legality rule, it is a semantic one: *"something an adult once said ABOUT a hatchling, or the
thing that person is known for doing badly."* `Hides-No-Coin`, `Sleeps-Eggs`, `Wades-Rain` and
`Buries-Nets` clear it. **Three of the twelve are not English** — `Sleeps-Doors`, `Bites-Ledgers`,
`Paints-Flies` — and a fourth, `Hides-Many-Names`, is closer to the title register the item forbids
than to the domestic one it wants. This is the item's own *How we lose* #4 — *"Rules produce validity, not character"* — landing
on the Tamrielic half rather than the Jel half. It is 12 names, so it is a small deduction, and the
remedy is small too: hand-pick the ~20 descriptive names the roster needs instead of drawing them.

**(iii) Eight villagers are named on words that are not naming stock, and this one is a breach.**
`jelCompoundCandidates()` is an unfiltered 75 × 74 root cross-product, and `ROOTS` includes the
lexicon's semantically loaded entries. RI-LOR04 §1: *"Attested exceptions (exempt because canon
predates our rules; **nothing new may be coined on their pattern**): `kaoc, naga, hist, jel,
lukiul`."*

```
  Lukiul-Skei, Lukiul-Teekh   lukiul = "salted one; an Argonian assimilated to foreign ways" — a slur
  Xan-Jel                     jel    = the root-tongue itself. A person called "Stone-Language".
  Shal-Naga, Wux-Naga         naga   = the serpent-form of the people, not a root
  Haj-Saxhleel                saxhleel = the Argonians' own word for Argonian. "Hidden-Argonian".
  Histlith, Shaa-Hist         hist   = the tree
```

Every one passes `jel-phonotactics.py`, because phonotactics is about sound. **A phonotactic
validator cannot catch a semantic error and this round has no other filter.** The remedy is a
`no_coin` flag on those five roots in `jel-lexicon.json` and one line in `jelCompoundCandidates()`.

## 4. RI-LOR04's validator (D) — the correction is upheld, and the band is not stuck

right.** `corpus/80-methods/jel-phonotactics.py` exists, takes `--culture jel`, and self-tests
33/33 today. What round 3 actually found — and phrased as absence — is that nothing could **band**

lesson: run the python's own harvester over `game/data` and it checks 2,463 "names" at 74.7%

wrong cannot be banded.** `lor04-validate.mjs` supplies the population and delegates every per-name

band ≥ 4; that is not the same question. `tools/lore/critic-w1-23-r4-band.mjs` asks it of the

## 7. The single biggest remaining gap

**Two placement mechanisms put objects into the same room without either one knowing where the
other's are, and four documents — one of them half of a registered contradiction pair — are placed,
drawn, standable-at, and open nothing when a player presses the button at them.**

`render/interior.js` allocates a wall slot per `readable[]` entry with a 2.6 m minimum separation
**computed over that list and nothing else**. `game/data/world/readables/site-marks.json` puts
twenty-five quest documents into interiors at **hard-coded coordinates**, by a different route.
`engine.js:3270` gives the interact press to the nearest prop inside its reach. Put a book on a
wall slot 0.50–0.90 m from a site-mark and the site-mark wins, every time, forever. Measured, with
two controls that opened, in §1a.

**Why this is the biggest one and not the naming band.** The naming band is the axis that pins the
score, and its remedy is cheaper (§7a). But this is the defect that is *invisible*, and invisibility
is what this project keeps dying of. `place-library --check` is green. The substring reach test says
163 of 163. `boot-check` and `check-content` are green. Nineteen of nineteen pairs report both sides
reachable. My own twelve chosen legs were 12/12. **Every instrument in the tree, including two I
wrote, says this is fine.** Only walking all forty-five documents in the loaded rooms found it, and
the same collision will silently eat the next piece's placements too, because nothing checks it.
`a-short-account-of-the-pacification` is one half of the drowning-at-the-ford dispute, which is to
say: the round's headline claim has a counter-example in it that nobody could have seen.

**Remedy, concrete and buildable, in the file that already owns the arithmetic:**

1. **Make the allocator aware of the other mechanism.** `render/interior.js`'s `farEnough(s)` tests
   a candidate slot against `taken`, the readables placed so far. Seed `taken` with every
   `site-marks.json` entry whose `at.interior` is this room, before the loop — the coordinates are
   static data in a file that is already loaded, so this is a read and a concat, not a new system.
   The same for `unique_item` and `containers`, which are placed by the same function a few lines
   above and have the same problem waiting.
2. **Arm it where a check can see it**, because a separation rule with no test is how this happened.
   Extend `tools/lore/place-library.mjs --check` — or better, `tools/check-content.mjs`, which runs
   for everybody — with the assertion my census makes: for every interior carrying a `readable`, no
   two interactable props within `min(reach_m)` of each other. That is a pure-data check; it needs
   no browser and it would have failed on this tree today.
