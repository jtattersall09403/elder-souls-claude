## 1. THE SINGLE BIGGEST GAP

> **`GAP-W1-bloodstain-invisible-from-most-bearings`**
> `subsystem_path: combat.death.corpserun` · severity **blocking** · size **M**

`RI-JRN06` M-D14 requires the bloom to be visible at 12 m in daylight and at 6 m in the dark. The
check shoots each viewpoint **twice** — at the bloom and turned 180° away — so a detector firing on
the marsh instead of on the bloom shows as a non-zero control. It does not: the control is 195 px
at every single bearing, and in every failing view the bloom frame is **also exactly 195 px**.

| Condition | Bearings where the bloom is drawn |
|---|---|
| daylight, 12 m | **1 of 8** (135° only) |
| dark, 6 m | **3 of 8** (0°, 45°, 315°) |

The bloom carries 4,200 souls at `[3834.57, 2.97, 3959.5]`. Walk up to it from 90°, 135°, 180°,
225° or 270° **in the dark, from six metres**, and the frame is pixel-identical to the frame with
your back to it.

**Why this is the gap and not the runner-up.** Everything else this piece protects is bookkeeping
that holds: the souls are conserved to the unit, the stain is single, the world does not soften,
nothing fast-travels to your corpse. All of it exists to make **one walk** matter. `RI-PRG04` §6
and `RI-JRN06`'s run-back block are about a player crossing ground they now know to reach a light
on the floor. There is no light on the floor from most directions. Round 1 could not see this
because it measured the bloom from one bearing; the round-2 CONSUMPTION artifact could not see it
because it used **`bloom_px: 195`** as its *recovered-state null control* — the same 195 that this
check reads as "nothing rendered". The number was in both artifacts and meant the opposite thing in
each.

**Remedy.** The bloom is drawn by `syncDeathMarkers()`. Whatever it emits is either single-sided,
sunk into the heightfield, or occluded by the marsh reed instancing at grazing angles. Make it a
camera-facing billboard with a vertical emissive component, or raise its origin above the local
ground normal, and re-run M-D14.

**Acceptance.** `m_d14_stain_visibility` reports **8/8 daylight at 12 m and 8/8 dark at 6 m**, with
the in-row 180°-away control still at its floor at every bearing, inside a full
`journey-run.mjs --journey jrn06-death` run rather than a standalone probe — and the same run's
`m_d8_second_death` goes green.

## 4. Souls now have a sink. On the state the whole round was measured in, the sink is a hole.

This is the runner-up gap and it is the one that makes round 2's headline hollow.

`tools/harness/critic-w1-13-r2-b.mjs`, two arms, one browser, identical frames, spends driven
through the real input path (arm the preview, confirm — `RI-UIX03` L6):

| Arm | VIGOUR ×8 | ENDURANCE ×8 | WILLPOWER ×8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`no-character`** — the shipped `default` state | 5,418 souls · `hp_max` **620 → 620** | 11,661 souls · `stamina_max` **120 → 120** | 20,573 souls · `focus_max` **94 → 94** |
| **`with-character`** — `setCharacter()` first | 5,418 souls · `hp_max` **300 → 508** | 11,661 souls · `stamina_max` **104 → 130** | 20,573 souls · `focus_max` **30 → 56** |

**The control goes green.** The instrument works. 37,652 souls bought nothing at all in the first
arm, and `sim._poolsDirty` is left **`true` forever** — the flag that says "re-derive the pools"
is set on every level and never cleared, because `Engine.applyDerivedPools()` opens
`const ch = this.sim.character; if (!ch) return null;`.

`sim.character` is null in every named state, every scenario, and **the shipped `default` state the
round-2 builder used for its entire levelling demonstration.** So *"spend through the real input
path, level 1→2, souls 4,200→3,782 = exactly `soulsToNextLevel()`"* is true, and it is arithmetic:
the souls left the purse and the character did not change. The `applyDerivedPools()` header comment
describes this failure as already fixed once — *"round 1 composed all of this correctly and then
wrote none of it anywhere the fight could see, which is why VIGOUR 6 and VIGOUR 18 both reported
`hp_max` 620"*. **VIGOUR 18 reports `hp_max` 620.**

### And the screen is offering attributes the character does not have

`probe-a1.json`, read off the **drawn elements** (`levelup.attr.<id>`), not off the data file:

```
STRENGTH 12   ENDURANCE 20   AGILITY 10   SPEED 10   VIGOUR 10
WILLPOWER 10  INTELLECT 10   HIST-BOND 10 PERSONALITY 10  LUCK 10
```

The character carries `{vigour 10, endurance 20, strength 12, dexterity 12, intelligence 10,
faith 10}`.

- **Seven rows** — agility, speed, willpower, intellect, hist-bond, personality, luck — are for
  attributes the character does not have. Each draws a constant `10`. Confirming one **mints the
  key at 11**: `_spendSouls(attrId)` is `prog.attributes[attrId] = (prog.attributes[attrId] || 10) + 1`
  with no validation against any set.
- **Three of the character's real attributes** — dexterity 12, intelligence 10, faith 10 — are
  **not on the screen** and cannot be raised at all.
- The screen's own preview for `speed` and `agility` reads **`derived: []`**. It promises nothing
  and delivers nothing. Across the whole build, `speed` is named by **no** pool, no reaction, no
  faction gate and no quest requirement — the one attribute that buys literally nothing.

**Two of the round-2 builder's own three headline spends were `agility` (hearth-lilmoth) and
`speed` (hearth-gideon)** — both recorded `pass: true` with `souls_matches_cost: true`, and both
minted a key at 11 that nothing in the game reads.

**Remedy (small).** Derive the pools from `progression.attributes` unconditionally and apply the
birthsign terms only when `sim.character` exists; and refuse `openMenu('levelup')` — or drive the
row list off the character's own map — when the two vocabularies do not match. `RI-PRG04` §1's *"the
only place levelling is possible"* is satisfied by a door that opens; it is not satisfied by a room
that takes the money.

## 5. Respawn drift 0.00 m at 6 of 6: the fix moved the measurement, not the body

The brief asked which. It is the measurement, and the perturbation is unambiguous.
`critic-w1-13-r2-d.mjs` D2, four arms per well, same run:

| Well | released | **held** | released, `traversal.reset()` stubbed | **held, `traversal.reset()` stubbed** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `hearth-archon` | 0.00 m | **9.78 m** | 0.00 m | **9.78 m** |
| `hearth-stormhold` | 0.00 m | **13.97 m** | 0.00 m | **13.97 m** |
| `hearth-gideon` | 0.00 m | *(12.86 m, three-well run)* | 0.00 m | — |

*(offset from the basin, 220 frames after the respawn frame)*

**Removing the line round 2 added changes nothing, to the digit.** `_afterRespawnPlacement()` calls
`traversal.reset()`; stubbing it out leaves 1.57 / 9.78 / 8.21 and 2.24 / 13.97 / 11.73 **exactly**
where they were. With the move axis released there is no velocity to reset, so the line is inert;
with it held the body re-accelerates on the next frame regardless. Round 2's 0.00 m at 6 of 6 is a
property of the probe letting go of the stick.

Round 1's finding therefore **stands unfixed**: a player killed while running — which is how most
players die — is 1.6–2.2 m off the basin on the respawn frame and 8–14 m away 3.7 seconds later
without touching the controller again. `RI-PRG04` D6's *"they are standing at the HEARTH"* is still
true for one frame. This is not a fabrication charge — the builder measured what it measured and
reported it honestly — but the round closed a finding it had not closed.

## 10. What round 2 actually earned, so a second FAIL does not erase it

The named gap of round 1 is **closed and it stays closed**: `HearthSystem.atHearth()` exists, the
screen opens at a walked-to basin with no `setAtHearth()` anywhere in the path, is refused 48.5 m
off it, and the spend survives a save/load audited on the live world. The mid-death save that
destroyed 4,200 souls is fixed across **nine** save moments, not one. `_inferCause` reads a lethal
cause the traversal system now writes, so the fall rule fires with the bloom 0.00 m from the ledge
and 63.64 m from the floor. The fog gate no longer degenerates at its exact centre. `M-D7` was
written from nothing and passes in the aggregate with an in-row control. `M-D4` went from a stale
volatile list to 4 seeds with a control that deliberately moves a bounty and a faction rank across
a death. The `WORLD-RUNS-ON-ENTRY` gate is **the best thing in the round** — I tried to make it lie
and it refused — and my own run proves it was needed, because `journey-run.mjs` really does hand
`jrn06` an open inventory screen.

And the builder filed two defects it did not fix, in its own status file, in plain words, rather
than shipping the passing number. One of them turned out to be a field bigger than it said.

What it did not do is look behind the door it opened. The souls come out of the purse and nothing
on the character moves; the screen lists ten attributes and the character has six; and the thing
the whole loop exists to walk back to cannot be seen from most of the directions you would walk
in from.
