output `game/data/dialogue/topics/main-quest-argument.json` — the twelve-topic conversation with
Ixtu-Meer, the Steward of the Count, that Q-MAIN-26/27/28 declare and that did not exist until this
piece wrote it. Judged against `RI-EXP05` LH6/LH7/LH8 (named by the handover as the thing a fresh

(disposition bands), `RI-CHR02` §5 (race gates the conversation) — all named in the file's own

**How I reached the scene.** Nobody reaches Act V by playing right now: `orchestration/status/
W1-VIABILITY-SPLIT.json` records a stratified walk of 40 characters, granted nothing, all 40
stopping at Q-MAIN-06 (`res_the_number`/`res_the_fair_copy` both refuse with "you do not know
rev_the_curve_predates" — `QuestEngine.reveal()` has no world-side caller). That is `W1-19`'s /
`W1-VIABILITY-SPLIT`'s defect, not this piece's, and I did not try to fix it. Two routes in:
(1) direct reading of the shipped JSON, and (2) the piece's own instrument,
`tools/quests/act5-argument-probe.mjs`, which places the player in front of Ixtu-Meer with
`H.spawnNPC({from_record: 'ixtu-meer'})` — a harness bypass the tool's own header admits is

declares `"npcs": [], "spawn": []` and relies on `populateSite()` reading `post.site` off his NPC
record instead. I did not re-run that probe in a fresh browser: `pgrep -c headless_shell` sat at
18–30 for this entire session (loadavg 10–14, against the project's stated cap of ~8), so per
`orchestration/RULES.md` rule 21 I did the work that needed no browser instead. What I built in its
place is not a re-implementation: it is a Node script (`corpus/90-verdicts/wave1/artifacts/
W1-17-act5-r1/gen-converse-node-repro.mjs`) that imports and calls the **actual shipped**
`game/src/character/converse.js` — `buildTopicIndex()` and `infoFor()`, the exact functions

`game/data/npcs/mainline.json`. That is the production code path, executed, not read.

## 1. Judged as writing

Ixtu-Meer answers; RI-DLG08's tonal-range exemption lets him run long, and the file's own
`prose_register_exemption` claims zero questions and zero exclamations as *characterisation* rather
than as evasion of the corpus's known defect (four question marks against Morrowind's 76 across ten
trials, a hundred punchlines in a row where Morrowind is flat). I checked whether that claim is
earned rather than convenient.

**`node tools/check-prose.mjs --file game/data/dialogue/topics/main-quest-argument.json`** (full
output: `artifacts/check-prose-output.txt`):

```
questions 2.8 < floor 39.8   — EXEMPT (declared, and only one instance in the whole file)
exclamations 0.0 < floor 5.3 — EXEMPT
andopen 5.5 < floor 16.0     — EXEMPT
contractions 0.0 < floor 43.8 — EXEMPT
sting 0.00 /10k   (reference 3.42) — NOT waivable, and it is clean anyway
fragopen 0.00 /10k (reference 0.08) — NOT waivable, and it is clean anyway
```

`sting` and `fragopen` are the two bars the file's own `prose_register_exemption_note` admits are
never waivable "from here" — they were fixed in the prose, the note says, not declared away. They
measure clean at 0.00, below the corpus-wide reference rate, which is the opposite of the corpus's
documented failure mode (every line landing a punchline). I counted by hand rather than trust the
tool once: across the file's 36 `x` strings there is exactly **one** question mark, and it is not a
dodge — it is Ixtu-Meer posing his own rhetorical question and answering it himself
(`whose-count-are-you`: *"Whose count are you? — There is one being kept on you."*), which is the
voice_note's claim ("he does not ask questions back") demonstrated rather than asserted. The
exemption is earned, not gamed.

**Does the other party have a case?** Yes, and it survives more scrutiny than a first read gives it
credit for. The three verified claims (`the-three-things-i-checked-myself`) are not decoration —
each resolves to a real quest resolution (`game/data/quests/mainline-act2.json` Q-MAIN-06/08,
`mainline-act3.json` Q-MAIN-12) whose `consequences.world_flags` sets exactly the flag the topic
gates on, confirmed by tracing `game/src/sim/quest/machine.js:762` (`for (const wf of
c.world_flags...) q.flags[wf]=1`). He does not merely assert his innocence; he restates the
player's own evidence back to them and stops: *"So I am not going to argue with you about any of
it."* That is `LH7`'s bar met by construction, not by a line that sounds humble.

The stronger case, though, is the one a player is more likely to actually weigh: **the allowance
argument**. *"Close the sill-gate entirely and every well in the province goes cold within the
season... Open it entirely and the wells will have all of it at once and then have nothing, ever,
because there will be nothing left to allow."* This is not a strawman kept-in-place-for-fear
position — the game backs it with a real cost on the "good" ending: `res_open_the_count`'s own
`outcome` text says wells "reissue slower and hold less... for everyone, permanently," and its
`world_flags` include `wells_reissue_degraded_province_wide` and `tithe_gourd_charges_reduced`
alongside `the_court_is_vindicated`. A reasonable player who values the status quo over an uncertain
better future has a textually supported reason to take `res_take_his_place` or `res_walk_out`
instead, and both are non-violent, and neither is framed by any later text as the wrong choice — see
§3 below.

Two structural choices earn real weight here. First, `why-it-is-filling-now`'s refusal —
*"I will not answer that one... you may write down that I refused... I would rather be recorded as a
man who declined than as a man who explained"* — is the one place he is evasive, and the game lets
that sit as a legitimate reason for suspicion rather than resolving it; RI-EXP05 does not require
every question answered, only that the ones he does answer are not refuted. Second, the Recension
reveal (`the-ninth-clause-of-the-drowned-tally`, d20) — *"I have never written in the Recension...
If the clause was made, it was not made down here, and I am the only person who could have made it
down here... it serves either argument exactly as well as the other"* — is a genuinely neutral fact,
verified by reading it against `mainline.json`'s own `adjudication_audit` ("No quest, book, NPC line
or ending... states whether the ninth clause is genuine"). It does not tip the scale either way, and
the file says so about itself, correctly.

## 2. LH8 — no refutation, re-checked independently rather than trusted

The item's own `refutation_audit` claims to have checked "all 32 quests' journal entries, all
resolution outcomes, all ending world flags, the aftermath quest." I did not take that on faith. I
read the four Act V ending resolutions (`res_open_the_count`, `res_take_his_place`, `res_kill_him`,
`res_walk_out`) and the backpath's equivalent (`mainline-backpath.json`) directly:

- `res_hear_him_out`: *"Nothing is settled and nobody is refuted."*
- `res_press_him_on_the_clause`: *"This neither proves nor disproves anything... the last word
  anyone in this game offers on it."*
- The backpath, reached after the player has already half-disproven his position by force: *"The
  conversation is the same conversation. He makes the same case, **which is still good**, and this
  time I have already done half of what he is arguing against and he knows it and he makes it
  anyway."*
- `res_kill_him`'s outcome text does not vindicate the killing — it describes the province getting
  "a slower and less predictable version of the same failure with nobody at the ledger," which if
  anything argues he was right to worry.

No hit anywhere for a narrator, ally, journal, or post-ending line asserting he was simply wrong.
**LH8 holds**, independently re-derived, not merely re-quoted from the piece's own audit.

## 3. LH6/LH7 — the topic count and the knowledge gates, against every id in `game/data/dialogue/topics/`

Every one of the twelve ids in `main-quest-argument.json`'s `topics[]` — `the-steward-of-the-count`,
`the-count-itself`, `the-chain-on-the-wall`, `the-allowance`, `the-three-things-i-checked-myself`,
`why-it-is-filling-now`, `how-the-count-opens`, `the-ninth-clause-of-the-drowned-tally`, `the-sill`,
`the-opening-of-the-count`, `whose-count-are-you`, `what-does-the-marsh-owe-you` — resolves against
the merged index `buildTopicIndex()` builds from the whole `game/data/dialogue/topics/` tree, and
each returns non-empty text for the signatures I drove (§4 below shows one exception in the text
that comes back, not in whether text comes back at all). Four are knowledge-gated on real flags
wired to real quest resolutions (traced in the opening section above), satisfying LH6's `>= 12
topics, >= 4 knowledge-gated`. **LH6 and LH7 both hold**, structurally, and driven against the real
index rather than read off the JSON's own `knowledge_gated` block.

## 4. The gap: the conversation's own opening line is unreachable for most of the cast

This is where "every topic id against `game/data/dialogue/topics/`" stops being a formality. Running
`tools/dialogue/build-graph.mjs`, I found `game/data/dialogue/topics/50-mainline.json` **also**
declares a topic record with id `the-steward-of-the-count` — the exact id `main-quest-argument.json`
uses for Q-MAIN-26's entry topic, and one of three ids the two files share (`the-sill` and
`the-opening-of-the-count` are the others: `artifacts/opens-by-topic-scan.json`). The handover
explicitly claims otherwise: *"main-quest-argument.json introduces no duplicate id."* That claim is
false, and it matters, because `converse.js buildTopicIndex()` **merges** same-id topic records
across files by design (the comment at line 79 says so) and scores the merged infos by specificity
— it does not prefer the "real" file.

`50-mainline.json`'s entry for `the-steward-of-the-count` is an old stub: `d: 20`, no race filter,
spoken in the third person about Ixtu-Meer rather than by him — *"Ixtu-Meer. He has kept the Count
since before the ninth recension and he has been waiting for a visitor to come down and ask him one
honest question about it."* `main-quest-argument.json`'s warmblood-specific greeting for the same
topic has no `d` field at all (it is written as an ungated `forbids: {race:[saxhleel,naga,dunmer]}`
fallback). `infoFor()`'s specificity score is `matchesActor(8) + cell + requires(4) + dScore +
forbids(0.5)`; the stub scores 8 + 1.2 (from `d:20`) = 9.2, the intended line scores 8 + 0.5 = 8.5.
**The eleven-year-old stub outscores the just-written scene by 0.7 and wins**, for every player whose
disposition clears 20 and whose race is not saxhleel/naga/dunmer.

Reproduced directly against the shipped engine module (`artifacts/converse-node-repro.json`), using
the piece's *own documented* eleven-signature disposition table:

| signature (from the file's own `derived_disposition_worked`) | disposition | delivered |
|---|---|---|
| blackrose Khajiit | 36 | **stub** ("Ixtu-Meer. He has kept the Count...") |
| foreign-born Nord | 30 | **stub** |
| foreign-born Imperial | 24 | **stub** |
| interior Saxhleel, interior Naga, lukiul Saxhleel, interior Dunmer, lukiul Nord/Imperial, foreign-born/lukiul Dunmer | — | main-quest-argument.json text (correct) |

Two of those three broken rows — foreign-born Imperial and blackrose Khajiit — are two of the **six
signatures the piece's own `tools/quests/act5-argument-probe.mjs` tests** (`reports/runs/
W1-17-ACT5-ARGUMENT.json`), and it reported `"ok": true, "failures": []`. It didn't catch this
because its only cross-signature check compares the two extreme dispositions (interior-saxhleel vs
lukiul-dunmer, `standing_delta`), both of which are saxhleel/dunmer and therefore immune, and because
every other check asks only "is the topic offered, and is *some* text returned" — never "is the
returned text the one authored for this scene." `build-graph.mjs`'s own unreachable-INFO lint (RI-
DLG01 step 4) doesn't catch it either, because it only flags a strict filter superset; this is a
cross-file id collision plus a specificity-score win, a different failure shape the lint was never
built to see. `check-quests.mjs` and `check-content.mjs` both pass clean, because neither reads
`infoFor()`'s output — only its existence.

Set against RI-DLG01's own scoring rule — *"Any orphan, or any unreachable INFO → FAIL, automatic,
no score"* — this is exactly that: `main-quest-argument.json`'s authored entry line for roughly a
majority of the race roster (7 of 10 playable races are outside {saxhleel, naga, dunmer}; every one
of them clears disposition 20 under most upbringings) is unreachable by any input a player can give.
The climax's first line, race-specific and carefully written, is invisible in play for most
characters and replaced by a flat, out-of-voice line that refers to the speaker in the third person.

**A second, related but currently dormant risk**, worth recording precisely rather than either
inflating or dropping: the actor id `a: "rootkeeper"` this file uses for every info — the engine_notes
claim it is *"ixtu-meer's actor slot... nothing in this file can be spoken by anyone else"* — is
shared by 25+ other NPC records across `game/data/npcs/*.json` (every settlement's generic
well-keeper archetype). Two of them, `keeper-ei-vashan` and `gallery-intake-keepers`, already carry
two of this file's twelve topic ids (`the sill`, `the opening of the count`) in their own `topics`
array, and reproducing `infoFor()` for them returns Ixtu-Meer's first-person climactic text
verbatim — *"Before you ask me that you should go and hear the ones who will have to live in the
answer..."* — spoken by a different, minor NPC. Today neither carries a `settlement` or `post.site`,
so `populateSite()`/`populateSettlement()` never spawns them and the leak is inert. But
`game/data/quests/mainline-act5.json`'s own `deceit.revealed_by` names `gallery-intake-keepers` as
the `talk_to_target` source of `rev_the_three_accounts` — the record is written to be reachable, not
decorative — and the state file these two would be placed by (`stone-wastes-count.json`) carries the
header *"owner: W1-GIVER-PRESENCE — GAP-W1-quest-givers-not-in-the-world,"* an actively worked area
of exactly this kind of placement fix. The exclusivity claim in the file's own engine_notes is not
enforced by any code path; it is presently true by the coincidence that two sibling records happen to
be unplaced.

## Arbitration

**AR-1 (Souls-authoritative combat).** Pass. No dice, no roll-to-hit, no combat quantity anywhere in
this file; the whole conversation is out-of-combat per S13 by construction (no `Voice`-typed record,
no `AddTopic`/`ModDisposition`/`Journal` call from a hostile actor).

**AR-2 (no markers, directions in prose).** Pass. `the-sill` and `the-opening-of-the-count` give
walking directions in prose only (*"Through the arch at the far end and down eleven steps..."*) —
no marker token, no coordinate, no UI string. The topic is not offered from a greeting tier, so it is
not a menu-quest / AR-2 violation either.

The number is lower than the writing deserves and that is deliberate: RI-EXP05's zero is item-owned

averaged against three genuinely-earned passes elsewhere.

## Biggest gap

**`GAP-W1-act5-argument-entry-shadowed`** — see the `.json` verdict for the full record. In short:
`the-steward-of-the-count` (Q-MAIN-26's own entry topic) is declared in two files sharing one id;
`converse.js` merges them by design; the eleven-year-old stub in `50-mainline.json` outscores the
just-authored, race-specific greeting for any player whose race is outside {saxhleel, naga, dunmer}
and whose disposition clears 20 — reproducibly 3 of the file's own 11 documented signatures, 2 of
which are in the piece's own shipped probe's test set, which still reported `ok: true`.

`the-steward-of-the-count`/`the-sill`/`the-opening-of-the-count` stub entries (their content is fully

assert the info actually delivered to each of the eleven documented signatures is one of *this
file's own* infos, not a merged-in stranger's — `build-graph.mjs`'s existing unreachable-INFO lint
does not see this failure shape and needs a sibling check for it. (d) Either give `keeper-ei-vashan`
