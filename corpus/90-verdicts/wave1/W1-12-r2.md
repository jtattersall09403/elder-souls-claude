## 2. The round overruled its own critic, and it was right — but not for the reason it published

Round 1 §11 prescribed gating the sprint on **closure**, `d(dist)/dt`. Round 2 built it, measured
it, rejected it, and shipped a rule that measures the **target's recession** instead — the player's
own displacement projected onto the line between them, which does not move when the enemy sprints.
Its published argument is a four-line frame trace showing a 37-frame RUSH/APPROACH oscillation
stalling at ~4.1 m.

**I rebuilt all of it. The trace is right.** My own closure arm, `inf_trash`, player walking away
at 2.0 m/s from 12 m:

```
f  28 RUSH        dist 12.93   (+28 f)
f  63 APPROACH    dist 11.92   (+35 f)
f  89 RUSH        dist 11.73   (+26 f)
f 101 APPROACH    dist 11.33   (+12 f)     ... a 38-frame cycle, 26 in RUSH and 12 in APPROACH,
f 127 RUSH        dist 11.14   (+26 f)         repeating unchanged for 491 frames
...
f 519 APPROACH    dist  4.92
f 534 CIRCLE      dist  4.76               ... and from here it never gets closer than 2.72 m
```

12.93 m → 4.92 m in 491 frames is 0.98 m/s of net closure — the average of a 4.6 sprint and a 2.2
walk against a 2.0 player, exactly as the round says — and then it stalls between 4.2 and 4.8 m.
The round's "twelve frames later" is precisely the APPROACH leg of my cycle. Verified.

**I then tested the confound the round did not, and the cure it did not.** Round 1's remedy is
written for the APPROACH site only; the shipped rule also fires from CIRCLE, so "closure vs
recession" and "one site vs two" were confounded in the round's comparison. And a control loop
whose input is its own output has a standard cure — hysteresis — which the round did not try.
Same tree, same seed, one predicate apart:

| arm | flee min gap | frames in strike | M3 on the item's fixture |
|---|---|---|---|
| **shipped** — recession, both sites | **1.612 m** | **294** | 0.4373 |
| recession, APPROACH only (= the round's arm E) | 1.795 m | 28 | — |
| closure, APPROACH only (round 1's literal remedy) | 3.314 m | **0** | — |
| **closure, both sites** (the fair comparison) | 2.720 m | **0** | **0.7448** |
| **closure + 30 f hysteresis on RUSH** | **0.900 m** | 144 | **0.7064** |

Two conclusions, and only one of them is the round's.

1. **The CIRCLE half is not the confound.** Closure with both sites still never enters the strike
   band. The round's rejection survives the fair comparison.
2. **The round's stated proof is the weaker half of its case.** Hysteresis cures the oscillation
   and *does* catch the walking player — 0.90 m, better than the shipped rule. What kills closure
   is not that it oscillates; it is that **closure is ~0 against a player who is standing still**,
   so the rule fires in the one fixture RI-AI01 M3 is built on and doubles the chase-bot metric:
   0.4373 → 0.7448, or 0.7064 with the oscillation cured. The round asserted this in one clause
   (*"against a stationary player recession is 0 and none of this fires"*) and never measured it.
   It is the whole argument and it is worth more than the frame trace.

**A builder overruling its critic was the best thing in this round.** The finding against it is
that it published the anecdote and kept the proof in its head.

## 6. S22: the ruling survives its contrary case, on better grounds than the round gave

The round ruled RI-AI01's bare `f` is `f@60`, from "three of the item's own seconds/frame pairs
against one contrary". I read the contrary case, and **the ruling stands but the margin does not.**

The round's own criterion is an identity between a row's *condition* (in seconds) and its *min
dwell* column (in frames). Applied consistently:

| row | condition | min dwell | implies | strength |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T06 | 12.0 s in SEARCH | 720 f | 60 Hz | **strong** — dwell and condition are the same clock |
| T04 | held 1.5 s | 90 f | 60 Hz | **strong** — same structure |
| **T01** | **2.0 s ±0.5 idle elapsed** | **60 f** | **30 Hz** | **strong — and it is the same structure as the two above** |
| T03 | alert < 20 for 2.0 s | 90 f | 45 Hz | inconsistent either way |
| T14 | 36 f elapsed | 36 f, "+0.6 s cooldown" | 60 Hz | **weak** — the cooldown is a *third* quantity, applied after the transition, and the item nowhere says it equals the 36-frame window |

So the honest tally is **two strong for 60 Hz, one strong for 30 Hz, one internally inconsistent,
and one inference that is not the same kind of pair at all** — not three to one. The round counted
T14 as a peer of T04 and T06, and demoted T01 to "evidence against" while classing the structurally
identical T03 as an item defect.

**The ruling is nonetheless right, and the decisive evidence is textual, in the item's first
paragraph of §A, and the round did not cite it:**

> All seven AI reference items compute over the same per-frame JSONL dump, one record per live
> enemy per simulation frame, **60 Hz fixed step**.

The item declares its own clock before it uses a single `f`. Every frame count in §D and §F counts
the frames §A defines. T01 and T03 are then simply defects in the min-dwell column — which is how
the round already classified T03, and how it should have classified T01 instead of counting it
against itself. **Upheld. Reversible on the same evidence the round named**: a rebase of RI-AI01
that anchors T05 in seconds. The consequence is unchanged — `aggro_entry_frames [20, 30]` is
0.33–0.50 s, ships correct, and sits 16 ms above RI-CMB12 §A's 317 ms reactability floor.

The rest of the S22 work is the strongest axis in the round. A document-level `unit`, a
machine-readable `units` block covering every dimensioned leaf, and `check-ai-units.mjs` with a
`--self-test` that injects three defects and asserts all three are caught. It found one on its
first run. I ran it: exit 0 on the shipped tree.

## 11. The single biggest gap

**`GAP-W1-12-m3-chase-bot-dwell-on-the-items-own-fixture`** — `combat.enemy.statemachine`,
severity **blocking**.

On RI-AI01 M3's own fixture — one enemy aggroed, the player holding position and never attacking
for 60 s — `min_dist_dwell` is 0.3183 to 0.4373 across the fighting roster, against a PASS of 0.10
and a **chase-bot hard fail of 0.35 that six of seven statblocks cross**. The cause is measured,
not guessed: on a passive player the enemy is in COMMIT or RECOVER for 1,896 of 3,600 frames, every
attack carries 0.6–1.2 m of forward root motion, and the AI cannot steer during the swing — so it
finishes each attack standing inside its own strike band and stays there for the whole recovery
(85% of recovery frames are inside 0.85·Ω). The number rises with duration at every start range.

**Why it matters:** the player learns the rhythm of the approach before the moveset, and an enemy
that spends 44% of its non-swinging time inside its own reach against someone who is not even
fighting back has no DANCE band from the player's chair, whatever the trace calls the states.

**Remedy — two levers, and the piece owns the first.**

1. **In `game/src/combat/ai.js`, re-space during `RECOVER`, not only after it.** `REPOSITION`
   already runs a sprint back-off toward `circle.preferred_band_multiple`; `RECOVER` currently
   only renames the leaf. Root motion is the body's and must not be cancelled (that is the
   commitment property, and it must survive), but the frames after `hit_active` ends are steerable
   in principle and are 85%-inside-band in practice. Declare the back-off's start frame as a leaf
   so the census can see it.
2. **In the corpus, fix M3's denominator** — "while the enemy is in the startup or active frames of
   an attack" rather than "while `state != COMMIT`" — as the round-1 critic proposed in §14.3 and
   this round endorsed. Filed again below. Until it moves, the item as written is the bar.

**Acceptance.** `node tools/combat/critic-w1-12-r2-m3-chart.mjs` shows `min_dist_dwell ≤ 0.35` for
**every** fighting statblock on the item's own fixture at 1800, 3600 and 5400 frames and at start
ranges 3, 6, 10 and 15 m — **and** `node tools/combat/w1-12-r2-teardown.mjs` shows
`flee_frames_inside_strike` no lower than round 2's base row for any statblock. The second half is
not optional: M3 can be passed by making the enemy timid, and that would undo the one thing round 2
got right.
