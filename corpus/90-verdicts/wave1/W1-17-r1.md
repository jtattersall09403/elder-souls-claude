**Verdict: FAIL. Score 5.0 (min over axes), wave-1 gate 7.0.**
Measured at `6c979e3` **working tree** — W1-17 is uncommitted, so every number below is a claim
about the tree as it stood, not about a commit (RULES 12; the builder recorded the same caveat).

## The short version

**The writing is the best in this project and a tenth of it cannot be heard.**

I went looking for the failure this piece is most likely to have — a flat topic menu with a
Morrowind skin — and did not find it. The graph is deep, the filters are real, the towns disagree
with each other, and the prose belongs to speakers rather than to a narrator. I then tried twice
to catch the author's hand showing through six mouths, with instruments built for the purpose, and
**both attempts came back refuted**. That is a genuine result and it is recorded below at the same
weight as the failures.

What fails is the join between that writing and the world. Three numbers:

| | builder reports | this critic measures |
|---|---|---|
| Unreachable INFOs | **3** | **133** (10.4% of 1,280) |
| Orphan topics | **0** (+23 "npc-advertised") | **23** — RI-DLG01's own definition, `≥1 = fail` |
| Archetypes with a speaker in the world | 6 | **5** |

The 44× gap on the first row is not carelessness. It is a semantics mismatch nobody has named:
**RI-DLG01 §A mandates first-match-wins and `converse.js infoFor()` scores instead.** Every
instrument in the round — the lint, the reorder fix, the shadow audit, the census that blessed it —
is built on the rule the engine does not follow.

## C. The graph — where it actually fails

### C1. The lint and the engine implement different rules

RI-DLG01 §A: *"first-match-wins in authored file order … It does **not** score specificity …
our implementation keeps first-match-wins semantics."*

`game/src/character/converse.js:284`:

```js
const score = (matchesActor ? 8 : 0) + (info.cell ? 2 : 0) + (info.requires ? 4 : 0) + dScore + (info.forbids ? 0.5 : 0);
if (score > bestScore) { best = info; bestScore = score; }
```

`tools/dialogue/critic-semantics.mjs` (break arm: comparing the shipped rule to itself gives 0
divergence, exit 1 — the comparator can report agreement):

```
resolutions (347 speakers x 4 players x 468 topics)      75,948
  decided by a TIE, i.e. by authored order                    0   (0.00%)
answers where first-match-wins and scoring DISAGREE           52
```

Three consequences, and they undo three of the round's headline claims at once:

**(a) The unreachable-INFO lint is measuring the wrong rule.** RI-DLG01 §D's superset test —
"filter[j] ⊇ filter[i] ⇒ j unreachable" — is correct under first-match-wins and is neither sound
nor complete under scoring. A strictly-narrower later info *outscores* its predecessor and is very
much reachable, which is exactly why `shadow-audit.mjs` finds all three survivors spoken. The
builder saw the symptom (*"build-graph's merge invents an authored order … the engine never uses"*)
and did not draw the conclusion.

**(b) `order-infos.mjs --write` was not an inert fix that happened to be safe — it was arithmetically
incapable of doing anything.** With **0 ties in 75,948 resolutions**, authored order decides nothing
in the shipped reader, so `answer-census.mjs --diff`'s "0 of 75,151 answers changed" was forced
before the tool ran. The measurement is true and it is not evidence. This is the third time an
inert fix has passed here alongside a passing measurement, and it is the first time the measurement
has been shown to be structurally unable to fail.

**(c) `shadow-audit.mjs` cannot find what the lint missed.** It audits exactly the three records
`build-graph.mjs` nominated and reports "3 shadow(s) audited … 0 genuinely unreachable". It is a
confirmer of the lint's output, not an audit of the corpus. The builder documented one blind spot
in it (byte-copy forgeries come back HEARD); the larger one is scope.

### C2. So how much dialogue is actually dead? 133 infos.

`tools/dialogue/critic-reach.mjs` asks the question the tree never asks: *is there any character I
could roll, and any person I could stand in front of, who would say this sentence to me?* It runs
the shipped scoring rule over 347 real speakers × a player space of 5,400 (10 races × 4 upbringings
× 15 dispositions × 9 knowledge sets), with the domains read out of the corpus rather than assumed.

```
INFOS NO PLAYER CAN EVER HEAR FROM ANY SPEAKER:  133  (10.4% of 1,280)
build-graph.mjs reports:                           3

by cause:
   40  no such actor in the roster
   90  no <actor> stands in <town>          (the cell ladder, 14 distinct pairs)
    3  always outscored
```

Break arm: forcing every player-side gate true moves the count to **226** and the "always
outscored" bucket from **3 to 96** — so the gates are read, and 93 infos in this corpus exist only
because a race, upbringing, knowledge or disposition gate above them can close.

### C4. `orphans_npc_advertised: 23` — honest disclosure, wrong bucket

Asked whether separating them is honest reporting or a category invented to move a number: **both,
and the disclosure is the larger half.** The builder found a fourth entry point the lint did not
model (`npc.topics`, read by `converse.js topicsFor()`), built the detector, named the count and
put it in the headline. That is the opposite of hiding.

But RI-DLG01 §D's row is *"Orphans (in-degree 0, not a root, **not journal-added**) — 0 by intent —
≥1 = fail"*, and `npc.topics` is not journal-added. On the item's own definition these 23 are
orphans and the row fails. **This verdict records `orphans: 23`.**

The deeper number the split conceals: `topicsFor()` offers `npc.topics` **without intersecting it
against `player.topics_known`** — only the nine roots are gated on what the player holds. Measured:

```
a cold player (topics_known = []) is offered a mean of 1.66 topics per person
distinct topics obtainable WITHOUT ever having learned the word:  126 of 468  (26.9%)
```

So `build-graph.mjs`'s `reachable_only_via_another_topic: 0.9808` is a property of the diagram.
Through the reader that runs it is **0.731**. That is not the star graph RI-DLG01 warns about — a
mean of 1.66 advertised subjects per person is nothing like "every NPC lists everything" — but the
98.1% figure should not stand unqualified.

## F. Voice (RI-DLG06) and the instrument built for it

### F1. The calibration is real, not fitted

`voice-metrics.mjs --calibrate` re-measured:

```
divayth fyr    item 6.50   ours 6.56    delta 0.06
caius cosades  item 9.00   ours 8.89    delta 0.11
aryon          item 10.40  ours 10.40   delta 0.00
ajira          item 9.30   ours 8.52    delta 0.78   <-- disagrees
nileno dorvayn item 9.40   ours 8.77    delta 0.63   <-- disagrees
```

**A tool tuned until it agrees would hit all five.** This one misses two by 0.6–0.8, prints
`<-- disagrees` next to them, and passes anyway on 3-of-5. The "Aryon 10.40 vs 10.40" that looks
suspicious is not: the item quotes one decimal, so anything in 10.35–10.45 renders as an exact
match. The method's parameters (exact-text dedup, `(?<=[.!?])\s+`, `[A-Za-z']+`, 500-token
STTR windows) are natural defaults, not fitted constants — and the STTR windowing is correct, which
kills my first hypothesis for why five of six archetypes miss it. Those misses are real.
**Calibration: accepted.**

### F2. But the instrument under-reads its own spec, and one headline failure is false

`speakers.json` names `tools/dialogue/voice-metrics.mjs` as `separation_requirements.checked_by`.
Occurrence counts in the 240-line tool:

```
second_regex          0        lexicon               0        lexicon_min_lift      0
lexicon_min_markers   0        friend_per_1k_max     0        centroid / variance   0
```

Five fields `speakers.json` declares for this reader are never read, and two of RI-DLG06 §B's six
binding separation requirements are not implemented at all (the ≥5-markers-at-≥5×-lift lexicon rule
and the "≥2 named NPCs must sit ≥3.0 w/sent from their archetype's centroid" type-breaker rule).
**The instrument written to satisfy rule 24 has the project's signature defect inside it.**

The consequence is not cosmetic. Measuring the tics myself with both declared regexes and
per-sentence anchoring:

```
archetype    voice-metrics    regex1    regex2    correct total    min
legionary        0.00/1k       0.39      4.73        5.12/1k       1.0   <- PASSES
magister         1.48/1k      16.30      0.00       16.30/1k       1.0
archivist        3.32/1k       3.13     23.05       26.17/1k       1.0
mudborn          0.24/1k       0.24       —          0.24/1k       1.0   <- genuinely MISSES
```

The builder's own most prominent self-reported failure — *"mudborn and legionary have no working
grammar tic … 0.24/1k and 0.00/1k"* — is **half wrong**. The legionary's tic fires at 5.12/1k
against a 1.0 minimum. Its `second_regex` (`writ|papers|contraband|muster|patrol|fine|bounty|arrest|
manifest|curfew`) is exactly what a legionary talks about, and the checker never looks at it:

> "Ask at the **muster** board. Names, ranks, watches. If they are not on it, they are not ours."
> "Do not draw in the street. Do not argue with a **writ**. Pay the gate fee. It is smaller than the **fine**."
> "We **patrol** three. There are nine. Where are you headed, citizen?"

`regex1` is also anchored `^(Halt|Move|Stand|…)` and applied per **entry** rather than per sentence,
which is why the magister reads 1.48 instead of 16.30. **Only mudborn's tic genuinely fails**, and
reading the mudborn lines confirms it — the deference is carried by "dry-one" (19.14/1k, which
passes) and by hedged clause-stacking that the authored regex does not describe. That is a spec
defect, not a prose defect.

### F3. The metric and the ear, on the three "indistinguishable" pairs

The brief asked for the judgement a metric cannot make. Read aloud:

**`sapcutter` vs `legionary` — the metric is measuring the wrong feature.** Δw/sent 0.23,
Δ≤6w 10.5pp, ΔSTTR 0.036: statistically identical. To the ear they are not remotely alike.

> sapcutter: *"Friend, this one was taught the depth and this one was taught the season, and this
> one keeps both."*
> legionary: *"Do not draw in the street. Do not argue with a writ. Pay the gate fee."*

Both are terse. One is terse-because-servile (third-person self-reference at 24.05/1k, "friend" at
42.40/1k); the other is terse-because-imperative ("citizen" at 9.45/1k). RI-DLG06 §B's separation
rule offers only w/sent, ≤6w and STTR — **three measures of sentence length and vocabulary
diversity, and none of mood.** The corpus already holds the discriminating evidence in the
`address` and `grammar_tic` blocks and the separation test does not consult them.

**`mudborn` vs `archivist` — the metric is right and the ear agrees.** Δw/sent 1.47.

> mudborn: *"You can hear the water change, dry-one. That's the whole of it."*
> archivist: *"Thirteen is the Office's number and it is not a good one."*

Distinguishable by *address form* and by subject, but the sentence architecture really is the same
shape — both build long qualified periods around a hedge. This pair is a genuine miss.

**`rootkeeper` vs `mudborn` — genuine miss.** Δw/sent 1.55 and both sit at 16–17 w/sent with
20–22% short sentences. The rootkeeper's impersonal passive is a strong marker, but the *rhythm* is
shared and RI-DLG06's spread requirement is about rhythm.

**Result: one of the three is a metric defect and two are real.** A rewrite is owed on two pairs,
not three, and the separation rule itself needs a mood axis.

## J. The single biggest gap

**`GAP-W1-17-an-archetype-with-no-mouth` — `dialogue.npc.identity`, severity blocking.**

Forty infos are written for `a` values no NPC record carries, including **all 23 `magister` lines —

player. A further 90 infos are gated to an (actor, town) pair no such actor stands in. The corpus's
own rule is that from the player's chair a model with no consumer and a missing model are the same
thing — so 10.4% of this round's best writing does not exist.

**Remedy, buildable, small:**

1. Add an NPC record for the House Dres magister the corpus already names (`magister-varo-dren`
   answers *"I am he"* and there is nobody to say it), or an `actor_aliases` table mapping
   `magister → dres-factor`, `notary → archivist`, `scribe`/`harbourmistress` → `clerk`. Nine of
   the forty are people who already exist under a different `actor` tag.

   `game/data/npcs/**`, and every `(a, cell)` pair must have at least one speaker. Exit non-zero.
3. Promote `tools/dialogue/critic-reach.mjs` (or its equivalent) to the gate in place of the
   superset lint, because the superset lint tests first-match-wins and the engine scores.
