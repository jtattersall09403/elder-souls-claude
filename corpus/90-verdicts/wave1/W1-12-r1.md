## 1. The headline: the state RI-AI01 requires, that the build does not have, is what carries M3

`combat/ai.js` never enters `RECOVER`. Grep its `_enter(` calls and you get nine of the
seventeen states it declares: `IDLE, SUSPICIOUS, SEARCH, RUSH, APPROACH, CIRCLE, FEINT_STEP,
COMMIT, REPOSITION, PUNISH_READ, LEASH_RETURN`. `PATROL, RECOVER, BLOCK_HOLD, STAGGER,
DISENGAGE` and `DEAD` are unreachable.

RI-AI01 §D **T15** says an attack whose `phase` leaves `active` transitions `COMMIT → RECOVER`,
and **T16** says `RECOVER → CIRCLE` when the recovery frames are exhausted. This build goes
`COMMIT → REPOSITION` at the retire and labels the *whole* swing — startup, active **and
recovery** — `COMMIT`. RI-AI01 **M3** computes `min_dist_dwell` as "the fraction of frames with
`dist_m < 0.85·Ω` **while `state != COMMIT`**". So the recovery frames drop out of the
denominator.

They are the frames that matter. Every attack in this roster carries 0.6–1.2 m of forward root
motion, so an enemy *ends* its swing standing on top of you, and then stands there for 20–76
f@60 while it recovers. Measured on the **builder's own fixture, seed and frame budget**
(`tools/combat/critic-w1-12-census.mjs --mode=recover`,
`reports/w1-12-critic/recover.json`):

| statblock | `min_dist_dwell` as the piece scores it | M3 | with RECOVER counted per T15 | M3 | recovery frames inside 0.85·Ω |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `inf_trash` | 0.0414 | **2/2** | **0.2521** | **1/2** | 623 / 736 |
| `guard_legion` | 0.0414 | 2/2 | 0.2521 | 1/2 | 623 / 736 |
| `drowned_lesser` | 0.0400 | 2/2 | 0.2529 | 1/2 | 623 / 736 |
| `drowned_greater` | 0.0411 | 2/2 | 0.2542 | 1/2 | 633 / 736 |
| `champion_hist_marked` | 0.0565 | 2/2 | 0.2425 | 1/2 | 583 / 600 |
| `beast_slitherfang` | 0.0208 | 2/2 | 0.1887 | 1/2 | 483 / 762 |
| `cst_sap_speaker` | 0.0443 | 2/2 | 0.1968 | 1/2 | 477 / 516 |

Seven of seven flip. **85% of the enemy's attack-recovery frames are spent inside its own STRIKE
band** — which is the shape M3's chase-bot clause exists to detect — and the metric does not see
them because the state that would expose them is the one state the machine skips.

This is not a builder cheating; nobody chose it, and `RECOVER` is not in the piece's
`declared_incomplete` list the way `PATROL`, strings and `BLOCK_HOLD` are. It is the project's
own named failure mode with the sign flipped: *missing* code that correlates with a passing
measurement. The picture is
`docs/shots/2026-08-08-w1-12-critic-the-frames-m3-never-counted.png` — the shaded columns are the
recovery frames and they land exactly on the dips into the red band.

It is still short of the 0.35 chase-bot **hard fail**, and I say so plainly: this drops M3 from
2 to 1, it does not void the piece.

## 2. The enemy cannot catch a player who is walking away — the piece's own central question

The survey (`reports/w1-12/survey.md` §3a) diagnoses the dead road with three compounding causes
and §7 claims one of them half-fixed: *"`souls` archetypes now RUSH at their own declared sprint
(INFANTRY 4.6 m/s), which beats a jogging player and loses to a sprinting one — deliberately."*

Measured (`--mode=flee`, `reports/w1-12-critic/flee.json`): straight line, from 10 m, 30 s,
alert pinned AGGRO.

| player | `inf_trash` min gap | entered RUSH? |
|---|---|---|
| walk 2.0 m/s | **8.14 m** | **no, not once in 1,800 frames** |
| jog 3.2 m/s | 11.63 m | yes |
| sprint 5.0 m/s | 12.55 m | yes |

Five of the seven statblocks never enter RUSH against a *walking* player, and none of the seven
ever reaches strike range. The mechanism is visible frame by frame: RUSH's entry gate is
`dist > 5.0·Ω` — 12.0 m for INFANTRY — so from 10 m the enemy is in `APPROACH`, and `APPROACH`
advances at `archetype.INFANTRY.walk_mps`, which ai.json sets to **2.20**. Against a 2.0 m/s
walk that is a closure of **0.20 m/s**, the identical arithmetic the survey attributes to the
pre-piece `ENGAGE.advance_mps` of 2.20, because it is the same constant. The enemy walks 880
frames at 0.2 m/s of closure, hits its 32 m hard leash, oscillates `REPOSITION ↔ LEASH_RETURN`
for 235 frames pinned at the boundary, and goes home.

The perverse part: the enemy does *worse* against a walking player than a jogging one, because
walking keeps the gap just under the band boundary that would trigger a sprint. **RUSH is gated
on a distance band, not on whether the distance is closing.** That is the defect and it is one
condition wide.

## 5. Commitment: this part is genuinely a Souls enemy

`--mode=commit` starts a fight, waits for the enemy to swing, and on the frame `b.move` is set
teleports the player 40 m away — the strongest possible reason to change its mind.

**47 of 47 swings across seven statblocks ran to their full declared clip length.** Not one
abort, not one early exit, not one substitution: `chop 154/154`, `thrust 122/122`,
`combo_a 72/72`, `combo_b 102/102`, `lunge 92/92`, `whip 84/84`, `staff_jab 100/100`,
`shoulder_turn 66/66`. Yaw movement after startup: **0.00 °/s** on every active and recovery
frame.

**How many frames after an attack starts can the enemy change its mind? Zero**, and it is
structural rather than tuned: `EnemyController.step()` calls `_idleBehaviour()` only in the
`else` of `if (b.move)`, so `SoulsAI.step()` is unreachable for the whole clip, and the retire
happens at the top of the step so the AI cannot shorten its own recovery. That is the correct
answer against the reference — Souls enemy attacks are uncancellable and the recovery *is* the
punish window — and it is the property the whole item is built on. I also tried to break it:
staggering an enemy out of a mid-swing COMMIT does **not** leak its attack token (it re-enters
`CIRCLE` via the `default` arm and releases on the next feint cycle), and in a five-body group
the concurrent-COMMIT count stayed at ≤ 2.

The other half of "punish" is weaker. `punish_read.trigger_player_states` declares
`["HEAL","ITEM","LONG_RECOVERY"]`; the piece's M9 drives only the heal. Applying M9's own bar
(≥ 80% of trials) to the whiff, over twelve settle offsets (`--mode=punish`,
`reports/w1-12-critic/punish.json`):

| trigger | `inf_trash` / `guard_legion` / `drowned_*` | `champion` | `beast` | `caster` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HEAL | 12/12 | 12/12 | 12/12 | 12/12 |
| whiffed heavy (`LONG_RECOVERY`, 78 f@60 of recovery) | **7/12** | 10/12 | 11/12 | 12/12 |

The cause is measured, not guessed: the enemy was already committed at trial start in 7 of the
12 misses. That is defensible Souls behaviour — a swinging enemy cannot punish — but it means
M9's 2/2 is a property of the heal fixture, and the most common punishable moment in the game
gets no answer better than a coin flip from the enemy the player meets most often.

## 6. S22: no arithmetic error found, and no unit written down anywhere

`tools/combat/critic-w1-12-s22.mjs`, `reports/w1-12-critic/s22.json`.

`game/data/combat/ai.json` ships **13 frame-valued leaves and declares no `unit` field**, while
every statblock it sits beside does (`inf_trash.json`: `"unit": "f@60"`). Eight of the thirteen
carry no machine-readable unit at all; **five have neither a unit nor a seconds anchor in the
source item**.

The reason that is not pedantry: **RI-AI01 was not in the wave-0 S22 rebase.**
`REBASE-S22-REPORT.md` §Job 1 names RI-CMB01, RI-CMB02, RI-CMB05, RI-CMB08, RI-AI02, RI-AI03,
RI-WPN01–06 and RI-CAM04 — fourteen items, and the item W1-12 is built against is not one of
them. Its §D table's ~20 bare `f` figures have never had their unit stated, so a value copied
out of it is a window of unknown length.

Where RI-AI01 gave **seconds**, the builder converted at 60 Hz and every conversion I checked is
right: `token_hold_cap_f` 180 ← §E's 3.0 s; `token_cooldown_f` [54, 90] ← §E's 1.2 s ± 0.3;
`search_to_leash_frames` 720 ← T06's 12.0 s; `feint_release_cooldown_f` 36 ← T14's +0.6 s;
`leash.no_los_seconds` 6.0 and `return_heal_seconds` 4.0 kept as seconds. **No doubled or halved
window was found, and no 30-tick figure is compared against a 60 Hz one anywhere in this piece** —
it makes no comparison to Souls reference data at all.

Where the item gave a bare `f`, the value was copied verbatim. The one with a consequence:

> `perception.aggro_entry_frames: [20, 30]` ← T05's *"mandatory 20–30 f aggro roar/stance-change;
> enemy may not attack during it"*.

At 60 Hz that is **0.33–0.50 s**. Read as `t@30` it is 0.67–1.00 s. `ai.json`'s own note says
this window exists because *"without it a 100-alert frame is also a swing frame and there is no
moment in which the player is told the fight has begun."* This project's own reactability floor
is `t_react ≥ 19 f@60` = 317 ms (`RI-CMB12` §A, quoted in every statblock's `_reactable_note`).
The shipped roar's *lower bound* is 20 f@60 = 333 ms — sixteen milliseconds above the floor for
"a human can react to this". If T05's `f` is a Souls tick, the correct figure is `[40, 60] f@60`
and the piece has shipped the announcement of the fight at half length. **Neither I nor the
builder can tell, because nobody wrote the unit down.** That is precisely what S22 exists to
prevent, and the fix is upstream: rebase and label RI-AI01.

The one place I could measure a §F ceiling being disobeyed, I measured it and it was not: the
hard-coded 180 °/s windup steer in `combat/enemy.js:259` is 1.5× `ai.json`'s declared 120, but
the *observed* windup yaw rate is 36.5–57.9 °/s with budgets of 17.9–30.4°, inside RI-AI02 §C's
trash row (90 °/s, ≤ 60°). Dead parameter, legal behaviour. Recorded that way rather than as a
violation.

## 7. AR-1: pass, and here is the call path a successor should re-litigate rather than rediscover

Nothing Morrowind-shaped decides anything inside the fight. `game/src/combat/` contains **zero**
occurrences of `Math.random`, of a disposition, a faction, a bounty, a level, a wall clock or a
weather term. There is no to-hit roll and no dice damage. The AI's six PRNG draws
(`ai.js:235, 342, 390, 391, 392, 411, 435`) go through the simulation's single counted global
`core/rng.js` instance and are exactly the ones RI-AI01 asks for — T11's seeded strafe reseed,
T05's roar length, the token cooldown, and move selection from a **range-gated eligible pool**
(`_tryCommit` filters on `dist <= omega + root_dz_m` and on stamina before drawing). Randomised
selection among moves that can actually reach is standard Souls move selection, not a chance
mechanic.

**The one path worth naming.** A Morrowind disposition does reach the enemy's aggro latch:

```
sim/step.js
  → character/encounter.js::stepEncounters()                      (per entity, per frame)
    → openingFor(data, encounter, character)                      encounter.js:69
      → behaviour_rule === 'hostile_below_disposition'            encounter.js:93
        → character/reaction.js::derivedDisposition({race, upbringing, birthsign})
        → hostile = d.value < encounter.hostile_below_disposition
      → returns { opening, aggro_at_m: hostile ? enc.aggro_at_m : null }
    → if (rule.opening === 'hostile' && dist <= rule.aggro_at_m && perceives)
        ctl.alertState = 'AGGRO'                                  encounter.js:219
      → combat/ai.js::step() reads ctl.alertState                 ai.js:186
```

Live on one shipped encounter: `deep-kin-war-brood` (`game/data/world/encounters.json`),
hostile below disposition 15, aggro at 18 m. **I rule this not an AR-1 leak**, and the reasoning
matters more than the ruling:

1. The disposition term is **binary**. It decides *whether* the party is hostile. It never
   modulates `aggro_at_m`, or any frame, band, poise, speed or damage value — `aggro_at_m` is
   either the encounter's declared constant or `null`.
2. ARBITRATION §1 puts the start of the fight at the aggro frame, and protects Morrowind's right
   to parley and talk down *during* one. Whether a party opens hostile on you is the pre-fight
   question.
3. AR-3 names *"a disposition that opens a door you would otherwise have to fight through"* as
   the crossing the corpus is asking for, and `engine.js::spawnEncounter` already records the
   project's standing reading in a comment: *"AR-3's legal lever is WHETHER, WHEN and HOW an
   encounter aggros … while any change to hp, poise, damage, archetype, moveset or frame data is
   an automatic AR-1 fail."*
4. S26 warns in terms that a check which fires on everything distinguishes nothing.

It is **one line from being a leak**, and that line is `aggro_at_m: 18 - disposition/5`. Named
here so the next critic argues with this paragraph instead of re-deriving it.

The wilderness sight-cone gate this piece added (`encounter.js:214`) reads `e.percept_los` and
the statblock cone, both Souls-side geometry. The perception meter behind it reads light and
therefore the world clock — but `RI-STL01` §1 explicitly owns the multipliers on RI-AI01 §B's
fill rates, and the AGGRO hysteresis at `stealth/system.js:405` implements RI-AI01 §B's *"once
AGGRO, drop to SEARCH only via the de-aggro rule, never via meter decay"*, so no clock can drop
an aggroed enemy mid-fight. Sanctioned seam, not a leak.

**AR-3: not sterile.** The piece narrows a Morrowind-side aggro latch with Souls-side perception
geometry and hands the resulting alert state to a Souls state machine, in both directions across
`character/encounter.js`.

## 11. The single biggest remaining gap

**`GAP-W1-12-approach-cannot-close-on-a-walking-player`** — `combat.enemy.movement`, severity
**blocking**.

RUSH is gated on a distance band (`dist > 5.0·Ω`) rather than on whether the distance is
closing, and APPROACH advances at `archetype.*.walk_mps`, which for INFANTRY is 2.20 m/s against
a player walk of 2.00. An enemy that starts 10 m away from a player walking in a straight line
closes at 0.20 m/s, never enters RUSH in 1,800 frames, never gets nearer than 8.14 m, reaches its
32 m leash and goes home. The wilderness posts still cannot reach you, which is the dead road the
piece was commissioned to fix and whose arithmetic its own survey §3a sets out.

**Remedy.** In `game/src/combat/ai.js` case `'APPROACH'`, add a closure test alongside the band
test: track `dist` over a rolling window and, when closure is below a declared threshold for a
declared number of frames, enter `RUSH` regardless of band; leave RUSH when closure recovers or
the band drops to DANCE. Both numbers belong in `ai.json` under `movement`
(`rush_when_closure_below_mps`, `rush_closure_window_f`), carrying `f@60` in their names or a
document-level `unit` field, so the census can see them.

**Acceptance.** `node tools/combat/critic-w1-12-census.mjs --mode=flee` shows, for every
statblock in the fighting roster and for player speeds 2.0 and 3.2 m/s, `entered_RUSH: true` and
`min_gap_m` ≤ `0.85·Ω` — i.e. the enemy reaches strike range at least once — while the 5.0 m/s
row still shows the enemy losing ground, because an enemy you cannot outrun at a sprint is a
different game. The two new leaves must appear in `--mode=census`'s consumed list, not its inert
one.

## 14. Three things filed against the corpus rather than against the piece

1. **`RI-AI01` was never S22-rebased.** `REBASE-S22-REPORT.md` §Job 1 moved fourteen items and
