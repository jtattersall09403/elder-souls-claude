## 5. Two things nobody in three rounds has looked at

### `projectPoint` cannot see a posed camera, and the round's own cross-check is inert because of it

`w1-13-r3-bloom-sight.mjs`'s header sells its projection read as *"a shape that cannot lie the way a
single count can"*. **All 96 rows of `bloom-sight.json` report `projected: {on_screen: false}`** —
including all sixteen `player-eye` rows scored visible, one of which carries a margin of
**27,134 px**. Two observables in the same file contradict each other in 100% of rows and the
round shipped without noticing.

The pixels are right and the projection is wrong, and the cause is three files deep:

- `engine.camera({pos, look})` (`engine.js:5669–5683`) writes `c.override`, then `c.pos`,
  `c.pivot` and `c.fov`. **It never writes `c.yaw` or `c.pitch`.**
- `engine.projectPoint()` (`engine.js:5750`) calls `projectNDC(this.sim.camera, …)`.
- `projectNDC` → `project()` (`sim/camera.js:1006`) calls `viewBasis(c, …)`, which is
  `basisAt(c.yaw + c.shakeYaw, c.pitch + c.shakePitch, …)`.

So a posed camera moves the eye and leaves the *orientation* wherever the rig last solved it, and
every `projectPoint` taken through a posed camera in this project is answering about a different
camera. I measured the yaw error directly — see §7.

This is not W1-13's model, but it is W1-13 that tripped over it, and it lands squarely on
`NEXT-DISPATCH.md` §1a — the standing instruction to re-read every visual verdict taken through a
posed camera. Any of those that used `projectPoint` to decide "was it in frame" was told `false`.
