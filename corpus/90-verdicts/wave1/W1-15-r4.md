**The brief asked whether a fail-open identical on both sides is correct or two wrongs agreeing.

defect, and while it exists the only defensible behaviour is that what is drawn and what is
simulated say the same thing — a divergence is strictly worse than an agreed guess, because a
player can learn a wrong rule but cannot learn an inconsistent one. `synthesized: true` and

temporary measure rather than a permanent one. The ruling is **reversible** on one condition: if
`interiorsMissingLights()` ever stops shrinking across rounds, the fail-open has become the
authoring policy and should be replaced by a `tools/check-*.mjs` that fails the build.

The round's `not_done` says: *"`archon-apothecary` saturates at L 1.0000 across its whole floor …
11 of 115 rooms are flat at noon for the same reason."* That is true, and it is a room-level

| the r3 critic's arm-01 control (0.75 flat) | 16,050 = **94.4%** | 63 |

**Seven-tenths of the province's indoor floor sits at the top of the light scale, at every hour,
and did so in round 3 as well.** The light term — the model both of the last two rounds were
convened around — is a constant 1.0000 over most indoor floor in this build. `archon-apothecary`
is 50 of 50 cells at 1.0000 with an ambient of 0.2264; the lamps alone put it there.

The round is right that this is a property of `authored_intensity_to_L_scale = 9.3275` and the
reaches rather than of its ambient — the derived ambient costs 2.4 percentage points at noon clear
and 0.3 at 03:00 — and it is right to say so. What it did not do is take the measurement at the
granularity where the size shows. "11 of 115 rooms are flat" and "71.8% of indoor floor cells are
clamped at maximum" are the same defect at two magnifications, and only the second one tells a
reader that the model does not discriminate.

The instrument is not at fault: `mean_spread` and `discriminating` are room statistics, and a room
with one dark corner and forty-nine saturated cells scores as discriminating. A `spread` over a
room whose interior is uniformly at the ceiling is a measure of its walls.

The staging is sound — the stager throws unless each cut string occurs exactly once, and it still

both of its shapes against the rule's own definitions and gets both backwards.

**On the disagreement measurement** the round reports *"TWO INDEPENDENT SITES."* The round-3

file's summary does not.

**On the eleven-rooms measurement** the round reports *"TWO GUARDS FOR ONE DEFECT."* Rule 6 defines
that shape as *"deleting either alone changes nothing and only deleting both moves the number,"*
and it is dangerous precisely because *"honest reporting of it looks exactly like an inert fix."*
Here deleting either alone **does** move the number — 0.1676 → 0.1102 and 0.1676 → 0.0974 — so
neither arm is inert, the danger the rule warns about does not arise, and the shape is the

This is a precision fault, not a measurement fault. Every number is right and the experiment is
well built. But a round that makes rule-6 shape reporting its centrepiece has to get the shape
right, and this one names the one shape it does not have and denies the one it does.

**One thing nobody commented on:** arm 11 (995) has *fewer* disagreeing cells than arm 10 (1215).
Cutting more of the fix improves the number. The cause is benign — with the ambient cut, fewer
cells sit near the 0.35 shadow threshold, so fewer can cross it — but a non-monotone 2×2 is worth a
