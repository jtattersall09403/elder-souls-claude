## 1. The ground plane: confirmed, widened, and one sentence of it is wrong

The round reports, from outside its own brief, that `MagicSystem.stepFall` was called every frame
as `M.stepFall(frame, M.groundY || 0)` with `M.groundY` written only by `beginLevitation`; that
the world's ground plane was therefore **0**; that a body standing still in any town was
permanently `airborne`; and that `castDropReason` (`system.js:556`) returns `'airborne'` for every
spell but `slowfall`. **All of that is true, and it is worse than one town.**

`tools/harness/critic-w1-14-r4-ground.mjs` sweeps **every** named state in `game/data/states/`,
presses the real cast button through `H.pressCast` (which queues `light` and reads the combat bus,
so a refusal measured here is the refusal a player gets), and runs the whole sweep twice —
once as shipped, once under `H.__breakGroundPlane`.

| state | player y | fix present | fix removed |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `town-lilmoth` | **2.68** | casts | **`INPUT_DROPPED reason: airborne`** |
| `town-gideon` | 2.71 | casts | `airborne` |
| `town-archon` | 3.42 | casts | `airborne` |
| `town-blackrose` | 2.59 | casts | `airborne` |
| `town-soulrest` | 8.13 | casts | `airborne` |
| `town-thorn` | 13.28 | casts | `airborne` |
| `town-helstrom` | 27.20 | casts | `airborne` |
| `town-stormhold` | **141.06** | casts | `airborne` |
| `default` | 2.68 | casts | `airborne` |

**Nine of nine towns**, with a control that goes red in every one of them. And the corollary the
round drew is a population fact rather than an anecdote: **26 of the 49 named states in the tree
put the player at exactly y = 0** — every `arena_*`, every `cam_*` fixture, every `wpn-*` loadout
rig, `dungeon_primary`, `interior_firelit`, `helstrom-market`, `stormhold-street`, `thorn-hall`,
`writ-house`. Four rounds of magic, three critics and every probe in `tools/harness/w1-14-*` ran
in that half of the tree. The defect is invisible wherever the floor is already zero, and that is
where all of it was measured.

**Rule 8's arm passes too**: a body that has *walked* 6.48 m in Lilmoth — placed by
`sim/traversal.js`, not by `loadState` — casts with the fix and is refused `airborne` without it.

### The one sentence that is wrong, and it is wrong in the round's favour

The round's status file says the pre-fix gravity *"pulled every body in the world toward y = 0 on
every frame"*. Measured with `airborne` beside the height (`motion.json` §B), that is half true
and the half that is true is the worse half:

| | `pos_y`, sampled every 10 f@60 | `airborne` |
|---|---|---|
| `town-lilmoth`, fix present | 2.68 flat | 0 |
| `town-lilmoth`, fix removed | **2.68 flat** | **1 for the whole window** |
| `cam_boardwalk`, fix present | 0.85 flat | 0 |
| `cam_boardwalk`, fix removed | **0.85 → 0.70 → 0.278 → 0** | 1, 1, then 0 |

In a province town the body is *not* dragged: `sim/traversal.js` re-places it on the heightfield
every frame and wins, so all that leaks is a stuck flag. In a camera-fixture cell nothing else
owns the height, and the body **sinks through the boardwalk to y = 0 in under thirty frames** —
which is also why my first sweep read `cam_boardwalk`'s broken arm as casting normally. That was
not an inert control; it was the body arriving at zero before the button was pressed.

### What this does to the round-3 verdict's numbers

**Nothing, and that is the point.** Every number in `W1-14-r3.md` was taken in `arena_flat` or a
pinned equivalent, and `arena_flat`'s floor is exactly 0. The defect could not have perturbed a
single one of them: `airborne` was false there, `castDropReason` returned null, and the casts
landed. So the round-3 verdict is **not retracted and not weakened** — its magnitudes, its area
survey, its touch multipliers, its determinism battery and its duration curves all stand.

What changes is the **scope claim** every one of those numbers silently carried. A verdict that
says "`fire_damage` at magnitude 60 takes a body 312 → 140" was, until this commit, a statement
about a test fixture and not about the game: in the province the button did nothing at all. That
is not a measurement error, it is a **generalisation error**, and it is the one this project's own
CONSUMPTION clause exists to catch. Four rounds of correct instrumented measurement inside a
volume where the system worked, and no round asked whether the system worked outside it. The
right correction is not to the numbers; it is to every future verdict's fixture list.

## 2. THE GAP: a bolt does not hit anything that walks sideways

Round 3 carried *"a spell does not connect with a moving body"* as its second-most-serious
finding, could only show it as an inconsistency (`fire_damage` `NOT_DELIVERED` in one arm where
`damage_health` delivered), and round 4 declares it untouched. It is now measured, with the
target's displacement demonstrated rather than assumed, and a near-range control that lands.

`damage_health`, `fire_damage`, `frost_damage`, `shock_damage`, `poison_damage`, magnitude 20,
`LIGHT`/`projectile`, one body, 200 f@60. The body is driven by `setEntityPos` one frame at a
time, so its path is a straight line at a stated speed rather than whatever the AI felt like.

| target | displacement | `damage_health` | `fire_damage` | `frost_damage` | `shock_damage` | `poison_damage` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| still, 14 m | 0 m | 48 | 43 | 41 | 43 | 29 |
| **walking sideways, 6 m** | 5 m | **48** | **43** | **41** | **43** | **29** |
| **walking sideways, 14 m** | 5 m | **0** | **0** | **0** | **0** | **0** |
| jogging sideways, 14 m | 10 m | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| retreating, 14 m | 10 m | 48 | 43 | 41 | 43 | 29 |

Read the third and fifth rows together. A body **retreating ten metres along the bolt's own axis
is hit for full damage**; a body **walking five metres across it is never hit by anything**. The
bolt is not failing to reach the target and it is not failing on speed — it is failing to steer.
`cast-classes.json` gives `LIGHT` `turn_rate_dps: 60` and `tracking_cutoff: 0.35`; a 5 m lateral
offset at 14 m is about 20°, and 0.9 s of flight at 60 °/s is 54° of authority, so the turn rate
is not the constraint and the cutoff is where I would look first. The near-range row is what makes
this diagnosable: inside ~6 m the bolt arrives before the cutoff bites.

**Why this is the biggest gap.** RI-MAG01 exists so that casting is a Souls action. Souls enemies
circle. A player who commissions a spell, walks to a spellwright to buy it, pays for it out of a
purse that now really moves, and then throws it at anything that is moving at *walking pace* more
than six metres away, watches it miss — every time, deterministically, on all five damage effects.
That is a first-minute deficit and it is the reason RI-MAG01 cannot leave the "we lose" band no
matter how good the frame data is. It is also the gap this round was told about and did not touch.

## 3. A — the alias fix, graded on a route round 4 did not add

The fix is real. I did not grade it by reading the field the same round introduced.

One residual, reported not charged: `h_bound_weapon` restores `prev` in its `_undo`, so two

## 8. F — the purse was a mirror, and it is one purse now

`MagicSystem.makeSpell` did `this.gold -= q.gold`, and `magic.gold` is a copy: `Engine._setGold`
is the one writer that moves `sim.progression.gold`, `combat.world.gold`, `sim.stealth.p.gold`
and `magic.gold` together, and it overwrites the copy on its next call. Every price this system
charged was charged against a number nothing else read.

Measured through the surfaces that exist, not through the field:

| | |
|---|---|
| `getGold()` before | 500,000 |
| quote | 2,145 |
| `getGold()` after | **497,855** — the spend is the quote, exactly |
| `saveState().progression.gold` | **497,855** |
| set to 1, then restore the save | `getGold()` **497,855**, and a second commission is **not** refused and takes it to 495,710 |

The fix (`engine.js:1113`, `this.magic._spendGold = (g) => this._setGold(this._gold() - g)`) is the
right shape and it is the same fix W1-16 made one system along.

**The residual, and why it is currently harmless.** One write still bypasses `_setGold`:
`game/src/save/state.js:592` sets `sim.progression.gold = blob.progression.gold` directly, which is
precisely the rule-7 shape (a field restored without its mirrors). I went looking for the stale
mirror it implies and could not produce one, because a load rebuilds the fight and `bindWorld`
re-seeds `magic.gold` from `sim.progression.gold` at `engine.js:1105`. So the direct write is
masked by a rebuild rather than being correct. Two other purses are worth a future look for the
same reason: `game/src/harness/api.js:2635` (`factionReadmit`) debits
`engine.sim.progression.gold` by hand and syncs *only* `magic.gold`, leaving `combat.world.gold`
and `sim.stealth.p.gold` behind — harness-side, so not charged, but it is the third instance of
this exact shape in the tree and the second one an agent has had to find by grep.

## 12. The single biggest gap

### `GAP-W1-magic-bolt-cannot-lead-a-body-that-walks`

> **Every projectile spell in the game misses a target that is moving laterally at walking pace at
> normal engagement range. Five damage effects, 14 m, a 5 m sideways walk: 0 damage, all five,
> deterministically — against 48/43/41/43/29 for the same body standing still, and 48/43/41/43/29
> for the same body *retreating ten metres*. Inside 6 m the same walk is hit for full damage.**

**Why it is the biggest.** It is not that magic is weak; it is that offensive magic does not
function against anything that behaves like a Souls enemy, and Souls enemies circle. Every other
thing this round built — a spellwright you walk to, a counter drawn on a real screen, a purse that
really moves, a spell nobody wrote, a ground plane that finally lets you cast outdoors — delivers
the player to the moment where they throw the thing they invented at something, and the thing they
invented misses. RI-MAG01 is the item that says casting is a Souls action; this is the reason it
is not one. It has been named for two rounds.

**Remedy.**
1. **Instrument the miss before fixing it.** The projectile record already carries `travelF`,
   `pos` and `hits`; emit a `spell_miss` (the vocabulary is closed — reuse `spell_hit` with a
   `miss: true` field rather than adding a name, `RULES.md` #15) carrying the target's position at
   spawn, at cutoff and at expiry, and the closest approach in metres. The number that matters is
   the closest approach: if it is 0.6 m the fix is the hit radius, and if it is 4 m the fix is the
   steering.
2. **Look at `tracking_cutoff` first.** `LIGHT` carries `0.35` and `HEAVY` `0.3` in
   `cast-classes.json`, and the near-range row lands while the far-range row does not, which is
   the signature of a homing window that closes before the flight ends rather than of a turn rate
   that is too low (60 °/s over 0.9 s is 54° of authority against a 20° offset).
3. **Do not fix it by making the bolt home harder.** RI-CMB01's dodge contract is what the cutoff
   exists to protect: a bolt that steers all the way to impact cannot be dodged, and
   `min_dodge_window_f: 20` becomes a lie. The right shape is *lead* — aim at where the body will
   be when the bolt arrives, then stop steering — which preserves the dodge and fixes the walk.

**Acceptance** — write it as a probe assertion, not as prose:
> For each of `damage_health`, `fire_damage`, `frost_damage`, `shock_damage`, `poison_damage` at
> magnitude 20, `LIGHT`/`projectile`, against one body at **6 m and at 14 m**, driven in a straight
> line by `setEntityPos` at **0 m/s, 1.5 m/s lateral, 3 m/s lateral and 3 m/s along the axis**, over
> 200 f@60:
> 1. **Every one of the 40 cells delivers non-zero damage**, and the damage at a given magnitude is
>    the same number in all of them (the bolt does not do less damage because it worked harder).
> 2. The `spell_miss` instrumentation reports **closest approach ≤ the class's `radius_m`** in every
>    cell that delivers, so "it hit" and "it was close" are the same statement.
> 3. **A dodge still works**: a body that rolls on the frame the bolt is `min_dodge_window_f` away
>    takes 0, in all 40 cells. This is the arm that stops the remedy from being "home harder", and
>    a fix that passes 1 and 2 while failing 3 is an AR-1 problem, not a win.
> 4. `--break=nolead` restores today's behaviour and the probe goes red on the eight lateral cells
>    at 14 m and **only** on those, so the teardown is the width of the fix.
>
> Today's reading against criterion 1 is **8 of 40 cells at zero**, and all eight are the lateral
> ones beyond 6 m.

## 14. Is commissioning a spell any *good*?

The brief asks the question that no measurement answers, and it deserves a straight answer:
**yes, and it is the best-written thing in this piece.**

Four spellwrights and three enchanters, and the split is doing work rather than saving it. The
three who refuse do not refuse with a greyed-out button; they refuse in character and then tell you
where to walk. Stormhold's Vitellia Carinus: *"I do not write spells. I put them into objects,
which is a different trade and a better-paid one… Helstrom is nearest from here and the road is not
the worst of them."* Soulrest's Wux-Shekh: *"Lilmoth has a spellwright and Lilmoth is downriver of
everything, which is the one convenient thing anybody has ever said about Lilmoth."* Blackrose's
Drelas Beleth: *"Gideon is four days upriver, and you will find the four days are part of what you
are paying."* That last line is the design thesis said by a character, which is the only place a
design thesis should ever appear, and it turns a locked door into a rumour and a journey.

The commissioning voice is right too. Gideon's Neloth Sedran opens with *"You have come up the river
to have a thing made that nobody has made. Good. Half of what walks in here wants a spell that
already exists"* — which is, precisely, the sentence a player needs to hear to understand what this
screen is for — and then *"Then we begin at the beginning: what should it do? Not what it should be
called. What it should do."* The counter's own rows are written in the same voice: not `Magnitude`
/ `Duration` / `Area` but **"what it should do"**, **"how strong"**, **"how long"**, **"how wide"**,
**"how it should reach — at what you are looking at"**, **"have it made"**, **"nothing today"**.
Morrowind's own spellmaking screen is a grid of sliders with a Buy button; this is a conversation
with a person who is pricing your idea while you describe it, and it reads as one. Helstrom's
Ashul-Vei — *"The bole writes better than I do. Tell it what you want and I will hold the chalk"* —
is doing something Morrowind's UI could not do at all.

And the discoverability is Morrowind's: `rumours.json` carries three lines that point at these
people without a marker. *"Tsona-Ixtu keeps a slate by the ledger house and will write you a spell
that has never been written, if you can say what it should do and pay for it. Most people cannot do
the first part."* That is how you learn a system exists in a Morrowind game.

**Would a player do it twice? Yes — and the second time is better than the first**, because the
first commission teaches you what the dials cost. My three spells cost 171, 171 and 193 gold at
their minimums; the same `fire_damage` at its maximum with duration and area on it quoted **315,187
gold and 761 Focus against a reservoir of 124**. That spread *is* the game: you learn that width and
duration are what you are really buying, and you come back with a smaller, meaner idea. That is
Morrowind's actual spellmaking loop and this build has it.

**Three things stop it being excellent, and none is prose.** The four spellwrights keep no hours —
`schedule: [{from: "00:00", to: "00:00", at: null}]` — so they stand at a post all day and night,
which makes them furniture rather than people with lives; Morrowind's shopkeepers close. The
counter cannot name what it makes (a spell is auto-named *"damage health and feather (Neloth
Sedran)"*), and naming your own spell is half the pleasure of the original system. And the thing
you make is undercut by §2: the best spell in the world does not connect with a body that walks.
**The door is excellent. What is on the other side of it does not work yet.**

## 15. How this verdict was reached

Boot was confirmed at HEAD (`node tools/boot-check.mjs` — PASS at `9fadada`) before anything was
measured, which is a change from round 3, where the tree did not boot and every number was taken
against a pin.

Six instruments, all under `tools/harness/critic-w1-14-r4-*`, plus two of the project's existing
ones re-run unchanged. What produced this verdict was, in order: taking the round's own headline
finding and asking it of 49 states instead of one (§1); refusing to grade a fix by reading the
field that shipped with it (§3); measuring the *width* of a teardown rather than accepting it
(§4); re-running the previous critic's own instrument to adjudicate a claim about that
instrument, rather than re-implementing it (§5); running the arm the round did not publish (§5);
walking to a person and buying something (§6); and — the one that found the gap — asking rule 8's
question with a target whose displacement I could show, after my first attempt at it could not
(§2, §11).

Source was read to locate mechanisms after behaviour was already demonstrated from output, except
in three places where I read first and say so: `combat/system.js spawnEnemy` and `save/state.js`
were read to establish **absences** (no other shared statblock object is written to; one purse
write bypasses `_setGold`), which is the one thing output cannot demonstrate, and
`cast-classes.json` was read to name a suspect for §2 that I have not confirmed.
