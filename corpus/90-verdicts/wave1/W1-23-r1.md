## 0. What is true, said first

The defect this piece exists for was real and is closed. `corpus/60-lore/data/canon-facts.json` had
two readers, both critic scripts, and nothing under `game/` had ever opened it. Today
`game/src/engine.js:2269 _installCanon()` builds a `CanonRegistry` from `game/data/lore/canon.json`,
resolves every `voiced_by` and every argued-on topic against the data that actually loaded, **throws**
on a dangle, and hands the register to `conversation.setCanon()`; `converse.js:230` consults it on
every info. That is a genuine world-side consumer, it is fail-closed, and the builder's own tool
proves both directions. `tools/lore/canon-census.mjs --self-test` passes 10/10 with each check broken
deliberately. `tools/lore/tribe-census.mjs` passes 6/6. The registry validator passes at 83 facts.
Nothing in this verdict is a claim that the work is decorative.

The verdict is about the size of the numbers next to that work, and about who, in this province, is
allowed to hold an opinion about the thing done to them.

## 1. Did a bar get satisfied by minting rows?

**Partly, and the inflation is at the "before" end, not the "after".**

The report's headline is a table whose first column reads *"Run against the build as it stood"*:

> | disputes **fully voiced** (bar ≥8) | **0 of 6** | **21 of 21** |

The "0 of 6" is not a measurement of the build. `voiced_by` and `holders` are fields **this piece
invented**, and the census's fully-voiced test is defined over `voiced_by`. A registry with no such
field scores 0 of N by construction, however loudly the province is already arguing. The registry's
own history, read out of git (`artifacts/W1-23-r1/voices.txt` §3):

```
  b96de7c   facts 54  disputed 6   voiced_by entries 0    positions with holders 0
  cdb2938   facts 71  disputed 21  voiced_by entries 93   positions with holders 48
  HEAD      facts 83  disputed 27  voiced_by entries 136  positions with holders 66
```

112 books were already on disk at `b96de7c`. Backfilling nothing but the field, **four of the six
seeded disputes — CF-D001, CF-D003, CF-D005, CF-D006 — were already fully voiced by books that
existed before the piece started.** The honest before/after is 4 of 6 → 27 of 27, not 0 of 6. The
second structural note in the report is sound and I confirm it: 6 < 8, so the bar genuinely could not
be cleared without new registrations, and new registrations were the right remedy.

**Were the new rows real?** Yes, on the test that matters. No dispute in the registry is voiced by
dialogue alone: **every one of the 27 has at least one shipped book on at least one position**, and
none is voiced entirely by the two files the lore pieces wrote. That is the check that separates
registering a dispute from writing one, and it passes.

**But apply the piece's own ruling to the piece.** The `not_disputes` block states the rule:

> A contradiction is a disagreement about what is the case. A disagreement about what is worth doing,
> what is fit, or what a man ought to carry is an ARGUMENT, and the province should be full of them.

By that rule the piece correctly excluded four martial edges, including *"whether the high grip on the
ricasso is worth the reach it gives up"*, on the reasoning: *"Worth, and an explicit trade: both
authors state the same exchange and price it differently. That is two schools."*

**CF-D021 is that same shape and it was registered.** Its claim is *"Is sight in this province a
competence the country takes away, or the wrong sense to have trained on?"* and its two positions are:

> **A** — "A trained man can put a spell into a body at eighty paces and this country takes that away
> from him, and there is no substitute for it. **The fault is the province's.**"
> **B** — "The eye was never the sense to train. You are hidden from the water, the reeds, the birds
> and your own smell, and **a person taught properly here is not working round a defect**."

Neither denies what the other asserts. Both agree on the fact — you cannot see far in the marsh —
and price it differently: whose fault it is, and which sense was worth training. "The fault is the
province's" is a judgement of fitness, not a state of affairs. That is two schools, and it belongs in
`not_disputes` beside the ricasso, not among the 27. The sting is that CF-D021 is the very entry the
report holds up as the model of how registration should happen (*"registered roughly four minutes
after its two books landed on disk"*) — the exemplar is the one that fails the rule.

CF-D009 (*"Does the labour-lease at Blackrose raise any question of consent?"*) is the same family
and I leave it standing, narrowly: position B makes a factual claim — *"The mark on the paper is the
mark of a woman answering a different one"* — and a claim about what a mark records is a claim about
what is the case.

**One argument in twenty-seven is not a bar satisfied by minting rows.** The count is honest at the
after end. It is the before end and the presentation that are inflated.

## 2. Is the hash trick real?

**Half of it. The answer is absent for the rulings that are prose and present for the rulings that
are a letter.**

`tools/lore/build-canon.mjs:57` writes `truth_seal = sha256(String(f.authorially_true))`, and its own
header states the design:

> A rule that depends on nobody writing the wrong function is weaker than a rule that depends on the
> bytes being absent.

For 17 of the 26 sealed disputes the ruling is a `partial:` sentence and the seal holds. For the
other 9 the ruling is the bare position id `"A"`, `"B"` or `"C"` — and the shipped file publishes the
position ids next to the seal. Three guesses:

```
CF-D003 = B    CF-D004 = C    CF-D006 = C    CF-D008 = B    CF-D009 = B
CF-D010 = C    CF-D020 = B    CF-D021 = B    CF-D023 = C
9 of 26 sealed disputes have their ruling recoverable from the shipped bytes alone
```

The claim in `world/canon.js`'s header — *"the running game cannot state it, because the string is
not in the build"* — is false for those nine. The string is not in the build; the **answer** is, at a
cost of one loop. The register delivers exactly the weaker guarantee its author names as inferior.

**And the two instruments that check for a leak are blind to precisely this case by construction.**
`build-canon.mjs:122` and `canon-consumption.mjs:118` both guard with `if (body.length >= 24 …)` and
`.filter((t) => t.length >= 24 …)`. A one-character ruling is under the threshold and is skipped by
both. The check that proves the answer is absent cannot see the rulings whose answers are present.

Two of the nine are the ones where a shipped adjudicator would do most damage: **CF-D008**, which the
registry's own note calls *"the province's central moral fact"*, and **CF-D003**, whose position A the
item describes as *"a wrong view held by real NPCs, and its wrongness is racial condescension."*

Credit where it is due: **CF-D007**, the dispute nearest the registry's one secret (CF-C010, the
engine under the Stone Wastes, `player_discoverable: late`), carries a long `partial:` ruling, is not
recoverable, and neither of its positions states the secret. The secret did not leak. The remedy is
one line — seal `f.id + '|' + authorially_true + '|' + <a per-registry salt kept in the corpus>` — and
it costs nothing, because nothing in the build reads the seal.

## 3. Is "336 of 336 people hold a position" meaningful?

**No. It is a statement about holder lists, and it is now 347 of 347 for the same reason: because
`holders.actors` are drawn over common actor names. It survives contact with nothing.**

The number that means something is how many people hold a position **and have a sentence for it**:

```
347 people: 156 hold a position AND have a line (45.0%)
            191 hold one with NO line for any side they hold
              0 hold none
30 of 66 positions have no dialogue voice at all
```

More than half the province holds an opinion it can never be asked about. And the register's total
behavioural footprint, measured by the delete-the-fix the piece did not run — every NPC against every
topic, once with the register and once with `canon=null`:

```
161,702 (npc,topic) pairs; the register changes 31 (0.019%) — 31 silenced, 0 swapped
```

**It only ever takes dialogue away. It never causes anyone to say anything.** Every cf-tagged info
also carries an actor field, and `converse.js:246` (`if (!matchesActor && info.a) continue;`) already
refuses an info written for somebody else's mouth. The builder's perturbation — *"Strike a speaker
out of the register's `holders` and their line **vanishes**. Not degrades — vanishes"* — is measuring
the register's lock sitting on top of an older lock that was already closed.

The demonstration the report leads with does not survive the ablation. Delete the register entirely
and re-ask the province (`artifacts/W1-23-r1/voices.txt` §2):

```
==== WITH REGISTER                    ==== REGISTER DELETED
  the-thinning: 5 distinct answers      the-thinning: 5 distinct answers
  slavery:      3 distinct answers      slavery:      3 distinct answers
```

Byte-identical, both topics, both arms. *"The province disagrees with itself"* is true and it is not
the register's doing. The 31 silencings are the register's doing, and several are genuinely good work
— a Wet Ledger clerk matches both sides of CF-D008 and is therefore given neither, which is
`stanceOf()`'s deliberate *"a speaker who matches two positions holds NEITHER"* rule catching a real
authorial collision. That is worth having. It is 31 lines, not a province.

**A third population sits between the two numbers.** A `voiced_by` of the form
`dialogue:<topic>#<actor>` is counted by the census as a voice whether or not the info it names
carries a matching `cf`/`pos`:

```
70 dialogue voices declared; 50 are tagged with that cf+pos; 20 are NOT
```

Those 20 are in the census's population and outside the consumer's — the register gates nothing
there, the perturbation cannot touch them, and `resolve()` passes them because it checks only that an
info exists for that actor. **All of CF-D002, CF-D003, CF-D004 and CF-D005 are in this set**: four of
the six disputes RI-LOR06 §2 seeds as the load-bearing ones, the ones the world is actually about —
who cut the first sapwell, who built the xanmeers, one Hist or many, what caused the Flu — have zero
cf-tagged dialogue, and the new consumer does not touch a single line of them. The census self-test
has a check for *"a voice attributed to an actor with no info there"* and none for *"a voice
attributed to an info that makes no claim"*, which is why 10/10 is green with 20 live instances on
disk.

## 4. The province's own subjects

**One registered fact is not enough. The tree is still speaking in the coloniser's voice, and the
registry has now written that down as though it were a resolution.**

The report's diagnosis is exactly right and I want it quoted before I disagree with what followed:

> A province that only ever says "lease" has adopted the Provincial Office's vocabulary **as
> narration**, which is exactly the move RI-LOR06 §1.2 forbids at the authorial level. The correction
> is not to sprinkle the word about. It is that the euphemism must be somebody's **position** and not
> the world's voice.

So CF-D008 was registered: *"Are subjects of the Empire held as property in this province?"* Here is
the whole of what the province says on the topic `slavery`, over all 347 people, with the register
installed (`artifacts/W1-23-r1/voices.txt` §1):

> **[clerk — Imperial]** *"No subject of the Empire may be held as property inside the Empire's law.
> Where it happens it happens outside that law, off foreign keels, and the Office has made
> representations. I've copied three of them myself."* — `cf: CF-D008, pos: A`
>
> **[dres-factor — Dunmer]** *"The House keeps a stock column and people are entered in it and taken
> out of it, and the law you are quoting changes the word on the paper and nothing else. You wanted an
> honest answer. Now go and be shocked somewhere I can't see you."* — `cf: CF-D008, pos: B`
>
> **[townsman — Saxhleel, to an Argonian player]** *"This one will not discuss it with you standing
> there, and this one is sorry."* — **untagged**

**Both registered positions on whether Argonians are property are voiced, in dialogue, by the
administration that legalises it and the House that runs it.** The only line written for an Argonian
mouth is a refusal to speak, it carries no `cf`, and it therefore contributes nothing to the 27 of 27,
nothing to the 43/43 perturbation, and nothing to the register at all. **227 Argonian NPCs live in
this province; 66 of them have any answer to `slavery`; between them they give three distinct
sentences and two of those three are a colonist's.**

The refusal is good writing. It is a real thing a person does and it is better than a speech. But it
is the *only* thing, and the registry now records the enslaved as holders of a position they cannot
utter: CF-D008/B's `held_by` reads

> `"House Dres factors", "the underwriters of the Wet Ledger", "every soul who has been on the
> Blackrose roll", "the second hand in the margin"`

and its machine `holders.actors` are `dres-factor, sapcutter, mudborn, outlaw` — of which **only
`dres-factor` has a line.** No sapcutter, no mudborn, no outlaw can say it. Both books voicing B are
Dunmer and Imperial hands: `ledger-and-journal-of-andrel-vorin`, a salt-factor's own ledger, and
`marginalia-in-a-borrowed-book`. RI-LOR06 §2 gives `held_by` one job — *"prevents: a 'contradiction'
nobody actually believes — a note dressed as a dispute"* — and here the field does the inverse: it
credits the people on the roll with a position and the build gives them no sentence.

The ruling is `B`, and it is one of the nine recoverable seals (§2). So on the one dispute the
registry itself calls the province's central moral fact, the answer is in the shipped bytes and the
argument is between two colonists.

This is the gap. It is not "add the word slave"; the report is right that that would be worse. It is
that CF-D008/B needs a voice from `mudborn`, `sapcutter` or `outlaw` — actors the registry has
*already declared* hold it — and that the townsman's refusal should carry `cf: CF-D008` with a third
position of its own, because a refusal to answer an Imperial question in front of an Imperial
building is a position about what is the case, held by the people it is the case about.

## 5. The six tribes: appended, not absorbed

The tribes piece landed six Argonian peoples, six disputes (CF-D022–CF-D027) and 32 of the register's
50 cf-tagged infos. `tools/lore/tribe-census.mjs` passes: each people has a body, a mouth, a text, a
registered position and a refusal. The dialogue is good — *"Distance is nothing to a root. A man who
dies at Tear is home by evening and the tree is no poorer"* against *"The pattern goes to whatever is
nearest and awake. That's not cruelty, it's reach."* — and the six disputes are all genuinely about
what is the case.

They are appended. Measured at `5c4c5b0`:

| people | NPCs | faction record | settlement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agacephs | 2 | none | helstrom |
| Paatru | 2 | none | blackrose / soulrest |
| Archein | 2 | none | gideon |
| Sarpa | 1 | none | helstrom |
| Miredancers | 2 | none | lilmoth |
| Dead-Water | 1 | none | thorn |

**Ten people, one or two per nation, in a province of 347.** `game/data/progression/factions.json`
lists two ids — `house-dres`, `shadowscale-order` — and neither the six nor `game/data/factions/`
exists. CF-C023, W1-23's own registration of the omission, states the condition the tribes piece was
supposed to meet:

> If tribes are wanted, they need **a faction each, not a mention each**.

They got a mention each and a speaker each. Six disputes were added to a 27-dispute headline while the
population able to argue them grew by ten.

**And CF-C023 is now false, is still shipped, and no instrument in the piece can see it.** Its
`measured:` field, projected verbatim into `game/data/lore/canon.json`, reads:

> "Of the tribes canon actually names: … and Agacephs, Paatru, Archein, Sarpa, Dead-Water and
> Miredancers **ZERO**."

Re-counted at HEAD: **Agacephs 53, Paatru 34, Archein 50, Sarpa 26, Dead-Water 10, Miredancers 47.**
CF-C023 is `tier: constructed`, and RI-LOR06 §3 step 6 is unambiguous — *"tier=constructed and the
claim contradicts it? → FAIL. Our inventions bind us exactly as canon does."* The census checks
disputes and contradiction edges and never a `measured:` claim on an undisputed fact, so the registry
carries a false constructed fact and every gate stays green.

This is the piece's own §4 finding — *"with no gate, the registry falls out of date with the shipped
tree within minutes on a busy tree"* — coming true inside the artifact built to prevent it, one wave
later, in a category the new instrument was not given eyes for. The remedy is in my probe already:
`staleMeasured()` in `tools/lore/critic-w1-23-r1.mjs` catches it in eleven lines and self-tests both
ways.

**A quieter version of the same defect is in `canon.js` itself.** `fold()` normalises faction and
settlement ids — the fix the report is rightly proud of — but `stanceOf():153` matches race with a raw
`h.races.includes(race)`. The province spells its own people two ways: **179 NPCs carry
`race: "argonian"` and 46 carry `race: "saxhleel"`**, and the register's `holders.races` lists only
`saxhleel` and `naga`. Three positions narrow by race — CF-D003/B, CF-D016/B, CF-D021/B, each of them
the Argonian side of an Argonian-versus-Imperial dispute — and folding the exonym to the endonym adds
**56 held stances** that are silently absent today. It is the exact defect the report names for
factions (*"sees a fraction of the people it is talking about, and it sees it silently"*), one field
over, in the module written to fix it, and on the axis where the endonym and the coloniser's word for
the same people are treated as two different races.

## 6. Two phantom paths inside two gates

- `game/data/books/manifest.json` **does not exist**, and RI-LOR06 comparison method §1 — the gate
  that *"runs in CI, blocks the wave"* — invokes `canon-check.py --validate-registry --books-manifest
  game/data/books/manifest.json`. Run as written it raises `FileNotFoundError`. The manifest is the
  clause that enforces *"a contradiction whose sources do not exist is not tracked, it is claimed"*,
  so the registry gate has been running for a wave with its anti-forgery half amputated. The report
  names this at §9.3 and it is still open.
- `game/data/factions/` **does not exist**, and RI-LOR01 comparison method §4 counts factions in it to
  decide the invention-density axis. That axis is unmeasurable by its own instrument and scores 0
  fail-closed, which is what caps RI-LOR01 here.
- RI-LOR06 method §2's triage is blind to this piece's content. Run over
  `70-disputes.json` and `75-tribes.json` it reports **`registry touches: 0`** across 50 infos that
  literally carry `cf: CF-Dnnn` fields — it matches on keyword overlap and does not read the tag the
  piece invented. Over the whole tree it exits 1 with **657 texts touching a deliberately-open or
  late/never fact**, which is not a reviewable set. The "zero secret leaks" clause of RI-LOR06's band
  4 is therefore not a finding here; it is a non-measurement, and RI-LOR06 is scored `partial`.
