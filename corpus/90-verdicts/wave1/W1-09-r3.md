## 1. The reach claim is true, and I checked it with my own instrument in the real game

The dispatch was right to warn about a builder shipping the probe that grades it. So I wrote two
of my own — `critic-w1-09-r3n.mjs` (node, imports `game/src/combat/*.js` and runs its own fights;
never calls `cmb-reach.mjs`) and `critic-w1-09-r3.mjs` (headless Chromium through
`window.__HARNESS`). Both, 0.05 m steps, 0 → 4 m, stationary target:

```
                       browser min/max     node min/max     interior gaps
  chop                   0.05 / 3.65        0.05 / 3.65      none
  thrust                 0.05 / 4.00        0.05 / 4.00      none
  combo_a                0.05 / 2.90        0.05 / 2.90      none
  combo_b                0.05 / 3.55        0.05 / 3.55      none
  dagger                 0.05 / 0.95        0.05 / 0.95      none
  straight-sword         0.05 / 1.80        0.05 / 1.80      none
  spear                  0.05 / 1.75        0.05 / 1.75      1.20, 1.25
  halberd                0.05 / 1.90        0.05 / 1.90      1.15, 1.20, 1.25, 1.30
```

The build's numbers reproduce. So does `reports/w1-09/cmb-reach.json`, which I re-ran and got
back unchanged. That deserves saying plainly.

**One correction to the framing.** 0.05 m is a *placement* distance. At the frame the blow lands
the two bodies are 0.5987 m apart — in the browser and in node alike — because the separation
force has already pushed the target to the boundary, and during a single chop the target is
shoved up to 1.625 m. S26 asks for the reaching distance "at contact range, against a stationary
target"; the target is not stationary, and the honest figure for what the champion can hit is
"anything whose body is touching mine".

### 1.1 The interior gaps are the seam between two volumes, and the attribution is honest

The build reports the spear and halberd gaps and blames W1-10's roster. Ablation says that is
half right. Delete `hitgeometry.json §body_hazard` from a copy of the loaded data:

```
                 body hazard ON            body hazard OFF (weapon only)
  spear          0.05 – 1.75, gap 1.20–1.25       1.30 – 1.75, contiguous
  halberd        0.05 – 1.90, gap 1.15–1.30       1.35 – 1.90, contiguous
```

W1-10's spear and halberd clips really do have an inner hole starting at 1.30 m and 1.35 m. But
the *gap* is manufactured by the two-band architecture: the body corridor covers 0.05–1.15 m
(spear) and 0.05–1.10 m (halberd) and stops there. Every other weapon happens to have its two
bands overlap. Nothing guarantees that they will.

## 4. `eatRate` is gone, and the danger is not a knob. I looked for the successor and there isn't one

Round 2 found `eatRate`: a per-swing probability that the bot declines to defend, whose own
comment said *"it is where rows 13, 14 and 21 come from"*. It is deleted.

The build offers `--greed-scale 0` as its own falsification. I ran it:

```
  13 Swings taken                 0.125   [0.05,0.35]  in band
  14 Damage taken / max HP        0.687   [0.4,1.6]    in band
```

Then I went looking for the knob that replaced it. The candidate is `wobble` — a 2–5 frame
*random* jitter on the bot's roll lead, which is exactly the shape of a tuned danger constant. I
copied `cmb-exemplar.mjs`, added a `--wobble` flag that changes nothing else, and ran it at **0**:
a bot that rolls at a fixed lead, every single time, with no randomness anywhere in its timing.

```
   8 Roll timing delta, sd        0.56    [0,8]        in band
   9 Roll timing delta, range     [-6,-3] [-24,8]      in band, 5 of 5
  13 Swings taken                 0.143   [0.05,0.35]  in band
  14 Damage taken / max HP        0.842   [0.4,1.6]    in band
```

**0.842 is the shipped median to the digit.** Neither greed nor the jitter drives the damage.
Round 1's exemplar had no danger, round 2's was a constant, round 3's is a consequence. That is a
real and hard-won result and it is the best thing in this round.

## 5. Collision resolution removes no hit — and, at its shipped radius, does nothing at all

The dispatch asked whether an actor can push a target out of a hitbox it would otherwise have
been struck by. Hit sets with separation on and with both radii zeroed, 4 enemy attacks × 7
player weapon classes, 0.05 m steps:

```
  lost_to_separation_m : []   for all eleven
  gained_m             : []   for all eleven
```

Nothing is lost and nothing is gained. Good.

The reason is less good. In a 2,617-frame scripted fight:

```
  frames interpenetrating      585 of 2617   (0.2235)
  minimum centre distance      0.015 m       required 0.60 m
  same numbers with both radii set to 0      585 / 0.015 m
```

**Byte-identical.** At 0.30 m with the push capped at 0.05 m/frame the model changes nothing a
player could see; the two bodies still stand at the same point. It *is* wired — raise the radius
to 0.8 m and the minimum distance becomes 1.05 m — but the stated purpose, *"this makes standing
there impossible, which is the half Souls actually relies on"*, is not achieved. `RI-AI01`'s own
hard fail is `min_dist_dwell > 0.35`; 0.2235 passes, at a depth of total overlap.

The 0.30 m choice against the statblock's 0.50 m is well argued in the data file. Nobody
mentions that `sim/world-collision.js` declares `PLAYER_RADIUS_M = 0.55` and
`ENEMY_RADIUS_DEFAULT_M = 0.40`. The player's body is 0.30 m wide to a sword and 0.55 m wide to a
wall.

## 6. The single biggest gap: the hole was covered, not closed, and the cover pays the sword's damage

`GAP-W1-enemy-weapon-volume-still-cannot-reach-and-the-body-pays-its-damage`,
`combat.hitbox.sweep`, **major**.

Delete `hitgeometry.json §body_hazard` from a copy of the loaded data and sweep again:

| attack | with body hazard | **without it** | round 2 measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| chop | 0.05 – 3.65 m | **1.20** – 3.60 m | 1.2 – 3.6 m |
| thrust | 0.05 – 4.00 m | **1.60** – 4.00 m | 1.6 – 4.0 m |
| combo_a | 0.05 – 2.90 m | **1.10** – 2.90 m | 1.1 – 2.9 m |
| combo_b | 0.05 – 3.55 m | **1.20** – 3.50 m | — |

**The weapon volume is exactly as it was.** Round 2's numbers, to the decimal. A 1.75 m greatsword
still cannot cut a stationary player standing one metre in front of it.

And the covering volume pays the weapon's damage. Per-distance, from my own sweep:

```
  chop:     0.05B/96  0.10B/96 … 1.10B/96 | 1.15W/96  1.20W/96 … 3.65W/96
  thrust:   0.05B/75 … 1.50B/75 | 1.55W/75 … 4.00W/75
  combo_a:  0.05B/63 … 1.05B/63 | 1.10W/63 … 2.90W/63
```

96 damage whether the blade cuts you at 3.65 m or the champion's chest walks through you at
0.05 m. It is the same on the player's side: the dagger's blade cannot cut inside 0.75 m and the
straight sword's inside 0.80 m, so 14 of the dagger's 19 connecting distances and 15 of the
straight sword's 36 are torso-checks dealing the blade's damage.

**Why it matters:** a player who walks inside the champion watches a greatsword sweep over their
head and loses 96 hit points to the chest that walked through them, and a spearman standing at
1.22 m cannot be hit by anything at all.

**Remedy (concrete, in the JSON).** Two changes, in order. (1) Give the enemy `chop_overhead` and
`thrust` clip archetypes in `clips.json` a low follow-through phase, so the swept hull covers the
0.4–1.1 m band the attacker walks through — at the champion's first active frame the socket
capsule spans z = [1.34, 2.57] with the root at 0.384, and never re-enters that band. Verify with
`--root-dz 0`, which isolates the blade from the corridor. (2) Give `§body_hazard` its own
`motion_value` and `poise_damage` in `hitgeometry.json` and use them in `resolve.js` step 8b, so a
shoulder-check is a shove and not a cut.
**Acceptance:** with `§body_hazard` removed, `min_hit_distance_m ≤ 0.35 m` for all four champion
attacks *and* all seven player classes with `root_dz_m` unchanged; with it present, every one of
the eleven attacks contiguous from 0.05 m; a body hit's `dmg` strictly less than the same
attack's weapon `dmg`; and the majority of hits in the browser turtle carrying `via: "weapon"`.

## 15. Where this leaves the piece

Round 3 did the hardest thing a remediation can do: it disproved the mechanism the ruling against
it was written on, and it was right to. The corridor an attacker's own body sweeps really was
untested by anything on either side, and testing it really does make the fight lethal — in the
shipping game the turtle now dies at every distance, which is more than the build claims for
itself. The exemplar's danger survived every attempt I made to find the knob behind it, and that
is the single hardest claim in this project to earn.

What it did not do is fix the geometry. The champion's blade is still 0.95 m in front of its own
root at the first active frame and still cannot cut a target standing one metre away; what hits
you at that range is its chest, for the blade's damage. Two of the seven player weapons have a
dead ring where the two volumes fail to meet. And in fixing the reach, the exemplar's bot stopped
spending its stamina bar, which took four rows out of band — one of them the item's own automatic
fail.

The pattern is the one this project keeps finding: the work is real, and the instrument that
grades it is measuring something adjacent to the game. Round 1 shipped a statistics file that did
not match the fight. Round 2 shipped an exemplar whose danger was a constant. Round 3 shipped an
arena in which the boss never turns around, asserted twice that a `--verify` flag checks it, and
graded its headline acceptance on it. Write the `--verify`.
