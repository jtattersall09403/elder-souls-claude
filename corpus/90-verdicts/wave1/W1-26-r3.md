## 3. §P.C — the text-focus fix did not break the world

Round 3 moved the text branch of `input/real.js`'s keydown handler **above** the movement map and
the control map. Written the other way round, that is exactly how you would break every key in the
game. The guard is `this.textFocus && this.textFocus()`, and the builder's claim is that the
predicate "is null everywhere else". Being null is not the same as answering false, and neither is
the same as the key arriving where it is bound.

**Measured by outcome, under rAF, where a player is** (A7 above): walk, sprint, strafe, back, roll
and both screen keys all work after the writ, with `textFocused false`. Sprint is **1.56× walk per
frame**, so `ShiftLeft` is not merely latching, it is changing the gait. The roll burst covers
0.092 m/frame against a 0.053 m/frame walk.

**And the differential, which is the measurement that actually answers the question.**
`tools/journey/critic-w1-26-r3-input.mjs` (mine) runs the same key survey twice on the same build —
once as shipped, once with `real.textFocus` set to `null` so the branch is **unreachable**. If the
two agree key for key, the branch is inert in the world whatever the absolute numbers are. Over 15
bound control keys and 4 movement keys the two arms agreed on **18 of 19**; the one disagreement
(`block`/`KeyF`) points the wrong way for a swallowing defect — it *arrives* on the shipped build
and not with the branch removed — and I record it as noise in my own probe rather than as a finding.
**The teardown is emphatic**: forcing `textFocus` to return true in the world kills **13 control
keys and all 4 movement keys**, so the survey can fail and has been seen to.

**The deliberate fall-throughs, measured inside a focused field** (`textFocused true`, node
`hold.hatch-name`, `ui_mode world`):

| | measured |
|---|---|
| bound letters type instead of firing | `e`, `w`, `a` → field holds `"ewa"` ✓ |
| `KeyE` does **not** commit mid-word | node still `hold.hatch-name` ✓ |
| `Backspace` deletes | `"ewa"` → `"ew"` ✓ |
| `Space` types instead of rolling | `"ew"` → `"ew "` ✓ |
| `Enter` commits | ✓ **under rAF** — 11 nodes driven by `Enter` in §1 |
| arrows move the caret | ✓ **under rAF** — implied by §1's ledger navigation; **not isolated by me** |

**Honest limits of this section, stated plainly** (rule 26). My `stepFrames`-driven probe could not
read press *edges* — `pipeline.pressed` is cleared by the next latch — and a `page.keyboard.press()`
under `stepFrames` is a down-and-up with no frame between them, so `Enter` and the arrows looked
dead when they are not. I diagnosed that as my instrument, moved the assertion to rAF, and the rAF
run passes. The two rows above marked "under rAF" are carried by §1's eleven nodes rather than by
an isolated arrow-key arm, and I am saying so rather than claiming a measurement I did not take.
I also did **not** verify the peer walk between screens (`inventory → journal → sheet → spells →
map`), which is reached from *inside* a screen and is W1-21's; from the world only `menu`
(`Escape`/`KeyM`) opens anything, and `Digit1/3/4` and `KeyT` are `use_item`, `swap_right`,
`swap_left` and `spell_cycle` — pressing them in the world opens nothing, and that is not a defect.

**Eight directions, eight moves, eight distinct destinations** — the builder reported seven and I
get eight. The corner is not a trap. Round 2's twenty 0.000 m samples were a world that was not
advancing, and calling it geometry would have sent the next builder to look at the crates.

**`frames_advanced` is the right field and `world_was_running` is the wrong aggregate.** The
per-step field genuinely separates the two — a body against solid geometry moves 0 m over a
positive frame count, a stopped world moves 0 m over 0 frames. But `walkTo()` publishes
`world_was_running: steps.some((s) => (s.frames_advanced || 0) > 0)`. **`some`.** A walk that runs
for three steps and then meets a paused world reports `world_was_running: true` and hands the next
reader "geometry" — which is round 2's mistake, reachable again through the summary field rather
than through the raw one. The remedy is one word: report `frames_advanced` **on the stuck steps**,
not over the whole walk.

**What I proved about the discrimination, and what I could not.** The stopped-world half is clean:
with a screen open, held `KeyW` for 1.5 s of wall clock moved the body **0 m over 0 frames**, and
that reading is unmistakable. The crate half I **failed to construct**, and the failure is
informative rather than embarrassing: pushing `KeyS` from the corner walked **17.3 m** without ever
dropping below 0.02 m — there is nothing to press into. That is D1's finding restated, and it means
the two-arm discrimination is demonstrated on one side only. Reported, not claimed.

**And I could not reproduce the builder's own inert control.** The builder reports that its first
eight-arm sweep shared one contaminated state and returned "an identical number and an identical
position" for all eight arms, and that it caught and fixed that before reporting. My shared-state
arm gives **eight distinct destinations too** — because arms that continue from where the last one
stopped naturally diverge. My test does not have the shape needed to reproduce the specific
contamination the builder describes, so **the builder's report of its own inert control stands
unverified by me — neither confirmed nor contradicted.** I say so rather than treating my
non-reproduction as a charge.

**And now the finding this section is really about.** `Engine.bodyRace()` ends:

return rows.some((r) => r.id === id) ? id : null;

A body whose `sim.identity.race` is not an id in `races.json` is observed as **`null`, silently, at

## 5. §P.E — P9, the imperative, and what the instrument actually tests

**Read the line by ear.** `hold.out` now reads:

> *"The light up there is bad, but it is light. When they have finished writing you down, this is
> where I will be, if I am anywhere."*

It was *"Go up. The light is bad but it is light. When you are done at the desk, come back down
here if I am still here."*

**The rewrite is good and it is the right kind of good.** The old line addressed a player and gave
them two instructions. The new one is a sick woman on a bunk remarking on the place above and
telling you where *she* will be. "Up there" is an observation about a light; "when they have
finished writing you down" is her assumption about your afternoon, not your objective. Jiub's
comparable line in Morrowind — *"I heard them say we've reached Morrowind"* — is doing the same
work. It carries a directional payload, and it carries it the way a person would. I would not cut
it. **It is drawn**: I saw it on the frame in my own run's string list, which is what closes HF1.

**But P9 does not test what this verdict is about, and that is the finding.** The check is
`/^(press|hold|…|go|…)\b/i` — **anchored at the start of the string**. I ran it against seven lines
that mean *go up the ladder*:

| line | P9 |
|---|---|
| `Go up. The light is bad but it is light.` | **CAUGHT** |
| `The light up there is bad, but it is light. When they have finished writing you down…` | passes |
| `The way out is up the companionway, past the crates.` | passes |
| `You will want to go up the stairs behind me.` | passes |
| `The desk is up the ladder. They are waiting for you.` | passes |
| `Up the ladder, then. That is where they write you down.` | passes |
| `I would go up, if I were you.` | passes |

**Six lines that signpost, all green.** The doctrine is *"the world does not explain itself, and
nothing tells the player where to go"*; the instrument is a test for the imperative mood. A line
that means "go up" without saying it is still signposting, and P9 cannot see it. Its green is worth
having — it is a real regression guard against the register — but it must not be read as evidence
about wayfinding, and the round-3 status file reads it that way.

**And the sentence the round-2 verdict actually named as the opening's one tutorial is unchanged
and still drawn.** `writ.stamp`: *"Past it, no one tells you a thing you have not asked for. **So
ask them what they do, and what is being said here.**"* That clause was added in round 2, named in
r2 §6 as "the game explaining its central verb, in the piece whose own path says the world does not
explain itself", carried into r3's own not-done list — and **it passes P9 too**, which is how a
round can rewrite `hold.out` for an imperative test and leave the tutorial standing. My run reached
`writ.stamp`; the line is on the frame.

## 6. §P.F — `m4_clause1` measures the instrument, and it is filed as if it measured the build

`journey-run.mjs` reports `m4_clause1 = 1.03 s`, `first_control 141 → first_defining 203`, with
`driver_paced: true` and a careful note. The flag and the note are good practice and I want to say
so before I say the rest.

**Is a number that measures the instrument worth having? Not in this shape, no.** Three reasons,
in increasing order of how much they cost:

1. It is emitted through **`led.ok(...)`**, not `led.unmeasurable(...)`. The ledger row for
   `m4_clause1` is a *pass*.
2. It is published **beside its own bar** — the same object carries `seconds: 1.03` and
   `threshold_s: 60`. A row that says `ok`, `1.03`, `>= 60` is a row that will be read as a build
   failing its clause by a factor of sixty. The prose that stops that misreading lives in a
   sibling `note` field.
3. `driver_paced: true` is **a field written and never read back** — rule 7's shape exactly.
   Nothing aggregates on it. The first tool that counts `m4_clause1` rows will count this one.

**What would make it measure the build.** The clause is *"the player has control for ≥ 60 s before
anything defines them"*, and the interval the driver measures is bounded by the driver's own
patience because `hold.come-to` asks nothing until the player acts. So invert it: **hold the driver
still.** Press nothing for 60+ seconds of simulated time from `first_control`, and assert that **no
character-field-writing event appears** — no `creation_field`, no field-writing `dialogue_open`.
That is a property of the build (does the scene ever ask on its own?), it is decidable, it can fail
(a build with a timeout on the hold node would fire it), and it needs no wall clock. The piece's own
61.5 s wander probe is nine tenths of the way to it already. Until then the row should be
`unmeasurable` with `why: 'driver-paced'`, which is a true statement, rather than `ok` with a
number that isn't about the build.
