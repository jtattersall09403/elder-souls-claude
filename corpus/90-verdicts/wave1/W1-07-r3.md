## 1. The biggest gap: the naming moment is still orphan text

`RI-CHR01` §4 says what the questionnaire is for, in its own words: the Warden-Scribe *"says the
name aloud — **which is the moment the route is for**"*. `RI-JRN09` M3 requires that line on the
frame in **100%** of named runs.

Measured at the node the line is spoken — `writ.birthsign`, which is its only opportunity, because
`spoken` carries only the reply to the last answer — across six races and two upbringings, every
run named:

```
saxhleel  interior   Sap-Reader      LINE_drawn_at_node=false  NAME_string_anywhere=true
saxhleel  lukiul     Root-Speaker    LINE_drawn_at_node=false  NAME_string_anywhere=true
dunmer    interior   Sap-Reader      LINE_drawn_at_node=false  NAME_string_anywhere=true
…
imperial  lukiul     Ledger-Hand     LINE_drawn_at_node=false  NAME_string_anywhere=true

naming LINE on the frame at the node it is spoken : 0  (0%)    <- the item's definition
class NAME substring anywhere in the scene        : 12 (100%)  <- what jrn09-exchange.mjs measures
```

The line exists and it is good — *"Xanmeer-Delver. Not to the letter — nobody is, and the ones who
are worry me — but of the words I am allowed to write…"*. The mechanism that eats it is nine lines
of `render/ui.js`:

```js
while (contentH > maxH && spokenLines.length) { spokenLines.shift(); … }
```

The panel is capped at 48% of frame height and clips, so the scribe's previous reply is dropped a
line at a time until the content fits. At `writ.birthsign` — nine birthsigns plus a two-line naming
sentence — the naming line is always what goes.

**And the builder's instrument cannot see it.** `jrn09-exchange.mjs` M3 tests:

```js
const blob = norm(w.nodes.map((n) => n.drawn_rows.join('  ')).join('  '));
if (blob.indexOf(norm(c.class_name)) >= 0) lineDrawn++;
```

— whether the class *name* appears anywhere in the whole walk's drawn text. The same round added
`Trade declared: %ClassName` to the writ's drawn identity block at `writ.stamp`. So the substring is
on the frame whatever the scribe did or did not say, and the check **cannot go red**. That is
`TOOL-LOOP` rule 3 question 2 — *can the tool fail?* — answered no.

The same sacrifice loop eats two more lines, and this is what makes `AC = 6 of 7` hollow. The seven
quotes the item asks me to publish are:

> `Name recorded: Silence-Under-Salt` · `Also called: Silence-Under-Salt` · `Observed as: Saxhleel`
> · `Raised at: black water, no bottom` · `Trade declared: Xanmeer-Delver` · `Tide drawn on: …`

**Every one is a field of the same printed form.** The lines that would make this an exchange — the
upbringing reply *"The interior. I will write the marsh. There are people here who will read that
and stand up straighter, and people who will read it and count their doors."*, the birthsign reply
*"The tide was in and the root had more than it needed. It gave you a swallow it did not owe you."*,
and the naming line — are all in `spoken` and all clipped. Only **race** carries both a drawn
responsive line and a form line.

`RI-JRN09` M2 states the one judgement it asks of a critic: *"a verdict whose seven quotes are seven
templates should say so in prose and score the axis at its floor."* They are seven templates from
one template. M2 scores 12 of 30.

`RI-JRN09` names the shape in its own CONSUMPTION section: **orphan text** — *"a string authored,
computed correctly, carried through the model, exposed through the harness, and never drawn."* Round
2's orphan was the question. Round 3's is the answer, one node further along, certified closed by the
round's own probe.

**Remedy, and it is small.** Give the naming moment its own node between the last question and
`writ.birthsign`, with the naming sentence as the node's `line` and an acknowledgement as its only
input — exactly the shape `writ.stamp` was given this round, for exactly this reason. `line` is the
one slot the sacrifice loop never touches. Morrowind gives Socucius Ergalla his own beat to say *"…
you are a Battlemage"*; this is that beat. Then have `UILayer._redraw` record what it sacrificed into
`metrics().sacrificed`, so the next probe can tell "not computed" from "computed and thrown away" —
this round's defect is invisible in every instrument the build ships.

## 3. Does the opening stand comparison with Morrowind's?

**No — and the gap is not the writing, and it is no longer the wiring either. It is the shape.**

Let me be exact about what is genuinely good, because it is a lot and two rounds of low scores
should not obscure it.

The prose is better than Morrowind's. Jeeh-Ei saying *"They will take the other one off you at the
writ house. Tell me the one they cannot."* is a better line than anything in the Census and Excise
office, and it is doing more work: it establishes that this province takes things off you, that a
name is a thing you can keep, and that the woman telling you this has been here before. The
dilemmas are better than the wounded-dog questions — *"The salt-road out of the Hills is the only dry
way north for a month. There is a house at the crossing that takes a toll, and the men in it wear a
mark you have seen on the sides of ships."* is a question about a place, not a moral abstraction.
The Warden-Scribe observing your race and mis-recording it is a better idea than Ergalla's form. The
writ is a better object than Morrowind's papers, because it says *"It is the only thing in this
province that says the bearer is a person rather than a shape somebody saw."*

And the room is real now. Not the twenty grey PNGs of round 1: a barge hold with beams and a
hanging lamp, a Writ House with a wall of eleven years of other people's reed-cases behind the desk,
the world visible behind the text at every one of the nineteen nodes, 366–527 distinct colours
outside the panel.

Here is what a player would still feel, in order.

**You are a form before you are a body.** Morrowind's opening is four or five minutes long and you
are not asked anything for the first two. You wake, you stand up, you walk the length of a ship, a
Dunmer prisoner asks your name and repeats it back, you climb a ladder, you step onto a dock in the
rain, you walk into an office. By the time Socucius Ergalla asks you a question you have *been* someone
for two minutes. Here: the title appears, you press New, and at frame 10 a woman asks for your
hatch-name. You have never moved. The barge hold exists — you walk out of it at `hold.out`, which is
a lovely beat — but it comes *after* two identity questions instead of before them. Everything else
in this critique is a defect; this one is the architecture.

**Nobody has a face.** Both screenshots this verdict cites show the same thing. The furniture is
detailed and the people are untextured capsules. Jeeh-Ei is a beige blob with a lighter blob on top.
Tuleeh-Ma is a green blob behind the panel. No eyes, no idle, nobody turns to look at you while they
talk. Ergalla is not a technical achievement either, but he has a head that turns and a scene that
depends on being looked at by a bored official. The builder records this honestly as "art, not
wiring" and it is out of scope for a wiring round — but it is in scope for the owner's question, and
**no bar in this corpus measures it.** M6 asks only that the speaker resolve to a live NPC record,
which it does, 18 of 18.

**Nobody tells you what you are.** Section 1. You answer ten questions and the scene moves on. Two
nodes later a noun appears on a form.

**And there is a progress counter on the panel.** The header at `hold.hatch-name` reads
`THE HOLD, TWO DAYS OUT OF GIDEON · 1 of 13`. `RI-JRN01` O8 forbids *"No 'Step 2 of 4'"* by name.
It is in fact the option-scroll index — W1-26 moved it into the header so it could not be clipped —
which is worse rather than better: in the one place on the screen where a player reads a progress
counter, there is a number that does not mean what it appears to mean. Morrowind's census has no
numerals on it anywhere.

The honest summary: **round 3 turned a form with a room painted behind it into a scene you can read.
It has not yet turned it into a place you were in before anyone asked you anything, and that is what
Morrowind's opening actually is.**

## 4. The four things the dispatch told me to attack

### 4.1 The panel-budget trade — verified independently, and the trade held

Round 3 raised `PANEL_MAX_FRAC` 0.42 → 0.48 and `OPTION_WINDOW` 5 → 9 to buy delivery, and shipped a

**Amended M5 forbids exactly that number**: *"the UI-area fraction is measured from a readback of
the frame and never taken from the build's own layout report… A number the party under measurement
computes is a free parameter."* The builder's counter-check is that free parameter.

Measured properly, with the round-2 critic's pixel detector verbatim so the two rounds are
comparable:

```
node                       selfrep  pixel    panel_h  world_cols
hold.hatch-name            0.3837   0.4778   0.4778   366
writ.race-observed         0.2356   0.4980   0.5537   433
writ.class-questions       0.3548   0.4980   0.5537   435
MAX self-reported 0.3837   MAX pixel readback 0.4980   ceiling 0.55
```

**M5 passes.** Round 2's pixel maximum on the same detector was 0.49; the trade moved it to 0.498 —
about 0.008 of frame area. So the answer to *"did buying one bar cost another"* is **no, it cost

evidence for. What it *did* cost is honesty about headroom: the claimed margin of 0.166 is actually
0.052, because the self-report understates the readback by 0.114.

*Method note, stated because it matters:* the detector takes any bottom-anchored dark region and the
Writ House interior is dark, so `panel_height_frac` reads 0.5537 where the panel is capped at 0.48.
It over-reads. That is the safe direction for a critic and it is the same instrument in both rounds,
which is what makes the comparison mean anything.

And the thing the panel budget *actually* spent is in section 1: the same 48% cap is what throws the
scribe's voice off the bottom of the vellum.

### 4.2 Do not trust the builder's instruments — one held, one did not

I wrote my own for `DTR`, `AC`, `NAMED` and the mute-pair census.

**`DTR`: the claim survives, and I withdraw my a-priori objection.** I expected to catch the accessor.
`jrn09-exchange.mjs` reads `getCensusState().surface.rendered_text`, which is `UILayer.metrics().text`
— an array assembled from the *layout variables* at the end of `_redraw()`, not from the `fillText`
calls. It reports `shownOpts.map(o => o.text)` where the frame received `ellipsise(...)`, and it is
blind to the panel's `clip()`. I re-measured through `__HARNESS.getRenderedText()` — W1-26's
draw-call register, which wraps `fillText` and shadows the clip state — and the two **agree at every
node**: zero strings claimed by `metrics().text` that the register never saw, zero clipped strings.
`DTR_q = 1.0000`, `DTR_scene = 0.9279`, reproduced to the digit. The questions genuinely reach the
frame. A critic that keeps an objection after measuring it away is not a critic.

**The denominator is a different matter**, and it is worth naming because it is structural. The
builder takes "the strings the model computes" from `getCensusModel()` — `renderer.ui.model`, the
object `buildCensusModel()` produced. That is the exact layer round 2's defect lived in:
`buildCensusModel` read `state.line` and never `state.question.text`. **A DTR whose denominator is
the model cannot detect a string the model builder dropped** — it is 1.0 by construction whenever the
renderer draws everything it was handed. Against what the census actually computes per node:

| Denominator | `DTR_scene` | `DTR_q` | worst node |
|---|---|---|---|
| `getCensusModel()` — the builder's | 0.9279 | **1.0000** | 0.6667 |
| `getCensusState()` — what the scene computes | 0.8673 | 0.8500 | 0.5000 |
| + the writ's body (which M1 names explicitly) | 0.8288 | 0.8500 | 0.5000 |

Worst node **0.5000** — HF1 fires below 0.50, so it misses by nothing. M1 scores 33 of 45.

**`NAMED`: the claim survives.** My sweep uses my own shape (all 40 race × upbringing pairs × 6) and
an independent LCG rather than a fixed a/b/c/d rotation, because an even histogram can still be
biased if question *k* always gets answer *k* mod 4. Result: **`NAMED_distinct` = 14 of 14**,
`NAMED_rate` 96.25%, zero classes never produced, histogram 592/539/629/640 (10.17% deviation,
`HF4` needs < 15%). This is the largest thing round 3 did and it is real.

**One thing the builder did not check, which I did.** `RI-JRN09`'s How-we-lose warns that *"`NAMED`
is met by widening the matcher until everything is named"*. The build's answer is
`nearest_profession_floor: 4`, chosen from a percentile table to leave ~4% unnamed. Sweeping it:

```
floor  0: NAMED_rate 100.0%  NAMED_distinct 14
floor  4: NAMED_rate  97.9%  NAMED_distinct 14      <- shipped
floor 10: NAMED_rate  51.7%  NAMED_distinct 13
```

At **no floor at all** the matcher still reaches 14 of 14. So the floor buys the honest *"I do not
have a word for what you are"* band and nothing else. That is not a cheat — the constant is read at

item explicitly protects exists because somebody picked a number, not because some answers resist
naming.

**`NAMED_line`: the claim does not survive.** Section 1.

### 4.3 What else passes for the wrong reason?

The dispatch was right that the Saxhleel finding is a warning as much as an achievement, and the hunt
found three more instances.

**First, the fix is real at the scope it was made.** My own bare-Node census over the shipped
`converse.js` and the shipped data, 15 NPCs × 10 races:

```
Q1 MUTE (npc,race) pairs offered zero topics: 0 of 150
Q2 per-race totals: saxhleel 57, naga 57, imperial 57, … dunmer 56   (ratio 0.982)
Q3 no settlement silent for any race
Q4 of 57 (npc,topic) pairs, 56 answered for all ten races, 1 subtractive
Q6 35 of 57 pairs vary the ANSWER by race (23 two-way, 12 three-way)
```

That is a good result and the direction is right: the gates almost never *remove*, they *swap the
answer*. Closing the mute gap dropped race-varying topic *lists* to one, and I count that as a gain,
not a loss — the differentiation moved from "the native race is offered nothing" to "the answer
depends on who is asking", which is what `RI-CHR02` §5 is for.

**Second — the fix was data, not a rule, and it held for one day.** `game/data/npcs/mainline.json`
(37 more NPC records) did not exist at the piece's commit `b3c6f43`; it landed at `227947b`.
`creation-audit` at my HEAD:

```
FAIL dialogue/CHR02-nobody-is-mute: 370 of 520 (npc,race) pairs have nothing to talk about
FAIL dialogue/CHR02-no-dangling-topic: 77 (npc,topic) pairs advertise a subject nobody can hear
FAIL dialogue/CHR02-every-npc-has-an-actor: 15/52
FAIL dialogue/CHR02-race-still-changes-the-answer: 31/134 = 23.1%, floor 30%
```

The builder's own status file records **95/96 PASS, 0 hard failures**. At my HEAD it is 90/96 with
**5**. The new NPCs are not W1-07's. The architecture is: the builder itself measured that *0 of 306
topics carries an actorless info*, which makes `infoFor()`'s third precedence level dead code — and
then closed the gap by hand-authoring 33 topics for the fifteen people it knew about rather than by
making the fallback work. **A consumer whose default for an unknown NPC is silence will produce this
result every time the world grows.**

**Third — a differentiation that is a five-way distinction dressed as a ten-way one.**
`greetingFor()` keys on `playerRaceClass()`, which is `{saxhleel, naga, dunmer, imperial,
other-foreign}`. Six of the ten playable races collapse into one bucket, so **a Nord and a Khajiit
receive byte-identical greetings from every NPC in the build, forever.** This is `RI-CHR02` §4c's own
12 × 5 × 5 design and is not a violation. It is recorded because "13 of 13 NPCs greet different races
differently" invites a larger reading than the data supports.

**Fourth — the ten answers are rounded away, and that is new this round.** Exact set-equality
matches: **1 of 240**. So in 239 of 240 runs the class comes from the new nearest-profession fit, and
`census.js` then sets `spec.custom = null; spec.classId = c.id` — the character takes the canned class
and the shape the answers derived is discarded. Over 233 named runs the mean overlap between the five
skills the player's answers produced and the five skills of the class adopted is **2.66 of 5**; 95 of
233 keep two or fewer; one keeps none at all. Before round 3, `named_class` was null in essentially
every play and the questionnaire gave you exactly your derived shape. **Closing HF7 made the route
name you something and stop listening to you in the same commit.** Morrowind does the same thing, so
this is defensible — but it is a real change in what ten questions do, made to satisfy a bar, and
nobody wrote it down.

### 4.4 The rendered-text accessor — honest, and structurally incomplete

M9 and M15 are now gated on a *named, demonstrated* accessor, because a canvas-only build passes a
grep by being unreadable. `render/text-register.js` is that accessor and it is genuinely well built:
fed by the draw call, clip-aware, and its own header explains at length why a register fed by the
layout would be a second copy of the layout's intentions. It reproduced the builder's DTR figures
exactly. It is not an echo of the model.

**It enumerates two of the renderer's three text surfaces**, under a comment claiming it enumerates
all of them:

```js
this.ui    = new UILayer(...);     textRegister.instrument(this.ui.ctx,    'dialogue');
this.title = new TitleLayer(...);  textRegister.instrument(this.title.ctx, 'title');
this.menus = new UISurface(...);   // the HUD and every menu screen. Not instrumented.
// "Every 2D surface this renderer owns is registered against the one rendered-text register"
```

So I instrumented the third at runtime, through the register's own public `instrument()`, and
re-read the same frames. **Zero entries.** The reason is one level down and it is structural:
`game/src/ui/glyphs.js` `drawText()` renders every glyph as a stroked Path — `moveTo` / `lineTo` /
`quadraticCurveTo` / `stroke` — and **never calls `fillText`**. The register wraps `fillText` and
`strokeText`. It is incapable of seeing W1-21's entire UI system whether it is instrumented or not.

Enumerated a different way — through `UISurface.elements[].text`, which the build already has and
`getRenderedText()` does not read — the opening draws five strings the accessor cannot see:

```
"5"   "0"   "LIGHT"   "Spark-Dart"   "Take A tithe-gourd, empty"
```

**M9's domain is "every string rendered *outside* a dialogue/journal/book surface."** That is exactly
the surface it cannot see. W1-26's own probe records `surfaces_searched: 'title (the only
non-dialogue drawn surface in the build)'`, which is false.

Per §0.1(a) I score **M9 and M15 `unmeasurable ⇒ 0`** rather than passing a grep over a set that
excludes its own subject. And `"Take A tithe-gourd, empty"` is an imperative second-person
interaction prompt of the *press X to Y* family O11 sets to zero, with the button name omitted — the
first clause of M9's own grep, which is prose and not a substring list. **This is round 2's failure —
a grep over an empty accessibility tree reading as a clean pass — reproduced one layer down by the
amendment written to close it.**
