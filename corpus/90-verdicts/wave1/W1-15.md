## The S21 ruling, which is the interesting one, and which the piece got right

`ARBITRATION` seam **S21** says keep the die where failure is permanent and delete it where failure
is a retry, and it names `RI-STL02` as the item to reconcile — because `RI-STL02` §5 says the
pickpocket has *no* roll while S21 says it keeps one. `ARBITRATION` §5 puts the seam above the
item's own method. The build followed the seam and documented the precedence rather than quietly
picking one. **The item's text is now stale and should be corrected.**

**Lockpicking — the die is deleted, and something was built in its place.** Measured live:

- The gate is deterministic and absolute: at Security 39 against a tier-3 lock (`req` 40) the
  interaction is **not offered**, 0 picks consumed, with a spoken prompt.
- The tolerance curve reads **4.00 / 12.25 / 20.50 / 34.00 degrees** at Security 40 / 55 / 70 / 100
  and clamps at exactly 34.0 at Security 140 — a 44 ms window at the requirement (2.67 f@60,
  roughly a Souls parry) opening to 378 ms at mastery. It is an execution challenge, not a button.
- A tier-3 collar in the loaded world **opened under three timed `interact` presses** over
  153 f@60 — the interaction exists in the game, not only in the module.
- **The engine's RNG counter moved by 0 across the entire live interaction.** Not "seeded" — zero
  draws. That is the assertion `RI-STL02` method 3 exists to make, and it is the one that catches
  a die returning through a side door as pick-break luck or collar jitter.
- The angles are authored, not generated: **719 angles over 235 locks, χ² = 226.1 on 35 df** against
  a uniform-rejection threshold of 57.34, 0 length mismatches, 0 duplicate vectors within an
  interior. 59 / 59 tier-4 and tier-5 locks carry `alt_routes`.

**Pickpocketing — the die is kept, and it is drawn, and it matters.** 60 seeds × 5 skill levels:

| Sneak | `notice_chance` | caught / 60 | RNG draws |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 0.357 | 19 | 60 |
| 25 | 0.278 | 17 | 60 |
| 45 | 0.209 | 15 | 60 |
| 70 | 0.137 | 8 | 60 |
| 100 | 0.070 | 6 | 60 |

Exactly one draw per completed hold, 300 of 300; the curve stays live across the whole skill range
rather than collapsing to a floor; and getting caught routes to a real crime record. This is S21
applied, not copied — and the builder's own note about rejecting a subtractive form that pinned at
2% by Sneak 45 is borne out by the shipped curve.

## The single biggest gap

> **`GAP-W1-stealth-crime-model-not-coupled-to-the-world`** — the stealth and crime models are
> computed *beside* the world instead of *by* it, and the coupling measures exactly zero at the two
> places the reference items make load-bearing: an INFANTRY reaches AGGRO in 0.500 s identically at
> `V` = 0.6669 and `V` = 0.0500 (the item says 1.03 s and 26.7 s), and an ALARM civilian watching a
> theft from 3 m produces `witnesses: []` and a bounty of 0.

**Why it matters:** nothing a player does with this piece has a consequence. You cannot sneak past
an enemy, because the darkness it took a whole lighting model to compute never reaches the thing
hunting you; and you can rob every house in Gideon in front of its owner and never acquire a bounty,
which puts the 17-crime schedule, the report chain, the three arrest answers, jail and the S10
decision all out of reach in play.

**The remedy is two call sites in code this piece already owns.**

**(A)** In `game/src/sim/entities.js`'s perception branch, replace `e.alert += 4` with
`e.alert += baseFill(sim.stealth.p.V, d, e.sight_radius_m) / 60` from
`game/src/sim/stealth/detection.js`, peripheral cone scaled ×0.45 per `RI-AI01` §B; add
`alert_channel` to the enemy trace record; route `soundRadiusFor()` into the same fill on the
hearing channel.
**Done when:** `RI-STL01` method 2, run exactly as written — one INFANTRY facing the player at 8 m,
the five configurations of §2, first frame of `alert_state == "AGGRO"` — lands within 5% of
{1.03, 3.94, 8.5, 26.7, 26.7} s, **and** method 4 (crouched, unlit, still, 1.0 m behind a stationary
enemy, 3,600 f@60) ends with `alert == 0.000`.

**(B)** In `game/src/sim/stealth/system.js`'s fixed step, whenever a crime record is created
(`takeObject`, `lockBegin`, a blow on a non-hostile, standing over a corpse), iterate the live
civilians and guards and call `this.crime.witness(crime.id, …)` for each whose `civ_state` advanced
to CHALLENGE or ALARM this frame with `V ≥ 0.10` and `dist ≤ 1.4 × R(npc)`, setting `identified`
from `V ≥ 0.30`; then drive `reportRoute()` from that entity's own path to the nearest guard entity
and flip it to the fleeing behaviour.
**Done when:** `RI-CRM01` method 3 passes end to end with **zero** `addWitness()` calls in the
script — a `witness` event on the crime frame, `reported == false`, bounty 0 until a `report` event
fires, and the 120 m run landing within 10% of 35.3 s.

Both are re-measurable with `node tools/harness/critic-w1-15.mjs`, whose
`RI-STL01-M2-enemy-detection` and `RI-CRM01-M3-witness-from-the-world` blocks were written for

## A closing note, because 2/10 will read as a verdict on effort

It is not. This is the most instrumented piece I have measured — 42 outputs, three probe suites,
191 self-assertions, and three genuine defects found in the *reference items themselves*, one of
which I recomputed from scratch and which is real. The lock ruling is exemplary: a deterministic
skill gate, a real timed interaction with a measured 44 ms window at the requirement, 719 hand-
authored ward angles, and an RNG counter that does not move once. The property data is the best in
the repository. Seam S21 was reasoned through rather than copied, and I measured the surviving die
working.

What failed is not care and it is not craft. The piece built its systems as modules with harness
entry points, never took the last step of making an entity in the running game read them, and then
measured the modules and reported the result as the game. The distance between those two things is
the whole verdict.
