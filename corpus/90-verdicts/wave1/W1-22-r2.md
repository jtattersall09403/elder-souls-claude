## 2. `AMENDMENT-W1-22-01` §A: the numbers are right, the arithmetic is wrong, and the build has
   already done the thing the amendment says it refused to do

### 2.1 The three reported figures reproduce

### 2.2 The derivation rests on an assumed number that its own data measures

§A's argument is:

> A short grain carries 6–10 dB of its own peak-to-RMS within its envelope, so the event's *window*
> level has to sit near **+10 dB relative to the bed**.

That second term — the grain's own peak-to-window-RMS — is not an assumption that has to be made.
It is `(peak_dbfs − bed_rms_dbfs) − rel_db`, and every one of those three columns is in the report
the amendment is quoting. Measured over **419 events**:

| | min | p10 | median | p90 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| grain peak-to-RMS (dB) | 4.41 | 7.46 | **10.94** | **17.30** | 23.02 |

**61.6 % of the shipped events sit above the 10 dB the amendment takes as its ceiling.** Because the
required event level is `20 dB − (that quantity)`:

| grain | required level rel. bed | §A's ceiling (with the tool's ±2 dB tolerance) |
|---|---|---|
| amendment's assumption | +10.0 dB | L3 +4, L4 +6 |
| measured **median** grain | +9.06 dB | — |
| measured **p90** grain | **+2.70 dB** | **inside the band** |

### 2.3 And the build contains the counterexamples already

Two beds get within a decibel of the judge's bar with **every event inside §A's band**:

| bed | crest | short of 20 dB by | loudest event, rel. bed |
|---|---|---|---|
| salt-hills (day and night) | 19.5 dB | 0.5 dB | **+1.49 dB** |
| thornmarsh (day and night) | 19.1 dB | 0.9 dB | **+0.01 dB** |

So "reaching the judge's bar means placing the events six to eight decibels outside the band" is
**false as a general claim**. It is true of the *median* grain and the amendment presents it as
true of the design space. The route to a high crest factor is a sharper grain, which is a
sound-design choice; it is not only a louder one, which is a mix change. Two beds with events dead
centre in §A's band are 0.5 and 0.9 dB short of a bar the amendment calls unreachable.

### 2.4 The harder half — the band has already been widened in fact

The amendment is emphatic that it has not taken its own Option 2:

> I am not proposing to widen §A's band, and I have not widened it. … Each item names what the
> build does *today*, which in every case is the conservative reading.

That is not what the build does today. The Hive ships `chitin_scrape` at **+10.23 and +10.61 dB
relative to the bed** — 8.6 dB outside §A's L3 cap of +2, which is precisely the "+10 dB" its own
arithmetic predicts would be needed. And that single event is the *only* reason the Hive clears the
judge's bar: at 23.3 dB of crest, removing it drops the clip to 19.1 dB.

Gate `O3_level_in_band` reports `pass: true` with `failures: []` on that same render, because **O3
grades the layer median**, and the Hive's eleven quiet `cell_tick`s hold that median at −2.00 dB.

**Ruling on §A: the tension it describes is real but overstated, and the resolution it proposes is
moot, because the build has already silently taken Option 2 for the one bed that clears the bar.**
Neither Option 1 nor Option 2 should be adopted as written. The defect to fix is §2.5.

§A's "Level (rel. bed)" column is a claim about **events**. It is enforced against a **median**, by
a gate whose corrective trim (`event_gain_db`) is also a single per-layer number. The statistic, the
gate and the fix are all the same shape, and none of them can see an individual event. Fix the gate
before arguing about the band.

## 3. The detector's refusal: real code, unreachable for its own purpose

`tools/analysis/ambience-onsets.mjs:416` guards **every subtraction the tool performs**:

```js
if (!muteWorks.ok || muteWorks.muted !== 0) {
  console.error('… Every subtraction below would be vacuous. Refusing to report.');
  process.exit(2);
}
```

The guard is the right idea: the whole measurement is `full − muted`, so if `mute` were ignored the
residual would be silence and every level in the report would be fiction.

**It checks that the muted arm fired nothing. It never checks that the unmuted arm fired anything.**
Its fixture is `blackwood, seconds: 4, sampleRate: 8000`, and the shipped report records:

```json
"mute_supported": { "full": 0, "muted": 0, "ok": true }
```

Both arms empty. Blackwood's L3 declares `interval_s: [8, 24]`; four seconds is shorter than the
shortest interval band in the province, so no bed schedules an event in the fixture whatever `mute`
does. A control whose two arms are both empty distinguishes nothing.

So the refusal **is** a real state on a reachable branch — it is not an absence dressed as a
decision in the sense of being unimplemented — but it is unreachable *for the reason it exists*: at
the shipped fixture it cannot be triggered by a broken `mute`, only by a capture error.

**Falsification, measured.** I ran the guard's own call four ways — a real `mute` and a **no-op
mute** (layer names that mute nothing), at the shipped fixture and at a duration long enough for
blackwood to schedule an event:

| fixture | `full.fired` | `muted.fired`, real mute | `muted.fired`, no-op mute | guard passes real / no-op |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **shipped: 4 s** | **0** | 0 | **0** | **true / true** — cannot tell them apart |
| 60 s | **3** | 0 | **3** | **true / false** — refuses, as designed |

The top row is the finding: at the shipped fixture a working `mute` and a `mute` that does
absolutely nothing produce **the same verdict**, so the guard cannot be failed by the defect it
exists to catch. The bottom row is the proof that the refusal is a genuine reachable state and not
an absence dressed up as a decision — given events, it fires correctly. **The guard is not broken;
its fixture is.**

**Remedy, one line:** assert `full.fired > 0` beside `muted.fired === 0`, and size the fixture to
the longest interval band in the province rather than to 4 seconds.

## 4. CONSUMPTION — `RI-MTH07` / `ARBITRATION.md` §3

**Result: the ambience model has NO entity-side consumer, demonstrated by perturbation against a
positive control that moved.** It does have an audio-side consumer, and the distinction is the
whole of §4.4.

### 4.1 The static position

`game/src/sim/**` and `game/src/ai/**` contain **zero** references to ambience. Every importer of
`game/src/audio/ambience.js` is on the audio or harness side: `engine.js` (constructs the driver and
steps it from `_afterStep`), `harness/api.js`, `audio/synth.js`, `main.js`. The dependency runs
world → audio: `_afterStep()` writes position, region, time of day and weather **into** the driver,
and `getAmbienceState()` / `ambienceCapture()` / `ambienceLog()` read it back out for instruments.

The perception model that *does* make entities behave differently because of sound —
`soundRadius()` in `game/src/sim/stealth/detection.js` — is a **separate model** that reads
`d.detection` and has no connection to the beds. Two sound systems; the one entities read is not
the one W1-22 ships.

### 4.2 The builder's evidence measures the wrong thing

`reports/w1-22/ambience-interior-consumption.json` perturbs `filter.hz` in a bed and reports
`spectral_distance` in the render. That is perturbing the audio model and observing **the audio**.
It is exactly the failure mode §3 names — *"a probe that calls a function measures the function"* —
and it cannot distinguish a bed the world reads from a bed nothing reads.

### 4.3 The perturbation, with a control that moves

`tools/audio/critic-w1-22-r2-probe.mjs` P2. One scenario, four arms, same seed, same frame count,
89 entities in the scene, the player walking (rule 8 — a still target hides every steering defect).
The readout is **never audio**: it is `getStateHash()` at six points through the run, every entity's
position, and the stealth alert value.

| arm | fields perturbed | world changed? |
|---|---|---|
| `null` | 0 | — (baseline) |
| **`ambience`** — every layer `level_db` to −60, every `event_gain_db` to 0, every event list emptied, every emitter removed, every `bed_gain_db` to −60 | **177** | **NO — every hash, position and alert identical** |
| `null` re-run *after* the destructive arm | 0 | identical to the first `null` — the in-place restore is sound, so the next line is not an artefact of a broken teardown |
| **`control`** — the stealth detection sound-radius table scaled ×8 | 21 | **YES — diverges at the first sample and stays diverged** |

The control arm is the falsification handle and it fired. A "no consumer" reading would have been
inadmissible without it, and the probe is written to exit 2 and say so rather than report a null
result it could not have distinguished from a broken instrument.

### 4.4 The ruling, and where I decline to apply §3 mechanically

`ARBITRATION.md` §3 says a model with no demonstrated consumer is *"unmeasurable ⇒ 0, exactly as a
missing model is"*. Applied literally to an audio subsystem that would zero this piece, and it
would zero every audio piece in the project regardless of quality, because **"an entity changes
behaviour" is the wrong test for a subsystem whose output is sound**. I am not going to pretend the
doctrine fits where it does not, so I will say exactly what is and is not true:

- **The model IS consumed, on the audio path.** `AmbienceDriver` is constructed in the engine
  constructor and stepped every frame from `_afterStep()`; `renderBedOffline()` turns it into PCM,
  and I measured 413 real events out of it at HEAD. This is *not* the "sixteen subsystems that
  shipped a model nothing reads" failure. Round 1's finding — that the R7 emitters produced no
  sound on any reachable path — was that failure, and it has been fixed.
- **The model has no consumer anywhere else in the world**, and that is now measured rather than
  assumed. Nothing an entity does depends on it, and nothing it does depends on faction, quest or
  disposition state.

So: **CONSUMPTION passes on the audio path and the piece is `seam_sterile: true`** — reported under
AR-3, where §3 itself says internal pieces belong, rather than scored 0 under a clause written for
a different failure. What I will not do is let the builder's `spectral_distance` evidence stand as
a consumption demonstration: it measures the audio model with the audio model, and the real
demonstration is the one in §4.3.

## 6. In-band-then-buried: **not fixed — fixed at the median**

This is the defect the piece exists to close, and it is the biggest gap.

### 6.1 The chain, end to end, by measurement

Three gain stages reach an event: the layer's `level_db`, the event's own `synth.gain_db`
(−5 to −13, **per event**), and the corrective `event_gain_db` (**per layer**).

A single per-layer trim can move a layer's whole spread up or down. It cannot, in principle, unbury
an event that is quieter than its layer-mates. Measured at HEAD (`tools/audio/critic-w1-22-r2-bands.mjs`):

| | measured |
|---|---|
| events outside §A's band (with the tool's own ±2 dB tolerance) | **54 of 413** |
| of those, **buried** below the floor | **33** |
| of those, **shouting** above the ceiling | 21 |
| bed/layer pairs passing O3's median while holding an out-of-band event | **11 of 33** |
| between-event spread within one layer, median over 11 multi-event layers | **10.82 dB** |
| worst | **17.48 dB** |

The worst individual events:

| bed / layer / event | rendered level, rel. bed | outside §A by |
|---|---|---|
| deep-marshes / L3 / `marsh_gas_release` | **−30.37 dB** | 22.37 dB |
| blackwood / L3 / `distant_axe` | **−23.10 dB** | 15.10 dB |
| well / L3 / `stone_shift` | −16.3 dB | 8.98 dB |
| dungeon / L3 / `block_settle` | −11.1 dB | 5.52 dB |

`blackwood/distant_axe` is one of the three sounds that bed's **own `brief` field advertises**
("canopy drip; a solid wall of insect noise; **distant axe-strokes**"). It renders 23 dB under the
bed. The round-1 defect — declared in band, buried by a second gain stage — is still present on
the shipped tree for individual events; what changed is that the *median* now passes.

### 6.2 The declared numbers still do not predict the sound

`level_db + synth.gain_db + event_gain_db` against the rendered level, per event id:

| median &#124;error&#124; | p90 | max | outside ±3 dB |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.00 dB | 14.19 dB | 20.78 dB | **30 of 47** |

The tool's own diagnosis — *"loudness is not the sum of the numbers you typed"* — is still true
after the fix that was written to answer it.

## 7. The blind pack

### 7.1 The r2 pack leaked the answer three ways and must not be cited

I audited `reports/w1-22-critic/r2/pack` **before** spending a judge on it — the discipline a
sibling critic established this morning when it voided the r4 prose pack for redaction marks that
ranked the classes. It is well designed in every other respect (measurement only, loudness *and*

the bytes on disk). It leaks anyway:

| # | tell | strength |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Serialised length.** Numbers were written to whatever width they needed. &#124;len(A)−len(B)&#124; has median 46 bytes on SAME trials and 86 on DIFFERENT. | a single threshold on **byte count alone** recovers **0.769** of the key (chance 0.500) |
| 2 | **Time of day *is* the class.** All 13 SAME trials are (day,day) or (day,night); 8 of 13 DIFFERENT are (night,night); **no DIFFERENT trial mixes the two.** | "the sides differ in time of day" ⇒ SAME, with certainty |
| 3 | **Sides never swapped.** A is always capture-seed index 0, and in all 13 DIFFERENT trials the alphabetically earlier region. | no direct leak (names are hidden) but the pack has no side randomisation at all |

Tell 1 alone lets a reader score 20/26 without understanding a single field.

### 7.2 What I checked for

Region and time-of-day strings anywhere in the bytes (none); A byte-identical to B (none); array
shapes (uniform, 6×120 and 120 everywhere); numeric precision (uniform); per-trial fields that
could carry the class (none); trial ordering against the truth sequence; A/B side balance;
serialised size per recording; and the best accuracy any single threshold could reach on each of
six mechanical features.

### 7.3 The rebuilt pack

`tools/audio/critic-w1-22-r2-pack.mjs` → `reports/packs/w1-22-r3-hard`:

- **Fixed-width serialisation.** Every value is a signed fixed-width dB string (`"+03.4"`), so

- **Time of day drawn independently on every side of every trial**, in both classes.

- **DIFFERENT pairs drawn from AMENDMENT §B's four duplicate triples** — spectrum-matched

## 8. What holds, and holds well

**The determinism fix is the strongest evidence in the piece, and the root cause is proved against
the platform rather than asserted about it.** `D0_platform_control` drives **bare browser audio
nodes with no project code involved**: summing 4 and 8 oscillators onto one node is non-identical
run to run (max 3.0×10⁻⁸ and 4.5×10⁻⁸, 12 901 and 18 219 nonzero samples of 32 000); the same
oscillators summed two at a time down a chain are bit-identical at every arity tested (1, 2, 3, 4,
8). It reproduces identically in the clean and the sabotage arms, which is what a platform property
should do. That is a genuine IEEE-754 non-associativity result, and it is unusually well shown.

**Delete-the-fix: 8 of 20 beds, not 2.** `--sabotage flatmix` restores the flat mixing topology and
`D1` lists 8 failing beds of 20 measured — crimson-coast, deep-marshes, eastern-rootlands, street,
well, stone-forest, valus-ridge, western-rootlands. (`pass: true` in that file is not a green build:
under `flatmix` the pass predicate is `d1.length > 0`, i.e. the falsifier is *required* to fire, and
it did. Note the bar would be met by **one** bed; the count 8 is reported, not gated.)

The round-2 critic's independent reproduction — 40/40 stored digests reproduced exactly from a
different tool, process, browser launch and commit, in reverse capture order — stands and I have
not repeated it.
