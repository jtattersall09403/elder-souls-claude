## The one-paragraph answer to the question that was asked

**The journal is closest to a pass, and what stands between it and one is two unrun checks, not a
defect.** It scores **10 of 12** — ladder **6** — and the two it misses are `JU5` (the append-only
byte-diff, which I did not run) and `JU12` (a blind pair I could not build without a fresh judge).

spells]` while the inventory, sheet and spells screens all advertise the map. The map exclusion is
journal-specific and deliberate.

**Second closest is the inventory**, and its best part is the part the corpus expects everyone to
fail. `RI-UIX03`'s §How-we-lose predicts `if (menuOpen) return;` at the top of the update loop as the
one-line deletion of seam S14. It is not there: the frame counter advances **0 of 120** with the
screen open out of combat and **exactly 120 of 120** with it open in a fight, the panel renders at
0.50 opacity over 57.2% of the frame, and 89.9% of the centre box's pixels move with the world under
the open menu. That is the whole of §A, measured, passing. What drags the item to **8 of 13** is data
and one broken screen, not design.

**The out-of-combat HUD is the real gap and it is the piece's biggest.** `ui.hud.world` has **1 of
its 6 elements**, and the one it has is at `[1782, 84, 96, 96]` — the top-right corner, where a
modern action-RPG puts its minimap, when `RI-UIX07` §D1 requires the bottom half of the frame and its
own §A measures Morrowind's entire persistent HUD along the bottom edge. `RI-UIX07` predicted both in
writing as R1 and R2 before any of this was measured.
