### Whose fault is it? — the W1-09 attribution is correct

The W1-09 round-3 builder attributed the spear and halberd gaps to this piece's roster movesets.
I tested that directly and **it holds**. The gap moves when W1-10-owned data moves:


The mechanism is `game/data/weapons/classes.json`'s `hitbox_span_m`, a per-class field this round

shaft can damage. A target standing in the shaft band is *inside* the blade's near end and
*outside* the S26 body corridor, so nothing tests it. That is also why five classes fail
`reach_min_m ≤ 0.60`: AXE 0.32, MCE 0.30, GHM 0.34 and HLB 0.75 are the smallest spans in the game.

**Not confined to spear and halberd**, which the W1-09 report did not know: `axe_bog_cleaver` has

## The single biggest gap

**`GAP-W1-weapon-impact-and-material-model-has-no-consumer`** · `weapon.feel.material` · **blocking**

**What.** The RI-WPN05 impact and material model is a data island, exactly as the 87 movesets were
in round 1. Six perturbations of the shipped tables; **five change nothing**:

| Perturbation of `game/data/weapons/classes.json` | Result |
|---|---|
| `hitstop.attacker.*.flesh` 8 → 40 | **CONSUMED** — hitstop held 3 f → 39 f |
| `hitstop.attacker.*.stone` → 99 | **INERT** |
| `hitstop.attacker.*.metal/chitin/wood/shield` → 99 | **INERT** |
| `materials.multipliers` **all** → 0.01 | **INERT** — damage still exactly 118 |
| `hitstop.knockback_m` all → 9 | **INERT** |
| `hitstop.deflect.hitstop_multiplier` 1.5 → 20 | **INERT** |

Only the `flesh` column is read, and it is read from a hard-coded lookup:

```js
// game/src/combat/moves.js:166
hitstop_frames: (s.hitstop_f || lib.classes.hitstop.attacker[moveset.weight_tier] || {}).flesh || 6
```

`moves.js:165` stores `hitstop_f_table` on the move and **nothing ever reads it**. And a grep across
`game/src/` for `materialMultiplier|deflects|knockbackFor|victimHitstopFor` returns exactly one
file:

```
game/src/harness/weapons.js
```

That is round 1's sentence verbatim — *"`MovesetLibrary` was imported by exactly one file — the
harness"* — surviving one layer down. There is also nothing for the model to apply to: **0 of the 14
enemy statblocks** in `game/data/combat/enemies/` declares a material at all.

**Why it matters.** From the player's chair, a mace, an axe and a spear hit a stone golem, a
chitin-shelled beast and a Hist-bonded plant creature with identical feedback: same hitstop, same
knockback, no deflection, no damage-type advantage. The 5×7 grid that round 1 called "the table
working" is a table nobody consults.

It matters a second time because the material system is this area's **only** seam crossing.
`WEAPON-CRITIC` §7 is explicit: AR-3 here *is* RI-WPN05 §B's materials — a lore fact
(root-golems are stone, the Hist-bonded are plant) that is also a combat answer, and a merchant
selling a mace selling a solution to an encounter. Unread, the crossing does not exist, and this
area becomes internally excellent and externally sterile.

And it matters a third time because of *how it survived*: the builder fixed the half of the piece
round 1's gap named, and the impact half was never re-examined. Every probe that calls the library
reports a healthy grid, because a probe that calls a function measures the function.

**Remedy.**
1. `game/data/combat/enemies/*.json` — add a `material` to all 14 statblocks. RI-WPN05 §B already
   writes down the lore assignment.
2. `game/src/combat/resolve.js` — at the frame a hit resolves, look up the victim's material and
   call `MovesetLibrary.hitstopFor` / `victimHitstopFor` / `knockbackFor` / `materialMultiplier` /
   `deflects`, instead of consuming the pre-baked `move.hitstop_frames`.
3. `game/src/combat/moves.js:165-166` — stop collapsing the per-slot hitstop table to its `flesh`
   column; pass `hitstop_f_table` through to the resolver, which already stores it and never reads it.
4. `game/src/sim/record.js` — expose the resolved material and resolved hitstop on the impact record
   so the grid is observable rather than inferred.

**Acceptance.** `node corpus/90-verdicts/wave1/artifacts/W1-10-r2/kritik-consumption3.mjs` reports
**6 of 6 CONSUMED**; the 5×7 attacker hitstop grid is recoverable from traces against seven
material-carrying dummies with every cell differing from the flesh column where §A says it should;
`ILS ≥ 0.80` **on the observed column**; and AR-3 `seam_sterile` becomes false with a driven
demonstration — a mace beating a stone enemy that a rapier cannot. Estimated size **M**.

## Bar pressure

1. **Is a passing score the thing the user asked for?** Not yet — and the shape of the answer
   changed this round. Taken at random, `hlb_saltcutter` differs from its class baseline by a longer
   sweep and a 0.495 m lunge, and a player cannot feel either, because the halberd's blade reaches
   **1.45 m against a declared 2.865 m** and has a hole in the middle of the band. The roster *is*
   subtly unique on paper. The two channels through which a player would feel it — where the weapon
   reaches, and what it feels like when it lands — are the two that are broken. The bar is right; it
   is measuring the right thing for the first time, which is why the score fell rather than rose.

2. **Which threshold does no work?** `SEP`. Its within-class ceiling is dominated by whichever
   same-class pair shares the most clips, which the `SHARE` cap already bounds, so it is largely a
   restatement of `ARI` with a different denominator. It has not been the deciding number in either
   round.

3. **Which threshold is gameable, and how cheaply?** `arc_sweep_deg` — and this round proves it is
   not hypothetical, because it is *already wrong*. D6 and G3 read a declared JSON field that 24 of
   28 clips do not honour, so a one-line `sed` over the SPR movesets moves `D_min`, `Dg_min` and the
   M5 shape census without touching a frame of animation. **Concrete proposed amendment, RI-WPN02
   §D row D6:** arc must be *measured from hitbox records and never read from the declaration*, with
   a near-axis guard and a two-estimator cross-check, and **FAIL if more than 2 of the 14 class
   baselines' `r1.1` arcs differ from their declaration by more than 10°.** Full replacement text in
   the verdict JSON. This is the same discipline S26 just imposed on reach, applied to the other
   geometric column.

4. **The tenth-hour version.** A player ten hours in complains that swings which visually connect do
   not damage. Nothing in the nine numbers asks how often a swing at a stationary target at ordinary
   spacing lands — and the blind pack answered it by accident: 0–4 hits from ~10 attacks per trace,
   two traces landing nothing. **Proposed new measure `CR` (connect rate):** the fraction of
   scripted `r1.1` presses that damage a stationary dummy at the class's own declared reach minus
   0.20 m; floor **0.95**. One line of harness work, and it would have failed this build in round 1.

5. **Is the roster the right size?** Yes, and more confidently than round 1 could say it. `ARI`
   0.4533 sits mid-band under the amended floor, the share histogram tops out at four, and the
   runtime motion-only blind pack — a far harder test than round 1's — still separated one class of
   three perfectly and yielded four real within-class behavioural differences. The remedy is not
   fewer weapons. It is the same weapons with hit volumes that match their blades and impact that
   reads the thing it hits.

**A second amendment is filed** against RI-WPN05 §F: `ILS`'s published nearest-neighbour classifier
recovers **zero** by construction over 35 distinct points, so the headline number is unrunnable as
written. Replacement text in the verdict JSON.

## Would the eighty-seven feel different in a player's hands?

The user's words are the bar: *"each weapon has a subtly unique attack pattern and unique set of
animations."*

**Partly, and less than the numbers say.** Two-handing genuinely changes the weapon — the tip
travels a different path by 0.40 m on a dagger and 3.05 m on an ultra greatsword. A roll-attack, a
backstep-attack, a running attack and a jump attack are four different animations with four
different frame triples, on every one of the 87, which is exactly what the user asked for and it was
not true eight hours ago. Three classes handed to me as raw motion produced three describable
fighting styles.

**But a mace and an axe would be told apart by weight, not by grammar** — they are still the closest
pair in the declared matrix — and on measured motion the nearest pairs are MCE–HLB at 1.393 and
AXE–HLB at 1.395, both under the bar. A **rapier and a great hammer** confused me on one trace each
when the spreadsheet was taken away. Every hit against every material in the game feels identical.
And a spear — the class whose entire identity is spacing — cannot hit a man standing 1.4 m in front
of it.

The animations are real and the grammar is real. What a player's hands would find is a roster whose
differences live slightly to one side of where the data says they are.
