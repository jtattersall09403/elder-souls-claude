## 5. NEW — the summon fix permanently mutates the archetype it scales

`bindHandler` scales four fields on the spawned body, and one of them is not the body's:

```js
if (body.moves && body.moves._weapon) body.moves._weapon.attack_rating = Math.max(1, Math.round(body.moves._weapon.attack_rating * power));
```

The comment above it reads *"`moves._weapon` is a per-body COPY (combat/moves.js §wpn) —
scaling it here scales this summon and nothing else in the game."* **That is true of the
player's roster path and false of every enemy.** `combat/moves.js` copies (`{ ...lib.weaponFor(…) }`);
`combat/enemy.js:47` — which is what `CombatSystem.spawnEnemy` actually calls — does

```js
const out = { _weapon: weapon, _movesetId: `enemy:${stat.id}`, … };
```

with `weapon = stat.weapon` (`system.js:249`), the object loaded once from
`game/data/combat/enemies/drowned_lesser.json`. There is no copy. The write is in place, on the
statblock, for the rest of the session.

**Six identical casts of one commissioned spell — `bind_lesser`, magnitude 40, `power` 2.22 on
every cast, Focus topped up between them so none can be refused, no perturbation of any kind:**

| cast | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| summon `attack_rating` | **191** | **424** | **941** | **2089** | **4638** | **10296** |
| summon `hp_max` | 577 | 577 | 577 | 577 | 577 | 577 |

That is 86 — `drowned_lesser.json`'s own declared `weapon.attack_rating` — multiplied by 2.22
six times, to four significant figures at every step. **The sixth summon hits 54× harder than
the first, from the same button.** In the opposite direction, five casts at magnitude 1
(`power` 0.25) run the same weapon *down*: 22, 22, 6, 6, 2, and the declared 86 never comes back.

`hp_max` is constant, because `hpMax` is rebuilt from `stat.hp` on every spawn. **Only
`attack_rating` leaks — and `hp_max` is the one field the builder's own AR-1 arm checked** ("20
identical casts from an identical arena produced 20 identical summons (one distinct hp_max)").
The four-field scaling law in `effects.json`'s `summon.scales` block is therefore three fields
and a global side effect, and because `stat.weapon` is the object the *statblock* owns, the
mutation is not confined to summons: every `drowned_lesser` the world spawns for the rest of the
session carries it. I could not confirm that last step from the outside because
`attack_rating` is not exposed on `getCombatState().enemies[]`, and I record it as inferred from
`combat/enemy.js:47` rather than measured.

This is not AR-1 (nothing rolls; it is perfectly deterministic and perfectly path-dependent),
and I am not diluting AR-1 to cover it — S26 forbids that. It is charged to RI-MAG02 and
RI-MAG06, and it is the reason RI-MAG06 falls from 6 to 5 in a round that otherwise improved it.

## 7. NEW — a `touch` spell casts itself four to seven times

`MagicSystem._spawnContact` sets `ticksEveryF: 1` and `activeTo = frame + classes[class].active`;
the volume loop at `system.js:843` applies the effect to every body inside the sphere on every
tick, with no per-target dedupe. So the number of times a touch spell lands is the class's
active-frame count.

`damage_health`, magnitude 20 — declared output 40 — against one stationary body, same arena,
only the range and the class varying:

| class | `touch` | `target` | `projectile` |
|---|---|---|---|
| CANTRIP | **4 applications, 176 damage** | 1, 44 | 1, 44 |
| LIGHT | **5 applications, 220 damage** | 1, 44 | 1, 44 |
| HEAVY | **7 applications, 308 damage** | 1, 44 | 1, 44 |

A HEAVY touch spell delivers **7.7×** its own declared output, and `focusBase()` charges
`range_mult` **0.85** for `touch` against 1.00 for `target` — the cheapest range in the table is
also the strongest by a factor of five to eight. `absorb_health` at magnitude 60 healed its
caster from 80 to a capped 300 on one cast for the same reason.

This also contaminates measurement, which is why it belongs in this section and not only in the
gap list: **both the census and the dials probe prefer `touch`** — `rangeFor`'s order is
`['self', 'touch', 'target', 'projectile', 'area_at_range']` for every non-area effect — so a
large part of the catalogue has been measured through a four-to-seven-times applicator for three
rounds.

**And it independently reproduces the superseded verdict's blocking gap from the other side.**
`crit-w1-14-r3-9f2c` found that a bolt does not connect with a body that moves. I found the
converse control it needed: against a *stationary* body all five damage effects connect at
`target` range (`damage_health` 44, `fire_damage` 40, `frost_damage` 38, `shock_damage` 40,
`poison_damage` 27, magnitude 20 each), and in my part A — where both bodies were aggroed and
therefore moving — `fire_damage` came back `NOT_DELIVERED` while `damage_health` delivered. That
gap is **open** and is carried forward as this verdict's second-most-serious finding.

## 8. Duration: the six blind dials are the real Morrowind gap, and here is round 4's number

The builder is right about this and identifies it as the largest thing it did not fix. The
mechanism is one line: `applyEffects` sets `remaining_f = round(duration_s × 60)` and pushes the
row onto `M.active`; `step()` decrements it and, at zero, calls `_undo`. A row is **a lease on a
mutation**, which is exactly right for `feather` and exactly wrong for `fire_damage`. In
Morrowind, *Fire Damage 10 pts for 5 s* is 50 damage delivered as a per-second tick; here the
duration on `fire_damage`, `frost_damage`, `poison_damage` and `restore_health` decides only how
long an unread row sits in a list.

**And it is now measured rather than inferred.** `fire_damage`, magnitude 20, one stationary
body, hp sampled every 20 f@60 for 600 f@60, duration the only thing that varies:

| duration | applications | damage | hp curve |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **0 s** | 1 | 40 | 412 → 372, flat for 580 frames |
| **5 s** | 1 | 40 | **byte-identical to the 0 s curve** |
| **30 s** | 1 | 40 | **byte-identical to the 0 s curve** |

One step, no staircase, no second tick, and the 30-second lease produces exactly what the
instantaneous cast produces. `restore_health` at 0 s and 30 s likewise moves a full-health body
by nothing at all. In Morrowind the 30-second row would have been **1080 damage** against this
40. The player is charged for the duration by `focusBase()`'s `0.7·D^0.95` and `0.55·M^0.80·D^0.45`
terms and receives nothing for it.

**Acceptance for round 4** — write it as a probe assertion, not as prose:

> For each of `fire_damage`, `frost_damage`, `poison_damage`, `restore_health`, at magnitude `M`
> and durations `D ∈ {0, 5, 30}` s, against one stationary body, sampling hp every 20 f@60 for
> 600 f@60 (short enough that out-of-combat regeneration cannot answer the question — this is
> the trap that voided my own first duration run):
> 1. **`total(D=0)` is one step and `total(D>0)` is a staircase of `D` steps 60 f@60 apart.**
> 2. **`total(D) / total(0)` = `D` within ±1 hp**, i.e. `N` points for `M` seconds is `N × M`.
> 3. `M.active` carries the row for exactly `D × 60` frames and the last tick lands on the last
>    frame of the lease, not after it.
> 4. The dial probe re-run reports `duration_blind` **≤ 2** of 37 (the two survivors being
>    `hist_sight` and `paralyse`, whose magnitudes are already denominated in seconds).
>
> Today's reading against criterion 2 is **`total(30)/total(0) = 1.00`**, on curves that are
> byte-identical.
> 5. `--break=notick` restores today's behaviour and the probe goes red on all four.

## 11. Spell-making — the model is Morrowind's; the game has no way in

The brief asks whether a player can build a spell out of the dials as Morrowind's system does.

**The model can, and it is good.** `quoteSpell`/`makeSpell` take a set of effects, each with its
own magnitude, duration and area, plus a shared range and cast class; there is no whitelist
anywhere in the build; the four gates in `enchanting.json` are the only things that refuse, and
three of them state a number. I commissioned a two-effect area spell that appears nowhere in
`spells.json` — `fire_damage` 25 pts / 6 s / 5 m plus `demoralise` 20 / 10 s / 5 m, HEAVY class,
`area_at_range` — for 9,187 g at tier 3, `focus_base` 64, and it came back with a real
`volume` geometry of radius 5. The clamps behave as declared: `damage_health` at magnitude 999,
duration 40 s, area 9 m is *made*, silently corrected to magnitude 200 / duration 0 / area 0
because that effect's own row forbids the other two. Commissioned spells appear in `saveState()`.
This is a genuinely combinatorial system and it satisfies RI-MAG03's M1 as that item words it.

**And there is no way for a player to reach it.** `makeSpell` has exactly one caller in the
entire build and it is `window.__HARNESS`. So does `enchantQuote`. The eight menus a player can
open are `inventory, journal, book, levelup, sheet, spells, container, map` — `spells` is a list
of what you have attuned. No NPC in `game/data/npcs/**` offers a spellmaking or enchanting
service; the service vocabulary in the data has 17 entries and neither word is among them; no
dialogue topic mentions either. `enchanting.json` names four spellwrights — `journeyman_lilmoth`,
`journeyman_gideon`, `journeyman_stormhold`, `journeyman_helstrom`, plus two masters and a
recluse — and adds, in its own note, *"Four in the world, and walking to one is part of the cost
(RI-MAG03 §A, S7). A single spellwright in the starting town would make spellmaking a menu and

## 13. The single biggest gap

### `GAP-W1-magic-spellmaking-has-no-world-side-surface`

> **The spell-making and enchanting systems — the entire Morrowind half of this piece, and the
> reason RI-MAG03 exists — are reachable only from `window.__HARNESS`. There is no menu, no NPC,
> no dialogue topic, no service and no in-world spellwright. `makeSpell` and `enchantQuote` each
> have exactly one caller in the build, and it is the test harness.**

**Why it is the biggest.** ARBITRATION §3's CONSUMPTION clause is unambiguous and it is the
clause that governs here: *"for every model a piece ships, the verdict must name the world-side
consumer that reads it… A model with no demonstrated consumer is `unmeasurable ⇒ 0`, exactly as
a missing model is — from the player's chair they are the same thing."* RI-MAG03's own bar is
blunter still: *"a critic must be able to build, buy and cast a spell that appears nowhere in
`game/data/magic/spells.json` as shipped… the moment a player realises they can walk into the
Mages Guild and construct an object that has never existed before… the game stops being
something you consume."* There is nowhere to walk to. Every dial this round spent itself
measuring — magnitude, duration, area, 97 of them — exists so that a player can turn it, and the
player has no hands on any of them. A shipped game in this state has a fixed list of 72 spells,
which is precisely the failure the brief names: *a fixed list of abilities with good frame data
is a failure of this piece however well it fights.*

It is also **cheap**, which is why it outranks the summon and the touch bugs in value even
though they are more clearly defects. The arithmetic, the gates, the clamps, the tier ladder,
the commission price, the save round-trip and the refusal prose all exist and all work. What is
missing is a person to talk to and a screen to talk on.

**Remedy.**
1. Place the seven enchanters/spellwrights `enchanting.json` already names as **NPCs in the
   settlements it already names them at** (`lilmoth`, `gideon`, `stormhold`, `helstrom`,
   `blackrose`, `soulrest`, and the Stone Wastes recluse), each with a `service` of
   `spellmaking` / `enchanting` in `game/data/npcs/**`. The ids exist; only the people are
   missing.
2. Give each a dialogue topic that opens the commission surface, so the entry point is
   Morrowind's (a topic list) and not Souls' (a vendor grid).
3. Add a `spellmaking` mode to `ui/system.js`'s `OPENABLE` list, driven by `quoteSpell` — the
   quote already returns everything the screen needs to render: `focus_base`, `focus_cost`,
   `tier`, `skill_req`, `gold`, the clamped effect terms and the refusal reason.
4. **Do not** put it in the starting town: the item's own note makes distance part of the price
   and S7 backs it.

**Acceptance** (`RI-MAG03` M1, re-worded so it cannot be satisfied by the harness):
> A probe that **never calls `H.makeSpell`, `H.enchantQuote` or `H.enchanterOpen`** walks the
> player to a named settlement, opens a conversation with a named NPC through `H.talkTo`,
> selects a spellmaking topic through `H.conversationSay`, and commissions **19 of 20** tuples
> that are legal under RI-MAG02 and absent from `spells.json` — including one three-effect
> spell, one at an effect's `magnitude.max`, one at `duration.max_s`, one whose `focus_cost`
> exceeds `focus_max`, and one deliberately nonsensical — asserting each appears in
> `saveState()`, each computes RI-MAG02 §D's `focus_base` exactly, and each castable one delivers
> its commissioned magnitude. The twentieth, the effect-count-over-skill-cap case, is refused
> with its number. **Gold decreases by the quoted price and by nothing else.**

## 16. How this verdict was reached

Boot was confirmed against the pin before anything was measured. The escalation ladder mattered
at rung 3 and rung 4 rather than rung 1: re-running the builder's own probe would have returned
its own numbers, and the numbers are mostly right. What produced this verdict was asking what
the *control* controls (§2), what the *consumption arm* is consuming (§6), and — the one that
found the most — asking a question the round never asked, which is what happens when you cast
the same spell twice (§5).

Source was read to locate mechanisms after the behaviour was already demonstrated from output,
except in two places where I read first and say so: `combat/enemy.js:47` and `engine.js:6213`
were read to explain measurements I had already taken, and `enchanting.json` and `ui/system.js`
were read to establish an **absence**, which is the one thing output cannot demonstrate.
