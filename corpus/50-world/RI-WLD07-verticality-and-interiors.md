## The bar

This is the sharpest seam in the world domain. Souls level design is **interconnected, vertical,
shortcut-unlocking and bonfire-anchored** — Undead Burg loops back to Firelink, the lift in Sen's
Fortress, the elevator from Undead Parish. Morrowind interior design is **flat, numerous, small and
disposable** — 92 caves and ~50 tombs that are two or three rooms each, exist to be found rather than
to be conquered, and have no shortcuts because they don't need any. Both are correct and they are
correct about *different things*. The ruling: **the Souls loop applies to a small number of large,
authored, boss-anchored dungeons; the Morrowind cave applies to the many small found ones.**
Concretely: **8 Souls-loop dungeons** (each ≥3 vertical strata, ≥2 unlockable shortcuts, 1 boss,
25–60 enemies, 12–25 min first clear) and **82 Morrowind-style small interiors** (1–4 rooms, no boss,
no shortcut, hand-placed named loot), plus **160 settlement interiors**. Terrain relief must span
**−40 m to +420 m** so that verticality exists outdoors too, and **35% ± 10% of playtime is interior.**

## The reference artifact

### 1. THE SEAM RULING (binding; do not re-litigate)

| Question | Ruling | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Are dungeons interconnected loops or flat caves? | **BOTH, by count.** 8 loops, 82 caves. | AR §1: "inside the fight, Souls wins" — a boss arena and its approach are inside the fight, so Souls owns their shape. A two-room smuggler cave is world content, so Morrowind owns it. |
| Do small caves get bonfires? | **No.** Bonfires only in the 8 loop-dungeons and in settlements. | Souls owns checkpointing (S5/S6), but a bonfire in every 2-room cave makes the world safe and trivialises the corpse-run. |
| Do small caves get shortcuts? | **No.** Shortcuts are the loop-dungeon's signature and must stay scarce to stay meaningful. | |
| Do loop-dungeons get Morrowind loot? | **Yes.** S12: hand-placed, named, weird. No procedural drop tables anywhere, including in the loops. | |
| Do small caves respawn enemies? | **Yes**, on bonfire rest (S5). Named/quest NPCs and merchants never. | |
| Can a loop-dungeon interior be an exterior cell? | **Yes** — three of the eight are open-air (the Xanmeer, the Clay Kilns, the Drowned Xanmeer). Souls' best levels are outdoors. | |
| Do interiors load as separate cells? | **Yes** for settlement buildings and small caves (Morrowind's model: a door, a load, a bounded space). **No** for the 8 loop-dungeons, which must be single continuous spaces so shortcuts are physically real. | A shortcut across a cell boundary is a teleport, and a teleport is not a shortcut. |

| Terrain elevation range | world | **−40 m to +420 m** | range < 150 m |

| **Relief range, per region, inside its `RI-WLD16` §3 class envelope** | **every region** | **13 of 13** | any region outside its envelope |

| Climbable/traversable verticality features (ladders, root-ramps, stilts, cliff paths) | world | ≥120 | <50 |
| Land below +5 m (marsh/tidal) | world, **descriptive** | 35–50% | — see note |
| Land above +100 m | world, **descriptive** | ≥12% | — see note |

> **AMENDED 2026-08-14 — Ruling W1** (`orchestration/OWNER-DIRECTIVES-2026-08-14.md` §6), implementing
> `reports/BAR-AUDIT-WORLD-20260814.md` §5.3. **No threshold was lowered; three were re-scoped and
> two were demoted to descriptive.**
>
> **The bad result that passed, and it was the shipped build.** The area-weighted mean slope of the
> world is **10.07°** — comfortably mid-band — while **7 of the 13 regions sit below 5.5°** (Eastern
> Rootlands 3.61, Western Rootlands 4.00, Stone Wastes 4.17, Marauder's Coast 4.99, Crimson Coast
> 5.15, Deep Marshes 5.24, Clay Moor 5.32). Valus Ridge at 30.28° and the Salt Hills at 16.62° carry
> the average for everyone else. A player does not walk the mean slope of the province; they walk the
> ground under their feet for the next six minutes.
>
> **Why a quantile and not the worst region.** The ruling permits either, and here the worst
> constituent is the wrong instrument: a tidal flat *ought* to be flat, and binding every region to
> 6–14° would fail the Eastern Rootlands for being what it is. So the shape of the ground is judged
> per region against its own declared landform class (`RI-WLD16` §3), and the province's overall
> verticality is judged on the **median region** — which stops two steep regions carrying eleven flat
> ones without forbidding any region from being low. The 3° hard floor still binds on the **worst**
> region, because nothing should be a plane.
>
> **Why two rows became descriptive.** "Land below +5 m: 35–50%" and "land above +100 m: ≥12%" have
> no honest per-region form — every region in a coastal marsh province is either almost all below
> 5 m or almost none, and a per-region band would be arbitrary. Their per-region job is done properly
> by the relief-range envelope, which asks whether each region has the vertical structure its
> landform class implies. They are kept as world-level description because they remain useful
> regression signals; **they no longer gate the item.** Stating this is preferable to leaving two
> aggregate rows in place that look binding and are not.
>
> **Measured 2026-08-14** by `corpus/80-methods/m-instance-bars.mjs` check C: 13/13 regions inside
> their slope envelope, 13/13 inside their relief envelope, **median region 5.32° against the ≥6°
> target**, 0 regions under the 3° floor. The envelopes are deliberately wide — they catch a playa
> built as a ridge, not a slope that wants tuning — so the median row is the one carrying this bar.
>
> **Reversal**: restore the four re-scoped rows to their world-aggregate form and delete the scope
> column. **Falsifier**: if the median-region row proves unreachable because the province is
> honestly a marsh, the right move is to lower *that* number with the census behind it, not to
> return to the world mean.

## Comparison method

**M33 — Dungeon census.** From world data, enumerate every interior/dungeon volume and classify by
declared kind. **Pass: 8 loop-dungeons, 78–86 small interiors, 150–170 settlement interiors.**
**Fail: <5 loop-dungeons, or <120 total interiors.**

**M34 — Loop-dungeon structural proof (per dungeon, all 8).**
1. Build the dungeon's navmesh connectivity graph with shortcut edges initially *closed*.
2. Assert ≥3 distinct elevation strata (cluster navmesh by Y; strata must be ≥8 m apart and each ≥15%
   of the dungeon's area).
3. Assert ≥2 shortcut edges exist, each with an in-world unlock actor (lever/lift/slab/door) reachable
   only from the far side, and each reducing entry→boss path length by **≥35%** when opened.
4. Assert ≥1 shortcut path returns to the entry bonfire.
5. Assert the whole dungeon is **one continuous space** — no cell-load boundary crossed by a shortcut.
6. Walk entry→boss with shortcuts closed and again with them open; record both path lengths and times.
   **Pass: closed/open ratio ≥1.5. Fail: <1.2** — a "shortcut" that saves 10% is a door.
7. Count enemies and aggro groups; assert 2.0–3.5 aggro events/min along the critical path (RI-WLD02 D10).

**M35 — Small-interior audit.** Sample 25 of the 82. Assert: 1–5 rooms; **no** bonfire; **no** shortcut
edge; ≥1 hand-placed named item; ≥12 distinct prop meshes; floor area ≥45 m²; and that its loot
contains **no procedurally generated entries** (S12 — dump the loot table and confirm every entry is a
named, authored item).

**M36 — Verticality audit.** Rasterise the terrain heightmap at 5 m. Compute the elevation histogram,
per-region maxima, slope histogram on walkable land, and the road-elevation profile. Compare to §4.
Additionally screenshot from 12 high points and confirm ≥3 landmark silhouettes each (feeds RI-WLD06).

**M37 — Interior/exterior ratio.** Run a scripted full-content session (all 8 loop-dungeons, 30 sampled
small interiors, all 8 named settlements, all road legs). Log time in interior vs exterior cells.
**Pass: 25–45% interior.**

**M38 — Bonfire spacing.** For 300 random road points, compute walk-minutes to the nearest bonfire via
the road graph. **Pass: max ≤6 min, median ≤3 min.** Also assert no bonfire exists inside any of the
82 small interiors (seam ruling §1).

**M39 — Seam-violation scan.** Assert: no loop-dungeon uses Morrowind cell-loading across a shortcut;
no small cave has a boss health-bar actor; no loop-dungeon uses procedural loot; no dungeon enemy has
a level-scaling component (S9). **Any hit fails the piece.**

## How we lose

- **The flat marsh.** Black Marsh is a *swamp*, so the terrain ships at ±8 m across 14.5 km². The world
  then has no sightlines (RI-WLD06 fails), no vistas (RI-WLD02 D12 fails), no Souls level design
  (no verticality to loop through) and no visual variety. Valus Ridge at 380 m and the Salt Hills at
  190 m are not decoration; they are the load-bearing counterweight to a marsh setting.
- **Shortcuts that aren't.** A door labelled "shortcut" that saves fifteen seconds. M34's ≥35% path
  reduction and ≥1.5 closed/open ratio are the numeric definition of the Souls feeling — the moment
  where the world folds and you understand it.
- **Shortcuts as teleports.** Implementing the lift as a cell-load. It works, it is cheap, and it kills
  the entire point: the Souls shortcut is a *physical* revelation that the place you fought through is
  right above the place you started. §1 forbids cell boundaries across shortcuts.
- **All dungeons become Souls dungeons.** Eight loops is thrilling; ninety loops is exhausting and
  unbuildable, and it destroys the Morrowind pleasure of finding a two-room cave with one weird item
  and a note. The 8/82 split is the whole ruling.
- **All dungeons become Morrowind caves.** Ninety two-room caves and no boss-anchored loop, so the
  Souls half of the brief is absent from the world entirely.
- **Empty boxes behind doors.** 250 interiors is a large number and the temptation is to grey-box them.
  M35's 12-prop / named-item / 45 m² floor is calibrated so that a grey box fails.
- **Bonfires everywhere.** One in every cave makes death costless, deletes the corpse-run (S6) and makes
  the 6-minute spacing rule meaningless. Bonfires belong in settlements and the 8 loops.
- **Interior time collapsing to zero.** All content pushed outdoors because interiors are expensive, so
  the world becomes a hiking simulator and Morrowind's "every door opens" promise dies. M37's 25%
  floor exists for this.
- **The tide-gated dungeon shipped without the tide.** The Drowned Xanmeer's entire identity is that it
  is only enterable at low tide (RI-WLD05 #21). If tide ships as a shader, this dungeon is just a cave.
