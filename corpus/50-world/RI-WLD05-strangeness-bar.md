## The bar

Morrowind's defining achievement is that **nothing in it looks like fantasy**. There are no horses —
you ride a giant flea. There are no inns on a hill — there is a city inside the shell of a crab. The
Telvanni do not build towers, they *grow* them; the Redoran do not quarry halls, they hollow out the
carapace of a dead emperor crab. Mushrooms are load-bearing. Ash falls like weather. A cliff racer
screams at you from a sky that is the wrong colour. Every one of those is a *specific object* a player
can point at, and there are dozens. Our bar is a hard inventory: **30 mandated strangeness elements**,
of which **≥22 must be encounterable in the first 30 minutes of play**, **≥8 must be systemic** (they
change how the game is played, not just how it looks), and **0 may be generic-fantasy**. The acceptance
test is the brief's own: show a screenshot to a fresh judge; **if it could be Skyrim, it fails.**

### 3. The banned list (generic-fantasy contamination — any instance is a defect)

Horses. Cobblestone streets with lamp-posts. Thatched cottages with flower boxes. Stone castles with
crenellations *except* Imperial forts (which must be visibly failing). Elves as we know them. Dwarves.
Orc warcamps with skull totems. Tolkien-shaped swords as common loot. Torch-lit stone-block dungeons
with iron-bound chests. Generic "swamp" cypress-and-Spanish-moss as the dominant look. Wolves. Bears.
Deer. Rats. Anything a UE5 marketplace swamp pack ships by default.

## Comparison method

**M22 — THE SKYRIM TEST** (`blind_pair: yes`; the item's primary instrument).
1. Sample 24 screenshots: 1280 × 720, eye height 1.7 m, random walkable points weighted to cover all
   13 regions and all 8 settlements, mixed time of day and weather, HUD stripped.
2. Show each, individually and unlabeled, to a fresh judge with the question:
   *"Could this screenshot be from Skyrim, Elden Ring, a generic UE5 swamp asset pack, or none of these?
   If 'none of these', name the specific object in frame that made you say so."*
3. **Pass: ≥22 of 24 answered "none of these" AND ≥18 of 24 name a specific object.** A "none of these"
   with no nameable object is scored as a fail for that frame — it means the image is merely murky,
   not strange.
4. **Fail: ≥4 of 24 identified as Skyrim/Elden Ring/asset-pack.** The brief's rule, literally applied.

**M23 — Inventory audit (the count).** For each of the 30 elements, query world data for its entity
class or system flag. **Pass: all 30 present; each visual element has ≥6 placed instances (≥1 for
singular ones: the Kothringi mirror-town, the Hive queen); each systemic element has a live component
that fires in a scripted probe.** **Fail: <26 present, or <6 systemic elements actually functional.**

**M24 — First-30-minutes audit.** Run a scripted new-game session for 30 minutes following the critical
path from the Lilmoth start. Log which of the 30 elements were seen (rendered on screen ≥1 s) or
experienced (system fired). **Pass: ≥18. Target: 22. Fail: <12** — a strange world that hides its
strangeness for an hour has failed the only player who matters, the one deciding whether to continue.

**M25 — Banned-list scan.** Grep the asset manifest and entity registry for the banned list by mesh
name, entity class and tag. Additionally, sample 40 random loot instances and 40 random enemies and
classify. **Fail on any horse, any wolf/bear/deer/rat as a normal wilderness fauna entry, or any
non-Imperial crenellated stone castle.**

**M26 — Blind pair against Morrowind.** Present 10 of our screenshots interleaved with 10 Morrowind
screenshots (Vivec, Ald'ruhn, Sadrith Mora, the Bitter Coast, Molag Amur, a silt strider, a netch, an
ancestral tomb, Red Mountain, a Telvanni interior), all unlabeled, downsampled to a common resolution
so 2002 fidelity is not the tell. Ask a fresh judge to rank all 20 by *"how much this looks like a
world nobody else has made"*. Record the blind ranking **before** the reveal. **We lose if our median
rank is in the bottom half.** Per the contract §6: if the judge ranks ours above Morrowind, distrust
the judge and re-run with a harsher lens.

**Fidelity separation (AR §4).** M22 and M26 judge *art direction only*. A critic citing a 2002
Morrowind screenshot when judging graphics quality, or a modern AAA screenshot when judging art
direction, has committed a hard error and its verdict is void.

## How we lose

- **The generic swamp.** Cypress trees, Spanish moss, green fog, a wooden shack on stilts, a crocodile.
  It is the default output of every asset pipeline and it is *exactly* what Black Marsh must not be.
  This is the single most likely way this whole project reads as forgettable, and M22 exists for it.
- **Strangeness as hero assets only.** One beautiful xanmeer on the loading screen and 14 km² of
  ordinary marsh. M23's ≥6-instances rule and M24's first-30-minutes rule together forbid it.
- **Strangeness that is only visual.** Thirty weird meshes and zero weird systems, so the world looks
  alien and *plays* like every other game. The S column is 15 for a reason: tide, rot, egg-law,
  mud-memory and the Hive's chord change decisions, not just pixels.
- **Hist trees as "big trees".** The most important element on the list, and the easiest to under-build.
  A Hist that is a large oak with glowing leaves is a fail. It must be architecture: you walk *inside*
  it, the door is a sphincter, and it is a person.
- **Argonians as lizard-people in leather armour.** The culture must be as strange as the flora:
  egg-clutches in the street, name-changes on maturity, no chairs, mud as a social material.
- **Imperial content used as the visual default.** Imperial architecture is the *contrast*; it exists in
  four places (Stormhold, Gideon, Blackrose, the border cairns) and it is always failing. If Imperial
  stone is the most common building material in the world, the world has become Cyrodiil.
- **The banned list smuggled in as "just a placeholder".** A placeholder horse becomes a shipped horse.
  M25 scans the manifest, not the intent.
- **Weirdness without coherence.** Thirty random strange things that don't share a logic. Everything on
  this list obeys one rule — *Black Marsh is made of living things and the dead architecture of a
  people who are still here* — and an element that doesn't obey it is noise, not strangeness.

## Provenance note

- `canonical-recall`, confidence medium-high: the entire Vvardenfell catalogue in §1 (Telvanni
  mushroom towers, Ald'ruhn's emperor-crab shell, Vivec's cantons, silt striders, cliff racers, ash and
  blight storms, Dwemer ruins, guar, netch, kwama egg mines, nix-hounds/alit/kagouti, bonemold and
  chitin, levitation, ancestral tombs, Daedric shrines, the Ghostfence, Red Mountain, mushroom groves).
  Recalled, not verified this session; the *count* of 18 is therefore approximate and must not be cited
  as measured.
- `community-data`, confidence medium, retrieved 2026-08-05 (UESP Lore:Black Marsh, ESO Murkmire
  material, UESP Online:Argonian Furnishings): Argonians (Saxhleel) were created by the Hist, a race of
  sapient spore trees; Hist roots run beneath the soil and white limestone of Black Marsh connecting
  all of them and thereby all Argonians; Argonians built stone structures across Black Marsh from
  solitary statues and wayshrines to stepped pyramids called **xanmeers**; many are shrines to Sithis;
  an intact xanmeer stands at Hissmir; mud nests serve as settlements; **naga** are a distinct Argonian
  people. These constrain elements 1, 2, 3, 6, 11, 16.
- `constructed` (binding): the thirty-element inventory itself, the E/S classification, the banned
  list, and every threshold in M22–M26. Elements 4, 5, 7, 9, 10, 13, 14, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 23–30 are
  invented for this project; several extend canon (voriplasm, wamasu, hackwing and Kothringi have
  canonical roots but their behaviour here is ours). The 30/22/8 counts are set deliberately above the
  recalled Vvardenfell figure of 18/6 because our land area is ~14.5 km² against Vvardenfell's ~13–14,
  and strangeness must be legible at a smaller scale.
