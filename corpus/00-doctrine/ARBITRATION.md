# The Arbitration Rule

> **Inside the fight, Souls wins. Everywhere else, Morrowind wins.**

This is the supreme law of the corpus. Every reference item, every critic, every builder
is subordinate to it. When a reference item from the Morrowind side and a reference item
from the Souls side make incompatible demands, this document decides which one is
authoritative — not the builder's taste, not the critic's mood.

---

## 1. The boundary: what counts as "inside the fight"

"The fight" begins at the first frame of hostile intent (an enemy aggro state entering
`COMBAT`, or the player's first attack input against a valid target) and ends when all
hostiles within the encounter volume are dead, dormant, or de-aggroed for >5s.

**But Souls' authority inside the fight is over HOW FIGHTING WORKS — not over whether the world
still exists while you are in one.** The brief grants Souls authority "where necessary for the
combat to feel like Souls". Frames, stamina, hitboxes, animation and enemy behaviour are
necessary. *Deleting every non-violent verb the moment someone swings at you is not.*

Morrowind therefore retains, **during** a fight:
- **The right to disengage.** Fleeing is a legitimate, supported resolution. Enemies leash (see
  RI-AI01); escape is not a failure state and must not be punished as one.
- **The right to yield, parley, bribe or talk down** — for anything that can talk. See S13 as
  amended: a fight with a *person* must have a non-lethal exit.
- **Non-lethal outcomes**: paralysis, calm effects, soul-trap-without-kill, sneaking away,
  surrender, driving off rather than killing.
- **Consequences**: crime, witnesses, bounty and faction standing all keep accruing mid-fight. The
  world does not pause because you drew a weapon.

Killing is *one* exit from a fight. A build in which it is the *only* exit has failed the brief,
regardless of how good the combat feels — and it would silently gut the ≥45% non-combat quest
resolution bar, since a quest target who becomes unreachable the instant they aggro cannot be
talked to. (Drift ID-01, intent audit 01.)

**Inside the fight, SOULS IS AUTHORITATIVE over:**

| Domain | Souls rule that wins |
|---|---|
| Frames & timing | Startup / active / recovery frames, i-frame windows on roll, animation-driven motion |
| Stamina | Stamina as the universal action currency; regen delay after spend; block-through-stamina |
| Hitboxes | Capsule/sphere hit volumes swept along weapon arcs; hurtboxes that move with animation |
| Animation | Root-motion-authoritative movement, committed attacks, no instant turn during attack |
| Enemy behaviour | Aggro radius, telegraphed windups, spacing/reposition AI, punish windows, poise/stagger |
| Damage resolution | Deterministic hit-or-miss by geometry — **never** a to-hit dice roll |
| Lock-on | Hard target lock with directional roll semantics |
| Recovery | Estus-equivalent healing with animation commitment and finite charges |
| Death | Corpse-run soul recovery, world reset to bonfire state |
| Poise / hyperarmour | Souls poise model, not Morrowind knockdown |
| I-frame accounting | Every dodge measured in frames at 60fps, not seconds. **UNIT WARNING (added wave 0, corpus-audit): Souls community frame counts — DS1, DS3 and Elden Ring alike — are quoted in 1/30-second ticks. A Souls figure of N frames is 2N of ours. No frame count in this corpus may be compared to a Souls frame count without that factor.** **REBASE APPLIED, wave 0 (`rebase-s22`): S22's rebase is no longer outstanding. Every frame figure in `RI-CMB01`, `RI-CMB02`, `RI-CMB05`, `RI-CMB08`, `RI-AI02`, `RI-AI03`, `RI-WPN01`–`RI-WPN06` and `RI-CAM04` is doubled and carries its unit as `f@60`; `RI-CMB03` is excluded by ruling; the `RI-CMB07` exemplar is invalidated pending regeneration. Write `f@60` for ours and `t@30` for a Souls tick — a bare frame count is a defect.** See `RI-CMB01` §A, `CORPUS-COHERENCE-01.md` §9/§14 and `REBASE-S22-REPORT.md` |

**Outside the fight, MORROWIND IS AUTHORITATIVE over:**

| Domain | Morrowind rule that wins |
|---|---|
| Progression | Levelling shape, skill/attribute breadth, build identity beyond weapon class |
| Faction gating | Rank requirements, skill+attribute thresholds, faction rivalry locks, expulsion |
| Dialogue | Topic-list dialogue, keyword discovery, disposition, rumours that differ per town |
| Journal | Numbered, dated, first-person-authored journal entries; no objective markers |
| Quest structure | Multi-stage, deceit-capable, quest-giver-lies, mutually exclusive resolutions |
| World structure | Hand-placed density, named interiors, walk-and-navigate wayfinding, **and a real in-fiction fast-travel network** (S7). Only warp-to-map-pin is banned |
| Lore | Unreliable in-world books, contradictory accounts, deep-time history |
| Economy | Gold as currency; merchant gold pools, barter, disposition-affected prices |
| Strangeness | Alien flora/fauna/architecture, dream-logic, non-Tolkien fantasy |
| Non-combat resolution | Many quests resolvable by talk, bribe, sneak, theft, or lore knowledge |

## 2. Contested seams and their rulings

These are the places where the two systems genuinely collide. Each is pre-decided.
Builders MUST NOT re-litigate; critics MUST enforce.

| # | Seam | Ruling |

| S2 | What does levelling cost? | **SPLIT.** Souls: souls buy levels, single soft-cap curve, bonfire level-up. Morrowind: what a level *gives you* is broad (attributes+skills shape build identity beyond weapon class). Souls owns the currency and the curve; Morrowind owns the breadth of the stat sheet. |

| S5 | Enemy respawn | **SOULS.** Bonfire rest respawns ordinary enemies. Named NPCs, quest actors, and merchants NEVER respawn and NEVER die to respawn logic. |
| S6 | Death consequence | **SOULS.** Drop souls, respawn at last bonfire. But: quest state, journal, faction standing, and world flags persist untouched — Morrowind owns the save-state semantics. |
| S7 | Fast travel | **MORROWIND — and Morrowind *has* fast travel, so we must too.** ~~Earlier wording ("no fast travel") was a drafting error that inverted the intent; corrected wave 0 after intent audit.~~ Travel is **outside the fight**, therefore Morrowind wins, therefore we build Morrowind's actual system: a **diegetic, node-to-node transport network you pay gold for and must physically walk to**. | S15 | Souls as currency | **NEITHER, by decree.** Souls level you and ONLY level you. **Gold is the only currency.** Merchants, bribes, training, travel, and repairs all cost gold. Souls cannot buy an item, ever. |
| S16 | Dungeon architecture | **SPLIT, by census.** Two kinds coexist and neither may crowd out the other. **8 Souls-loop dungeons**: interconnected, multi-strata, shortcut-unlocking, HEARTH-anchored, with a closed/open path ratio ≥1.5 — these are the set-piece descents and they obey Souls level design. **82 Morrowind caves**: flat-ish, hand-furnished, discovered by walking, often holding one secret, one story, or one corpse — these are the texture of the world and they obey Morrowind. Anything else outside the fight remains Morrowind's. |
| S21 | Where the die survives | **Keep the die where failure is permanent; delete it where failure is a retry.** S1 bans dice inside the fight, but it says nothing about the world, and the corpus needed a principle rather than a case-by-case argument. This is it. A random check whose failure costs you something you cannot get back — a persuasion attempt that lowers disposition, a pickpocket that gets you caught, a spell that fails and consumes the magicka — keeps its roll: the uncertainty is real and the outcome is a story. A random check you can simply repeat until it passes — Morrowind's lockpicking at 12% per attempt is a 100% chance after eight attempts — is not a gate at all, it is a tax on time, and it must be replaced by a **deterministic skill threshold plus an execution challenge**: the gate lives in the skill (Morrowind), the uncertainty lives in the player's hands (Souls). Applied by RI-STL02 (lockpicking becomes a timed ward-collar), and it is why RI-DLG04's persuasion correctly keeps its roll. **Scope correction, intent audit 02 (ND-01):** this ruling named three surviving dice and the corpus then deleted two of them — RI-STL02's pickpocket check and RI-MAG03's blanket 'no dice anywhere in this system' — leaving persuasion as the only die in the entire world model. That is the S7 shape again: a Morrowind system deleted in the name of Souls purity. **A retry-able check loses its die; a check whose failure is permanent keeps it.** Pickpocketing keeps its roll (getting caught is permanent and consequential); magic keeps a failure die wherever failing consumes the resource or is witnessed. RI-STL02 and RI-MAG03 must be reconciled to this. Ruling added wave 0 at the stealth/crime author's request. |
| S22 | The frame is 1/60 s, and upstream frame counts are 1/30 s | **REBASE, not adopt.** Souls community frame data — DS1, DS3 and Elden Ring alike — counts in **1/30 s ticks**. Our simulation is fixed 60 Hz. The corpus adopted the upstream *numbers* without converting the *unit*, so every duration in the combat area is **half its real wall-clock length**: our light roll is invulnerable for 217 ms against DS3's 433 ms, and lasts 433 ms against DS1's 800 ms. Attack tables, parry windows and critical windows carry the same ~0.5 factor. Because every ratio stayed internally consistent, this passes every M-script, every trace statistic and every blind pair — **and surfaces only as "Souls-ish but wrong" in a human's hands**, which is precisely the failure mode this project exists to avoid. Ruling: **all combat durations are rebased to 60 Hz by doubling the upstream tick count**, and every frame figure in the corpus must state its unit. A frame count without a stated framerate is meaningless and is a defect. The RI-CMB07 exemplar trace is invalidated by this and must be regenerated — that cost is accepted, because the alternative is shipping combat at double speed and discovering it only when someone plays it. Ruling added wave 0 on the Souls verification pass. **APPLIED wave 0 by task `rebase-s22`; see `corpus/00-doctrine/REBASE-S22-REPORT.md` for every figure moved, every figure deliberately not moved, and the four things the ruling did not anticipate.** |
| S23 | Equip load: two systems, one owner each | **SPLIT BY DOMAIN.** RI-CMB01 and RI-PRG07 define incompatible equip-load systems — five tiers at 30/55/80/100 versus four at 30/70/100, with i-frame columns of 13/11/9/7 versus 13/11/5/0 — and their own method scripts fail each other. Ruling: **RI-CMB01 owns everything the tier does inside the fight** (tier boundaries as they gate roll behaviour, i-frames, roll distance and recovery) because that is Souls' domain under the supreme rule. **RI-PRG07 owns encumbrance outside the fight** and may keep finer granularity there — carrying capacity, movement on the world map, fatigue, what you can loot and haul — provided its extra tiers have **no in-fight effect whatsoever**. | S25 | Water at the waterline | **SPLIT, and the line is the waterline.** Water may change **where** you fight and **what it costs to be there** — reach, footing, disengage routes, which archetypes own a space, how far you can see and hear. It may **never change a frame number.** No startup, active, recovery, i-frame or telegraph value varies with depth; instead, actions above knee depth are *denied* rather than degraded (no sprint, no roll), and stamina is charged for standing in it. This keeps AR-1 clean while making water a genuine tactical terrain rather than a debuff. **Boss arenas are capped at W2 (knee)** so no boss fight is ever adjudicated by wading. Enemies declare a `water_max_band`, which makes deep water a real disengage route — and gives water-native archetypes a domain land enemies cannot enter. Depth is read off the player's own silhouette against anatomical landmarks (ankle, knee, hip, chest), which is why **S18's permanent third person is load-bearing for water**, not merely a camera preference. Proposed by RI-WLD10, adopted wave 0. |
| S24 | The marsh is not all marsh | **MORROWIND, and the map is authoritative.** Black Marsh is the *name*, not the terrain. `corpus/50-world/black-marsh-map-source.jpg` is the binding source and it shows a **region of sharply distinct environments**: the mountains of **Valus Ridge** and the peaks along the Morrowind border; the arid, rocky **Stone Wastes** in the south-west; **Blackwood**'s dense jungle; **The Stone Forest**'s petrified growth; the dry uplands of **The Clay Moor**; the red littoral of the **Crimson Coast**; the **Eastern** and **Western Rootlands**; **The Hive**; the **Deep Marshes**; **Marauder's Coast** and the **Salt Hills**; **Thornmarsh**; and two genuinely different waters — **Topal Bay** to the west and the **Padomaic Ocean** to the east. Standing water, tides and wetland are the properties of **some** regions, never a global property of the world: an implementation in which everything is swamp with a recoloured fog has failed this ruling regardless of how good the swamp is. Enforcement is already specified — RI-WLD04's blind test requires a fresh judge to identify a region from an unlabelled screenshot at ≥33/39, and every region pair must differ on ≥6 of 9 measurable axes. **Every critic touching world, art direction, audio or encounter design must check its piece against this ruling**, and a region that is only distinguishable by fog colour is a defect. | S20 | Source authority across eras | **MORROWIND-ERA SOURCES WIN.** Our game sits in the late Third Era, contemporary with Morrowind (~3E 427). Two constraints follow from other rulings and are not negotiable: the teleport effects Mark/Recall and Intervention are part of the **S7 travel network** and obey its rules — they move you between places you already know, above ground, out of combat, never into or within a dungeon, boss arena or locked area. What is forbidden is teleport as a *level-design solvent*: no recall out of a fight, no warping past a shortcut you have not opened, no intervention as an escape button. ~~Earlier wording banned utility magic from being a teleport network outright, contradicting S7; corrected wave 0, drift ID-03.~~ And no spell may restore the roll-to-hit die (S1). Ruling added wave 0 in response to BAR-CRITIQUE-01 G4. |
| S18 | Camera and perspective | **SOULS, absolutely and everywhere.** The game is **third-person** at all times — an over-the-shoulder/behind-the-back orbital camera with a Souls collision-and-spring arm. Morrowind's first-person option does **not** survive: the player character's body, animation and silhouette are load-bearing for Souls combat (you read your own recovery frames off your own animation), so a first-person mode would break the fight. This binds outside the fight too — exploration, dialogue, menus and cutscenes are all third-person, because a perspective that changes at the combat boundary would break the seam that S-rulings exist to keep clean. Every Souls camera behaviour is in scope: spring-arm collision and pull-in, lock-on framing that keeps both combatants in frame, soft-lock steering, camera-relative movement with the character turning to face its velocity, target-switch flick, vertical clamp, auto-recentre-on-sprint, and the fixed-height pivot. | S31 | When a telegraph and a derived column collide, the telegraph wins | **SOULS, and the column is re-derived.** Referred by the W1-09 round-4b builder, which got three of four champion attacks to satisfy both the inboard-geometry floor and the windup budget and found the fourth genuinely incompatible: `combo_b`'s label lands at 22 frames, so reaching `t_react ≥ 19` requires `lie ≤ 3`, and `f_vis` is a threshold crossing with nothing in between — **one frame of reactability costs 34% of a declared peak-tip-speed column**, with the binding row a real one rather than an unplayed artefact. It shipped the safe value and referred the decision instead of choosing, which is correct. **Ruling: reactability wins and the kinematic column is re-derived beneath it.** The decisive fact is in `RI-CMB04` §B's own provenance note — that column *"exist[s] to justify the substep count… if our animations differ, the substep count must be re-derived."* It is an instrumental figure, downstream of the animation, and it was never a statement about how an attack should look. Reactability is not instrumental: whether a player can see an attack coming is the thing the brief hands to Souls outright ("telegraphed windups" sits in the §1 authority table), and `RI-CMB12` exists precisely because every prior combat bar measured frames-over-frames and would have passed a game nobody could read. The alternative on offer — dropping the attack's `reactable` flag — is worse than either: an unreadable attack is the defect, not a way of accounting for one. **General rule for the wave: a bar that exists to justify another number yields to a bar that describes what the player perceives.** A builder that hits this pattern should say so and refer it, as this one did, rather than trading one silently. Referred by the W1-09 round-4b builder, ruled wave 1. |
| S30 | There is no map, because there is nowhere to put a pin | **MORROWIND'S INTENT OVER MORROWIND'S FURNITURE — the item is upheld and my brief was wrong.** The W1-21 builder was told by me that "the map is Morrowind's, and the map has no quest pins", noticed that `RI-UIX04` JU8 makes a map's *existence* a hard fail plus an AR-2 trigger, followed the item rather than the instruction, and said that overruling it would have to be a seam ruling. It was right on both counts and this is that ruling. Morrowind does have a map, so the surface reading favours building one. But the item's reasoning is better than mine: every objective-marker convention this project bans has to live *somewhere*, and a map is the somewhere. **S8 bans the marker; JU8 removes the surface that markers need**, and its own note puts it exactly — *"the fix is that there is nowhere to put a pin."* A quest-link that says "show on map" leaks S8 through a screen the journal does not own, which is why reachability alone was never sufficient. What replaces it must be **diegetic and partial**, which is the Morrowind-shaped answer rather than the Morrowind-shaped furniture: a carried compass you stop and open, and a pilot's prose chart-book — things you consult, that can be wrong, and that never draw your position for you. Being lost, and getting un-lost by reading and asking, is the experience Vvardenfell is remembered for; a live map with a dot on it is the thing that replaced it in every game since. Builders may not re-litigate this; a critic that finds a map reachable *or* existent scores Q7, S8 and AR-2. Raised by the W1-21 builder against my own brief, ruled wave 1. |
| S29 | Recall is travel, and travel does not happen mid-fight | **SPLIT, on the same line as S27.** Asked by the W1-14 round-2 critic, which commissioned a LIGHT-class Recall and an Intervention and teleported **84.7 m and 44.8 m out of a live fight**. (The critic proposed this as S28; that number was already taken by the road ruling below, so it is renumbered here — the question is unchanged.) Morrowind's Mark, Recall and the Interventions are among its most characteristic spells and S7 mandates fast travel, so they must exist. But a fight you can leave instantly by casting is not a Souls fight: every commitment the combat rules impose — the 102-frame parley, the uncancellable stance switch, the fixed Focus charge — assumes the fight is a thing you are *in* until it resolves. An instant exit deletes all of it at once, while every frame number stays correct. **Ruling: Recall, Mark and the Interventions are world travel and may not be cast while in combat**, on exactly the terms S27 sets for Focus — refused outright, not merely slowed, while any hostile entity is aggroed, and for 300 frames (5 s at 60 Hz) after the last hostile action. This is not an AR-2 failure and must not be scored as one; per S26, a defect is judged as a defect. Note that ARBITRATION §1's amendment already preserves the non-lethal exits — fleeing on foot, yielding, parley — so a fight remains escapable by the routes Morrowind actually cares about, just not by a keystroke. Asked by the W1-14 round-2 critic, ruled wave 1. |
| S28 | The settlements are pinned; the road between them is not | **MORROWIND, within an S17 envelope.** Asked by the W1-01 round-3 builder, which could not close the road-relief measure (27.3% against 30%) without moving leg lengths that had been treated as fixed. The legs were pinned to keep THE CROSSING's hour reproducible, which is right in intent and too strong as applied: it froze the *route* in order to protect the *duration*, and a road that may not bend cannot be interesting. **Ruling: settlement positions are authoritative and immovable** — they come from the map, which S24 makes authoritative — **but the route between any two of them may be re-cut freely**, provided (a) THE CROSSING's total walking time stays inside the declared 52–65 minute band, (b) no single leg's length changes by more than ±5% from its declared value, and (c) every re-cut leg is re-verified against the full water census, the slope histogram and reachability, not merely re-timed. | S27 | Focus comes back at the hearth and nowhere else, so the Dry Well takes the hearth | **SOULS, and the drawback attaches to the refill.** Referred independently by two round-2 builders who had found opposite halves of the same contradiction: W1-14 reported the birthsign *The Dry Well* restoring Focus in the field, which RI-MAG01 §A forbids; W1-07 reported that the sign's drawback — "no Focus regeneration" — was **definitionally unobservable**, because §A already denies Focus regeneration to everybody, so the drawback removed a system nobody had. Round 1 scored the piece 0 for a contradiction between two items rather than for anything the builder did. *(An earlier draft of this ruling permitted out-of-combat regeneration at a capped rate. That was wrong and is withdrawn: it would have weakened the rest-to-refill loop this ruling exists to protect, and neither builder asked for it. The W1-07 reading is adopted instead.)* **Ruling: Focus never regenerates — not in combat, not in the field, not slowly.** It is restored by resting at a hearth, exactly as Souls restores its charges at a bonfire, and that is the only route. A birthsign, item or enchantment may grant Focus as a **discrete, consumed, one-shot** effect, never as a rate. **The Dry Well's drawback is therefore that its bearer's hearth rest does not restore Focus at all** — the one thing §A leaves available is the one thing the sign takes away. That is a legible, painful, permanent cost of the kind Souls builds characters out of, and it makes the sign mean something without touching a frame number. RI-CHR03's drawback text is amended to say so; a sign whose drawback cannot be observed is not a drawback. Referred by the W1-14 and W1-07 round-2 builders, ruled wave 1. |
| S26 | A hitbox with a hole in it is a defect, not a leak | **NEITHER SIDE — AR-1 IS NOT THE INSTRUMENT, AND A NEW POSITIVE REQUIREMENT IS.** Asked by the W1-09 round-2 critic, which found that an enemy attack's swept volume does not cover the attacker's own root translation, so a champion's chop cannot reach a stationary player inside 1.2 m — exactly its declared `root_dz_m` — and a player who stands still at 0.8–1.1 m mashing light kills a 2,876-HP enemy taking zero damage. The critic observed that this behaves empirically like a 1.2–3.6 m distance band, which is the shape AR-1 exists to forbid. **The ruling is that it is not an AR-1 failure.** AR-1 asks whether a *Morrowind mechanism* was imported into the fight — a to-hit roll, dice damage, a level-scaled enemy. Nothing was imported here; our own geometry is broken. Reading every combat defect as a seam violation would destroy AR-1's diagnostic value, because a check that fires on everything distinguishes nothing, and it would cap a piece at 2 for a bug no doctrine text names. A hitbox defect is judged as a defect, under the combat items, at the severity its measured consequence deserves — and "the player cannot be hit" deserves a great deal. **But the critic's underlying point stands and is what needs fixing:** the reason this survived a full critic round is that *nothing in the corpus tests an attack's hit volume against a translating attacker*. So this ruling also imposes a positive requirement, binding on every attack, player and enemy alike: **a moving attacker's own body is a hazard, and its sweep must be tested.** *(Amended after the round-3 builder disproved the mechanism this ruling was first written on. The original text required that an attack's swept volume cover the attacker's root translation — and that was already true: the rig double-buffers weapon sockets in world space and applies root motion before evaluating the pose, so the per-frame hull already spanned the root delta. Measured at 0.8 m, the blade's near end sits 0.95 m in front of the attacker's own root and the root travels a further 0.79 m while active. The blade was never behind the target; it was always in front of it, and **the attacker walked straight through the target without the volume its own body swept ever being tested by anything, on either side of the exchange**. ## 3. How critics enforce this

Every critic prompt MUST include the Arbitration Rule verbatim (or by reference to this
file) and MUST run the following two checks in addition to its dimension-specific work:

- **AR-1 (Souls leakage):** Did any Morrowind-flavoured mechanic contaminate the fight?
  Roll-to-hit, dice damage, pausing mid-fight, untelegraphed instant attacks, animation
  cancels, level-scaled enemies → **automatic fail of the piece**, regardless of score.
- **AR-2 (Morrowind leakage):** Did any Souls-flavoured convention contaminate the world?
  Objective markers, bonfire warp, minimal/absent dialogue, procedural loot, soul-currency
  purchases, item descriptions replacing NPC dialogue as the primary lore vector →
  **automatic fail of the piece**, regardless of score.

- **AR-3 (seam sterility):** AR-1 and AR-2 police *leakage* — they ask whether the two halves contaminated each other. They never ask whether the halves ever **touch**. A build can pass both perfectly and be two products stapled together: a competent Souls arena bolted to a competent Morrowind world, sharing a save file and nothing else.
  Every critic must therefore ask: **does this piece create or carry at least one interaction that crosses the seam?** A faction rank that changes what an enemy does. A lore fact that is also a boss's weakness. A disposition that opens a door you would otherwise have to fight through. A spell learned from a book that solves a fight. A quest whose resolution changes an encounter's composition.
  A piece with **zero** boundary-crossing interactions is not automatically failed — some pieces are legitimately internal — but it MUST be reported in the verdict as `seam_sterile: true` with a justification, and the project-level floor in `RI-CMP01` (cross-system payoff matrix) governs how many such pieces are tolerable. Sterility is the failure mode that no amount of per-subsystem excellence detects, and it is the one this project is most likely to die of.

- **CONSUMPTION (not a seam check — read `RI-MTH07`):** AR-1, AR-2 and AR-3 all presuppose that
  what the piece ships is actually *running*. Wave 1 proved that presupposition wrong four times in
  four independent subsystems, and no critic caught it from the doctrine alone. Character creation
  rendered 20 byte-identical screenshots while returning correct values; 87 weapon movesets were
  unreachable from the running game; 50 of 55 magic effects were a single generic applicator behind
  distinct labels; the stealth visibility term was computed every frame into the trace while the
  enemy alert meter filled at a flat rate that never read it. In every case the builder's own
  probes passed honestly, because a probe that calls a function measures the function.
  Therefore, for **every model a piece ships**, the verdict must name the **world-side consumer**
  that reads it, and demonstrate the consumption by **perturbing the model and observing an entity
  change behaviour**. A model with no demonstrated consumer is `unmeasurable ⇒ 0`, exactly as a
  missing model is — **from the player's chair they are the same thing.**
  This is deliberately *not* filed as an AR check. AR-1 and AR-2 ask whether one game's mechanics
  contaminated the other; S26 rules that diluting them to cover ordinary defects would destroy
  their diagnostic value. This asks something different and prior: is any of it plugged in?

A critic that reports "no gap found" has failed its own job and its verdict is void.
Every verdict must name exactly one **single biggest remaining gap** with a concrete,
buildable remedy.

## 4. Visual judgement is bifurcated

- **Art direction** is judged against Morrowind (and the strangeness references). 2002
  screenshots are legitimate references here.
- **Visual fidelity / graphics quality** is judged against current-generation references
  (Elden Ring, Skyrim SE/AE and equivalents). It is a **hard error** for any critic to
  cite a 2002 screenshot when judging fidelity, and equally an error to cite a modern
  AAA screenshot when judging art direction.

## 5. Precedence order (when even this document is ambiguous)

1. The Arbitration Rule (§1) — inside/outside the fight
2. The seam rulings (§2)

4. The dimension owner's judgement, recorded as a new seam ruling in §2 by amendment

Amendments are append-only. Nothing in §2 is ever deleted; superseded rulings are struck
