## The bar

Both reference games spend their first hour doing the same eleven things in almost the same
order, and neither of them spends it on a tutorial. The hour is a **contract**: it tells the
player what kind of attention this game rewards, it takes something away from them, it hands
them an object they cannot use and a name they cannot reach, and it ends with them somewhere
they chose to be. A first hour that is *pleasant* and *clear* and contains none of those
things is the most common way a homage dies before hour two, and it dies invisibly, because
every subsystem in it passed.

This item is the **beat and pacing** view of the opening: what happens, when, in what order,
with what tolerance. It is not the interaction view — the moment-to-moment verb grammar, the
input affordances and the "what does the player actually do with their hands" account of the
same sixty minutes belongs to `corpus/88-journeys/RI-JRN01` and `RI-JRN02` and is **cited, not
duplicated, here**. Where the two disagree about the same minute, the ordering rule is
`CORPUS-CONTRACT` §4 precedence by `judges:` path: JRN owns the interaction, EXP owns the beat.

> One sentence a builder can aim at: **by minute 60 the player must have been released, named,
> lied to, refused, killed, and given a destination they have not reached — and must have
> found at least two things nobody told them about.**

### C. The shared structural beats (the derivation)

Eleven beats appear in both, in near-identical order. This list — not the two tables — is what
binds our build.

| # | Shared beat | Morrowind | Dark Souls | Why it is load-bearing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SB1** | **Confinement, then release into weather** | ship's hold → Seyda Neen dock | cell → asylum courtyard | The first free step is a *contrast*. A game that starts outdoors has spent this beat. |
| **SB2** | **Identity by interrogation, not by cutscene** | census questionnaire | class/gift selection + name | The player *answers*; they are not shown who they are. |
| **SB3** | **An object you cannot use and a name you cannot reach** | sealed package + "Caius Cosades, Balmora" | Estus Flask + "ring the two bells" | The hour's spine: an unresolved obligation pointing off-screen. |
| **SB4** | **An authored defeat or an authored deception** | Fargoth's ring; the taxman's murderer is right | the Asylum Demon you must run from | The game demonstrates early that it will not protect you or flatter you. |
| **SB5** | **Something found, not given, within 10 minutes** | ring in the barrel | key from the falling corpse | The first reward for looking. If everything in the first 10 min is handed over, the player learns not to look. |
| **SB6** | **A named person with a stated opinion about another named person** | Hrisskar on Fargoth | Crestfallen Warrior on everyone | Turns population into society. |
| **SB7** | **An unexplained authored event with no quest attached** | Tarhiel falls out of the sky | the crow; the giant in the tower | The `N-odd` beat. Two reference games, both do it in the first half-hour, neither explains it. |
| **SB8** | **A death the player earned and understood** | Icarian scroll / the swamp | the Burg, or the Demon | Not a scripted death. A death with a legible cause. |
| **SB9** | **A hub with visible exits you are not strong enough for** | Seyda Neen → swamp/Bitter Coast/road | Firelink → graveyard/New Londo/Burg | Choice under uncertainty. Nothing is fenced. |
| **SB10** | **A refusal** | Fargoth's disposition; locked doors; prices | Andre/the merchant's stock; the Black Knight | The world says no for a reason you can act on. |
| **SB11** | **The loop closes or the destination is named and unreached** | arrive in Balmora → told to go away and come back | shortcut back to Firelink | The hour ends by pointing at the next twenty. |

**The one thing neither game does:** tell you what you are. Morrowind's census officer does not
say "Nerevarine"; Caius does not say it at minute 58 either. Dark Souls' Oscar says the
prophecy and then says he doubts it. This is the strongest single negative constraint in the
whole item and it is the hard fail below.

**Partial order (binding, tolerance-independent).** `B03 < B05`; `B04 < B06`; `B10 < B12`;
`B10` before any journal entry naming the tithe-taker; `B08 < B11`; `B13` before `B14`;
every one of `B04, B09, B10, B15` before `B18`. A beat may slide inside its tolerance; it may
never cross one of these.

## Comparison method

Executable against the real harness (`HARNESS.md` §3) plus the `A-EXP1` amendment and the
session driver specified in `PLAYTHROUGH-CRITIC.md` §5.1.

**Step 0 — isolation.** The driving agent is invoked per `PLAYTHROUGH-CRITIC.md` §5.5 at level
`enforced`: a materialised inbox containing this item's §C list **only** (never §D — an agent
that has read the beat sheet cannot measure `T_found`), the game URL, and nothing from
`game/data/**`. `tools/experience/isolation-check.mjs` diffs the tool-call log against the
inbox manifest. `attested` isolation caps this item at 6.

**Step 1 — the run.**

```bash
node tools/experience/session-run.mjs \
  --session exp-w<N>-firsthour --brief corpus/95-experience/briefs/first-hour.md \
  --seed 1337 --minutes 60 --render-policy on-demand --profile first-hour
```

The brief is instruction-neutral (`PLAYTHROUGH-CRITIC.md` §4.2): it states the premise and
"you have an hour", and names **no** activity this item measures. The brief is committed as an
artifact. No debug calls: `teleport`, `spawn`, `aggro`, `setTimeOfDay`, `setWeather` and
`loadState` are refused by the driver under `--profile first-hour`; any invocation voids the run.

**Step 2 — the beat log.** `tools/experience/beat-extract.mjs --in reports/sessions/<id>`
scans `trace.jsonl` for the beat signatures below and emits `beat-log.json`, one record per
detected beat with `{beat_id, f, t_min, evidence:[trace line numbers], confidence}`.

| Beat class | Trace signature |
|---|---|
| release / area change | `events[].type == "load"` with a region transition in `player.pos` |
| character creation | `getQuestState().flags` gains the origin flag; `topicsKnown[]` non-empty for the first time |
| found-not-given | an `item` event whose item id is **absent** from every `journal[]` entry and every `topicsKnown[]` topic at the time it fires |
| given | an `item` event within 30 s of a `topic` event naming the same item |
| combat | first `attack_start` with `enemies[].alert_state == "AGGRO"` |
| death | `events[].type == "death"` with `owner == "player"` |
| refusal | a `topic` event whose response record is flagged `refused` (disposition/rank/knowledge gate) |
| lie | a `topic` whose text asserts a proposition contradicted by a world flag at that frame — resolved in the verification stage against `game/data/**`, never during play |
| odd | an authored `events[]` entry (`spawn`, `journal`-less scripted event) with no `quest_stage` within ±60 s and no journal entry ever |
| hub exits | ≥3 distinct navmesh exits from the start settlement with no blocking volume, per `getWorldStats()` + a route probe |
| tutorial text | UI string events; the driver captures `setUIVisible` overlays and counts characters |

**Step 3 — the diff.** `tools/experience/beat-diff.mjs --beats corpus/95-experience/RI-EXP01.beats.json
--log reports/sessions/<id>/beat-log.json` emits `beat-diff.json`: per beat, `hit|late|early|missing`,
signed minutes off target, and the derived-target table computed. `RI-EXP01.beats.json` is the
machine-readable §D table and is generated from this file by `tools/experience/beats-from-md.mjs`
so the two cannot drift.

**Step 4 — the blind pair (`blind_pair: yes`).** A *different* agent (`CRITIC-DOCTRINE` §5)
strips our beat log and the two reference beat logs in §A/§B to a common form — `t_min`, verb,
G/F, odd, lethal, with all proper nouns replaced by `[NPC-1]`, `[PLACE-2]`, `[OBJECT-3]` — and
presents three unlabelled hours. The judge is asked, before the reveal: *(a)* rank them by
"which hour is the opening of a game people still talk about in twenty years"; *(b)* name the
tell. Record blind pick, rationale, reveal. Per `CORPUS-CONTRACT` §6, our hour ranking first is
a signal to distrust the pack and re-run it with a harsher stripper.

**Step 5 — the negative artifact.** For every `missing` beat, `beat-diff.json` must carry the
search that would have found it and its empty output (`PLAYTHROUGH-CRITIC.md` §6).

**Step 6 — cross-check with the interaction view.** If `corpus/88-journeys/RI-JRN01/02` produced
a verdict this wave, `beat-diff.json` records `jrn_agreement`: for each beat, whether the
journeys item observed the same minute. A disagreement > 5 minutes on any of B01–B05 is
reported as a referral, not silently averaged.

## Scoring

Native scale 0–100, then translated per `SCORING.md` §1.2.

| Component | Weight | Full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Beat coverage (`beats_hit`) | 30 | 18/18 inside tolerance |
| Ordering (`order_violations`) | 10 | 0 |
| Discovery (`T_found`, `N_found`) | 15 | `T_found ≤ 10` and `N_found ≥ 3` |
| Lethality (`T_death`) | 10 | first death in 18–32 min, cause legible from the trace |
| Strangeness (`T_odd`, `N_odd`) | 10 | ≥2 unexplained authored events, first by minute 25 |
| Society (`N_persons`, `T_lie`, `T_refusal`) | 15 | ≥4 opinionated named NPCs, a verifiable lie and a stateful refusal inside 20 min |
| Verb breadth (`V_taught`) | 10 | ≥7 verbs, none taught by instructional text |

| Native | Band | Ladder ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 85 | Meets the bar | 8 |
| 70–84 | Below bar — named remedy required | 6 |
| 50–69 | Loses outright | 4 |
| < 50 | We lose | 2 |

**Native → ladder anchors (required by `SCORING.md` W7 discipline):** native 55 → ladder 4;
native 75 → ladder 6; native 88 → ladder 8.

**Hard fails — any one caps the item at 2 and, per `PLAYTHROUGH-CRITIC.md` §2, makes the wave
`DEAD`:**

1. **The player is told what they are before minute 45.** Any line, journal entry, title, UI
   element or achievement asserting the player is chosen, prophesied, special, the last of
   anything, or the only one who can. (Both reference games' single sharpest restraint.)
2. **No death in the hour**, or the only death is scripted/unavoidable.
3. **No discovery**: `N_found == 0`, or every item obtained in the hour was named by an NPC or
   the journal first.
4. **No unexplained event**: `N_odd == 0`. Everything that happened had a reason attached.
5. **A fenced exit**: any of the ≥3 hub exits is blocked by an invisible wall, a level check, a
   "you are not strong enough" message, or a soft-lock corridor. Lethality is the only legal fence.
6. **The hour is a tutorial**: `tutorial_text_chars > 1500`, or `V_taught` verbs are introduced
   by instructional overlay rather than by consequence.
7. **The run could not be produced** (`session-run.mjs` absent or exit ≥ 10) — `unmeasurable → 0`,
   fail-closed, per `CRITIC-DOCTRINE` §7.3. Not an excuse.

## How we lose

- **We build a tutorial and call it an opening.** Because a first hour is the first thing anyone
  demos, and demos reward clarity. Symptom: `tutorial_text_chars` in the thousands, `T_found`
  never fires, every verb is introduced by a popup. Both reference games teach the roll by
  killing you.
- **We front-load the identity.** Somebody writes a beautiful line at minute 12 that tells the
  player they are the one the marsh has been waiting for, because it is the most exciting thing
  we have and it feels wasteful to hold it. Hard fail 1 exists because this is the single most
  likely creative decision in the whole project.
- **The odd thing gets a quest attached.** B09 is authored with no explanation; the first content
  reviewer says "so what's the *point* of the falling body?" and it acquires a journal entry, a
  reward and a resolution. It is then no longer the beat. `N_odd` is measured as *events with no
  quest*, precisely because the failure is additive rather than subtractive.
- **The exits get fenced.** Difficulty tiers are real (`S9`), so somebody puts a soft wall on the
  tier-4 road out of Tidewrack "so new players don't get lost". That single decision converts
  the hub beat (SB9) into a corridor and deletes the reason anyone remembers Firelink.
- **B10 gets a marker.** The tithe-taker's body is found-before-told; the quest system wants a
  giver, so the quest is offered first "for discoverability", and the strongest beat in the
  Morrowind opening — *the world had a plot and did not wait for you* — inverts into an errand.
- **The beat sheet is satisfied and the hour is still dead**, because every beat landed as a
  cutscene. This is why `V_taught` requires **exercised twice**, `N_found` requires *absent from
  the journal*, and the blind pair in step 4 exists at all.
- **The hour is measured by the agent that wrote the content.** `PLAYTHROUGH-CRITIC.md` §8. The
  isolation level is recorded per run; `assumed` produces no admissible evidence.
- **Tolerances get treated as targets.** Eighteen beats at ±5 min is not a schedule; it is a
  band. A build that lands every beat exactly on target is a build that scripted the hour, and
  the ordering invariants — not the timestamps — are the part that matters.

## Provenance note

`provenance: canonical-recall`, `confidence: medium`. §A and §B are reconstructed from memory of
both games and cross-checked against public references for the named entities, sequence and
mechanics: UESP and community wikis confirm the Seyda Neen census-office → Fargoth's ring →
Processus Vitellius ("Death of a Taxman", body NW of town, Foryn Gilnith) → Tarhiel ("A Falling
Wizard", Scrolls of Icarian Flight, Fortify Acrobatics 1000 pts / 7 s) chain and the
"Caius Cosades in Balmora" hand-off; Dark Souls wikis confirm the Northern Undead Asylum →
Asylum Demon (beatable only with the Firebomb gift, otherwise run past) → Oscar of Astora and
the Estus Flask → the crow → Firelink Shrine hub → Undead Burg chain, and the Firelink shortcut
structure.

**The timestamps in §A and §B are the untrustworthy part and are labelled as such.** Neither
game has a scripted clock in its opening; the minute values are median first-time-player
positions, not engine facts, and a critic may not cite them as measured. What is *not*
soft is the **order** and the **presence/absence** of each beat, which is why the binding
instrument in §D is the partial order plus the derived targets, and why the tolerances are wide.

§C (the shared beats) is a derivation, and derivations are arguable: SB4 in particular fuses two
things — Morrowind's deception and Souls' authored defeat — under one heading, on the claim that
both are the game demonstrating it will not protect the player. A critic who thinks that fusion
is wrong should file an amendment rather than score around it.

§D is `constructed`: our targets, our tolerances, our proper nouns. It is binding anyway, per
`CORPUS-CONTRACT` §3 — a constructed bar we can measure beats a recalled number we cannot.
Proper nouns marked *(proposed)* are advisory and owned by `RI-QST06` / `RI-WLD03`;
Tidewrack's tier and region and Lilmoth's identity come from `settlements.json` and
`regions.json`, which are authoritative.
