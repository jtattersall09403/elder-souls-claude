## The bar

The ending is the least-played content in any project and the most-discussed content in any game
people still talk about. Both facts are true at once and the second is why this item exists.

The mistake this item is written against is not "the ending is bad". It is **the ending being
spectacular and meaningless**: a final hour that is a corridor of arena rooms, a boss with six
phases, a cutscene that resolves a story the player was a passenger in, and a credits roll that ends
the game. Every one of those is expensive, every one is what a team builds when it is out of time and
wants the last thing to land, and together they produce an ending that could have been appended to
any playthrough of the game.

Morrowind's ending is the counter-example and it is the harder thing to build. You reach the heart of
a mountain, you have a conversation with a man who has been right about several things, you break a
machine, a goddess thanks you briefly, and then **you are still in the world, on a Tuesday, with a
journal full of things you did not finish.** It is anticlimactic and it is earned, and the two are
the same property: the weight came from the nineteen hours, not from the last ten minutes.

> One sentence a builder can aim at: **the last hour must be low on spectacle and high on
> specificity — a short, quiet, arguable ending whose text and world state could not have been
> produced by anyone else's playthrough — and the player must be able to keep standing in the world
> afterwards, or be refused in fiction.**

This item owns the final hour, the point of no return, both ending routes, and the world afterwards.
`RI-QST06` owns the five-act architecture, the backpath and the antagonist's Act V requirements and
is **cited, not duplicated**, here; where the two disagree, `CORPUS-CONTRACT` §4 precedence applies —
`RI-QST06` owns `quests.main.*`, this item owns `experience.ending.*`. `RI-EXP02` owns whether the
ending was remembered; `RI-DLG05` owns the journal's voice in the final entries.

## The reference artifact

### A. Morrowind's last hour (canonical-recall)

| Beat | What happens | The property being demonstrated |
|---|---|---|
| The approach | Red Mountain's interior is a **populated place**, not a boss corridor: Sixth House bases, Ash Vampires with names who must be killed to weaken the antagonist, corridors that are dungeons | The final approach is content, not a hallway |
| The point of no return | **There is barely one.** The Ghostgate is a place you walk through and can walk back out of. The game does not stop you, warn you, or confirm anything | Irreversibility is fictional, not modal |
| The conversation | Dagoth Ur talks first, at length, pressably. He is **right** that the Tribunal stole the Heart, that they murdered Nerevar, that the Empire is an occupation. He offers the player a place | The antagonist is not refuted by the game |
| The fight | A short fight in a mechanical heart-chamber. Two ways to end it: kill him, or destroy the Heart with Sunder and Keening — **the second is the real one and it is a tool-use puzzle, not a damage race** | ≥ 1 non-violent-in-spirit resolution |
| The resolution | Azura appears, says a few lines, thanks you. No cutscene of note. **No credits.** | Low spectacle |
| The world after | You are dropped back in. Blight storms stop. Some NPCs comment. Dagoth Ur's dreamers fall silent. Ash storms continue. **Every unfinished quest is still there and you can go do it.** | The world persists and you are in it |
| The alternative | Kill the wrong person at any point and get *"the thread of prophecy is severed… you may still"* — the game keeps going, harder, on a backpath | The ending is reachable by breaking the intended path |

**The two things Morrowind does that almost nothing else does:** the ending does not end the game,
and the antagonist wins the argument on points and loses on force.

### B. Dark Souls' last hour (canonical-recall)

| Beat | What happens | The property being demonstrated |
|---|---|---|
| The approach | The Kiln of the First Flame: short, grey, ash, four Black Knights. Quiet. There is a bonfire | Low spectacle, and the last new place |
| The point of no return | The Lordvessel-gated fog to the Kiln. **Signalled by fiction** — you had to place the Lordvessel and collect four Lord Souls; everyone told you what it was for. No dialog box | Signalled by the world, not by UI |
| The conversation | **There is none.** Gwyn does not speak. Everything he would say was said by Frampt and Kaathe, who contradict each other, and neither is trustworthy | The argument can be delivered *before* the last room |
| The fight | Gwyn is short, sad, and **parryable** — mechanically the easiest late boss in the game, deliberately. He is a man who set himself on fire | Anticlimax as authored meaning |
| The resolution | Link the fire, or walk away. Two endings, no commentary, no ranking, no "good/bad" label | The game does not tell you which is right |
| The world after | **There is none.** NG+ resets. The game refuses the aftermath | A refusal, stated in fiction |
| The alternative | The Dark ending requires having found Kaathe, which requires a route most players miss | Reachable by breaking the intended path |

### C. The extracted rules (the derivation — this is what binds)

Seven properties appear in both, and they are the item's spine. Note the third and fourth are
opposites of what most games do at this point.

| # | Rule | Morrowind | Dark Souls |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E1** | **The final approach is a place, not a corridor.** It has inhabitants, optional content, and a route through it the player picks. | Red Mountain interior | the Kiln + Black Knights |
| **E2** | **The point of no return is signalled by the world and crossed without confirmation.** No modal, no "are you sure", no autosave prompt. | Ghostgate (barely a PONR at all) | the Lordvessel fog |
| **E3** | **The antagonist is not refuted by the game.** His case survives, cites facts the player independently verified, and is answered by the player's action rather than by the narrative. | Dagoth Ur on the Tribunal | Frampt vs Kaathe; Gwyn's silence |
| **E4** | **Spectacle is low.** The ending is short, quiet, and mechanically modest relative to the run's peak. | a small chamber, two artefacts | a parryable man |
| **E5** | **Specificity is high.** What the ending says is about *this* playthrough. | your faction, your crimes, who you killed, the severance state | your covenant, your route, which liar you believed |
| **E6** | **≥ 2 endings that differ in the world, not in a flag.** | intended vs severed-thread backpath | Link vs Dark |
| **E7** | **The aftermath is either playable or refused in fiction.** Never a fade to a menu. | playable, world changed | refused: the age turns, NG+ |

**E4 and E5 together are what "anticlimactic but earned" actually means**, and stating them as two
measurable quantities is the main thing this item contributes. Anticlimax is not a feeling; it is a
low spectacle budget. Earned is not a feeling; it is the ending citing things only this player did.
A build can be graded on both.

### D. OUR required last hour (binding)

Setting per `RI-QST06`: five acts, Act V is `The Argument`, the point of no return is Act V quest 4,
the backpath becomes reachable at the end of Act III. Proper nouns are `RI-QST06`'s and may change
without touching this item.

| id | Requirement | Bar | Fail |
|---|---|---|---|

| **LH2** | **PONR position.** The point of no return sits at ≥ 0.94 of the run's total content by quest count — it is a *last-hour* threshold, not a mid-game lockout | ≥ 0.94 | < 0.85 |

| **LH6** | **The conversation precedes the violence.** Per `RI-QST06` Act V: a long, pressable, pre-violence conversation, ≥ 12 topics, ≥ 4 of them unlocked by things the player learned elsewhere | ≥ 12 topics, ≥ 4 knowledge-gated | ≤ 5 topics |

| **LH10** | **Spectacle cap (E4).** Non-interactive scripted seconds in the final hour ≤ **90 s** total; final boss phases ≤ **3**; the final encounter's DPS-check requirement no higher than the run's 75th-percentile encounter | all three | > 240 s of cutscene, or ≥ 5 phases |
| **LH11** | **Specificity floor (E5).** The ending's delivered text (final conversation responses + closing lines + the final journal entries) cites ≥ **5 player-specific facts** drawn from ≥ **3 distinct categories**: faction and rank, a named NPC the player killed or spared, a quest resolution taken, a crime or bounty, a breakage from `RI-EXP06` the player used, a book the player read, the route taken to the PONR | ≥ 5 facts, ≥ 3 categories | ≤ 1 fact → **hard fail** |

| **LH14** | **No terminal furniture.** Zero credits gates, zero score screens, zero "achievement" or completion-percentage surfaces, zero grade letters | **0** | ≥ 1 |

## Comparison method

**Step 0 — dosage.** This item is scoreable **only at `FULL` tier** (`PLAYTHROUGH-CRITIC.md` §3). At
`FRAGMENT` and `PARTIAL` every check is `unmeasurable ⇒ 0` and the item scores 0 — fail-closed, and
reported to the wave as "the ending was not reached", which is a finding about the wave, not about
the ending. **P1 requires both routes**: the intended ending once, and the backpath once per project.

**Step 1 — both routes.** Two chains from the same seed, diverging at the `RI-QST06` Act III backpath
branch, each played to an ending by fresh segment agents under the normal baton protocol.

```bash
node tools/experience/session-run.mjs --session exp-w<N>-endA-s01 --resume <act3 state> \
  --brief corpus/95-experience/briefs/ending-intended.md --seed 1337 --minutes 90
node tools/experience/session-run.mjs --session exp-w<N>-endB-s01 --resume <act3 state> \
  --brief corpus/95-experience/briefs/ending-backpath.md --seed 1337 --minutes 90
```

Both briefs are instruction-neutral (`PLAYTHROUGH-CRITIC.md` §4.2): they state the premise and that
the player intends to finish, and name **no** property this item measures. Both are committed.

**Step 2 — the PONR probe.** `tools/experience/ponr-probe.mjs`:
- locates the PONR as the first world flag whose set-transition makes ≥ 1 previously-reachable quest
  stage unreachable, verified by a `loadState` fork that attempts the old route and fails;
- computes `ponr_position_fraction` from `game/data/quests/**` completed-behind vs total;
- scans the preceding 90 minutes of trace for the ≥ 3 signal channels, each with its evidence line;
- counts `ponr_modals` from `setUIVisible` overlays and UI string events in the crossing window;
- **walks back through it.** If the fork can return, LH5 fails and the PONR is decorative.

**Step 3 — ending state diff.** `tools/experience/ending-diff.mjs --a <endA chain> --b <endB chain>`
exports, for each route: final `getQuestState().flags`, the final journal, and — via `loadState` on
each ending's `state-out.json` — a scripted sweep asking **the same 6 topics of the same 40 named
NPCs** in both worlds. Emits `world_flag_delta`, `npc_reaction_delta` (string-inequality on response
ids, not on prose similarity), and `availability_delta`. **The sweep is the load-bearing part**: flag
counts are trivial to inflate and NPC responses are not, because a differing response requires
somebody to have written one.

**Step 4 — specificity resolution.** Every noun-phrase in the ending's delivered text is resolved
against the *player's own trace* by `tools/experience/ending-specificity.mjs`: a cited fact counts
only if it names something this playthrough actually did, locatable in the chain. A line that would
be identical in every playthrough scores zero. This is the same resolution rule as `RI-EXP02` §E and
uses the same resolver.

**Step 5 — the antagonist audit.** Each claim the antagonist makes in the LH6 conversation is
extracted and classified by a judging agent that **did not play** (`PLAYTHROUGH-CRITIC.md` §8) as
`verified` (resolvable to an earlier trace event the player observed), `unverifiable`, or
`contradicted`. Then the same agent scans all post-conversation text — closing lines, journal
entries, ally dialogue, any post-ending topic — for **authored refutation**: any assertion, by any
authored voice, that the antagonist was simply wrong. `antagonist_refuted_by_game = true` if one
exists. The judge is not told which answer is wanted.

**Step 6 — the blind pair (`blind_pair: yes`).** A *different* agent (`CRITIC-DOCTRINE` §5,
`RI-MTH03`) prepares three final conversations, stripped to a common form — speaker tokens
`[ANTAGONIST]` / `[PLAYER]`, all proper nouns tokenised consistently within each transcript,
identical formatting, identical length budget: ours, a reconstructed Vivec/Dagoth Ur exchange, and a
reconstructed Frampt-and-Kaathe pair. The judge is asked, **before the reveal**:
*(a)* which of these believes its own argument; *(b)* for each, state the antagonist's strongest
claim and whether the transcript refutes it; *(c)* which one you would cut. Record blind pick,
rationale, reveal. Per `CORPUS-CONTRACT` §6, **ours winning is a signal to distrust the pack**, not a
pass condition. The scored outputs are `judge_named_our_antagonists_strongest_claim` and
`judge_did_not_find_our_refutation` — both of which we want true, and neither of which is a win rate.

**Step 7 — the aftermath run.** After each ending, 20 further simulated minutes with an
instruction-neutral brief ("you are still here; do as you like"). `tools/experience/aftermath-diff.mjs`
diffs the observable world against the pre-ending state: NPC dialogue, weather/ambient state, faction
presence, road and interior availability, hostile roster. `post_ending_observable_changes` counts
changes a player could notice without opening a menu. If the aftermath is refused instead, the
refusal's delivery is captured and its pressable topics counted.

**Step 8 — the sabotage control (mandatory, `PLAYTHROUGH-CRITIC.md` §4.5).** This item is one of the
two that **SAB-C (choice collapsed)** must detect: every quest reduced to its first resolution,
refusals removed, from the same commit, by data filter. Two matched 90-minute segments covering the
Act III→V stretch, labelled A/B by recorded coin flip. Required margin: **consequence-persistence
count lower in the sabotaged variant by ≥ 70%**, and `ending_specific_facts` lower by ≥ 50%. Failure
to separate ⇒ **`VOID`, not `FAIL`**.

**Step 9 — the negative artifact.** Every failed check carries the query that would have found it and
its empty output. "LH11 = 1 fact, resolved: the player's faction; the resolver's full candidate list
and its 4 unresolved noun-phrases are attached" — not "specificity low".

## Scoring

Native scale 0–100, translated per `SCORING.md` §1.2.

| Component | Weight | Full marks |
|---|---|---|
| The approach as a place (LH1, LH15) | 10 | both |
| Point of no return (LH2–LH5) | 20 | position ≥ 0.94, ≥ 3 channels, 0 modals, 4 threads closed in fiction |
| The argument (LH6–LH8) | 20 | ≥ 12 topics, ≥ 2 verified claims, no authored refutation |
| **Anticlimax: spectacle cap + specificity floor (LH10, LH11)** | **20** | ≤ 90 s scripted, ≤ 3 phases, ≥ 5 facts across ≥ 3 categories |
| Two real endings (LH12, LH16) | 15 | flag/NPC/availability deltas all met; severance path honoured |
| Aftermath and furniture (LH9, LH13, LH14) | 15 | non-violent route exists, aftermath playable or refused in fiction, zero terminal furniture |

| Native | Band | Ladder ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 85 | Meets the bar | 8 |
| 70–84 | Below bar — named remedy required | 6 |
| 50–69 | Loses outright | 4 |
| < 50 | We lose | 2 |

**Native → ladder anchors:** native 55 → ladder 4; native 75 → ladder 6; native 88 → ladder 8.
Ladder 9–10 additionally requires step 6 producing `judge_named_our_antagonists_strongest_claim = true`
**and** `judge_did_not_find_our_refutation = true` while returning the opposite for at least one
reference transcript — i.e. our antagonist argues better than a reference antagonist, with the
transcripts as the artifact.

**Hard fails — any one caps the item at 2:**

1. **The two endings differ only in a final flag** (LH12): `world_flag_delta < 5` or
   `npc_reaction_delta == 0`. `BAR-CRITIQUE-01` §2.3's stated hard fail, kept verbatim in force.
2. **The antagonist is refuted by the game** (LH8): any authored voice asserts he was simply wrong.
   `BAR-CRITIQUE-01` §2.3's second stated hard fail.
3. **`ending_specific_facts ≤ 1`** (LH11). The ending could have been appended to anyone's
   playthrough. This is the operational form of "spectacular and meaningless".
4. **A modal at the point of no return** (LH4), or any "you cannot return" UI string. The world says
   it or nobody says it.
5. **`antagonist_verified_claims == 0`** (LH7). He is a villain rather than a position.
6. **The ending is unreachable**, or the backpath is unreachable, or the severance path produces a
   game over (LH16, `S10`). A `not_reachable` ending is a `DEAD` wave per `PLAYTHROUGH-CRITIC.md` §2,
   not an excuse.
7. **Credits gate the world** (LH14): the player cannot return to the world and no in-fiction refusal
   was delivered.
8. **`FULL` tier not reached** — `unmeasurable ⇒ 0`, fail-closed.
9. **The sabotage control was not run, or failed to separate at the stated margins** ⇒ **`VOID`**.

## How we lose

- **We build the spectacle because it is the last thing anyone sees.** Six phases, a four-minute
  cutscene, an orchestral swell, a credits roll. Every one of those is a positive decision made by
  someone trying to make the ending land, and together they produce LH10's failure and LH11's. The
  spectacle cap is deliberately a **cap** — a number that goes down — because it is the only kind of
  requirement that survives a team's enthusiasm.
- **The ending text is written before the playthrough exists**, so it cannot cite anything the player
  did, so LH11 fails structurally. Specificity is not a prose problem; it is a data problem, and if
  the ending's strings are not authored as templates over world flags they can never be specific. The
  remedy names a file, not a rewrite.
- **Someone answers the antagonist.** An ally gets a closing line explaining why he was wrong,
  because the alternative feels irresponsible. Hard fail 2 exists because this is the single most
  likely creative decision in Act V, and it is the exact inverse of what both reference games do.
  Dagoth Ur is never refuted; he is *stopped*.
- **A confirmation dialog appears at the point of no return**, added late, by someone reasonable, to
  stop players locking themselves out. It is the correct modern decision and it deletes E2. LH3's
  three-channel requirement exists so that the diegetic version is *better* than the modal, not
  merely purer: three channels over 45 minutes tells the player more than a dialog box ever does.
- **The backpath is built as a flag.** `RI-QST06` requires it; the cheapest way to satisfy it is a
  second ending string behind a boolean. LH12's NPC sweep is the defence and it is expensive
  precisely because it cannot be faked: forty NPCs × six topics × two worlds is content or it is
  nothing.
- **The aftermath is cut.** It is the last thing built, it is only 20 minutes of play, and no verdict
  before this one measured it. Then the game fades to a menu and Morrowind's single most distinctive
  ending property — *you are still here, and your journal is still full* — is gone. LH13 accepts a
  refusal as an alternative specifically so that the cheap correct option (Dark Souls' NG+) stays
  available and the *silent* option does not.
- **The final area is a boss corridor** because the last area is built last and a corridor is what
  fits in the remaining time. LH1's four sub-requirements are all cheap individually and the corridor
  happens anyway.
- **Nobody finishes the game.** `P1` requires the main quest completed and the backpath once per
  project; twenty hours × two routes is the most expensive single requirement in the whole experience
  area, and it will be the first cut. Then this item is `unmeasurable ⇒ 0`, the wave is `DEAD` for
  unmeasurability, and the pressure will be to reclassify. `PLAYTHROUGH-CRITIC.md` §10's last bullet
  is about exactly this moment.

## Provenance note

`provenance: canonical-recall`, `confidence: medium`.

**§A and §B are reconstructed from memory of both games** and cross-checked against public references
for the named entities and sequence: community references confirm Morrowind's Red Mountain interior
containing Ash Vampire lieutenants, the Dagoth Ur confrontation in the heart chamber, the
Sunder/Keening route to destroying the Heart, Azura's post-victory appearance, the absence of a
credits gate, the persistence of the world afterwards, and the *"the thread of prophecy is severed…
you may still"* message on killing a quest-critical NPC. Dark Souls references confirm the
Lordvessel-gated Kiln, the four Black Knights, Gwyn's parryable and comparatively easy fight, the
Link-the-Fire / Age-of-Dark ending pair with no authorial ranking, the Frampt/Kaathe contradiction,
and NG+ replacing an aftermath.

**What is untrustworthy is the emphasis, not the facts.** The claim that Morrowind's ending is
"anticlimactic but earned" is an interpretation, widely shared and not universal — a substantial
number of players consider it simply a weak ending, and that reading is not obviously wrong. This
item takes the interpretive position that the anticlimax is load-bearing and encodes it as LH10 +
LH11. **A critic who rejects that position is rejecting the item's central claim and should file an
amendment rather than score around it.** The strongest counter-argument, stated so it is on the
record: Dark Souls' ending is anticlimactic *and* the game refuses the aftermath, so E4 and E7 may be
independent rather than complementary, and a build could satisfy E4 by being small and dull.
`LH11`'s specificity floor is the only thing separating "small and meaningful" from "small", and it
is the number to attack if the item is wrong.

**§C is a derivation.** E4/E5 as a *pair* is this item's contribution and appears nowhere upstream.
E3 is `RI-QST06`'s Act V requirement restated as a measurable, and is the least arguable rule here.

**§D is `constructed`** — our thresholds, our counts. Binding anyway per `CORPUS-CONTRACT` §3. The
asserted numbers most likely to be wrong are `0.94` for `ponr_position_fraction` (chosen so the PONR
is unambiguously in the last hour of a 20-hour run; a 5-act game might reasonably place it at 0.90),
`90 s` of scripted time (a round number, defensible only as "shorter than one Morrowind conversation
and shorter than any modern final cutscene"), and `≥ 25` world flags for LH12 (which will be wrong in
whichever direction our flag granularity turns out to be — the NPC-reaction delta is the robust half
of that check and should dominate if they disagree).

Until the SAB-C control has run at least once, this item carries `calibrated: false` in the verdict
and **may not exceed ladder 6** (`PLAYTHROUGH-CRITIC.md` §4.5).
