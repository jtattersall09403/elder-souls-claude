## The bar

Morrowind's world is *dated*. Every quest in it sits on a specific political moment — the Empire has
just installed a puppet king, the Great Houses are jockeying under an Imperial charter, a plague is
coming down off Red Mountain — and because the moment is specific, ordinary NPCs can have ordinary,
contradictory opinions about it, and the player can take sides that cost them something. The bar is a
political substrate concrete enough that a quest designer never has to ask "would this be allowed?"
and a dialogue writer never has to ask "what does a fisherman in Archon think about the Legion?" One
page, eight settlements, five powers, four live grievances, and a stated answer to *why now*. The
failure state is a timeless fantasy province where nothing is currently happening and every quest is
therefore a favour for a stranger.

## The reference artifact

### 1. The era: 3E 426 Sun's Dusk → 3E 427 Evening Star. Play opens **Second Seed, 3E 427.**

**In-world Argonian dating (constructed):** interior Argonians do not use the Imperial calendar. They
count by the Hist's cycle — *sap-falls*. Our year is **"the Ninth Sap-Fall since the Rootless Egg."**
An NPC who gives you an Imperial date is telling you something about themselves.

**Why this year, argued:**

| Reason | Consequence for the game |
|---|---|
| **Slavery is still legal in Morrowind.** House Dres raids Black Marsh for Argonian slaves and will keep doing so for years yet (CF-062). Abolition is not close enough to be hope. | The single most emotionally live grievance in Argonian fiction is a *current event*, not a memory. Every Argonian NPC over twenty has lost someone this decade. |
| **Morrowind is distracted.** In 3E 427 Vvardenfell is consumed by Blight, the Sixth House, and the Nerevarine crisis. | Diegetic cause for two things we need: (a) Dres pushes *harder* south because its northern estates are failing; (b) the Legion's Black Marsh cohorts are under-strength because reserves went to Vvardenfell (CF-C001). Our province is starved of Imperial attention *for a reason the player can learn*, and the reason is a game they may have played. |
| **The Empire is at its late-Septim nadir.** Uriel VII is old, the Elder Council rules by procedure, provincial governors are effectively unsupervised. | Imperial corruption is structural rather than villainous. The Blackrose labour-lease scandal is possible without inventing a moustache-twirling governor. |
| **The Blackwood Company is not yet what it becomes.** Canon documents it in Leyawiin in 3E 433 with a stolen Hist in its cellar (CF-064) and states no founding date (CF-065). | We can stage the *acquisition*. In 3E 427 the Company is a young Leyawiin outfit in the marsh looking for a tree — which makes our game a prequel to a canonical fact without contradicting one. **This is the cleanest invention hook in the entire dossier.** |
| **The An-Xileel do not exist yet** (CF-067). | We get isolationist politics with none of the An-Xileel's baggage, and the sentiment we depict can be read as the soil the party later grows in. We must never name them. |
| **Six years before the Oblivion Crisis.** | Dread with no answer. Marsh omens, dreams, wrong hatchings — Morrowind did exactly this with the Blight. Nothing in our game explains them, and nothing should. |

**Rejected alternatives, for the record:** *2E 5xx (Knahaten Flu)* — the plague swallows every other
plot and the outcome is fixed. *2E 582 (ESO)* — a crowded, heavily-authored period with an Alliance
War we would have to serve. *4E 40s (Umbriel)* — the An-Xileel own the province and the story is
already told, in a novel. **Late 3E is the least authored, best documented, most politically loaded
window in Black Marsh's history, and it is the one Morrowind itself occupies.**

### 2. The five powers

| Power | Wants | Will not do | Leverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Empire** (Provincial Office, Gideon; Legion cohorts on the coast) | Tax revenue, quiet coasts, Blackrose full, no Dres incident that reaches the Elder Council. **Not conquest** — the interior has been written off for three hundred years. | Send troops inland. March on Helstrom. Enforce anti-slavery law it has not passed. | Ships, coin, the only courts, and the road to Cyrodiil. |
| **House Dres** (Morrowind; salt-factors, ships, inland raiding parties) | Bodies. Specifically hatchling-to-adult Argonians, taken from the Rootlands and the Deep Marshes, shipped from the Crimson Coast. | Raid an Imperial-chartered settlement in daylight. Attack a Hist directly — Dres slavers are superstitious about the trees and this is exploitable. | Money, Imperial officials on retainer, and the fact that no law forbids them. |
| **The Hist** (through rootkeepers, sapwells, and dreams) | *Unstated by canon* (CF-013). **Our answer:** circulation. The Hist wants the soul-tide to keep moving — deaths drunk, patterns kept, hatchlings issued. It is indifferent to who rules and acutely concerned with who *drains*. | Speak plainly. Take a side in a war between two kinds of foreigner. Explain itself. | It decides what hatches. It can withhold. It can, if pressed, do to the province what it did to the Deadlands (CF-011). |
| **The interior tribes** (Helstrom, the Deep Marshes, the Stone Forest) | To be left alone; to have the coast cities emptied; to stop losing kin to Dres; increasingly, to punish the *lukiul* who trade with the takers. | Unite. There is no Argonian nation, and building one is not a thing the tribes want — it is a thing an *outsider* would want for them. | Numbers, terrain, and the fact that no foreigner can navigate the interior alive. |
| **The coastal Argonians / lukiul** (Gideon, Archon, Lilmoth, Soulrest) | Contracts, protection, the river tolls, and not to be murdered by their own kin for the crime of literacy. | Go inland. Give up Imperial law, which for them is the only thing standing between their families and a Dres net. | Money, translation, and the fact that every faction in the province needs a guide. |

### 3. Who holds each settlement, 3E 427

*(Governance below is `constructed` — canon names no officials for this era, CF-072. Place names are
binding canon-for-this-project, CF-080..CF-088.)*

| Settlement | Nominal authority | Actual power | The live tension |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gideon** (west, Blackwood, Cyrodiil border) | Imperial Provincial Office; a Colovian Governor who has not left the compound in two years | The **Wet Ledger** — a compact of lukiul river-factors and Imperial merchants who own the tolls, the warehouses, and most of the Governor's debts | The Ledger is quietly buying the Legion's supply contracts. Interior Argonians consider every Ledger factor a traitor and have begun saying so with knives. |
| **Blackrose** (south-central) | Imperial prison-fortress; a Legion Prefect commands | The Prefect, running a **labour-lease**: prisoners rented out to coastal plantations and, through intermediaries, to Dres factors. Legal on paper. Slavery in fact. | If the lease reaches the Elder Council it ends careers. Several people would rather it did not. This is a Morrowind-shaped scandal: everyone knows, nobody will say it in writing. |
| **Lilmoth** (far south, coastal) | Imperial charter, technically | Nobody. Four harbourmasters, a rotting quarter no one enters, and a Hist inside the city whose rootkeepers do not acknowledge the Empire exists | Lilmoth is where the Blackwood Company is sniffing. The city is too broken to notice or care. |
| **Soulrest** (south-west, by the Stone Wastes) | An Imperial customs post | The **Drowned Court** — a mortuary order, mixed-race, that buries or burns the dead of every people for a fee and has for four hundred years | The Court knows more about what happens to souls in this province than any priest, scholar, or tree-keeper, and it is selling that knowledge to someone. |
| **Archon** (east coast) | Imperial charter | A fishing-and-salvage town run by its captains; the Legion presence is one under-manned cohort | Dres ships put in at Archon "for water." Everyone knows what is in the holds. The captains take the money. |
| **Stormhold** (north, under the Morrowind mountains) | Imperial garrison — the largest in the province | The garrison, genuinely; Stormhold is the one place the Empire is strong | And it is strong for the wrong reason: it faces *Morrowind*, not the marsh. Stormhold is where the Flu began (CF-045); the quarter it began in is still walled off, and the Legion uses it as a stores depot. |
| **Thorn** (north-east) | Imperial on paper | Contested. Interior tribes have been closing the tracks around Thorn for two seasons | Thorn is the province's early-warning system. When Thorn falls silent, the interior has decided something. |
| **Helstrom** (deep interior) | None. The Empire has never held it | The **Xul-Aneekh** — "the Deep-Kin," rootkeepers and their war-broods; not a government, a consensus | The Deep-Kin have started saying that the coast cities are a wound and wounds are cut out. This is the sentiment that becomes the An-Xileel six years from now. We never name them that. |

### 4. Our factions (all `constructed` unless marked)

**Original — these carry the game:**

1. **The Xul-Aneekh (the Deep-Kin)** — interior isolationists out of Helstrom and the Deep Marshes.
   Not evil, not wrong, and willing to kill lukiul children. Rank titles in Jel. Joining them closes
   the Wet Ledger and most of the coast to you.
2. **The Wet Ledger** — Gideon's lukiul-and-Imperial merchant compact. Contracts, tolls, ledgers,
   deniability. The only faction that will make you rich. Every gold it pays you came off a barge that
   passed a Dres ship at Archon and said nothing.
3. **The Ixtu-Vakh (the Sap-Cutters)** — Argonian heretics who cut sapwells that were never meant to be
   cut and sell the sap in stoppered phials to foreigners. They are the reason the Blackwood Company
   knows what Hist sap does. The most hated faction in the province and the best-paid.
4. **The Drowned Court of Soulrest** — undertakers with a theology. They believe the soul-tide is
   failing and they have the corpse-count to prove it. Ambiguous allies; the main quest's best source.

**Canon powers, era-adapted (borrowed — keep under 25%, CF audit):**

5. **The Ninth Cohort / Imperial Provincial Office** — Legion, customs, Blackrose. Under-manned (CF-C001).
6. **The Dres salt-factors** — slavers (CF-062, hard canon).
7. **The Blackwood Company, early** (CF-066, constructed) — a young Leyawiin outfit in the marsh, hunting.
8. **Shadowscales / ku-vastei** (CF-028..CF-030, hard canon) — not a joinable guild but a *service*: an
   arbiter who arrives when a thing has gone too far. Perfect Morrowind quest-giver, zero invention cost.
9. **The Twin Lamps** — canon Morrowind abolitionist underground; here only as a contact network with a
   single cell, run by original characters. Contact, not questline.

### 5. Main-quest substrate (handed to `quests.main`, not resolved here)

The political brief above is stable. The thing that makes it *move* is the metaphysical crisis defined
in RI-LOR05: **the Rootward Tide is failing.** Deaths are not arriving at the roots. Sapwells that
should re-issue are giving back nothing; hatchings are coming out wrong; the Drowned Court's ledgers
show it as a curve, not a rumour.

Every faction reads this through its own interest, and none of them is simply right:

- **Deep-Kin:** foreigners are drinking the marsh. Cut the coast off.
- **Wet Ledger:** superstition, and bad for freight. Fund a study, quietly, in case.
- **Sap-Cutters:** we have been cutting for thirty years and nothing happened. Prove it was us.
- **Drowned Court:** it started before the cutting. Something *below* is taking.
- **Empire:** an Argonian religious panic, to be managed.
- **Dres:** irrelevant, unless it lowers the price of stock.
- **Blackwood Company:** if the trees are dying, the sap is worth more. Buy now.
- **The Hist:** does not say. Withholds. Then, once, tells the player something plainly — which should
  be the most frightening moment in the game, because it means the tree is out of other options.

The quest team owns what is actually taking. RI-LOR05 §5 lists the three candidate causes and rules
that exactly one is authorially true and the other two must remain live in-world theories.

## Comparison method

Executable by a fresh agent with only this file and the game data directory.

1. **Anachronism sweep.** Grep all shipped text (`game/data/**/*.json`, `corpus/60-lore/*.md`) for terms
   that do not exist in 3E 427:
   ```
   grep -riE "an-xileel|umbriel|oblivion crisis|mehrunes dagon|nerevarine|red year|hero of kvatch|thalmor|dragonborn|great war|septim.*dead" game/data/
   ```
   Any hit outside a deliberately-prophetic omen registered in `canon-facts.json` → **FAIL.**
2. **Slavery liveness.** Count NPCs and books that treat Dunmer slaving as (a) a current practice vs
   (b) history. `history / (history + current) > 0.15` → **FAIL** (the era's central grievance has been
   defused into backstory).
3. **Settlement authority coverage.** For each of the eight settlements, at least three NPCs must be
   able to answer "who runs this place?" with an answer consistent with §3, and at least one must
   answer *cynically*. Any settlement with zero → **FAIL** (that settlement has no politics).
4. **Rumour divergence.** Sample the ambient rumour pool per settlement. Two settlements sharing >40% of
   their rumour lines → **FAIL** (Morrowind's rumours differ by town; see ARBITRATION §1, Dialogue).
5. **Faction originality ratio.** From `game/data/factions/`: `borrowed / total`. §4 gives 4 original of
   9 total = 0.56 borrowed by headcount, but weight by *content volume* (quest count × dialogue lines):
   borrowed-weighted share must be **< 0.25**. The four originals must carry the questlines.
6. **Substrate reachability.** Pick 10 random side quests. For each, ask: does its premise depend on any
   row of §2 or §3? Fewer than 6 of 10 → **FAIL for free-floating quests** (the substrate is decorative).
7. **Date discipline.** Any in-game date must be either Imperial-calendar 3E 426–427 or a sap-fall count.
   A mixed or wrong date on a journal entry → minor fail, logged.

## Scoring

| Score | Condition |
|---|---|
| **5** | Zero anachronisms; all 8 settlements politically legible from three NPCs each; borrowed-weight <0.15; ≥8/10 side quests hang off the substrate; slavery current in ≥90% of mentions |
| **4** | Zero anachronisms; ≥7 settlements legible; borrowed-weight <0.25 |
| **3** | ≤1 anachronism in flavour text; ≥6 settlements legible; ≥6/10 quests on-substrate |
| **2** | Politics present but generic — "the Empire is bad, the natives are angry" — with no settlement-specific power holders |
| **1** | Era unstated in play; player cannot determine what year it is or who rules anywhere |
| **0 — FAIL** | Any anachronism that breaks the timeline (An-Xileel present, Oblivion Crisis referenced as happening), or slavery treated as abolished/historical |

**Failure threshold: below 3 blocks the wave.** A 2 is the likeliest real score and the most dangerous,
because a generic-politics build *looks* finished.

## How we lose

1. **A timeless province.** No date, no current events, no sense that anything happened last month. The
   player could be in 2E 582 or 4E 201 and would never know. This is what happens when nobody reads this file.
2. **The Empire as cartoon villain.** Sneering legionaries who kick Argonians in the street. The real
   version is worse and more Morrowind: a tired Prefect running a labour-lease he inherited, who is
   polite to you, who has a daughter in Cyrodiil, and who will not stop.
3. **Slavery as wallpaper.** Chains as scenery, a slave NPC who says one sad line and stands there
   forever. Morrowind's slaves had names, prices, owners, and an escape network with a password.
4. **Unanimous Argonians.** All natives agreeing that outsiders are bad. The actual fracture — coast vs
   interior, lukiul vs Deep-Kin, the ones who trade with the takers vs the ones who cut throats over it —
   is the entire point, and it is the thing a lazy build flattens first.
5. **Eight identical towns.** Same rumours, same authority, same guards, different skybox. §3 exists to
   make this measurable.
6. **Naming the An-Xileel.** The single most likely canon break, because a builder will look up "Argonian
   faction," find the wiki page, and use it. It is six years early. It is a **0**.
7. **The substrate ignored.** A quest team that never reads this file and writes twelve fetch-quests for
   strangers. Check 6 exists to catch exactly this.
8. **Politics with no cost.** Factions the player can all join. Morrowind's Great Houses lock each other
   out; ours must too (Deep-Kin and Wet Ledger are mutually exclusive; Sap-Cutters expel you from both).

## Provenance note

- **Era-anchoring facts** (Dres slaving legal at 3E 427, Vvardenfell crisis contemporaneous, Blackwood
  Company undated founding, An-Xileel post-dating our window, Knahaten Flu origin at Stormhold) are
  drawn from RI-LOR01's registry with the confidence levels recorded there — `community-data` from
  search snippets, or `canonical-recall`. Direct source fetches were blocked (see RI-LOR01 provenance).
- **Everything in §2 (except the Empire's and Dres's stated aims), all of §3, all of §4's originals, the
  sap-fall calendar, and §5 are `constructed`.** No NPC, book, or narrator may present them as canon,
  and no future agent should treat them as Bethesda's. They are recorded in `data/canon-facts.json`
  under `tier: "constructed"` with IDs CF-C001..CF-C012.
- The choice of 3E 427 specifically is an editorial judgement, argued in §1 and open to challenge on the
  merits — but once content ships against it, it is frozen. Changing era after wave 1 is a re-write of
  every faction, not a config change.
