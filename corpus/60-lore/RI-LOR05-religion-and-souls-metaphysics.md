## The bar

This game has a Souls skeleton: you kill things, you collect souls, you kneel at a checkpoint, you spend
souls to become stronger, you lose them when you die, you run back for them. In Dark Souls this is
explained by the setting — the Age of Fire, the Dark Sign, the curse of undeath — and the explanation is
so tightly wound into the fiction that the mechanics *are* the story. Morrowind's side of our arbitration
demands a world that feels inhabited, contradictory and specific. If our souls-and-bonfires layer is
merely *mechanical* — an ungrounded HUD number and a glowing campfire — we will have shipped a Souls
game wearing a Morrowind coat, and the seam will show at every checkpoint, several hundred times a run.

The bar: **every Souls-derived system in this game must have an in-world cause that a Black Marsh
villager could explain to you in their own idiom, that follows from established Hist canon rather than
sitting beside it, and that a quest can put pressure on.** The last clause is the real test. If our
explanation is decorative — true but inert — it has failed. The checkpoint system should be something
factions *argue about*.

## The reference artifact

### 1. What canon actually gives us

Three hard facts, and they are extraordinary:

- **CF-002** — an Argonian hatchling receives its soul by *drinking sap*. Not by divine grant. By ingestion.
- **CF-003** — when an Argonian dies its soul **returns to the Hist**, is held, and may be **issued again**
  to a later hatchling.
- **CF-004** — the Hist **shapes Argonian bodies**, deciding what hatches, and reshapes them to meet need.

Read together with the flat statement that **a Hist tree's sap is its soul**, this means canon has already
established that the Hist is a **soul reservoir with write access to bodies**. Bethesda built a
souls-and-respawns metaphysics into Black Marsh in 1994 and never made a game out of it. We do not need
to invent a justification for a Souls layer here. We need to *notice the one that is already there*.

Two more, load-bearing:

- **CF-006** — Hist sap in a **non-Argonian** is a hallucinogen that drives bloodlust, and the drinker
  *does not perceive what they are killing*. (Canon: the Water's Edge massacre.)
- **CF-010** — the Hist say the Void is "where all roots lead," that they rejoin it, and that they may
  invoke the "Songs of Sithis."

### 2. The Rootward Tide — our metaphysical model (`constructed`)

> Everything that dies in Black Marsh goes **down**. This is not a belief. In the marsh's own idiom it is
> **plumbing**.

A death releases soul-stuff. In most of Tamriel it disperses — to Aetherius, to the Void, to whatever
claims it. In Black Marsh the water table is threaded with Hist root, and the roots **drink**. What they
drink they hold as **pattern** (`eixa`) — the shape a thing was held in — and what they hold they can
issue again. Sap is the medium: sap is soul in liquid form, which is why Jel has one word, `vei`, for
blood and sap both.

Three consequences, each doing work:

1. **The tree keeps the shape, not the story.** Pattern survives; memory does not. This is our resolution
   of CF-D001, and it is deliberately positioned so that *both* in-world authorities are partly wrong
   (RI-LOR03 Exemplars 1 and 2; RI-LOR06).
2. **Wounds are the exception.** A root cut open and kept open — an **`ixtu-xul`**, a *sapwell* — is a
   place where the tide is exposed to the air. Wells do not merely hold pattern; **wells remember what the
   tree forgets**, because a wound is a place where something happened. This is the single constructed
   claim that makes the whole checkpoint system work, and the corpus states plainly that it is ours.
3. **Below the roots there is no root.** Soul-stuff that misses the roots keeps falling, into Sithis, into
   the Void. To an Argonian this is not damnation — it is simply the part of the system that is not the
   system. What horrifies them is not the Void. It is **spillage**: a death that happens too far from any
   root to be drunk. Every Argonian taken north in a Dres hold and worked to death in Tear is a spill.
   **This is why the slave trade is a metaphysical atrocity here and not only a moral one**, and it is the
   single best piece of fiction-to-politics wiring the corpus has.

### 3. Sithis, the Void, and the gods that came on ships

| Object | Status in the marsh | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Sithis** | Not worshipped as a god; acknowledged as a condition. "All roots go down. Below the roots there is no root." Argonians do not regard Sithis as evil (hard canon). | Xanmeers are identified as shrines built "in both reverence to and fear of Sithis" (CF-042). The reverence and the fear are the same gesture. |
| **The Void** | Where the unheld go. Not hell, not reward. | CF-010: the Hist claim to rejoin it. |
| **The Shadowscales** | Argonians given to the Dark Brotherhood at birth under the sign of the Shadow (CF-028). | Not a cult in the marsh — an **institution**. A Shadowscale returning as *ku-vastei*, "needed change," is a legal officer with a knife (CF-029). Ordinary Argonians find them normal and unlucky, the way a village finds a coroner normal and unlucky. |
| **The Nine Divines** | A colonial import with buildings. Chapels at Gideon, Stormhold, and the Blackrose garrison. | The Imperial Cult's structural problem in this province: **it has nothing to offer an Argonian.** It cannot promise a soul a home, because the soul already has one, in a tree, eight miles away, that the convert can visit. Its actual congregation is soldiers, factors, and *lukiul* who converted for work. See RI-LOR03 Exemplar 1's chaplain. |
| **Daedra** | Present, unevangelised, transactional. | The marsh does not have Daedric *cults* so much as Daedric *arrangements*. Domains are hard canon; the local attributions below are `constructed`. |

**Daedric presence, constructed for our marsh:**

- **Namira** — rot, decay, the things under the water. The most at-home Prince in Black Marsh, and the
  least dramatic: her shrines are ordinary, tended, and unremarkable, which is far more unsettling than
  a skull altar.
- **Vaermina** — dreams. Contested territory, because the Hist also sends dreams (`omu`), and telling
  the two apart is a genuine problem for a rootkeeper. Excellent quest material.
- **Hircine** — the hunt, in the Stone Forest and the Valus foothills.
- **Molag Bal** — **soul gems**, and therefore the patron of `xul-hesh`, root-theft. The soul-gem trade
  in this province has a Daedric shape whether or not the traders know it. This is our wiring from
  Imperial/Telvanni commerce to cosmic offence.
- **Hermaeus Mora** — the xanmeers, the buried counting, the knowledge lost at Duskfall.
- **Mehrunes Dagon** — absent. Six years early. Say nothing (RI-LOR02).

**`Xul-hesh` — root-theft.** A soul gem takes a soul that would have gone down and puts it in a rock.
To an Argonian this is not "dark magic"; it is interception of the water supply. An enchanter working
openly in Lilmoth is doing something that reads, locally, as somewhere between poisoning a well and
kidnapping. Foreign characters will not understand why the reaction is so extreme. **They should not be
told; they should be shown, repeatedly, and left to work it out.**

#### 4a. Sap-taint: the cost of the checkpoint

Canon states that Hist sap in a non-Argonian causes hallucination and bloodlust, and that the drinker
**does not perceive what they are killing** (CF-006, the Water's Edge massacre). Our checkpoint system
requires a non-Argonian player to ingest sap dozens of times.

We do not soften this. We charge for it.

| Taint band | What the player experiences |
|---|---|
| 0 — clean | Nothing. Argonian PCs stay here permanently. |
| 1 — *tasted* | The well's sap smells like something from your childhood. NPCs notice your eyes. |
| 2 — *carrying* | Ambient audio gains a second layer nobody else hears. Rootkeepers will now speak to you, which is worse. |
| 3 — *sap-drunk* | **Enemy models begin to lie.** A `xul-ei` may render as a Legion soldier, a mudcrab as a child. Behaviour, hitboxes, frame data and telegraphs are **unchanged** — only the skin lies. Some corpses are what you saw; some are not. |
| 4 — *rooted* | The Hist addresses you directly and often. Certain dialogue and one ending path open. Certain NPCs will not be in the same room as you. |

**Arbitration compliance:** this is legal because it never touches the fight's fairness. Souls owns
frames, hitboxes, telegraphs and damage geometry (ARBITRATION §1) and **none of them change** — the fight
is exactly as readable and exactly as deterministic. What changes is what the model is wearing, which is
world-layer, which is Morrowind's. An enemy that *behaved* differently under taint would be an AR-1 fail.

**Argonian PCs pay a different price, not a smaller one.** The well knows them; sap is food; there is no
taint. Instead the Hist **talks to them**, from the first rest, and begins asking for things. The
Argonian cost is obligation. The foreign cost is corruption. Neither is free, and the two playthroughs
argue with each other.

#### 4b. Cutting a new well is a transgression

Sapwells are not lit; they are **cut**, with a `skei` — and cutting one is either (a) done by a
rootkeeper, with the tree's consent, over days, or (b) done by you, with a stolen knife, in minutes.
A foreign player character opening a new checkpoint in the deep marsh is committing the exact act the
**Ixtu-Vakh (Sap-Cutters)** are hated for (RI-LOR02 §4). Every new checkpoint should be a small crime,
and the interior should know who cut it. **This is the clause that makes the checkpoint system
politically live rather than decorative**, and it is the clause a builder is most likely to drop for
convenience.

### 5. What travel is, since bonfires are not doors (S7)

The roots carry knowledge and the Hist knows where the channels have moved. Travel is therefore
**poled barge along Hist-advised routes** — a `vokh`-poler who asks the tree in the morning which way
the water went in the night, and is right. Routes run coast-to-coast and up the great rivers; **nothing
goes to Helstrom that the interior has not agreed to.** This is our silt-strider: in-fiction, purchasable
with gold, schedule-bound, refusable, and severable as a plot event. It is not fast travel; it is
transport.

### 6. The main-quest substrate: the Tide is failing

Deaths are not arriving. Wells that should re-issue give nothing back. Hatchings come out wrong. The
Drowned Court of Soulrest has it as a curve in a ledger, not a rumour (RI-LOR02 §5).

Three causes are proposed in-world. **All three are supported by real evidence. Exactly one is
authorially true; the other two are contributors that would not, alone, have done this** — which is the
Morrowind structure exactly, where several accounts of the same crisis are each partly right.

| # | Cause | Who believes it | Evidence that is genuinely good |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Root-theft.** A soul-gem operation on the coast, filling gems not by trapping individuals but from the ambient fall — interception of the tide itself. Molag Bal-shaped whether the operators know it or not. | Deep-Kin; most Argonians | The gems exist. The shipping manifests are real. The trade grew fivefold this decade. |
| **B** | **Too many wounds.** Thirty years of Sap-Cutters opening wells for sale; the tree bleeding capacity. | Wet Ledger; rootkeepers of the middle rivers; Imperial naturalists | The cutting is real, undeniable, and the cutters have no defence except "it did not start with us." |
| **C** | **Something below is filling.** Under the Stone Wastes near **Soulrest**, a pre-Duskfall xanmeer engine built to *hold* souls — "in reverence to and fear of Sithis" (CF-042) — is either running again or never stopped. | The Drowned Court, alone, and they are not sure | It started before the cutting. The curve is older than the gem trade. And the wells work **because of** what is below: the ancients built the reservoir the Hist has been using ever since. |

**Authorial ruling: C is true.** A and B are real aggravations and both accusations are partly just, so
the player who acts on either does genuine good and does not fix anything. **Registered in
`canon-facts.json` as CF-C010 with `authorially_true: true` and `player_discoverable: late`.**

The consequence is the fusion the whole corpus is aimed at: **the sapwells work because of the thing
that is killing the marsh.** Resolving the main quest means deciding what happens to the reservoir — and
therefore to the checkpoint system. An endgame in which the player can end the crisis and **lose
respawning** is a Souls mechanic that a Morrowind quest decision reaches into and switches off. Nothing
else in this design would justify the two halves being in the same game.

*(The quest team owns the plot. This file owns the metaphysics and the ruling on C. Refinements must be
registered, not improvised.)*

### 7. Rules this fiction imposes on the mechanics — binding on designers

1. Souls **never** buy an item, a bribe, a repair, a fare, or a training session (S15).
2. Bonfires **never** teleport (S7). No exceptions, no unlockable exception, no late-game exception.
3. Named NPCs, merchants and quest actors **never** re-issue (S5). If one dies, they are gone, and the
   world adapts (S10).
4. Sap-taint **never** alters frames, hitboxes, telegraphs, damage or AI. Skins and audio only (AR-1).
5. The Hist is **never** an enemy encounter. Killing a Hist tree is not available to the player at any
   point, under any faction, in any ending. A tree may die; the player may fail to prevent it.
6. The Hist speaks **rarely**, and never to explain the plot. When it speaks plainly it is because it
   has run out of other options, and that should happen at most twice in the game.
7. Levelling at a well is **broad** — attributes and skills, Morrowind's stat sheet — because the tree
   is re-growing a body, not awarding experience (S2).
8. No UI string anywhere says "bonfire," "Estus," "souls" as a currency label, or "checkpoint." The
   vocabulary is `sapwell`, `tithe-gourd`, `sap-debt`, and it is fixed in
   `jel-lexicon.json:coined_terms` (RI-LOR04).

## Comparison method

1. **Terminology lock.**
   ```
   grep -riE "bonfire|estus|checkpoint|respawn point|xp|experience point" game/src game/data
   ```
   Any hit in a **player-visible** string → **FAIL.** (Internal identifiers are exempt; a critic must
   check `game/data/**/*.json` strings and UI templates, not variable names.)
2. **Diegetic coverage audit.** For each of the 12 rows in §4, find at least one **in-world source** in
   shipped content that explains it — an NPC dialogue topic, a book, or a rootkeeper's line. A mechanic
   with zero in-world explanation → one fail each. **≥3 uncovered rows → FAIL the item.**
3. **Ask-a-villager test.** Pick 5 random non-quest Argonian NPCs. Each must have a dialogue topic
   ("sapwell", "the tide", "sap-debt") answering in *their own idiom*, not in exposition. A critic reads
   all 5 and must be able to reconstruct §2 from them. **Fail if fewer than 3 have such a topic**, or if
   any two give identical text.
4. **Souls-purchase probe.** Attempt to spend souls anywhere except a sapwell level-up:
   ```
   grep -rn "soul" game/src/**/{shop,merchant,barter,train,travel,repair}* 
   ```
   Any code path where `souls` decrements outside `levelUp` → **automatic S15 fail.**
5. **Warp probe.** Any code path that changes player world position from a sapwell UI → **automatic S7 fail.**
6. **Taint fairness harness.** Record two combat traces against the same enemy at taint 0 and taint 3.
   Diff startup/active/recovery frames, hitbox volumes, aggro radius, damage. **Any non-zero diff →
   AR-1 fail.** Only the material/mesh/audio manifests may differ.
7. **Hist-restraint count.** Count lines in which the Hist speaks plainly to the player. **>2 in the
   whole game → FAIL** (rule 6). Count Hist-as-boss encounters. **>0 → FAIL** (rule 5).
8. **Politicised-checkpoint check.** Count sapwells the player cuts themselves. For each, at least one
   NPC or faction must react to it having been cut. **<50% reacted-to → FAIL** — the checkpoint system
   has gone decorative, which is this file's central failure mode.
9. **Cross-check against RI-LOR06.** Every metaphysical claim in shipped dialogue must map to a
   `canon-facts.json` entry, and any claim contradicting CF-002/003/004/006 → **hard-canon fail**.

## Scoring

| Score | Condition |
|---|---|
| **5** | All 12 §4 rows explained in-world; ≥4/5 villagers pass the ask-a-villager test in distinct idiom; taint diff exactly zero; ≥80% of player-cut wells provoke a reaction; Hist speaks plainly ≤2 times |
| **4** | ≤1 uncovered row; 3/5 villagers; taint diff zero; ≥50% wells reacted-to |
| **3** | ≤3 uncovered rows; terminology clean; no S7/S15/AR-1 violations |
| **2** | Fiction exists in the corpus but not in the game — the player could finish without ever learning why they respawn |
| **1** | Souls layer wholly ungrounded; sapwells are campfires with a different mesh |
| **0 — FAIL** | Any S15 (souls buy something), S7 (bonfire warp), or AR-1 (taint alters combat) violation; or the Hist appears as a boss; or a shipped string says "bonfire" |

**Failure threshold: below 3.** A 2 is the expected default and is the outcome of building the mechanics
first and reading this file afterwards.

## How we lose

1. **The explanation stays in the corpus.** We write this beautiful account of the Rootward Tide, and the
   game ships with a glowing tree stump and no NPC who mentions it. **The most likely failure by a wide
   margin.** Checks 2 and 3 exist solely for it.
2. **Souls become money.** A designer needs a gold sink, souls are right there, and S15 dies of
   convenience. This also destroys the fiction: sap-debt is the smell of what you killed, and nobody
   sells bread for it.
3. **Bonfire warp "for quality of life."** Playtesters complain about backtracking; someone adds
   well-to-well travel; the poled-barge network becomes vestigial; the marsh stops being impassable
   (CF-089) and the province stops being Black Marsh.
4. **Sap-taint softened into a debuff.** A -5% stamina malus with a green icon. The canon fact is that
   the drinker *cannot tell what they are killing*, and the only honest implementation is the one where
   the player kills something and finds out afterwards.
5. **Sap-taint made unfair.** The opposite failure: taint alters enemy timings, and the fight stops being
   readable. AR-1, automatic fail. Check 6 is the guard, and the line is exact — **skins may lie,
   frames may not.**
6. **The Hist explains the plot.** A talking tree that dispenses objectives. Rule 6 exists because this is
   the single most tempting move available, and because a Hist that talks freely is not the Hist.
7. **The Hist as final boss.** The one canon violation everyone would forgive us for and no one should.
   The Hist is not a villain and cannot be defeated by killing a tree (RI-LOR01, shut doors).
8. **Checkpoints with no politics.** The player cuts eleven wells across the marsh and nobody ever
   mentions it. Check 8. This is the difference between a diegetic system and a re-skinned one.
9. **Metaphysics that contradicts the fiction we already shipped.** Exemplar 1 says the sap dissolves;
   Exemplar 2 says the egg speaks twice; if a third source "settles" it, we have destroyed the
   contradiction discipline of RI-LOR06 and flattened the world into a wiki.
