## The bar

Morrowind's Dunmer are not one people. A player who has spent four hours in Balmora can tell a
Redoran from a Telvanni without being told which is which, because the two want incompatible
things, keep different company, eat at different tables, and hold each other in a specific and
articulate contempt. Then the game puts an Ashlander in front of you and the Great Houses stop
looking like the range of Dunmer opinion and start looking like one end of it. The Ashlanders are
not a fifth House. They are the people the Houses are a departure *from*, and the moment you meet
Sul-Matuul the map you had been building of Vvardenfell turns out to have had its edges cropped.

This province has cities, institutions, a Legion cohort, a merchant compact, a mortuary order and
a heresy. It has no peoples. Every Argonian in it is an Argonian, and the four constructed bodies
that organise the world — Deep-Kin, Wet Ledger, Sap-Cutters, Drowned Court — are things a person
*joins*, not things a person is born into and cannot leave. Registered by the lore builder as
**CF-C023**, counted over all of `game/data` on 2026-08-07: Agacephs, Paatru, Archein, Sarpa,
Dead-Water and Miredancers each score **zero**.

The bar is this: **a player should be able to tell, from what somebody eats, what they will not do,
and how they talk about a tree, which people they came out of — and should find that those peoples
disagree with each other about matters of fact, in front of the player, with nothing correcting
them.** A province whose natives are undifferentiated has a *race* where it needs a *society*, and
a race is the thing the Provincial Office already thinks it is dealing with.

## The reference artifact

Six peoples, their five differentiating axes, the structural claim that reorganises the Deep-Kin
around them, six registered disputes and five deliberately unregistered arguments. §1 is the
decision and the rejected names; §2 is the peoples; §3 is what the Xul-Aneekh turns out to be; §4
sorts the disagreements under W1-23's ruling; §5 is what happens to the province's euphemism.

#### Why not invent

The brief's instruction was *do not invent a people the world has never implied*, and the honest
finding of the research is that **there was nothing left to invent.** Canon names far more Argonian
peoples than it describes — CF-027 records exactly this asymmetry — and the count is deliberately
open: a Miredancer elder asked how many tribes live in the swamps pointed at the torchbugs and said
*"as many as the lights"* (*Tribes of Murkmire: Bright-Throats*). Adding a seventh name of ours to a
list the source material has already left unbounded buys nothing and costs provenance legibility,
which is the axis RI-LOR01 scores by minimum.

What canon does **not** supply, and what is therefore genuinely ours, is everything below §2's first
line in each entry: the diet, the taboo, the position on the Empire, the position on Argonians who
took Imperial names, and the thing each people will not do. CF-034 says in as many words that canon
gives no tribal politics for 3E 427. That is the invention space, and it is where all the work is.

#### Why six, and not twelve

`W1-23`'s registry report ruled against scattering tribe names as texture: *"four bodies a player
can tell apart beat nine tribe names they cannot… If tribes are wanted, they need a faction each,
not a mention each."* Six is the largest number that survives that test with a mouth each, a region
each, a diet each, and a registered position each. The seventh would be a mention.

The uncountability is preserved a different way, and it is the way canon does it: the *Bright-Throats*
book does not enumerate the tribes, it says the count is beyond enumeration and then describes one.
So our books say the same. An Agaceph guide who is asked how many peoples there are gives a number
that does not agree with the Miredancer's number, and neither is corrected.

#### The canon names deliberately not used, and why

Recording these so the next agent does not read the omission as an oversight.

| Name | Status | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| **Naga** | In the build already, 388 hits, as a **playable race**. | A body-form, not a people. The Dead-Water are a tribe *of* Naga, which is what canon says (*Naga-Kur*), and that relation is now the build's answer to why both words exist. |
| **Kothringi**, **Lilmothiit** | In the build, 22 and 4 hits. Extinct at 2E 601 (CF-046). | History, not society. CF-049's one impossible survivor stays a person, not a people. |
| **Black-Tongues** (*Kota-Vimleel*) | Canon, Murkmire. | The Shadowscale Order is already a shipped institution here. Giving it a tribal breeding programme would turn the province's one genuine terror into a supply chain, which is a loss disguised as a connection. |
| **Bright-Throats** (*Wasseek-haleel*) | Canon, Murkmire. | Cheerful coastal artisan-traders. The Wet Ledger already occupies the "Argonians who deal with outsiders and are resented for it" position, and doing it twice weakens both. |
| **Ghost People** (*Veeskhleel*), **Root-House People** (*Tum-Taleel*) | Canon, Murkmire. | Both are *known chiefly by rumour* in their own source; the Drowned Court and the river-bands already carry those two functions. Left on the table on purpose — they are the obvious wave-2 additions. |
| **Shoss-kaleel**, **Horwalli**, **Orma**, **Yespest** | Canon, one mention each. | One mention in the source is not enough to differentiate on, and differentiating on nothing is how a tribe becomes a hat. |

#### The era note, taken seriously

Four of the six are named in ESO material set in 2E 582 — roughly seventeen centuries before our
3E 427. Tribes are not institutions and do not need continuity of leadership to persist; what needs
defending is any *specific* claim carried forward. So: the Miredancer office of **Sap-Speaker** and
its sap-poisoning symptoms (gold tongue, bark-scale) are carried forward, because the source
presents them as an ancient office and a medical fact about sap. The specific Sap-Speaker Thumarz is
not. The Dead-Water practice of wearing a fallen comrade's bone is carried forward as a practice.
Their specific 2E chieftains are not. Nothing in this file names a person canon has placed.

### 2. The six, differentiated

Each entry runs the same five columns, and the five columns are the Ashlander test: **what they eat,
what they do about the tree, what they do about the Empire, what they think of Argonians with
Imperial names, and what they will not do.** If two peoples answer any three of those the same way,
one of them is decoration.

#### 2.1 The Agacephs — the Deep Marshes

**Canon** (*The Argonian Account, Book 4*): an interior people of many bright colours and long
needle-like faces, living near the Hist, "finding little in the outside world worth seeing." They
find a lost Imperial clerk, feed him, and pole him out on a raft without ever once asking what he
came for.

- **Eat:** nothing that had to be run down. Rootmat, mudfish taken from a fixed weir, and the fruit
  of the marsh crown, which is bitter and which they eat anyway. A people who do not chase.
- **The tree:** they live under it and do not tap it. An Agaceph settlement has no sapwell. Sap is
  taken by a hatchling at hatching and by a dying person at dying, and at no other hour of a life.
- **The Empire:** no dealings and no hostility. They have never signed anything. They will not
  refuse a lost man, which is not the same as welcoming him, and the distinction matters to them.
- **Argonians with Imperial names:** pity. Not contempt — the Agaceph position is that a man who
  answers to a written name has been given a second thing to carry and gets nothing for it.
- **Will not:** give directions. If you must go somewhere, an Agaceph takes you, and will not tell
  you the way even after. The Wet Ledger has been trying to buy a chart off them for thirty years.
- **Faces:** the four bodies. The Agacephs are the largest single voice inside the Xul-Aneekh
  consensus and the most reluctant member of it.

#### 2.2 The Paatru — the Clay Moor

**Canon** (*The Argonian Account, Book 4*): "the toad-like Paatru," interior, named among the
peoples who "would have killed him on the spot."

Canon gives us the hostility and no reason for it. Ours is that **it is a law with a season**, not a
temper. In the wet, the clay holds the year's eggs and any stranger in it is there to take them,
because for as long as anyone has counted, any stranger in it has been. In the dry, the moor is
walkable and the Paatru trade at the edge of it, politely, and will not discuss the other half of
the year.

- **Eat:** eggs that did not take, buried in clay and left a season. Nothing green. The smell of a
  Paatru camp is the thing coastal Argonians know them by and will not name in front of one.
- **The tree:** their Hist is in a pit and they go down to it, one at a time, on a rope. Every other
  people in this file finds that either obscene or enviable and none of them is neutral about it.
- **The Empire:** has never met one. Two are on the Blackrose roll and both were taken in the dry
  season, at the trade-edge, which the Paatru regard as the single most important fact about the
  Empire and which the Empire has not recorded.
- **Argonians with Imperial names:** not enemies. Dead. A Paatru will not fight a lukiul for the
  same reason he will not fight a stone, and lukiul who have understood this find it worse than
  hatred.
- **Will not:** cross running water on a bridge another people built. They ford, in season, and lose
  people doing it.
- **Faces:** nothing. The Paatru are in no body and no consensus, and the Xul-Aneekh count them
  anyway, which the Paatru have not been asked about.

#### 2.3 The Sarpa — the Stone Forest

**Canon** (*The Argonian Account, Book 4*): "the winged Sarpa," interior, hostile to strangers.

- **Eat:** carrion, without embarrassment, and eggs taken from nests that are not theirs. They think
  the squeamishness of the ground-peoples about this is the funniest thing in the province.
- **The tree:** their Hist is a stump. They say a root that goes down is a root that grew wrong, and
  that the true tree is overhead, and they take sap from the stump anyway and do not think that is
  a contradiction. Every other people in this file thinks it is.
- **The Empire:** they weigh Imperial coin, find it interesting, and drop it. There is no Sarpa word
  for a boundary drawn on the ground, and a treaty is a boundary drawn on the ground.
- **Argonians with Imperial names:** they cannot see the difference from where they are, and say so,
  and mean it as a statement about altitude rather than as an insult, and it lands as one anyway.
- **Will not:** come down into a settlement they have not been asked into. They will circle it for a
  day first. Thorn has learned to put a mark on the roof.
- **What only they have:** they have seen the xanmeers from above, all of them, in one sweep. Every
  claim in this province about the shape of the deep past that is not a guess is a Sarpa claim, and
  the Sarpa are hostile, illiterate by choice, and asked about it roughly once a generation.

#### 2.4 The Archein — Blackwood, and the estates east of Gideon

**Canon**, and the most useful thing found in the research. The Pocket Guide 3rd edition: Imperials
rule the coastal cities "but most have native Argonians as advisors. These Archeins also act as
governors in the rural areas that still make up the majority of Black Marsh." *The Argonian Account,
Book 4*, an Agaceph on the subject: "pompous, assimilated, slaver kleptocrats." ESO's *From Argonian
to Saxhleel*: "Archein tribe scum in the employ of House Dres." And in Book 3, Archein Right-Foot-Rock,
riding past his own field workers: *"One of my she-workers gave birth to twins. Twins! Fortunately,
there's a good trader in town for those."*

**This is the answer to the second finding in the brief.** The build says *lease* and *Blackrose*
2,240 times against *slave* and *slavery* 42, and W1-23 was right that the correction is not to
sprinkle the harder word about. It is that the euphemism must belong to **somebody**. It belonged to
the Provincial Office, which is a foreign institution, and a euphemism only a foreigner uses is one
the world can hold at arm's length. Give it to the Archein and it stops being the Empire's word: it
becomes the word an Argonian landholder uses about Argonians, in Jel, with an Argonian Hist walled
in his own yard, and there is no arm's length left anywhere.

- **Eat:** Imperial food, badly, from a written menu, in courses. Saltrice off their own fields, which
  their own field hands are not given.
- **The tree:** an estate Hist behind a wall, and the hatchlings are brought sap **in a vessel**
  rather than brought to the tree. Whether that is a hatching at all is CF-D024 and is the most
  violent disagreement in this file.
- **The Empire:** they *are* the Empire's rural governance and think of that as a load-bearing wall.
  An Archein will tell you the interior is only unadministered because nobody competent has offered.
- **Argonians with Imperial names:** they took theirs first, on purpose, and got titles for it. They
  invented the category and resent being counted inside it, and the word *lukiul* is one they use
  downward and will not accept upward.
- **Will not:** sell at the quay. An Archein deals through a factor, never with a Dres captain
  directly, and holds that this distinction is the whole of his honour. It is the most Morrowind
  sentence in the province and it is the one that should make a player put the book down.

#### 2.5 The Miredancers — *Gee-Rusleel*, the Eastern Rootlands

**Canon** (*Tribes of Murkmire: Miredancers*): the most introspective and most pleasant Argonians the
surveyor met; artisans in Hist amber and eggshell; peerless navigators and cartographers; obsessive
players of games with no interest in winning; defined above all by piety, which has earned them the
right to name a **Sap-Speaker**, who goes into the roots or the canopy for days, is fed only by the
tree, and comes back with something to interpret. The price is sap-poisoning: gold tongue,
bark-scale, hallucination.

Everything in that paragraph is carried forward. What is ours is what the office is like **now**.

- **Eat:** whatever is put in front of them and a great deal of it, at length, with a game running.
  They will feed anyone. A Miredancer who will not share a meal with you has already decided
  something about you and will not say what.
- **The tree:** the closest relationship any people here has, and the only one that is *institutional*.
  The Sap-Speaker is the province's one office that exists to be spoken to by a Hist.
- **The Empire:** cordial, uninterested, and unhelpful in a way that takes outsiders a season to
  notice. They have signed things. They do not regard signing as an event.
- **Argonians with Imperial names:** the softest position of the six, and it is theological rather
  than political — a name is a thing you are given and are between, and so is a tribe. This is the
  *Tribal Connections* doctrine and it is why the Miredancers are the only people here who will bury
  an Archein.
- **Will not:** stop the Sap-Speaker drinking. Even now.
- **And now:** the current Sap-Speaker goes down and comes back with **nothing**, and has done four
  times, and has the gold tongue and the bark-scale to show for it, and is going down again. The
  Rootward Tide failing is an argument between institutions everywhere else in this build. Here it is
  a person with a stained mouth walking towards a hole.

#### 2.6 The Dead-Water — *Naga-Kur*, the Thornmarsh and the Hive

**Canon** (*Tribes of Murkmire: The Dead-Water Tribe*): a Naga tribe controlling vast tracts of
country, greatly feared by the villages around them, who "fight all the days of their lives" and
adorn weapons and armour with the faces, claws and leg bones of fallen comrades — because, as the
guide Reelus explains it, "this lets them fight after death too."

- **Eat:** what they take, and it is taken from other Argonians as often as not. They do not think
  of this as theft and neither do the villages, which is the hardest thing in this file for an
  outsider to hold in his head at once.
- **The tree:** they do not keep a Hist. They *visit*. A Dead-Water war-band drinks at whichever
  tree it is nearest to when the time comes, which every other people here regards as somewhere
  between rude and blasphemous, and which the Dead-Water have never been told is unusual.
- **The Empire:** the tracks around Thorn close when the Dead-Water close them, which the Provincial
  Office reads as a seasonal hazard and books as such. RI-LOR02 already says *"Thorn is the
  province's early-warning system. When Thorn falls silent, the interior has decided something."*
  This file names who decides.
- **Argonians with Imperial names:** they take them the same as anyone, and are the only people here
  with no opinion at all about it, which coastal Argonians find more frightening than hatred.
- **Will not:** leave a body. Ever, under any conditions, at any cost, and they have paid the cost.
- **Faces:** the loudest voice in the Xul-Aneekh, and the reason the consensus frightens the coast.

### 3. The structural claim: the Xul-Aneekh is a consensus **of** peoples, not a people

RI-LOR02 already says the Deep-Kin are "rootkeepers and their war-broods; not a government, a
consensus." Until now there was nothing for it to be a consensus **of**, so it read as a faction
with unusual internal decoration.

With the six in place it is a real object with a real weakness:

- The **Dead-Water** want the coast emptied and have started.
- The **Agacephs** are the largest voice and the most reluctant, because emptying the coast means
  guiding somebody there, and guiding is the one thing they do and the one thing they will not do
  for this.
- The **Sarpa** attend, from a branch, and leave when it stops being interesting.
- The **Paatru** are counted in it and have never been asked.
- The **Miredancers** attend and will not vote, and are therefore trusted by everybody and
  represented by nobody.
- The **Archein** are the thing it is a consensus against, and half the Archein estates were Agaceph
  ground four generations ago, which the Agacephs mention and the Archein date differently.

The Ashlander lesson holds: this is not five more factions. It is the discovery that the four bodies
were the *coastal* map of the province and had their edges cropped.

**Joining rule, for the faction system:** you do not join a people. A player may be *taken in* by one
— which is a quest outcome, not a rank ladder — and being taken in by one closes two others by
arithmetic, because of what each will not do. The Archein will take a player of any race and charge
him for it. The Paatru will not take anyone, in any season, ever, and that is the fact the player
should learn by being told no.

### 4. What they disagree about — and the ruling that sorts it

Binding, from W1-23, and applied to every candidate below before it was written:

> **A contradiction is a disagreement about what IS THE CASE. A disagreement about what is WORTH
> DOING is an argument, and the province should be full of them.**

#### 4.1 Registered as disputes — six new entries, CF-D022 … CF-D027

Each one is a question with an answer, on which real shipped people give incompatible answers, none
corrected. Full schema in `corpus/60-lore/data/canon-facts.json`.

| ID | The question | Positions |
|---|---|---|
| **CF-D022** | Does the soul of an Argonian who dies far from his own Hist reach it? | **A** Archein: distance is nothing to a root, and a man who dies in Tear comes home. **B** Miredancer: the nearest listening root takes what it can and issues him out a stranger. **C** Agaceph: distance costs, what arrives is short, and the ground where he fell gets heavier. |
| **CF-D023** | Is the tree behind the wall on the Gideon estates a Hist, or a cutting? | **A** Archein: ours, and older than the Office. **B** Agaceph: a cutting taken off the Rootlands, and a cutting holds nothing. **C** Miredancer: a true tree that has stopped answering, which is a third thing and worse than either. |
| **CF-D024** | Is a hatchling given sap from a vessel given a soul? | **A** Archein: sap is sap. **B** Miredancer: the tree must be present, and what comes out of a cup is a body with something borrowed in it. **C** Drowned Court: yes, and they come back short, and the burial figures have said so for four hundred years. |
| **CF-D025** | Was the Duskfall one night, or generations? | **A** Sarpa: one night — every xanmeer fell at the same angle and you can only see that from above. **B** Miredancer archive: generations, and the Sap-Speakers' own tablets change hand three times inside it. **C** Paatru: three burnings, because the clay has three layers and clay does not lie. |
| **CF-D026** | Does the bone a Dead-Water warrior wears hold the dead back from the root? | **A** Dead-Water: it holds him here and he fights. **B** Drowned Court: the pattern left at death and a bone is a bone. **C** Miredancer: it holds nothing and it delays the return, and the delay is a real cost paid by somebody who is not consulted. |
| **CF-D027** | What were the elevated stone walkways between the xanmeers for? | **A** Sarpa: roosts, and we were on them. **B** Agaceph: roads, and nobody flew. **C** Imperial antiquarian: aqueducts, on the evidence of a fall of one part in four hundred. |

Two notes a critic should check rather than take.

**CF-D022 and CF-D024 do not contradict hard canon and were checked against it.** CF-003 says the
soul returns to its Hist and may be issued again; CF-002 says the hatchling's sap gives it its soul.
Neither states the Hist's *reach*, and neither states whether the sap must be taken at the tree.
Canon is silent in exactly the gap these two disputes occupy, which is the only place a dispute is
allowed to live.

**CF-D025 and CF-D027 are the deep-time entries**, and they exist because of W1-23's §5 measurement:
21 distinct years are named in the shipped build and **not one of them is outside the Third Era**,
while "four hundred" appears 121 times and "four thousand" 18 as span phrases. A province that
gestures at depth and dates nothing has atmosphere instead of a past. These two are dated by nobody,
argued by three peoples each, and — this is the part that matters — **checkable by a player who
walks a walkway and looks at what wore it.** A dispute the player can settle for himself is texture.
A dispute about a date he can never reach is a typo with a schema.

#### 4.2 Written as arguments — deliberately **not** registered

Every one of these was a candidate dispute and was rejected under the ruling. They are written into
books and dialogue as arguments, with heat, and no registry entry, because there is no fact under
them.

1. **Whether the coast should be emptied.** Dead-Water yes, Agaceph not like this, Miredancer will
   not say. Worth doing, not is-the-case.
2. **Whether an outsider may be guided inland.** The Agacephs do it. The Paatru hold that doing it is
   the crime and the outsider is only the instrument.
3. **Whether the Sap-Speaker should keep drinking now the sap has stopped answering.** The most
   painful argument in the province and there is no fact under it at all: everybody agrees she is
   being poisoned and everybody agrees she is coming back with nothing.
4. **Whether an Argonian with an Imperial name may be buried at a well.** Miredancers will. Agacephs
   will if asked by the family and not otherwise. Paatru have not been asked.
5. **Whether the Archein distinction — factor, not quay — is a distinction.** The Archein will argue
   it for as long as you will stand there. Nobody else in the province concedes it and none of them
   can name the fact it turns on, because there isn't one. **This is an argument and registering it
   would flatter it**, which is the sharpest illustration in this file of why the ruling exists.

### 5. The lease, and whose word it is

The measured ratio was fifty to one and the build's vocabulary was the Provincial Office's. After
this file the word *lease* is still the most common word in the province for the thing, and three
things have changed about it:

1. **An Argonian says it, about Argonians, on his own ground.** The Archein hold CF-D008 position A —
   the Empire's line — and are now the loudest holders of it. A euphemism that only the occupier uses
   is a foreign object. A euphemism the occupied have adopted and improved is a colony.
2. **The people it is about have mouths and use other words.** The Paatru do not say *lease*: two of
   theirs are on the Blackrose roll and the Paatru word for what happened is the word for being taken
   in the dry season, which is a season-word and not a commerce-word, and it does not translate, and
   the Assize clerk who wrote it down wrote *"detained, voluntary presence at trade-edge."*
3. **The Archein "will not" is the mechanism.** *Never at the quay, always through a factor.* That is
   how a chain of custody becomes a chain of clean hands, and the player meets the factor.

The self-test breaks each check on purpose — a people with no NPC, a people with no info, the books
removed, the register credited to nobody, the refusal topic emptied, a seventh people copied off the
first — and confirms each goes red. Two of the nine assertions bracket the collision arithmetic from
both sides: three shared axes must fail and two must not.

**The one honest weakness, stated where a critic will find it.** The five axes live in prose in §2
and the tool's table is a *transcription* of them. A builder who changed §2 and not the table would
pass. What the tool does guarantee is that the collision count is not computed by the person who
wants it to come out clean, and that the presence, position and refusal checks read the shipped
build rather than this file.

### 7. What the register does with them

Measured on the same build with the instruments W1-23 left behind, before and after this item landed:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| registered disputes | 21 | **27** |
| disputes fully voiced from every position | 21 / 21 | **27 / 27** |
| contradiction edges in shipped books, registered | 50 / 50 | **57 / 57**, unregistered 0 |
| topics on which two real people give incompatible registered answers | 4 | **10** |
| registered lines that vanish when a holder is struck from the register | 11 / 11 | **43 / 43** |
| people in the province holding at least one registered position | 336 / 336 | **347 / 347** |
| registered facts carrying a date or an explicit undated horizon | 12 (8 pre-Third-Era) | **14 (10 pre-Third-Era)** |

The perturbation is the number that matters. Strike the Agacephs out of CF-D027 position B and the
Agaceph guide stops having a view about the high walks — not a duller view, no view. A people's
position is a game object here, which is the whole difference between a society and a set of
adjectives.

**And the fail-closed direction was tested rather than assumed.** Pointing CF-D027/A at an actor no
NPC carries takes `boot-check` from PASS to a thrown `canon register: 1 unresolved reference`. The
content was written before the assertion, and the assertion was then confirmed to be live by
breaking it.

## Comparison method

Runnable by a fresh agent with this file and the game data directory. No browser.

1. **Presence.** Each of the six is named in shipped content, and named by more than one kind of
   source:
   ```
   node tools/lore/tribe-census.mjs
   ```
   Fails if any of the six has **zero** dialogue infos, **zero** books, or **zero** NPC records; and
   fails if any people is present *only* as a mention — the check is a mouth, a body and a text.
2. **Differentiation.** For each of the five axes (diet, Hist, Empire, lukiul, refusal), no two of
   the six may hold the same answer on three or more axes. Two peoples colliding on 3+ → **FAIL**;
   the second is decoration.
3. **Registry.** Both halves, exit-coded:
   ```
   python3 corpus/80-methods/canon-check.py --validate-registry
   node tools/lore/canon-census.mjs
   node tools/lore/build-canon.mjs --check
   ```
   Any dispute in CF-D022..CF-D027 with a position voiced **zero** times → that position is fictional;
   fix or delete it (RI-LOR06 §4).
4. **The consumption test, which is the one that matters.** The register is only real if a tribe's
   position is a *game object*:
   ```
   node tools/lore/canon-consumption.mjs
   ```
   Strike a tribe out of a position's `holders` and its lines must **vanish**, not degrade. A build
   where they survive the strike has tribes as flavour text and has failed this item regardless of
   how well the flavour text reads.
5. **Adjudicator hunt**, per RI-LOR06 §5, run over the new material specifically. Any text that
   settles CF-D022..CF-D027 → **FAIL**.
6. **Invented-people guard.** Every people named in `game/data` must appear in §1's table or in §1's
   rejected-names table with a reason. A seventh people appearing in shipped text without an entry
   here → **FAIL for silent invention**, which is the failure this file's whole first section exists
   to make impossible.
7. **Prose.** `node tools/check-prose.mjs --file` on every file this item writes. The two bars this
   material is most at risk on are `questions` (floor 39.8/10k) and `sting` (ceiling 6.0/10k):
   peoples explaining themselves to a stranger is the exact shape of writing that produces a lecture
   with a punchline at the end of every paragraph.
8. **Opacity.** `node tools/world/opacity-resolve.mjs --leak-scan` must stay CLEAN. The Sarpa see the
   province from above and the Paatru dig; both are one bad sentence away from CF-C010.

## How we lose

1. **Six hats.** Each people gets a colour, a weapon and an adjective, and behaves identically. The
   test is check 2 and it is deliberately mechanical, because taste will pass six hats.
2. **The noble savage, six times.** Every interior people wise, every coastal one corrupt. The
   Paatru bury eggs and kill strangers on a calendar; the Dead-Water take from other Argonians; the
   Archein are the only people here who read, and the file must not reward them for it or punish
   them for it.
3. **The tribes become a fifth faction.** Ranks, a joining quest, a questline each. They are not
   organisations and the moment one has a rank ladder it has become the Fighters Guild in a mask.
   §3's joining rule is the guard.
4. **The Xul-Aneekh gets tidied.** A later agent notices the consensus is incoherent and makes the
   six agree, because a faction that disagrees with itself is hard to write quests for. **The
   incoherence is the content.** This is RI-LOR06's "How we lose" #1 arriving through a quest
   designer rather than a critic.
5. **The lease gets fixed by find-and-replace.** Somebody reads §5, greps for *lease*, and swaps in
   *slave*. That destroys the finding: the point is that the word stays and acquires an Argonian
   mouth.
6. **Somebody answers CF-D025.** The Duskfall is undated in every in-world source *on purpose*
   (W1-23 §5), and a tidy agent with three positions in front of it will want to pick one. The
   registry's ruling is sealed out of the build; the pressure is not.
7. **The Sarpa become a mount, or a race option.** Winged people are the single most likely thing in
   this file to get promoted out of the fiction and into a traversal system.
8. **Twelve tribes by wave 3.** The names are free and the differentiation is not. §1's rejected
   table is there so that adding one is a decision with a paragraph attached.
