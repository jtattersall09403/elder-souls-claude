## The bar

A player double-clicks a link and, without reading anything, without being told anything, and
without crossing a single screen that is not either the world or a piece of paper inside the
world, ends up **standing in a place, in a body, holding an input device that works**, with
somebody in front of them who wants something. Everything that defines the character is
extracted from them *by that person, in that place, as part of what that person is doing* —
and the world is visible behind their head while it happens.

That is Morrowind's opening and the reason it is still the most-praised opening in the genre
twenty-four years later. It is not praised for its content; the content is a customs officer
doing paperwork. It is praised because **character creation is a scene rather than a screen**.
The questions are asked by a bored bureaucrat who is going to stamp a form; the answers are
recorded on that form; you walk out of the room carrying the form. Nothing is ever described
to you as a "class", a "build", or a "step 2 of 4". The game does not begin after character
creation — character creation is the first ten minutes *of the game*, and you are walking
around inside it.

The bar, stated so a builder can aim at it: **zero full-screen non-diegetic panels between the
page load and the first meaningful choice**, one title surface, control of a body before the
game asks a single question about that body, every character-defining input delivered as an
answer to a named NPC standing in a room, and not one word of instruction that a person
standing in that room could not plausibly be told.

## The reference artifact

### A. `MW/OPEN` — Morrowind, Seyda Neen, load → out of the door (canonical-recall)

Beat-for-beat, with the property each beat establishes. Times are from a cold start on
period-typical hardware and are recalled, not measured.

| # | t (approx) | What happens | Player state | Property established |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00 | Title. A menu with **New / Load / Options / Credits**. No splash video that cannot be skipped. | no body | One surface, one decision |
| 2 | 0:05 | Black. A voice: *"Wake up. We're here. Why are you shaking? Are you okay?"* | no body | The game addresses you before it defines you |
| 3 | 0:20 | You are **in control**, in the hold of a ship, undefined. Jiub is standing there. Crates. A door. | **CONTROLLABLE, UNDEFINED** | **You get a body before you get a character** |
| 4 | 0:40 | Jiub asks your **name**. You type it. He remarks on it. | controllable | Name is asked by a person who then uses it |
| 5 | 1:10 | Up the stairs, onto the deck. Weather, gulls, a coastline, a guard telling you to get moving. | controllable | The world is shown before it is explained |
| 6 | 1:40 | Down the gangplank into the **Census and Excise Office**. Socucius Ergalla behind a desk. | controllable | The interface for creation is a desk |
| 7 | 2:00 | *"...and your race?"* You choose. He writes it down. | controllable | **Race is a fact recorded on a form** |
| 8 | 2:30 | *"Under which sign were you born?"* You choose. He writes it down. | controllable | Birthsign is asked as small talk |
| 9 | 3:00 | Class: **(a)** name a profession from a list, **(b)** describe yourself in detail (custom class), or **(c)** *"answer some questions"* — ten in-fiction dilemmas ("A wounded dog lies in the road…") that infer the class. | controllable | **The class system is a personality quiz administered by a customs officer** |
| 10 | 5:00 | He confirms the record aloud, stamps it, hands you a **document**, and tells you to see Sellus Gravius in the next room. | controllable | Creation ends with an object you carry |
| 11 | 5:30 | Sellus Gravius: a package, a note, orders to go to Balmora and find Caius Cosades. He does not explain who that is. | controllable | The main quest arrives as a job, not a prophecy |
| 12 | 6:00 | You open a door and are **outside, in Seyda Neen**, with no objective marker and no instruction. | controllable | The tutorial ends by ending |
| 13 | ~8:00 | Fargoth is looking for a ring. The ring is in a barrel. You may give it back, keep it, or tell him where it is. | controllable | **First meaningful choice with two persistent consequences** |

**Screens crossed between load and control: one** (the title menu).
**Full-screen non-diegetic panels between control and the door: zero.**
**Tutorial pop-ups in the entire sequence: zero.**
**Number of times the player is told what they are: zero.** (You are not told you may be the
Nerevarine until hours later, and the first person who says it does not believe it.)

### B. `DS/OPEN` — Dark Souls, Asylum → first bonfire (canonical-recall)

The Souls half of the comparison, and the half we **lose** on where it conflicts.

| # | t | What happens | Property |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00 | Title, then an unskippable-once cinematic (skippable thereafter) | — |
| 2 | 1:30 | **Class + Gift selection: a full-screen menu on a black background** | The non-diegetic pattern we reject |
| 3 | 3:00 | You wake in a cell. A corpse drops a key through the grate. | Control arrives with no explanation |
| 4 | 4:00 | Corridor. **Scrawled messages on the floor teach the verbs**: attack, roll, parry. | **Diegetic-adjacent instruction: writing on the ground, not a pop-up** |
| 5 | 5:00 | The **Asylum Demon** drops through the ceiling. You have a broken sword hilt. You cannot win. You run through a side door. | The game teaches you to leave |
| 6 | 6:30 | **First bonfire.** | Checkpoint established before the first real fight |
| 7 | 8:00 | Sword, shield, the roll corridor, then the demon again — now winnable | The loop is taught by repetition, not by text |

**Ruling under ARBITRATION.** Character creation is *outside the fight*, therefore **Morrowind
is authoritative** and Dark Souls' class-select-on-black-background **loses**. We take Souls'
*instruction* method — writing scratched into the world at the exact place the verb is first
needed — because Morrowind's opening has no combat instruction at all and ours must (see
`RI-JRN03` M-DISC). We take Morrowind's *creation* method entirely.

### Naive pass (isolation: `enforced`)

A fresh agent (fleet role `JC-01N`) receives: the URL, the driver's stdio protocol, and one
sentence of premise. It receives **no corpus, no data files, no source, no screenshots from
anyone else**. It is asked, in this order, and its answers are recorded verbatim before any
reveal:

1. Before touching anything: *"You are about to start a third-person action RPG in a browser.
   Write down what you expect the first two minutes to be."* (This is the pre-registration
   that makes M17 computable.)
2. Play until you are outside the first building. Then: *"Describe everything that happened,
   in order. Mark each thing: **the game told me**, or **I found it**."*
3. *"Who are you? How do you know?"*
4. *"What were you asked, and by whom, and where were you standing?"*
5. *"What are you supposed to do now, and who told you?"*
6. *"List everything you did not understand."*

| # | Check | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| **M16** | The agent can answer 3, 4 and 5 **without** naming a menu | 3/3. **Hard fail:** the answer to 4 is "a character creation screen". |
| **M17** | Expectation delta | The agent's pre-registered expectation (1) mentions a title screen and a character creator; the actual sequence must differ from it in a way the agent describes as *the game starting earlier than expected*. Recorded as a quotation, not a number. |
| **M18** | `found_fraction` — items in answer 2 marked "I found it" | ≥ **0.30**. A first ten minutes that is 100% delivered is a corridor. |
| **M19** | Confusion census (answer 6) | Every entry is triaged: **legitimate mystery** (the world withheld something on purpose) vs **defect** (the player could not act). Defects ≤ 1; **hard fail** ≥ 3, or any defect that blocked progress. |

## How we lose

Written pessimistically, in advance, so a critic can tick them off.

1. **The title screen with a Play button.** Load → logo → "PLAY" → a second menu → "NEW GAME" →
   a loading bar → a character creator on a black background → *then* the game. Five surfaces,
   two of which exist because a framework generated them. This is the single most likely
   outcome and it is a hard fail.
2. **Character creation is a React component.** Sliders, a rotating model on a gradient, tabs
   labelled Race / Class / Appearance, a "Randomise" button, and a big "BEGIN" at the bottom
   right. Every individual piece is competent. The whole thing is the opposite of the bar.
3. **The bureaucrat is a skin on the menu.** An NPC portrait is drawn next to the same sliders,
   the world is a blurred backdrop image rather than the live scene, and the questions are
   *"Choose your class:"* with a dropdown. Diegesis is claimed and not delivered. M5's frame-area
   and live-entity checks exist specifically to catch this.
4. **The questionnaire is cut for time.** It is the most expensive of the three class routes to
   write (ten dilemmas, each with three answers, each mapping to a profession weighting) and the
   easiest to defer to "wave 3". It never gets written, and the opening becomes a dropdown.
5. **Control arrives after definition.** The build does creation first because that is the order
   the data needs, so the player's first thirty seconds are a form. Morrowind's actual trick —
   *you walk around as an undefined person* — is the cheapest and best part of the opening and
   will be the first thing lost to load-order convenience.
6. **A tutorial appears because the controls are not discoverable.** `RI-JRN03` fails, so
   somebody adds a control overlay to the opening to compensate, and O11 dies to fix a different
   item's gap. **Cross-rule: an instruction pop-up may never be added as the remedy for an
   `RI-JRN03` or `RI-JRN04` gap.**
7. **"Quest added: Deliver the package."** A toast, because a toast is what every engine ships
   with and because it feels helpful. AR-2 fail, and it costs nothing to not do.
8. **The opening is built for mouse.** Creation uses a cursor, hover states and drag; it works
   beautifully on a desktop and is unusable on a phone with a GameSir clamped to it. M13 catches
   it; the fix is expensive if it is found late, which is why parity is a hard fail here rather
   than an observation.
9. **An unskippable studio logo.** Two seconds, once, forever, on every reload the critic does.
   It is also the thing the player sees forty times during their first hour of dying.
10. **The stamped document is a flag, not an object.** It exists in the save file, is never
    rendered, cannot be read, and no NPC mentions it. O10 is satisfied on paper and delivers
    nothing.
11. **The first meaningful choice is not in the first settlement.** The opening is beautiful and
    then the player walks for twelve minutes to reach anything with two outcomes. `T_choice` is
    met by a technicality and the opening does not close.
12. **Audio unlock becomes a surface.** A "Click to enable sound" panel appears because autoplay
    policy required a gesture and nobody folded it into the New Game click. One more screen.
13. **Everything works on the second run and nobody tested the first.** Cold cache, cold IndexedDB,
    no save present, first-ever pointer lock request. The critic must always measure with a fresh
    browser profile — recorded in the artifact or the measurement is void.
